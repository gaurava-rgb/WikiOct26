# Amazon: the store that rents out its own tools

**Thesis:** Amazon keeps building giant tools for its own use (warehouses, servers, planes, chips), then rents those same tools to everyone else, rivals included.

## The whole arc

Amazon took nine years to turn a profit ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher ch.8]], p.237). For years it put nearly every penny into warehouses and cloud capacity, and it now harvests profits from that build ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher ch.8]], p.249). It sold computing as AWS, and in 2026 it began selling its freight and delivery network the same way ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability|Amazon's Durability]]). Now it is doing the same with AI chips, while outside AI shopping agents send it only a small share of shoppers so far ([[2026-04-30-Amazon-Earnings-Trainium|Amazon Earnings, Trainium]]).

## The eras

1. [[journeys/amazon-journey-1997-2022|Amazon, 1997 to 2022: building the tools]]. Patience, warehouses, AWS, Kindle, planes. Rival: Walmart.
2. [[journeys/amazon-journey-2023-2026|Amazon, 2023 to 2026: renting out AI, freight and power]]. AI bets, Bedrock, Trainium, freight for rent, the power wall. Holds "Moving forward."

## The lesson

Thompson argues Amazon turns running costs into up-front building costs, then sells that capacity to other firms ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability|Amazon's Durability]]). The idea behind it is [[First-Best-Customer]]: use a tool first, then sell it.
