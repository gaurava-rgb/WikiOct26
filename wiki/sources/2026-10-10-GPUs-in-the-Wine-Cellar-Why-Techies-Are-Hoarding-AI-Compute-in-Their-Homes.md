# GPUs in the Wine Cellar: Why Techies Are Hoarding AI Compute in Their Homes

Nick Wingfield, Aaron Tilley, The Information, published 2026-10-10.

## Summary

Tech workers are filling basements, attics, garages and even wine cellars with GPUs and Macs to run AI at home instead of renting cloud compute. They cite cost, privacy, reliability, price hikes like Cursor's, and fear that governments could cut off access, and agent tools like OpenClaw added fuel. Apple and Dell report strong sales from this crowd. The catch is heat, noise and costly power and cooling upgrades.

## Key claims

- Former Microsoft executive Gary Flake built a home computer with seven Nvidia GPUs from discounted parts for under $6,000 to train AI models on terabytes of brain-scan (EEG) data. (§ Gary Flake's basement rig: “building his own machine from discounted parts cost under $6,000”) ^clm-aa19f4786540bd177cfa
- Flake says owning the machine beat paying AWS or Microsoft monthly cloud fees, even after $10,000 to $20,000 of electrical upgrades and a new cooling system to handle its heat. (§ Gary Flake's basement rig: “That holds even after $10,000 to $20,000 he spent on electrical upgrades”) ^clm-605d52746fad376287c2
- Gary Flake summed up his choice to run AI on his own hardware: I want to own. I don't want to rent. (§ Gary Flake's basement rig: “I want to own. I don't want to rent.”) ^clm-dad52f48b3f6d6153303
- The Information says running AI in attics, basements and home offices is an unexpected outcome of the AI boom, while leading AI firms run models in data centers with tens of thousands of GPUs. (§ A local AI movement: “Running AI in attics, basements and home offices is an unexpected outcome of the AI boom”) ^clm-7256df866c5a353f6878
- A growing local AI movement of small developers and tinkerers runs AI on their own computers for cost, privacy and convenience, and some libertarian-minded developers see it as a guard against governments cutting off access. (§ A local AI movement: “Some libertarian-minded developers believe local AI ensures governments cannot take away their access”) ^clm-c21986d73b9b6f2c73a8
- Autonomous agent platforms like OpenClaw, which work with a user's local apps and files, helped fuel the home AI movement. (§ A local AI movement: “Autonomous agent platforms like OpenClaw, which can do complex tasks with a user's local apps”) ^clm-dba8ac85ff9d7aca17f9
- In September 2026 Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke went viral with a photo of three racks of AI gear in his Toronto wine cellar, including a Dell computer with an Nvidia GPU marketed as the ultimate deskside supercomputer. (§ Tobi Lütke's wine cellar: “Nvidia markets as the "ultimate deskside supercomputer."”) ^clm-3399b95c98f4ff41d3c5
- As of October 2026 Apple is seeing booming sales of its Mac mini and Mac Studio, both favorites of AI agent developers running machines at home. (§ Computer makers benefit: “Apple is seeing booming sales of its Mac mini and Mac Studio”) ^clm-9340c5a0271c016edb71
- Dell says it sees huge growth from the home AI community, and Dell engineer Marc Hammons said sales grew explosively after OpenClaw appeared earlier in 2026. (§ Computer makers benefit: “Dell saw explosive growth after OpenClaw appeared earlier in 2026”) ^clm-4c5df0f705cfd29eaa4b
- Home AI rigs follow a decades-old homelab tradition: early-2000s media servers for MP3 and video files, then home crypto mining rigs that drove demand for powerful GPUs. (§ Homelabs have a long history: “Later, crypto mining drove demand for powerful home GPUs”) ^clm-9b70bde02cf52603e36d
- Crazy Egg CEO Hiten Shah, who mined Ethereum on eight home GPUs in 2020, now runs 18 computers at home for AI development, including 14 Mac minis and Mac Studios. (§ Hiten Shah: from crypto to AI: “has 18 computers in his Redwood Shores, Calif., home, including 14 Mac minis and Mac Studios”) ^clm-89bbcbc153d8e4b8e1e8
- Hiten Shah says local compute gives him more control over his software's performance because cloud AI models suffer outages and slowdowns, and models get janky for days after each new cloud release. (§ Hiten Shah: from crypto to AI: “Every time there's a new model in the cloud, you see models get janky for days”) ^clm-a8a377fcfa5eae76808a
- Heat is the hidden cost of home AI rigs: Hiten Shah's home office sometimes reaches 85 degrees Fahrenheit from his machines. (§ Hiten Shah: from crypto to AI: “his home office sometimes hits 85 degrees Fahrenheit (about 29 Celsius) from the machines”) ^clm-0deeaf3805c3fe13e9ff
- The YouTuber Digital Spaceport runs a garage home data center with five AMD GPUs, running Alibaba's open model Qwen 3.8 27B to edit and analyze his videos. (§ YouTubers with home data centers: “He currently runs the Chinese open-source model Qwen 3.8 27B on it”) ^clm-dc478aa8b6b3f0f993f3
- YouTuber 0xSero moved his four Nvidia 6000 GPUs from his home office to the attic in Poland because they were noisy and ran so hot they dried out the room. (§ YouTubers with home data centers: “were noisy and ran so hot they dried out the room”) ^clm-6ca746675e402bb5befd
- 0xSero says Cursor raising its prices in late 2024 was the moment he decided to switch from cloud AI to local AI. (§ YouTubers with home data centers: “That was the point that I needed to figure out local AI.”) ^clm-e5827b0d2bf5e01792a3
- 0xSero argues people should own and control their AI systems because governments may one day try to restrict access to the technology. (§ YouTubers with home data centers: “governments may one day try to restrict access”) ^clm-3f50f3682fac2df96006
