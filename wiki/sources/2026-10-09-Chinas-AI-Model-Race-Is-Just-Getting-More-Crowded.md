# China’s AI Model Race Is Just Getting More Crowded

Juro Osawa, The Information, published 2026-10-09.

## Summary

China's AI model race is getting more crowded as game maker miHoYo, phone maker Xiaomi and delivery app Meituan build their own models next to ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent and five model startups. Unlike the U.S., where OpenAI and Anthropic lead, no Chinese lab has pulled ahead. The piece says firms distrust each other and prefer to build their own AI, distillation of U.S. frontier models levels the field, and an IPO-or-nothing culture blocks the acquisitions that would consolidate the market.

## Key claims

- Three big Chinese tech firms from outside AI, game maker miHoYo, phone and car maker Xiaomi, and delivery app Meituan, are now building their own large language models from scratch. (§ The core story: “Three big Chinese tech names from outside AI are now building their own large language models”) ^clm-5b7dc6af8c5e7d825a9c
- The Information argues the new entrants will further intensify China's AI race, where competition on talent and price has already produced models that significantly narrowed the gap with the best U.S. models. (§ The core story: “produced models that have significantly narrowed the gap with the best U.S. models”) ^clm-6d0dbe7e5d250c2122f7
- In the U.S., OpenAI and Anthropic hold a clear technical lead over followers such as Meta, SpaceX and Google, while in China no lab has pulled meaningfully ahead as of October 2026. (§ How China differs from the U.S.: “OpenAI and Anthropic hold a clear technical lead over a few followers”) ^clm-447355abfc5320e8e964
- ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent are still racing to build their own AI models, funded by profits from e-commerce, social media and other businesses. (§ How China differs from the U.S.: “The three giants, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent, still race to build models”) ^clm-0016ff04fab013211aab
- Five Chinese AI startups earn recurring revenue from their own models: Z.ai and MiniMax went public in Hong Kong in January 2026, and DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and StepFun are preparing to go public soon. (§ How China differs from the U.S.: “Zhipu (also called Z.ai) and MiniMax, went public in Hong Kong in January 2026”) ^clm-5347f7b2be93258ddac0
- miHoYo, the Shanghai maker of Genshin Impact, plans to invest up to 100 billion yuan ($14.9 billion) in AI development over the next three years and aims to be a top-tier Chinese lab within a couple of years. (§ miHoYo: “miHoYo plans to invest up to 100 billion yuan ($14.9 billion) in AI development”) ^clm-e4dd0d4edd45e68625f4
- miHoYo is trying to get access to Nvidia's Blackwell chips to train more competitive models, like leading Chinese AI firms do, per a person with direct knowledge. (§ miHoYo: “trying to get access to Nvidia's advanced Blackwell chips to train more competitive models”) ^clm-ddae05e3b2dd3e68e31e
- Xiaomi builds the AI for its devices in-house, with CEO Lei Jun planning to put its MiMo models into gadgets and cars, unlike Apple, which partnered with Google for the models behind Apple Intelligence. (§ Xiaomi: “Unlike Apple, which partnered with Google for the models behind Apple Intelligence”) ^clm-78a4856a0ace5daf7a53
- Xiaomi made a splash in 2025 by poaching a star researcher from DeepSeek. (§ Xiaomi: “Xiaomi made a splash last year (2025) by poaching a star researcher from DeepSeek”) ^clm-4bc3dd716383e4e545f3
- Xiaomi's flagship MiMo-V2.6-Pro, released in September 2026, ranks ninth on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, on par with xAI's Grok 4.7 and ahead of the flagships Alibaba and Z.ai launched weeks earlier. (§ Xiaomi: “ranks ninth on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index”) ^clm-3ab0c87a5cd99e1c7bc0
- The consolidation many predicted during China's 2023 'war of 100 models' has not happened, because Chinese tech firms deeply distrust each other and see building their own AI as cheaper than buying rivals. (§ Why no consolidation: “executives see building their own AI as cheaper than buying rivals”) ^clm-ac0678eb7f78e646a12c
- Meituan CEO Wang Xing said on a 2025 earnings call that the only sensible AI strategy is to play offense by investing in Meituan's own models rather than defending its existing business. (§ Why no consolidation: “the only sensible AI strategy is to "play offense" by investing in its own models”) ^clm-d85f580272abc0e8f2d8
- Meituan says its LongCat 2.0 model, released in June 2026, gives it a structural advantage in long-term cost control, though the model still trails many Chinese models. (§ Why no consolidation: “LongCat 2.0, released in June 2026, gives Meituan a structural advantage in long-term cost control”) ^clm-b6bd5048a531949a34b6
- Bernstein analyst Robin Zhu says Chinese founders worry that not owning a model could leave them vulnerable if they lose access to outside platforms. (§ Why no consolidation: “lacking their own models could leave them vulnerable if they lose access to outside platforms”) ^clm-a3de32689e7be77e8971
- Employees of Chinese AI labs say distillation, training a model on outputs of frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI, lets many Chinese firms compete, and U.S. chip export controls pushed labs toward it as a shortcut. (§ Distillation levels the field: “U.S. export controls on advanced chips pushed many Chinese labs to use distillation as a shortcut”) ^clm-f0f821e8037a63275856
- The Information argues reliance on distillation may make it harder for Chinese labs to stand apart, but widely known techniques also level the playing field for all of them. (§ Distillation levels the field: “also level the playing field for every Chinese competitor”) ^clm-12bdac29fbe4b625e05a
- Big AI acquisitions like AMD's $8 billion purchase of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs or SpaceX's $60 billion takeover of Cursor are unheard of in China; Alibaba and Tencent take minority stakes in Chinese AI labs but buy none. (§ No big M&A in China: “AMD's recent $8 billion purchase of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs”) ^clm-d981189ee40e220b35c7
- Investor Rui Ma says China developed an IPO-or-nothing mentality, so buyers rarely offer prices founders accept and never build acquisition skills, which keeps China's AI field crowded. (§ No big M&A in China: “Ma says China developed "an IPO-or-nothing mentality,"”) ^clm-7bb4b567f50ddcc581e7
