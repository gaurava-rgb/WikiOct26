# Data Center Discontent, Understanding the Opposition, Fixing the Problem

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-18.

## Summary

Local pushback against data centers is spreading fast in the US, and polls show most people do not want one nearby. Thompson says the anger is really about AI, since people fear it will take jobs, and unlike with factory closures they now get a vote. Tech has no friends on the left or the right, so politics will not save it. Better messaging and control of misinformation will not work well either. His fix is to pay nearby residents cash every year, which he says would amount to a kind of basic income paid for by AI.

## Key claims

- Hill County, Texas, voted 3-2 to put a one-year moratorium on data center and power plant construction in unincorporated areas, citing up to eight planned data centers. A moratorium is a temporary ban. (§ Data Center Discontent: “voted 3-2 Tuesday to put a yearlong moratorium on data center and power plant construction”) ^clm-c447b57d4c1518d18911
- In Missouri, a small town unhappy with its city council's approval of a data center voted out all four incumbents running for reelection. (§ Data Center Discontent: “voted last month to oust all four incumbents running for reelection”) ^clm-1d16b3c93fe7f91fdaca
- North Carolina Governor Josh Stein says sales tax exemptions for data centers cost the state up to $57 million a year. A sales tax exemption means the company skips the state's tax on things it buys. (§ Data Center Discontent: “sales tax exemptions for data centers cost the state up to $57 million every year”) ^clm-7c42ffed24eae01acf4c
- Texas has hundreds of data center sites operating or in development, second only to Virginia among US states. (§ Data Center Discontent: “Texas has hundreds of data center locations operating or in development, second only to Virginia”) ^clm-210e100263a362871521
- Some state officials are having second thoughts about data centers and are looking to roll back tax incentives. (§ Data Center Discontent: “some are looking to roll back tax incentives”) ^clm-d350912e9e998d417326
- A Gallup poll found 71% of Americans oppose building AI data centers in their area, 48% strongly, while only 7% strongly favor them. (§ Data Center Discontent: “Seven in 10 Americans oppose constructing data centers for artificial intelligence in their local area”) ^clm-9067680c25cd99433361
- In the same Gallup survey, 53% oppose a local nuclear plant, and nuclear opposition has never been above 63% since Gallup began asking in 2001. (§ Data Center Discontent: “Since Gallup first asked the nuclear power plant question in 2001, the high point in opposition has been 63%”) ^clm-34aa796c9d5daec53f88
- Majorities of every party oppose a local data center, but Democrats are more likely to be strongly opposed than Republicans (56% vs 39%), with independents at 48%. (§ Data Center Discontent: “Democrats are much more likely than Republicans to be strongly opposed, 56% vs. 39%”) ^clm-cbf1b169c46aeac38884
- Thompson argues that not building out nuclear power fifty years ago was a huge mistake that feeds data center opposition today, through worries about energy bills. (§ Data Center Discontent: “one that contributes directly to data center opposition today, given questions about the impact on energy bills”) ^clm-9ab6aee4d22fca8360ea
- Thompson argues there is a lot of misinformation about data center water use, but fixing it through education or calling people stupid will not work, because people believe what they already want to believe. (§ Understanding the Opposition: “trying to solve the misinformation problem through education”) ^clm-5c9fbd4e861f9e4e5aa6
- Thompson argues there was little opposition when data centers were built for the general Internet, so today's opposition cannot be separated from fear of AI taking jobs. (§ Understanding the Opposition: “the current wave of opposition can't be divorced from AI”) ^clm-578d8d714b8640c48be0
- Thompson argues that much of the huge AI investment rests on the thesis that AI will cut costs by replacing some human jobs. (§ Understanding the Opposition: “replace the jobs of some number of humans”) ^clm-21de7e1efddac603798e
- Thompson contrasts AI with globalization: towns had no say when factories moved to China, but data centers need permission, so people now get a say and use it to block them. (§ Understanding the Opposition: “people who didn't have a say in globalization are suddenly finding they do have a say about AI”) ^clm-6bd4d53fc4261fd9dd4b
- Thompson argues tech has no constituency in partisan politics: the left opposes Big Tech, and the right's base hates Big Tech almost as much, after what it saw as censorship during COVID. (§ Understanding the Opposition: “the tech industry has no constituency in partisan politics”) ^clm-77f751bff91fc9aadf00
- Thompson argues tech's messaging is hard to fix: many at leading AI labs truly believe most jobs will go away, future benefits are hard to describe, and tech has always been bad at relating to the rest of society. (§ Fixing the Problem: “genuinely believe that most jobs are going away”) ^clm-3a9e55b05944e3eb9cb9
- Thompson notes TikTok is a major vector for data center opposition, and its algorithm is still controlled by a Chinese company, while China has an interest in the US not building more AI. (§ Fixing the Problem: “one of the major vectors for data center opposition is TikTok”) ^clm-43df69af8d2bd17403cd
- Thompson argues Meta has learned not to overtly censor misinformation again, and so will not defend its own interests on data centers. (§ Fixing the Problem: “doesn't want to overtly censor again”) ^clm-2b048fa2087d899e0306
- X is now owned by SpaceX, and Thompson argues X has an interest in being anti-datacenter because SpaceX's upcoming IPO pitch includes building data centers in space. (§ Fixing the Problem: “a big part of SpaceX's upcoming public offering is the possibility of building data centers in space”) ^clm-6dbd4e53f39759adf4d3
- Meta goes beyond stressing local economic benefits of its data centers, including funding workforce training, but Thompson argues this is not concrete enough to offset AI anxiety. (§ Fixing the Problem: “I don't think any of this is concrete enough to offset people's anxiety about AI”) ^clm-a1d8d2298a88e82c2e2b
- Thompson argues the best fix is to pay nearby residents directly, such as a yearly check, rather than promise tax benefits that local governments may not spend well. (§ Fixing the Problem: “the most obvious solution is the most crass: simply start giving people money”) ^clm-c61d7909eb527396d987
- The planned DeForest, Wisconsin data center was expected to be 1.6 GW and could bring in about $3 billion a year in operator revenue; the village has about 11,500 people. (§ Fixing the Problem: “the data center up the road was expected to be 1.6 GW”) ^clm-4080c2c93e2d388e3eb8
- Thompson calculates that paying every DeForest resident $10,000 a year would cost 3.8% of the data center's annual revenue, and says operators could pass that cost on to users. (§ Fixing the Problem: “You could pay every person in DeForest $10,000 a year for 3.8% of annual revenue”) ^clm-61e929d1ee7ff87830a6
- Thompson calls QTS's $50 million community commitment for its Dane County data center relatively pathetic next to that math. (§ Fixing the Problem: “it also highlights how relatively pathetic QTS's”) ^clm-231051d37e6a5dcb42c3
- Prediction: Thompson predicts that once Big Tech pays people off for data centers, the US will have a kludgy, AI-funded Universal Basic Income, which will get data centers built and drive growth. Universal Basic Income means regular cash paid to everyone with no strings. (§ Fixing the Problem: “we'll suddenly have AI-derived Universal Basic Income”) ^clm-dc56479a88543fddf5b6
