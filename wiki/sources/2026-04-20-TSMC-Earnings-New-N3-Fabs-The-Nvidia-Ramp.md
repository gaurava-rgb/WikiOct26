# TSMC Earnings, New N3 Fabs, The Nvidia Ramp

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-04-20.

## Summary

Thompson reviews TSMC's first quarter 2026 earnings, where the company raised its 2026 revenue growth outlook to above 30% and said capex would land near the top of its $52 to $56 billion range. He is most struck by CEO C.C. Wei describing a shift from query-mode generative AI to command-and-action agentic AI, a sign TSMC now believes agents will drive compute demand. TSMC also announced new 3nm fabs in Taiwan, Arizona, and Japan, breaking its habit of never adding capacity to a node once it hits its target. Thompson argues these are in effect Rubin fabs: Nvidia always joins a node late because its huge chips need mature yields, but its demand is now so big that the original 3nm capacity is not enough. He concludes AI is transforming TSMC into something more like Intel, and that Wei, long the most bubble-worried, is now changing TSMC's approach to capitalize.

## Key claims

- Thompson corrects an earlier item: he had suggested Anthropic's Mythos model was trained on Nvidia Blackwell chips, but as of April 2026 it appears it was trained on Google TPUs, and he apologizes for passing along rumor as fact. (“it now appears it was trained on TPUs”) ^clm-f7f307234d0ded06437f
- TSMC raised its 2026 revenue outlook in April 2026 to growth of more than 30% in US dollar terms, up from earlier guidance of below 30%. (§ TSMC Earnings: “expecting revenue growth of more than 30% this year, an upgrade from previous guidance”) ^clm-5f348cc5d9c62b6bef01
- TSMC said in April 2026 that its 2026 capex (capital spending on fabs and equipment) would trend toward the high end of the $52 to $56 billion range it gave the prior quarter. (§ TSMC Earnings: “its 2026 Capex spend would fall towards the high end of the $52–$56 billion range”) ^clm-c47f0ea0a27f70e26951
- Thompson recalls finding TSMC's fourth quarter 2025 earnings alarming because the leading foundry was not investing nearly enough to meet current AI demand, let alone demand over the 3 to 5 years it takes to build and ramp a new fab. (§ TSMC Earnings: “wasn’t investing nearly enough to keep up with current AI demand”) ^clm-758511e1da6bcdc35818
- Thompson says TSMC's first quarter 2026 earnings brought no big capex news beyond landing at the top of the range, which he calls trending in the right direction. (§ TSMC Earnings: “That’s certainly trending in the right direction!”) ^clm-d794b0694613b38c7e0f
- TSMC CEO C.C. Wei said in April 2026 that the shift from generative AI in query mode to agentic AI in command-and-action mode is driving another step up in tokens consumed and in demand for leading-edge chips. (§ TSMC Earnings: “The shift from generative AI and the query mode to agentic AI and the command and action mode”) ^clm-6073f85642bdb7b2bdec
- C.C. Wei said in April 2026 that TSMC's customers and their customers, mainly cloud providers, keep sending very strong signals, so TSMC's conviction in a multi-year AI megatrend remains high. (§ TSMC Earnings: “our conviction in the multi-year AI megatrend remain high”) ^clm-ca8f3d28b95f0bb10688
- Thompson says that in years of reading TSMC earnings calls he does not remember a TSMC executive being this specific about end-user applications as Wei was about agentic AI. (§ TSMC Earnings: “a level of specificity in terms of end user applications that I don’t ever remember”) ^clm-01c6dd56d5349ed653e8
- TSMC said in April 2026 it cannot tell conventional server CPUs apart from AI server CPUs, so it still leaves CPUs out of its AI accelerator revenue count, though it might include them later. (§ TSMC Earnings: “we are not able to differentiate conventional server CPU vs AI server CPU”) ^clm-95fb4619ec6c06734323
- Thompson argues Wei and his team were convinced, perhaps by Jensen Huang, that agents are real and will drive a massive expansion of compute demand, which matches Thompson's own thesis but matters far more for the industry if it is Wei's too. (§ TSMC Earnings: “agents would drive a massive expansion of compute demand”) ^clm-08ee611359fa50cdf5b2
- Wei said TSMC historically does not add capacity to a node once it reaches its target, but in April 2026 TSMC is stepping up capex to expand 3nm (N3) capacity worldwide to meet strong AI demand. (§ New N3 Fabs: “Historically, we do not add additional capacity to a node once it reaches its targeted capacity”) ^clm-0c7b769229a0f28e1f13
- TSMC announced in April 2026 a new 3nm fab in its Tainan GIGAFAB cluster in Taiwan with volume production in the first half of 2027, and said its second Arizona fab, already built, will run 3nm with volume production in the second half of 2027. (§ New N3 Fabs: “Volume production is scheduled for the first half of 2027. In Arizona, our second fab”) ^clm-c4014ba552170c54068e
- TSMC said in April 2026 it now plans to use 3nm technology in its second fab in Japan, with volume production scheduled for 2028. (§ New N3 Fabs: “we now plan to utilize 3-nanometer technology in our second fab”) ^clm-a360d387b499f1853f08
- In an update Thompson quotes from 2024, N3 was already 15% of TSMC revenue, followed by 35% from N5 and 17% from N7, with N3 mostly Apple and lowering margins in its early years. (§ New N3 Fabs: “N3 is already up to 15% of TSMC revenue, followed by 35% at N5”) ^clm-cb4b0ebed76dab64ba5c
- Thompson argued in 2024 that TSMC, once a trailing-edge foundry profiting from long-depreciated fabs, was shifting to an Intel-like model driven by leading-edge revenue and high prices, which was changing how it manages its fabs. (§ New N3 Fabs: “TSMC’s shift to a more Intel-like model in terms of its revenue drivers”) ^clm-e0c6be68c0fba6f81b34
- Thompson argued in 2024 that one of Intel's challenges in becoming a foundry is that it lacks much depreciated trailing-edge capacity throwing off cash, because it repurposed old fabs for each new node. (§ New N3 Fabs: “they simply don’t have much depreciated trailing edge capacity throwing off cash”) ^clm-9fe0c1b069a4ba161dcb
- Thompson argues that TSMC building brand-new 3nm fabs years after the node began ramping in 2022, rather than reworking 5nm fabs as in 2024, could not be more different from its traditional model, meaning AI is transforming TSMC completely. (§ New N3 Fabs: “AI is transforming TSMC completely”) ^clm-cb1fedeeaed66d156d7a
- Thompson says Apple has long been TSMC's most important customer for each new process, paying and suffering early defects to be first, as with the N3B process for the A17 Pro and M3, which he calls a dead end that was still worth it to Apple. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “the N3B process used for the A17 Pro and M3 chips was a dead end”) ^clm-403ee54b9f51f80a63e4
- Thompson describes TSMC's usual node order: Apple ramps first and moves on, then Qualcomm, then AMD, and in the pre-AI era Nvidia and AMD GPUs came last. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “That’s when Qualcomm would come onto the process, and then AMD”) ^clm-46531074f8272357d711
- Thompson explains Nvidia has always come late to new TSMC processes because its datacenter GPUs are as big as the reticle allows, so one defect ruins a whole GPU instead of 1 of 8 or 9 Apple A-series chips, making early yields too punishing. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “it simply isn’t economical to be the launch customer, because the yields are too punishing”) ^clm-39787932c25e0251a8ef
- Nvidia's Blackwell is made on N4P, a variant of TSMC's 5nm process, and Rubin will be Nvidia's first chip on TSMC's 3nm process, as of April 2026. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “it’s Rubin that will be the first chip on the 3nm process”) ^clm-30401694f635c927ca44
- Thompson argues Nvidia used to be able to take over Apple's old capacity on a node, but its needs are now so large that TSMC's initial 3nm capacity is not enough, so the new 3nm fabs are basically Rubin fabs. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “now their capacity needs are so large that the initial 3nm capacity isn’t enough”) ^clm-201742be86edacc8f0b1
- Prediction: Thompson predicts TSMC's new 3nm fabs will probably use an Intel-style copy-exact approach, cloning proven lines, so they should be reliable and fast, with large-scale production by 2027. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “which will probably be built using an Intel-style copy-exact approach”) ^clm-335244167b7afd70ac2b
- Thompson says C.C. Wei had been the person most worried about an AI bubble, for good reason, but as of April 2026 is not only saying AI is real but meaningfully changing TSMC's approach to capitalize on it. (§ The Nvidia Ramp: “Wei has been the person most worried about a bubble”) ^clm-83daf64063fbe047a9fb
