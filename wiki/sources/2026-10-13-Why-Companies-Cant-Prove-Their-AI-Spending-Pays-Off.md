# Why Companies Can't Prove Their AI Spending Pays Off

Laura Bratton, The Information, published 2026-10-13.

## Summary

A Wharton and GBK Collective survey of 1,400 employees at large U.S. firms finds most companies measure AI returns as hours saved and fewer errors, not new revenue, and that hard financial impact is very difficult to find. Some 80% of managers report positive returns, but the strongest returns come at firms that track growth outcomes. AI spending is still rising in 2026, though fewer managers expect substantial budget increases in 2027. Separately, Workday followed Salesforce in letting outside AI agents operate inside its software, and will charge per agent action from February 2027.

## Key claims

- A Wharton School and GBK Collective study surveyed 1,400 U.S. employees at firms with at least 1,000 staff and over $50 million in revenue, finding most companies track AI returns by worker efficiency rather than new revenue. (§ The Wharton study: “Most companies still track AI ROI by worker efficiency, not by new revenue”) ^clm-40158a6f5e4fc1b55270
- In the 2026 Wharton study, 40% of managers measured AI's impact by hours saved per employee and 37% by fewer worker errors, while only about 27% track revenue from customer-facing AI products. (§ The Wharton study: “Only about 27% of managers said they track revenue from AI features”) ^clm-496364acb7b1e40ae496
- Prediction: In the 2026 Wharton study, 44% of managers said AI's main role over the next one to two years will be productivity, and just 8% said new revenue or new products. (§ The Wharton study: “just 8% said it will be to drive new revenue or create new products”) ^clm-e48cc3173b4f13aba37c
- Wharton study author Jeremy Korst said it is very difficult to find materially measurable financial impacts from AI spending, and that measuring returns today rests on managers' perception more than hard numbers. (§ What the author of the study says: “measuring returns today is more about vibes and managers' "perception" of a return”) ^clm-37cdc14873393fdbd01e
- Jeremy Korst argues the current level of AI return is normal for an early rollout phase and in line with past technology adoption cycles. (§ What the author of the study says: “in line with past technology adoption cycles”) ^clm-dc13bc3249a640ba47be
- In the 2026 Wharton study 80% of managers reported positive AI returns, and the 28% reporting strongly positive returns were more likely at companies measuring growth outcomes such as new revenue. (§ Who reports returns: “were more likely to work at companies that measure AI by growth outcomes”) ^clm-e6dbd9f8af2243d3329f
- In a recent survey of The Information's subscribers, 35% said they get a multiple return on AI spending, but only 52% reported a positive return overall. (§ Who reports returns: “only 52% said they get a positive return overall”) ^clm-094223155316fc92633f
- Novo Nordisk built AI agents to audit and coordinate clinical trials and expects extra revenue of tens or hundreds of millions of dollars a year. (§ Few revenue examples outside tech: “Novo Nordisk built AI agents to audit and coordinate clinical trials”) ^clm-4a3d658565208477985e
- Walmart CEO David Guggina said in September 2026 that customers using its AI assistant Sparky spent 40% more per order on average. (§ Few revenue examples outside tech: “customers using its AI assistant Sparky spent 40% more per order on average”) ^clm-d5f9fa15dea742464d15
- The Information reports that non-tech firms with revenue gains from AI are few and far between; most examples it has cited are labor and time savings. (§ Few revenue examples outside tech: “non-tech firms with revenue gains from AI are few and far between”) ^clm-79a34190397d6dc64775
- About 45% of managers said their companies will spend $10 million or more on AI in 2026, up from 39% in 2025. (§ Spending outlook: “will spend $10 million or more on AI in 2026, up from 39% in 2025”) ^clm-2542116e422fa2f9546b
- Prediction: Only 10% of managers expect their company's AI spending to rise substantially in the next 12 months, down from 19% in 2025, suggesting fewer big budget increases in 2027. (§ Spending outlook: “just 10% of managers expect their AI spending to rise "substantially" in the next 12 months”) ^clm-376322e5c0a094e020be
- The Wharton study suggests corporate AI spending will keep rising fast only if companies show strong returns tied to growth outcomes like new revenue and output, not cost cutting. (§ Spending outlook: “will keep rising fast only if companies show strong returns tied to growth outcomes”) ^clm-349bd87faa0936d8299b
- On October 13, 2026 Workday unveiled tools letting customers use AI agents from Anthropic, Microsoft and OpenAI to automate tasks in Workday, similar to Salesforce's SIforce, earlier called AIforce and Headless 360. (§ Workday joins Salesforce in going headless: “The tools resemble Salesforce's SIforce, which was called AIforce a month earlier and Headless 360”) ^clm-e173711615ae09d3702d
- Some employees and investors fear these headless tools turn Workday and Salesforce into dumb infrastructure, basically glorified databases, while their leaders say they will still make plenty of money. (§ Workday joins Salesforce in going headless: “basically glorified databases”) ^clm-295717aa76ad0220d408
- Workday CEO Aneel Bhusri said Workday is giving customers choice, and that headless use through other companies' AI will be equally good for Workday, though he prefers its own interface. (§ Workday joins Salesforce in going headless: “headless will be equally good for Workday”) ^clm-6440c2b7a83e5192c4e4
- Starting in February 2027, after a fee-free grace period, Workday will charge customers based on how often AI agents tap connectors and take actions in Workday. (§ Workday joins Salesforce in going headless: “Workday will charge customers based on how often AI agents tap connectors and take actions”) ^clm-abc24be218565eca8040
