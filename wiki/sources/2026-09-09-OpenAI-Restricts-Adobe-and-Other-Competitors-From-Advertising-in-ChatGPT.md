# OpenAI Restricts Adobe and Other Competitors From Advertising in ChatGPT

Alix Coutures and Amir Efrati, The Information, published 2026-09-09.

## Summary

OpenAI quietly told partners it will no longer run ChatGPT ads for image and audio generation tools, which compete with its own features, catching advertisers like Adobe off guard. Media firms often refuse rival ads, but an expert says platforms usually do this only once they are large. ChatGPT ads launched in February 2026 and reached a $1 billion annualized pace by August, short of a $2.4 billion goal for the year. The more products OpenAI builds itself, the more advertisers it may turn away.

## Key claims

- OpenAI told some business partners it will no longer take ChatGPT ads for products that generate images or audio, which compete with ChatGPT's own features. (§ The core story: “it will no longer take ChatGPT ads for products that generate images or audio”) ^clm-31c73ece6850b39caaac
- The ad restriction had not been reported before, is not in OpenAI's published ads policy, and caught advertisers including Adobe off guard. (§ The core story: “It caught existing advertisers off guard, including Adobe”) ^clm-8a5284fbe5eac02172c2
- Adobe was part of ChatGPT's early 2026 ads pilot and ran ads for Acrobat Studio and its Firefly image generator. (§ How it came out: “It ran ads for Acrobat Studio and Adobe Firefly”) ^clm-841c401649f44b31c968
- Adobe's Doug Wyatt said ChatGPT is narrowing both paid and organic pathways for tools in categories where it competes, with image and audio closed and video still open. (§ How it came out: “both the paid and organic pathways”) ^clm-2381092abe0c959d3f77
- The restriction came as OpenAI announced ChatGPT Images 2.5 in September 2026, after unveiling ChatGPT Live voice models in July. (§ How it came out: “OpenAI announced ChatGPT Images 2.5 (an image model update) on Tuesday”) ^clm-6dec9984c018c2be81ee
- Consultant Alan Chapell argues platforms usually do not cut off partners until they reach a certain size, and he expected this clash only a year or two later. (§ Is this normal?: “platforms usually do not cut off partners until they reach a certain size”) ^clm-6e4763de252d9e391c28
- More than 1 billion people use ChatGPT at least once a month, and most do not pay. (§ OpenAI's ad business: “More than 1 billion people use ChatGPT at least once a month”) ^clm-f306b04b571634dc170c
- ChatGPT ads started showing for some US users in February 2026, with launch advertisers such as Ford. (§ OpenAI's ad business: “ads started showing for some U.S. users, with big launch advertisers such as Ford”) ^clm-5a5f5abf98264911381d
- ChatGPT ads have spread to more than 40 countries, with more expansion planned. (§ OpenAI's ad business: “Ads have since spread to more than 40 countries”) ^clm-7679ac403322713dd3c3
- OpenAI said in August 2026 that its ad revenue hit an annualized pace of $1 billion. (§ OpenAI's ad business: “ad revenue hit an annualized pace of $1 billion”) ^clm-a3af98f6937213bce2b8
- Prediction: OpenAI had told investors it hoped for $2.4 billion in ad revenue for all of 2026. (§ OpenAI's ad business: “it had told investors it hoped for $2.4 billion in ad revenue for all of 2026”) ^clm-cb413cee154db854d54c
- In August 2026 OpenAI called ads one pillar of its business, next to consumer subscriptions, enterprise offerings and usage-based APIs. (§ OpenAI's ad business: “OpenAI called ads one pillar of its business”) ^clm-d45a3a85b8a3e8c518c7
- The Information notes that if the ban spreads to other competing products, it could shrink OpenAI's potential ad revenue just as ads are meant to monetize free users. (§ The tension: “it could shrink OpenAI's potential ad revenue”) ^clm-9115d4594d1c62677ad4
