# Nvidia Earnings, The AI Stack, Nvidia’s New Reporting

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-26.

## Summary

Nvidia's April 2026 quarter was a record, with $81.6 billion in sales, and Thompson calls it predictable in a supply-constrained world. He notes Nvidia grew without Anthropic, the fastest-growing lab, which only now is adding Nvidia capacity. He lays out a ten-layer AI stack and argues that in the long run value goes to whoever integrates around a bottleneck, then weighs Nvidia's moats: CUDA, first claim on supply, and selling networking and whole systems. The most interesting change was Nvidia's new reporting, which splits data center sales into hyperscalers (about half, and the customers most eager to reduce Nvidia to a chip maker) and ACIE (AI clouds, industrial, and enterprise), which buy the full Nvidia stack. Thompson reads the rising ACIE share as bullish lock-in, and ACIE as the natural market for Nvidia's new Vera CPU, though much neocloud capacity still serves hyperscalers and labs.

## Key claims

- Nvidia's sales for the April 2026 quarter reached $81.6 billion, up 85% from a year earlier and 3.4% above the $78.9 billion analysts expected. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Sales for the April quarter reached $81.6 billion, up 85% from the year-earlier period”) ^clm-73fc816544ee9e5af08d
- Nvidia's net income for the April 2026 quarter was $58.3 billion, more than three times a year earlier and 36.5% above the $42.9 billion analysts predicted. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Net income was $58.3 billion for the quarter, more than three times the year-earlier result”) ^clm-2773b6dafd62fe461c0c
- Nvidia's networking hardware sales tripled from a year earlier to a record $14.8 billion in the April 2026 quarter. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Sales of networking hardware tripled from a year ago to a record $14.8 billion”) ^clm-19b6d0a8b0032ce9abc8
- In May 2026 Nvidia announced an $80 billion share buyback and raised its quarterly dividend from one cent to 25 cents a share, and CFO Colette Kress said it intends to return 50% of this year's free cash flow to shareholders. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “an $80 billion share buyback plan and an increase in the company’s quarterly cash dividend”) ^clm-0f6e18cf54efeac42276
- Jensen Huang said in May 2026 that demand has gone parabolic because the era of agentic AI is here. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Demand has gone parabolic,” Chief Executive Jensen Huang said”) ^clm-e1ca90a02f8e1fcca1c6
- Thompson says Nvidia's May 2026 earnings were fairly predictable, as expected in a supply-constrained world, while it sharply raised the cash it returns to shareholders. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “as you would expect in a supply-constrained world, fairly predictable”) ^clm-5f284f351c258e645883
- Colette Kress said in May 2026 that mainstream AI has moved from one-shot inference to reasoning and now to agentic, and that growth at Anthropic and OpenAI is accelerating, including breakout growth in OpenAI's Codex since GPT-5.5. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “including breakout growth in OpenAI’s Codex since the launch of GPT-5.5”) ^clm-1b40279b3d0597b3cde4
- Thompson notes that although OpenAI had announced a deal to run on Trainium, as of May 2026 all of OpenAI's training and inference ran on Nvidia chips, so more OpenAI usage means more Nvidia usage. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “for now all of their training and inference run on Nvidia chips”) ^clm-b77c656a72328b241648
- Jensen Huang said in May 2026 that Nvidia's coverage of Anthropic had been largely zero until just recently, because Anthropic ran on Google TPUs and Amazon Trainium after taking their investment. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “coverage of Anthropic has been largely zero until just recently”) ^clm-ec4a08996f8ff7dc6781
- Huang said in May 2026 that Nvidia added Anthropic as a partner that year and is securing capacity for it across Azure, AWS, and CoreWeave, and that the capacity for Anthropic this year and next will be very significant. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “the amount of capacity that we’re going to bring online for Anthropic this year and next year”) ^clm-586b27815a762bc12e52
- Huang said in May 2026 that Nvidia is growing share in inference very quickly because the number of frontier model companies grew, naming Cursor, Perplexity, TML, and Reflection. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “We are growing share in inference, and we’re growing share in inference very, very quickly”) ^clm-1621b3f5934df1923e5f
- Thompson argues it was an under-appreciated bullish point that Nvidia grew so fast without the fastest-growing lab, Anthropic, but that this also shows Nvidia's place in the AI stack is not unassailable. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Nvidia’s position in the AI stack is not unassailable”) ^clm-67d90a173da76eab9316
- Thompson lays out the AI stack from the bottom: semiconductor equipment, logic fabs, packaging, memory fabs, chip makers, networking, data centers, model makers, applications, and users. (§ The AI Stack: “The AI Stack looks something like this, starting from the bottom”) ^clm-21a1f5cd4284deac9a29
- Thompson argues that while shortages last there is money at every layer of the AI stack, but once supply meets demand most value goes to whoever integrates around a bottleneck and commoditizes everyone else. (§ The AI Stack: “integrate around a bottleneck in the stack and commoditize everyone else”) ^clm-98b0a2a99e507632b017
- Thompson argues Nvidia's traditional CUDA moat matters to model makers but not most AI developers, and Anthropic and Google have shown it is not essential for training and serving a leading edge model. (§ The AI Stack: “CUDA does matter to model makers, but Anthropic (and Google) has already demonstrated that it’s not essential”) ^clm-9fa8a432ceca0f27f666
- Thompson argues Nvidia's first claim on supply from TSMC and memory makers is a short to medium-term moat but not a lasting source of value once more supply comes online. (§ The AI Stack: “this is not a sustainable source of value capture in the very long run”) ^clm-3abe4c27279f8c23b842
- Thompson names Nvidia's third moat as selling networking and whole systems, which Huang described as extreme co-design across chips, systems, networking, and software built over three decades. (§ The AI Stack: “extreme co-design across chips, systems, networking and software”) ^clm-03e06dda651a5db9fa9a
- In May 2026 Nvidia moved to new reporting: Data Center and Edge Computing platforms, with Data Center split into hyperscale and ACIE (AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise). (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “hyperscale and ACIE, which incorporates AI Clouds, Industrial and Enterprise”) ^clm-92e00913b224872b6253
- Hyperscalers made up about 50% of Nvidia's Data Center revenue as of May 2026; Thompson calls them frenemies, since they are the most interested in and able to reduce Nvidia to just a chip maker. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “they’re a big part of the business — 50% of Data Center revenues”) ^clm-d6d049f7af6dbbef7640
- Thompson argues the main motive for Nvidia's reporting change was to break out ACIE, data centers that need the full Nvidia stack and so will let Nvidia capture outsized value in the long run. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “the driving motivation behind this change was to break out the new ACIE submarket”) ^clm-12e182bb4e4695c9f0fd
- Huang said in May 2026 that AI native clouds, enterprises, industrial firms, and sovereign AI clouds want to buy and operate systems rather than design them, so semi-custom chips do not apply, and Nvidia's share there is very large. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “semi-custom chips just don’t apply because these data centers want to buy systems”) ^clm-a4e055c7b6b236a249f5
- Thompson argues that ACIE's share of Nvidia sales has risen over the last year and that this is bullish, because in a supply-constrained world Nvidia picks its customers and every point of share outside hyperscalers means lock-in for future sales. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “every point of share that is elsewhere is not just an indicator of current Nvidia sales but also lock-in”) ^clm-b71877bfbcf3bc1c68bb
- Thompson cautions that ACIE growth is not true demand diversification for Nvidia, since much neocloud capacity ultimately serves the hyperscalers and frontier labs. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “a huge amount of the neocloud capacity is actually just going to serve the hyperscalers”) ^clm-c802cf53b2dccf29a5a2
- Kress said in May 2026 that Nvidia's Vera CPU opens a new $200 billion market, will deliver up to 1.5x faster performance per core than x86 alternatives, and that Nvidia sees nearly $20 billion in CPU revenue this year. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “We have visibility to nearly $20 billion in total CPU revenue this year”) ^clm-9e869213c8687f125404
- Thompson argues $20 billion would not make Nvidia the largest CPU maker, and that neoclouds in ACIE, not hyperscalers with their own CPUs and AMD and Intel ties, are the obvious buyers of Vera. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “the far more obvious customers are the ACIE category, particularly the neoclouds”) ^clm-a312c81a74ef04a0807b
- Thompson argues a 50/50 revenue split between hyperscalers and ACIE implies far more units to hyperscalers, since ACIE buys much more than chips and likely pays more as a captive customer base. (§ Nvidia’s New Reporting: “a 50/50 split in revenue between hyperscalers and ACIE almost certainly implies far more units”) ^clm-4ced57b562e183c9e76f
