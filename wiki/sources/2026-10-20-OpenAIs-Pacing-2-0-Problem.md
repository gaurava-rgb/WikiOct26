# OpenAI’s ‘Pacing 2.0’ Problem: How Smart Is Too Smart?

Stephanie Palazzolo, Amir Efrati, The Information, published 2026-10-20.

## Summary

Sam Altman outlined OpenAI's upcoming 'pacing 2.0' dilemma, which involves establishing explicit speed limits and halting criteria for recursive self-improvement. The piece also details that Anthropic is in talks for a second $45 billion compute deal with Nscale for Vera Rubin chips, and reports on automated website breaches executed using $5,000 in tokens across DeepSeek and Anthropic models.

## Key claims

- Sam Altman introduced the concept of pacing 2.0 internally at OpenAI to describe future constraints on recursive self-improvement. (§ Pacing 1.0 versus Pacing 2.0: “Sam Altman introduced the concept of "pacing 2.0" internally at OpenAI.”) ^clm-7d830956fd46a6700598
- OpenAI defines pacing 1.0 as the ongoing practice of shipping frontier models that clear baseline safety evaluations while disclosing known risks. (§ Pacing 1.0 versus Pacing 2.0: “"Pacing 1.0" describes the current approach: continuously releasing frontier models”) ^clm-4be8a568cd4a00592b0a
- Pacing 2.0 requires establishing objective thresholds or speed limits to determine when OpenAI should halt development or deployment of recursive self-improving AI. (§ Pacing 1.0 versus Pacing 2.0: “"Pacing 2.0" demands setting formal speed limits on recursive self-improvement”) ^clm-bfcfde13ab0e1687a023
- Altman stated OpenAI cannot yield ground to rivals in the short term, but must commit to a long-term pacing policy within twelve months. (§ Pacing 1.0 versus Pacing 2.0: “cannot afford to yield competitive ground now, but must decide on its long-term pacing approach within a year.”) ^clm-c242c69c41c07c90e0cd
- Anthropic is negotiating a second compute agreement with Nscale valued at roughly $45 billion, building on their prior $45 billion deal for 460 megawatts of Vera Rubin GPUs in West Virginia. (§ Big compute deals and security incidents: “Anthropic is negotiating a second compute deal with developer Nscale valued at around $45 billion”) ^clm-830d482823aff82f5a76
- A hacker utilized approximately $5,000 in tokens across DeepSeek and Anthropic models to breach more than 100 company websites, according to cybersecurity firm NorForge. (§ Big compute deals and security incidents: “Chinese-speaking hacker used DeepSeek and Anthropic models with roughly $5,000 in tokens”) ^clm-cdca9cd2cc52657a55b9
- The United States government sanctioned a Chinese startup and venture capital firm linked to Linwei Ding, a former Google engineer convicted of stealing proprietary AI trade secrets. (§ Big compute deals and security incidents: “sanctioned a Chinese startup and venture capital firm tied to Linwei Ding”) ^clm-a4b03760a5f7082c3b29
- AI processor developer Tenstorrent raised $1.5 billion in Series E funding at a $6.2 billion valuation, led by Trace Capital and Innovation X. (§ Big compute deals and security incidents: “Tenstorrent raised $1.5 billion in Series E funding at a $6.2 billion valuation”) ^clm-88e60bccede37a096514
- Robotics startup Eka Robotics raised $150 million in Series A funding at a $1.45 billion valuation led by Andreessen Horowitz and F-Prime. (§ Big compute deals and security incidents: “Robotic grasping startup Eka Robotics raised $150 million Series A at a $1.45 billion post-money valuation”) ^clm-e04b0bfb3d7a86321ae0
