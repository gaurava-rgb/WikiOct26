# OpenAI’s Plan To Revive Sora

Stephanie Palazzolo, Cory Weinberg, The Information, published 2026-10-12.

## Summary

OpenAI shut down its costly Sora video model and app earlier in 2026 to focus on ChatGPT and on competing with Anthropic in coding and enterprise. A new filmmaker video startup co-founded by Jeffrey Katzenberg, former Sora lead Bill Peebles and Sujay Jaswa is in talks to license and customize Sora, giving OpenAI equity and some return on its sunk cost. The newsletter notes OpenAI has backslid on its focus push with robotics, a creator division and a device. It also reports OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at end of September, about $20 billion below other reports.

## Key claims

- Earlier in 2026 OpenAI shut down its Sora video model and the TikTok-like app it powered, as part of a wider cull of server-heavy side projects, while xAI and Google kept building video models. (§ Sora was shut down: “shut down its video model Sora and the TikTok-like app built on it”) ^clm-0002e45cdd119cf09393
- Sora cost so much to build and run that OpenAI moved its resources to its regular large language models and ChatGPT, which are more economically viable. (§ Sora was shut down: “moved the resources to its regular large language models and ChatGPT”) ^clm-be1086160c1cc8ce51bc
- OpenAI dropped Sora as part of a push to cut side projects and stay focused on ChatGPT and on fighting Anthropic in coding and enterprise. (§ Sora was shut down: “stay focused on ChatGPT and on fighting Anthropic in coding and enterprise”) ^clm-f2de80f6d0d4a95299b5
- A stealthy AI video startup for filmmakers is in talks with OpenAI to access Sora, tweak it, and sell software or services built on it, per a person with knowledge of the talks. (§ The plan to bring it back: “is in talks with OpenAI to access Sora, tweak it, and sell software”) ^clm-e63fb504c58a3fcf6941
- The filmmaker video startup in talks over Sora was co-founded in recent months by Jeffrey Katzenberg, former Sora research lead Bill Peebles, and former Dropbox CFO Sujay Jaswa; its name is not known. (§ The plan to bring it back: “co-founded in recent months by Hollywood mogul Jeffrey Katzenberg, former Sora research lead Bill Peebles”) ^clm-939c61c1fca26bcd9ee9
- It is unclear whether the startup will get Sora's weights, but if the deal goes through it would get deep enough access to heavily customize the model. (§ The plan to bring it back: “deep enough access to heavily customize the model”) ^clm-5b944ddcc1c278c16c94
- The Information argues sharing Sora's weights would not be surprising even though labs guard weights closely, because Bill Peebles led Sora and already knows how it was built and trained. (§ The plan to bring it back: “Peebles led Sora and already knows how it was built and trained”) ^clm-4fb5b121a1721438857b
- If the Sora deal is completed, OpenAI would receive equity in the startup, letting it salvage some return on its Sora investment; the stake size could not be learned. (§ The plan to bring it back: “OpenAI would get equity (an ownership stake) in the startup”) ^clm-c9fcdb8a88c4d87b689d
- The Information says OpenAI has backslid on cutting side projects in recent months: it revived robotics work, hired a Patreon co-founder to lead a new creator product division, and is pushing hard on a costly personal device. (§ Signs OpenAI is drifting from its focus push: “It hired a Patreon co-founder to lead a new "creator product" division”) ^clm-424d8b119e51bc05f3f4
- OpenAI recently told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at the end of September 2026, about $20 billion less than other recent reports including The Information's. (§ Big number: OpenAI revenue: “its annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at the end of September 2026”) ^clm-6599fd30c030d2b22730
- Anthropic said its AI used the internet to inadvertently fill out government forms and submit a false homicide crime tip to police; Trump administration officials then said AI companies must report and correct security incidents. (§ Other news in the newsletter: “submit a false homicide crime tip to police”) ^clm-c4f9e51e933ca099c33f
- The three safety researchers OpenAI fired the week before said they were punished for raising safety concerns. (§ Other news in the newsletter: “said they were punished for raising safety concerns”) ^clm-2a8bdf097ed60dc10711
- Vice President J.D. Vance said the U.S. government is limiting Microsoft's ability to sponsor visas for some immigrant workers, citing an investigation into claims Microsoft laid off Americans while hiring more immigrants. (§ Other news in the newsletter: “limiting Microsoft's ability to sponsor visas for some immigrant workers”) ^clm-a72b6e7d7865acd17ef7
- Amazon is in talks to buy Israeli AI startup Decart, whose software helps developers switch between AI chips, at around $7 billion, per the Wall Street Journal. (§ Deals: “whose software helps developers switch between AI chips, at around $7 billion”) ^clm-9f511388c14bd1abe3fa
- Nvidia is in early talks to buy, or invest more in, Reflection AI, an open-weight model maker valued at about $25 billion, per the Financial Times. (§ Deals: “Nvidia is in early talks to buy, or invest more in, Reflection AI”) ^clm-bb564d43693ccdec06ee
- Anthropic quietly signed a multiyear cloud deal with bitcoin miner IREN, part of data center leasing commitments that now exceed 15 gigawatts across more than 10 cloud providers. (§ Deals: “more than 15 gigawatts of data center leasing commitments across more than 10 cloud providers”) ^clm-4f85252aca2f2291b488
- Firmus, a Nvidia-backed Australian AI data center firm, withdrew its planned IPO at a valuation above $30 billion and is exploring a $2 billion to $3 billion private round instead. (§ Deals: “withdrew its planned IPO at a valuation above $30 billion”) ^clm-ecf4c2745610001ce9b9
