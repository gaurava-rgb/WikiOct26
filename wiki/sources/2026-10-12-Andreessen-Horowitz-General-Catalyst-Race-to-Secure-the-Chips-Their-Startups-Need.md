# Andreessen Horowitz, General Catalyst Race to Secure the Chips Their Startups Need

Julia Hornstein, Dakin Campbell, Phoebe Liu, The Information, published 2026-10-12.

## Summary

Venture capital firms are securing AI compute for their portfolio startups as a chip shortage leaves smaller companies short of GPUs. Andreessen Horowitz, which built a 20,000 GPU cluster two years ago, is in talks to expand it with Switch and Volta, two data center firms it backed. General Catalyst is building its own data center, and Coatue, Crucible Capital and Kearny Jackson run their own compute efforts. Firms can resell spare capacity, so compute access is becoming both a recruiting tool for founders and a possible profit line.

## Key claims

- Andreessen Horowitz set up a cluster of 20,000 GPUs for its portfolio startups around 2024 and is in talks to expand it, possibly through two data center companies it has backed, per people familiar. (§ The core story: “set up a cluster of 20,000 GPUs (graphics processing units, the main AI chips)”) ^clm-16ab43eed3086425a400
- General Catalyst is working to secure a data center to supply compute to portfolio companies from early stage to growth stage, per people with knowledge of the project. (§ General Catalyst: its own data center: “the firm has been working to secure a data center for this compute”) ^clm-2442c986fd3ebbf2218d
- Earlier in 2026 the chip shortage led Microsoft and other large cloud providers to divert GPUs to internal teams or bigger customers, making supply harder for smaller startups. (§ Why VCs are doing this: “divert GPUs to their internal teams or bigger customers”) ^clm-4dd8806082749cc010dd
- Shadeform COO Jeff Jewett said compute access will be a requirement for VC firms to attract the hottest startups. (§ Why VCs are doing this: “absolutely going to be a requirement”) ^clm-d7ee5816b1952eb1e3b3
- In 2023 Daniel Gross and Nat Friedman pooled advanced Nvidia chips into the Andromeda Cluster and offered them to portfolio companies at a discount; it has since pivoted to matching compute buyers and sellers. (§ Why VCs are doing this: “Their Andromeda Cluster has since pivoted to matching buyers and sellers of compute”) ^clm-a89a122dcd3b4c5b6dc6
- Andreessen Horowitz has discussed partnering with data center developer Switch and data center platform Volta to expand its GPU cluster, per people familiar with the talks. (§ Andreessen Horowitz: Switch and Volta talks: “the firm discussed partnering with Switch, a data center developer and operator”) ^clm-020d0317512bbb59f458
- Andreessen Horowitz co-led a $300 million round in Volta in August 2026 at a $2.4 billion valuation, and reportedly was in talks in July 2026 to lead a $2 billion round in Switch. (§ Andreessen Horowitz: Switch and Volta talks: “It co-led a $300 million round in Volta in August 2026 at a $2.4 billion valuation”) ^clm-fd14531020da94dc4dd1
- Andreessen Horowitz's hardware and AI infrastructure bets are led by general partner Martin Casado; if startups do not use all the cluster capacity, the firm could resell the rest. (§ Andreessen Horowitz: Switch and Volta talks: “the firm could resell the rest to other buyers”) ^clm-0e3218056a58a75d16a7
- In May 2026 General Catalyst hired Alexis Black Björlin, who formerly helped run Nvidia's cloud computing division, as chief strategy officer focused on infrastructure. (§ General Catalyst: its own data center: “She formerly helped run Nvidia's cloud computing division”) ^clm-5f89af033eb4a84d5057
- Earlier in 2026 General Catalyst CEO Hemant Taneja surveyed founders on their compute access, and the firm weighed a shared capacity pool or negotiating on startups' behalf. (§ General Catalyst: its own data center: “CEO Hemant Taneja surveyed founders about their access to compute”) ^clm-5f8a8c1adacd75dfd4e0
- Coatue launched data center developer Next Frontier last year, which has at least two joint ventures with neocloud Fluidstack, partly to secure scarce compute for Coatue's investments. (§ Other firms: “It has at least two joint ventures underway with neocloud Fluidstack”) ^clm-d5fe53e93599c9307d6f
- Large VC funds could finance data center pushes through side vehicles, loan guarantees, or lease commitments that let a partner raise the money, and could profit from them. (§ Other firms: “They could also guarantee a loan or sign a lease commitment”) ^clm-be343117bed9e996b2d2
- Crucible Capital built a 576-chip Nvidia B300 cluster as an experiment and now sells part of the capacity to a frontier lab while its startups use it for tests and pilots. (§ Venture payback: “Its cluster has 576 Nvidia B300 chips”) ^clm-9be48f0650cb834f91b6
- SF Compute founder Evan Conrad said every major fund is attempting to own clusters to secure compute for its portfolio, and SF Compute sells VCs' excess capacity until startups need it. (§ Venture payback: “every major fund is attempting to do this”) ^clm-77078f1ba8deae7d8ae6
- In early October 2026 Kearny Jackson general partner Sriram Krishnan offered hundreds of AMD MI350 chips for lease or purchase and was flooded with founder requests. (§ Venture payback: “It was a bit of a hurricane.”) ^clm-522e01da50f1b543d34f
- Some founders told Sriram Krishnan that GPU access is like having oil during an oil shortage; he will give his GPUs to portfolio companies at no profit to attract new founders. (§ Venture payback: “having oil during an oil shortage”) ^clm-0b9f24dbb64bd68713f6
