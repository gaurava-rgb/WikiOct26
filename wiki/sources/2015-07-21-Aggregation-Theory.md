# Aggregation Theory

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2015-07-21.

## Summary

The founding statement of Aggregation Theory. Before the internet, distributors won by owning supply. The internet made distribution free and transactions nearly costless, so winners now own the user relationship and commoditize suppliers. Examples: Google, Facebook, Amazon, Netflix, Snapchat, Uber, Airbnb. These markets tend to be winner-take-all.

## Key claims

- Thompson argues the way to outsize profits in a consumer market is a horizontal monopoly in one part of the value chain, or integrating two parts into a vertical solution; before the internet that depended on controlling distribution. (“The best way to make outsize profits in any of these markets is to either gain a horizontal monopoly”) ^clm-ea99cdd6d591a9f35575
- The internet made distribution of digital goods free and transaction costs zero, which Thompson calls its fundamental disruption. (“the Internet has made distribution (of digital goods) free”) ^clm-6ce3985b602e7bd94720
- Under Aggregation Theory the best aggregators win on user experience, which attracts users, which attracts suppliers, which improves the experience in a virtuous cycle. (“the best distributors/aggregators/market-makers win by providing the best experience”) ^clm-6d0301444f447a0ae0e2
- Value shifts from incumbents that integrated backwards into supply (newspapers, publishers, networks, taxis, hotels) to aggregators, as the Conservation of Attractive Profits predicts. (“The result is the shift in value predicted by the Conservation of Attractive Profits”) ^clm-009c7dc19250d35cc7fd
- Google modularized individual web pages via search and integrated search with user data to sell highly effective advertising. (“Google modularized individual pages and articles, making them directly accessible via search”) ^clm-e33c937d8f146e6575b7
- Amazon modularized book distribution, first through e-commerce and then e-books, and integrated customer data and payments with distribution. (“Amazon modularized distribution first via e-commerce and then via e-books”) ^clm-59641cdc7304f857438c
- Netflix modularized broadcast availability by offering its whole library on demand, and integrated content buying with customer management. (“Netflix modularized broadcast availability by making its entire library available at any time in any order”) ^clm-187a7038c9cb66f60de9
- Facebook's suppliers are its own users, so it has exclusive access to content it gets for free, a stronger position than Google's. (“its suppliers are its users, so while it, like Google, aggregates content that it gets for free”) ^clm-94f2e127d9808ed908c8
- Airbnb and Uber digitized trust (and, for Uber, dispatch) to disrupt industries without a digital product. (“what makes it work is the way it has digitized — and thus commoditized — trust”) ^clm-ac57be0b9bb05a7d7f18
- Aggregators have strong winner-take-all effects because they get better as they serve more users and can serve every user on earth, which is why consumer tech firms are so highly valued. (“in all of these examples there are strong winner-take-all effects”) ^clm-1ab84e54ef9d59c807c6
- Prediction: Thompson predicted in 2015 that nearly every industry will discover a critical function that can be digitized and commodified, triggering this shift. (“I suspect that nearly every industry will belatedly discover it has a critical function that can be digitized and commodified”) ^clm-d7fa49cacbb2dc261a47
