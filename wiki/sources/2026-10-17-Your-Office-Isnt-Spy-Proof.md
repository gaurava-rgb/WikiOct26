# Your Office Isn’t Spy Proof

Abram Brown, The Information, published 2026-10-17.

## Summary

The FBI arrested former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan for allegedly planting listening devices wrapped in toilet paper inside OpenAI conference rooms. The Justice Department described him as a Saudi Arabian national, raising concerns over foreign intelligence gathering at leading AI labs. Security experts warn that fierce AI recruiting competition leads to lax background vetting, while counter-espionage startup Strider Technologies has raised $110 million to detect insider leaks.

## Key claims

- The FBI arrested former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan for allegedly planting listening devices in OpenAI offices. (§ The OpenAI espionage scandal: “The Federal Bureau of Investigation arrested former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan”) ^clm-88318307d80a5e016f87
- Alfozan allegedly placed a listening device wrapped in toilet paper inside an OpenAI conference room. (§ The OpenAI espionage scandal: “Alfozan allegedly placed a listening device wrapped in toilet paper inside an OpenAI conference room”) ^clm-983a7e1e4760b978c4f7
- The United States Department of Justice press release prominently described Alfozan as a Saudi Arabian national, implying an effort to spy for Saudi Arabia. (§ The OpenAI espionage scandal: “The United States Department of Justice press release prominently described Alfozan as a national of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia”) ^clm-22b1934b0be8ed81ff70
- Alfozan attempted to plant two listening devices, but mislaid a second bug inside a bag and retrieved it from the OpenAI lost and found. (§ The OpenAI espionage scandal: “Alfozan attempted to plant two listening devices; he mislaid a second bug inside a bag and retrieved it”) ^clm-bb2de4897151e57f42ca
- Former FBI counterintelligence official Bill Priestap stated companies recruiting under extreme pressure often bring employees on board with minimal background vetting. (§ Vulnerabilities from rapid hiring and cheap bugs: “often bring candidates on board with minimal background understanding”) ^clm-cf69002ec2c754ca6b00
- Commercial listening devices cost under $100 and can be concealed in phone chargers, fake coffee cups, or smoke alarms. (§ Vulnerabilities from rapid hiring and cheap bugs: “commercial listening devices cost less than $100 and easily hide inside phone chargers”) ^clm-8cc3506da0335cf7fa84
- Counter-surveillance startup Strider Technologies has raised $110 million to sell AI-driven communications monitoring software that detects corporate espionage. (§ Commercial counter-surveillance startups: “Strider Technologies, led by Eric and Greg Levesque in Salt Lake City, has raised $110 million”) ^clm-74e9526c5ab455c7d72a
- Strider Technologies co-founder Greg Levesque stated corporate targets usually face legitimate engineers coerced by outside handlers rather than trained intelligence officers. (§ Commercial counter-surveillance startups: “legitimate engineers or scientists who were coerced or approached by outside handlers”) ^clm-26b681ab1c9cbcaca2e7
