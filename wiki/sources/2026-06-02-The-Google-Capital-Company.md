# The Google Capital Company

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-06-02.

## Summary

Thompson reads Alphabet's June 2026 $80 billion equity raise, including $10 billion from Berkshire Hathaway, as a bet that capital, not margins, will decide AI. He contrasts asset-light Google Services with capital-hungry Google Cloud, whose revenue grew from 6% to 22% of Services' size between Q4 2019 and Q1 2026, and compares the pair to Berkshire using See's Candies cash to buy BNSF Railway. He asks why Google chose equity over debt, predicts more debt will follow if compute demand is as big as Google thinks, and argues TPUs give Google the lowest cost if compute becomes a commodity. If compute runs short, the company that can bring the most cash may win, and Berkshire is betting that is Google.

## Key claims

- Warren Buffett said at the 2017 Berkshire Hathaway annual meeting that GEICO had paid Google about $10 or $11 per click and that you have almost never seen a business like it, yet Berkshire never invested in Google. (“we were paying them $10 or $11 a click, or something like that”) ^clm-e530baf078a265b4d8f8
- Thompson argues Google has the most beautiful business model of all time: its supply (web content) is free, advertisers compete to raise its prices, users decide which advertiser pays, and it only pays a nominal bit of depreciation on its infrastructure. (“imagine that your customers willfully compete against each other to raise your prices”) ^clm-4939df748bc03825bda6
- Thompson argues Aggregators like Google maximize absolute value at the expense of relative value: each visitor, click, or result is worth less, but the total amount of value is larger in the long run. (“they maximize absolute value at the expense of relative value”) ^clm-b13d737cc3f1fa9b6866
- Thompson argues investors have valued the best tech companies for the opposite reason: they are asset-light and take a high-margin skim, so the relative amount of money made mattered more to Wall Street than the absolute amount. (“it was the relative amount of money made that was generally more important to the market”) ^clm-86d286698a18dae77edf
- Berkshire Hathaway bought See's Candies in 1972 for $25 million, when sales were $30 million and pre-tax earnings under $5 million; by 2007 sales were $383 million and pre-tax profit $82 million, with only $32 million reinvested since 1972. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Productive Capital: “We bought See’s for $25 million when its sales were $30 million”) ^clm-b85c0e45d8aecf44ce38
- BNSF Railway, bought by Berkshire Hathaway partly with See's Candies profits, consumed $3.8 billion of capital in 2025 and earned net income of $5.5 billion on revenue of $23.4 billion. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Productive Capital: “BNSF’s net income was $5.5 billion on revenue of $23.4 billion”) ^clm-ca6f81f39e4c3511bf3e
- Thompson estimates Berkshire Hathaway's total earnings from See's Candies are probably under $3 billion (the last disclosure, in 2019, was over $2 billion), less than capital-hungry BNSF Railway earned in one year. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Productive Capital: “the total amount that Berkshire Hathaway has made from See’s Candies is probably less than $3 billion”) ^clm-a2a62bfee43d1d85ca35
- In Q4 2019, the first year Alphabet disclosed Google Cloud revenue, Google Services made $43.2 billion in revenue and $13.5 billion in operating profit, while Google Cloud made $2.6 billion in revenue and lost $1.2 billion, 6% of Services' size. (§ Google Cloud’s Runway: “Google Cloud made $2.6 billion in revenue and lost $1.2 billion”) ^clm-55585662883c9610890d
- Google Cloud made its first profit in Q1 2023: $0.2 billion on $7.5 billion of revenue, while Google Services made $21.7 billion in profit on $62.0 billion of revenue; Cloud revenue was 12% and its profit 1% of Services. (§ Google Cloud’s Runway: “In Q1 2023, Google Cloud made a profit for the first time”) ^clm-32c681241217d6bbf32e
- In Q1 2026 Google Services made $89.6 billion in revenue and $40.6 billion in profit, while Google Cloud made $20.0 billion in revenue and $6.6 billion in profit; Cloud was 22% of Services' revenue and 16% of its profit. (§ Google Cloud’s Runway: “Google Services made $89.6 billion in revenue and $40.6 billion in profit”) ^clm-aa4af92023e02fb444f6
- From Q4 2019 to Q1 2026 Google Services more than doubled revenue and tripled profits, but Google Cloud grew faster, and its operating margin of 33% in Q1 2026, versus 45% for Google Services, is expanding more rapidly. (§ Google Cloud’s Runway: “33% last quarter as compared to 45% for Google Services”) ^clm-a7e36597b8dd23ee7519
- Thompson argues advertising is only a fraction of the economy while Google Cloud's growth is AI, which could take over the whole economy, so Google Services may prove to be the cash flow that built a lower-margin but absolutely bigger business, as See's Candies helped fund BNSF. (§ Google Cloud’s Runway: “Google Services provided the cash flow to build a business with relatively worse margins”) ^clm-2ebdf1c7006307e0ed9d
- On June 1, 2026 Alphabet said it would raise $80 billion in equity: a $40 billion at-the-market share program starting in Q3, $30 billion of underwritten shares and mandatory convertible preferred stock, and a $10 billion deal with Berkshire Hathaway. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “Alphabet Inc. is raising $80 billion through a package of equity offerings”) ^clm-4965a19ddf180c9f3553
- Thompson says a decent portion of Alphabet's at-the-market share program will go to paying tax on Google employee equity awards, which are large because of the stock's run-up. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “going towards paying tax obligations on Google equity awards”) ^clm-1ffc3abda3c4fdbeab15
- Google has so far funded its AI capital spending with free cash flow, and as of June 2026 held around $81 billion of debt against $126 billion of cash, leaving substantial room to borrow more. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “the company does have around $81 billion in debt”) ^clm-c393cc4a5ef022d1d8a8
- Thompson argues debt is normally the preferred way to fund investment, since interest is tax-deductible and existing shareholders keep all the benefits, while equity removes debt risk at the cost of giving up a share of future profits. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “Debt is, all things being equal, the preferred instrument for investment”) ^clm-19dbb9143fdac36cda79
- Prediction: Thompson predicts the simplest explanation for the equity raise is that Google will also start issuing a lot more debt, meaning everyone continues to underestimate demand for compute. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “Google is also going to start issuing a lot more debt as well”) ^clm-ccf06617656b46004561
- Thompson says the bearish reading is that Google is unsure about the return on its AI capex and wants to share the risk and upside; if no substantial Google debt issuance follows, that reading may be right. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “Google is uncertain about the return on investment of all that capex”) ^clm-e47032db9dd7242e7c1e
- Berkshire Hathaway invested in Google at only a slight discount to its all-time high, now that Warren Buffett no longer makes investment decisions and Greg Abel is his successor as CEO. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “Buffett is no longer making investment decisions, and Greg Abel, his successor as CEO”) ^clm-151de28e0270cc2b3d0b
- Berkshire Hathaway had $373 billion in cash at the end of Q1 2026 and generated $25 billion of free cash flow in 2025. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “Berkshire Hathaway had $373 billion in cash, and $25 billion in free cash flow in 2025”) ^clm-3e0f9df826258012a3f5
- Thompson argues Greg Abel is replaying Buffett's strategy, with Berkshire Hathaway now playing See's Candies (the cash machine) and Google playing BNSF Railway (the capital-hungry business that earns large absolute profits). (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “this time Berkshire Hathaway is See’s Candies, and Google is BNSF”) ^clm-e83ea5215438a04a2692
- Thompson argues Google has optionality on AI outcomes: its Services business benefits from the investment, it competes at the model layer with Gemini, and it can sell capacity to the frontier labs. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “its Services business benefits from the investment, it is in contention at the model layer”) ^clm-016366868b9b4f047ec6
- Prediction: Thompson predicts TPUs give Google a sustainable cost advantage, so if compute ever becomes a commodity Google is the hyperscaler poised to make the most profit. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “that capacity has a sustainable cost advantage because of TPUs”) ^clm-7a4f9b7f20fba95a7f1b
- Thompson argues the $10 billion Berkshire deal is small for both companies and is mainly a signal: Google expects demand far greater than anyone thinks and will fund supply by any means, including equity, and Berkshire's money endorses that view. (§ Berkshire Hathaway and Google Equity: “perhaps the primary utility is as a signaling mechanism”) ^clm-740079675b73379fc161
- Thompson says his April 2026 thesis, that OpenAI's compute deals were not dispositive because the best products win the users and cash to buy compute, was proven correct weeks later when SpaceX supplied Anthropic's compute at a high price. (§ Cash the Ultimate Commodity: “That thesis was proven correct just weeks later”) ^clm-41d892eb4e81c28323b9
- Prediction: Thompson asks what happens if there is not enough compute in the world to buy: the battle for compute may become who can bring the most cash to bear, compounding into more compute and more cash, and Berkshire Hathaway is betting that company is Google. (§ Cash the Ultimate Commodity: “becomes a matter of who can bring the most cash to bear”) ^clm-bc216972430f231ad37b
- Thompson notes Waymo has rejected an asset-light model of licensing its software to carmakers and instead owns and operates its own cars; he says that choice always felt at odds with Google Services but may fit Google Cloud and a capital-heavy Google. (§ Cash the Ultimate Commodity: “has so far rejected an asset-light model of licensing their software to OEMs”) ^clm-94e351877a3a2ffa9906
