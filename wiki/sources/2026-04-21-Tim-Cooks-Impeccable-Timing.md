# Tim Cook’s Impeccable Timing

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-04-21.

## Summary

Thompson assesses Tim Cook's 15 years as Apple CEO as he announces he will become Executive Chairman, with John Ternus taking over. He argues Cook's timing was fortunate: Steve Jobs died just after creating the iPhone, so Cook got to scale Apple's most important product, and he did it brilliantly through operations, new wearables, and a fast-growing Services business. But Thompson says Cook repeatedly chose the financially optimal path over long-term sustainability: a hard line on App Store fees, heavy dependence on China, and an AI strategy that relies on Google's Gemini instead of Apple's own models. He calls Cook's exit prudent, after Apple's best-ever quarter, and says only time will tell if Cook set up a crash by forgetting his own Cook Doctrine.

- Thompson argues the timing of Jobs' death made Tim Cook arguably the greatest non-founder CEO ever: founders usually stay past their peak, but Jobs left Apple only a few years after the iPhone, so Cook was the one who grew Apple's most revolutionary device. (“making Cook arguably the greatest non-founder CEO of all time”) ^clm-aef418d49a754f515fee
- Thompson says that as an intern on Apple University's team in 2010 it was clear to him the program, launched in 2008 and credited publicly to Steve Jobs, was driven by Tim Cook. (§ The Cook Doctrine: “the program’s driving force was Tim Cook”) ^clm-fe46a9fe9f114895ca1f
- On Apple's January 2009 earnings call, during Steve Jobs' first leave, Tim Cook said Apple believes it must own and control the primary technologies behind its products and say no to thousands of projects to focus on a few. (§ The Cook Doctrine: “we need to own and control the primary technologies behind the products we make”) ^clm-1e3b19c907f7d5a14b6e
- Thompson calls the Cook Doctrine brilliant but argues it is ultimately about maintaining, nurturing, and growing what Steve Jobs built, not about creating new things. (§ The Cook Doctrine: “ultimately about maintaining, nurturing, and growing what Jobs built”) ^clm-70b4c9046017294e6a6a
- After joining Apple in 1998, Tim Cook shut down Apple's own factories and warehouses and moved its manufacturing to China, building a just-in-time supply chain of worldwide suppliers; Thompson says there was no significant product issue or recall under Cook. (§ Cook’s Triumphs: “methodically shut them down and shifted Apple’s manufacturing base to China”) ^clm-cb493ed725e5657dff84
- Apple's Wearables, Home, and Accessories category, led by AirPods and Apple Watch, delivered $35.4 billion in revenue in 2025, enough to rank 128 on the Fortune 500 on its own. (§ Cook’s Triumphs: “delivered $35.4 billion in revenue last year, which would rank 128 on the Fortune 500”) ^clm-4a479d3c770b350938ef

(§ Cook’s Triumphs: “originated in 2002 with an agreement to make Google the default search service for Safari on the Mac”) ^clm-e05bd1882f94eb6c6fb0

(§ China and AI: “the Chinese aren’t just doing the operations anymore, they also have industrial design”) ^clm-8c2be4a256a3731747e5
