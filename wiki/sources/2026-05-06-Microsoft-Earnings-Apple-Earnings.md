# Microsoft Earnings, Apple Earnings

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-06.

## Summary

Thompson reviews Microsoft's and Apple's March 2026 quarters. Microsoft beat estimates with Azure up 40% and used the call to recast Microsoft 365 from a per-seat business into seats plus consumption, since agents threaten to cut seat counts; Thompson notes the shift also forces a change in how Microsoft sells and exposes it to rivals whose AI gets the usage. Apple posted record iPhone-era margins and strong guidance but faces rising memory costs and chip shortages at TSMC. Thompson admits he was wrong that on-device inference would drive Mac demand; agent harnesses like OpenClaw are what sold out Mac Minis and Studios, and Apple did not see it coming.

## Key claims

- Microsoft's March 2026 quarter sales were $82.9 billion, up 18% from a year earlier, with net income of $31.8 billion, both above Wall Street expectations. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “Sales reached $82.9 billion, an 18% increase from the March quarter a year ago”) ^clm-443eebe3fc17d6bc76f2
- Prediction: Microsoft forecast 13% to 15% revenue growth for the June 2026 quarter, and shares were roughly flat after hours. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “Shares were roughly flat in after-hours trading after the company forecast 13% to 15% revenue growth”) ^clm-d5478bab1457ba9a37f7
- Microsoft said its capital spending (money spent on data centers and equipment) will reach $190 billion this year, up 61% over 2025, while the quarter's capex of about $31.9 billion came in more than 8% below projections. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “Capital spending this year will reach $190 billion, a 61% increase over 2025”) ^clm-62f7af0146d3b723c319
- Azure grew 40% in the March 2026 quarter, in line with expectations, after Azure growth had slowed in the prior report. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “Growth in the company’s Azure cloud business reached 40%, in line with expectations”) ^clm-df846070170dc82a2f65
- Microsoft said Copilot had 20 million paid users, up 33% since January 2026, after struggling to turn its user base into paying AI customers. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “Microsoft said Copilot now has 20 million paid users, up 33% since January”) ^clm-296913003fc39eab35eb
- Satya Nadella opened the call saying agents will become the dominant workload and change the entire tech stack. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “one of the most consequential platform shifts that will change the entire tech stack as agents proliferate”) ^clm-347657afceb3455a9637
- Thompson argues agents are an existential problem for Microsoft 365 because it charges per seat, while agents do more than one person, use far more compute, and reduce how many seats customers need. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “replace or reduce the number of persons necessary, reducing the number of Microsoft 365 seat licenses”) ^clm-f91667c7635b2438944f
- Nadella said business-app customers are shifting from the traditional seat model to seats plus consumption. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “customers shift from traditional seat model to seats plus consumption”) ^clm-56b52f0a3a29c37b8688
- Amy Hood said Microsoft 365 bookings will look different over time: still a per-seat license, but also a usage meter like Azure that may bill outside bookings. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “It will still have that per seat license logic, but it will also have a meter, just like you see in Azure”) ^clm-fcde8e41c45165d096ca
- Prediction: Nadella said every per-user Microsoft business, across productivity, coding, and security, will become a per user and usage business, and that this is already happening at scale in coding. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “any per user business of ours, whether it’s productivity, coding, security, will become a per user and usage business”) ^clm-81157905ef0f6b19b5c2
- Thompson argues the shift is also an internal lift: Microsoft must change its sales motion and figure out how to pay its salespeople and partners to sell usage instead of seat coverage. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “figure out how to incentivize its own employees to make the case for this new model”) ^clm-2c695fed38d5201dd132
- Thompson argues the real danger for Microsoft is customers gaining from AI, but not Microsoft's AI, so the usage goes elsewhere, most likely to the model companies. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “customers reaping the benefits of AI, but not Microsoft’s AI, which means the usage is going elsewhere”) ^clm-096cb87f4c06d97e46f5
- Nadella argued the integration that matters is a multi-model harness coupled with constantly updated company data (meetings, documents, e-mail, Teams, SharePoint), not any one model. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “the models brought together with the harness that’s multi-model”) ^clm-8d4b35f9aad5a964def8
- Hood said the IP Microsoft gets from its partnerships (OpenAI's models) is free to it for a long time, which will help its margins. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “the IP we get from our partnerships is obviously free to us for a long time”) ^clm-16e767084ea4e08d117e
- Prediction: Thompson predicts that, despite its multi-model talk, Microsoft may become very motivated to make one model (OpenAI's) work best because it is free. (§ Microsoft Earnings: “Microsoft will suddenly find itself very motivated to make one particular model work best”) ^clm-064ad8487b73f169618e
- Apple's March 2026 quarter revenue was $111.2 billion, above expectations, with iPhone revenue up 21.7% to nearly $57 billion on iPhone 17 upgrades. (§ Apple Earnings: “revenue from iPhones rose 21.7% to nearly $57 billion in the second quarter”) ^clm-f7fd93fb00efa9fb8d8d
- Prediction: Apple guided June 2026 quarter revenue growth of 14% to 17%, far above analysts' estimate of 10%, and shares rose 2% after hours. (§ Apple Earnings: “projecting that revenue will leap between 14% and 17% during the current quarter”) ^clm-75474dfe1dd8002217b7
- Apple's gross margin (share of revenue left after the direct cost of making products) hit an iPhone-era record of 49.3%, and Kevan Parekh said results could have been better without constrained chip supply. (§ Apple Earnings: “came in at an iPhone-era record of 49.3% for the company”) ^clm-8fea09cf2cbccc69e57d
- Prediction: Tim Cook said memory costs had minimal impact in the December quarter, were partly offset by carry-in inventory in March, will be significantly higher in June, and will have an increasing impact after June. (§ Apple Earnings: “beyond the June quarter, we believe memory costs will drive an increasing impact on our business”) ^clm-75b75942d480c071b655
- Cook pitched the Mac as the best platform for AI, citing Apple Silicon's ability to run advanced models locally. (§ Apple Earnings: “Mac is the best platform for AI, with Apple Silicon delivering exceptional performance”) ^clm-4c2d054f2b6c9fbff30f
- Thompson says he was wrong in 2025 that on-device inference would make the Mac compelling for AI; Mac Mini and Mac Studio shortages come from agent workflows using harnesses like OpenClaw, which lean on the CPU and call cloud models by default. (§ Apple Earnings: “I was wrong that the primary reason that Mac’s would be compelling would be for on-device inference”) ^clm-3c00a13f2af91394f6d6
- Apple admitted it did not foresee the demand explosion for Mac Minis and Mac Studios and said it would take months to catch up, which Thompson reads as Apple not taking his Mac-as-AI-platform argument seriously. (§ Apple Earnings: “Apple definitely didn’t take my argument seriously”) ^clm-9514075cefc7885dfe44
- Apple is short of A19 Pro chips, and per Tim Culpan the popular MacBook Neo used up Apple's stockpile of A18 Pro chips, with TSMC having no spare capacity for either. (§ Apple Earnings: “Apple blew through its stockpile of A18 Pro chips”) ^clm-2ffa078fe722fc0059d6
- Prediction: Thompson speculates Apple may update the MacBook Neo sooner than its planned yearly cycle to consolidate chip demand on the A19 Pro. (§ Apple Earnings: “update the Neo sooner rather than later in order to consolidate its demand on one chip”) ^clm-f294454256e3ca2be9b5
- Thompson argues John Ternus inherits an Apple that, despite its supply chain mastery and favored TSMC status, is now second priority to AI's insatiable demand for memory and fab capacity. (§ Apple Earnings: “even Apple is a 2nd priority to the insatiable demand for everything from memory to TSMC fab capacity”) ^clm-d92222f82789e0db3d3b
