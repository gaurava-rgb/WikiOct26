# Netflix to Sell Streaming Services?, Streamers as Aggregators, Revisiting Roku

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-08-25.

## Summary

Netflix is signaling it may sell or carry other streaming services such as Peacock and Fox One, after integrating French broadcaster TF1 in June 2026. Thompson reads this as a rational pivot after the failed Warner Bros. bid: if Netflix cannot buy the content, it can distribute rival services and take a cut, ideally in the Amazon Prime Video Channels model where Netflix owns the customer. He places it in a wider race among Amazon, YouTube, and Roku to be the first app people open on the TV. Revisiting Roku, he says Netflix was right to spin Roku out in 2007 but should now have pivoted to free ad-supported streaming by buying Roku, a chance Fox took instead.

## Key claims

- Netflix co-CEO Greg Peters, asked on the Q2 2026 earnings call about distributing third-party streaming services, did not rule it out and said Netflix would consider more deals that serve members, partners, and Netflix. (§ Netflix to Sell Streaming Services?: “if we see additional deals that similarly serve our members that work for our partner, that work for us, we'll certainly consider them”) ^clm-89abadf6df22da654ed5
- Netflix integrated French broadcaster TF1 into its service in France in June 2026, its first such move, and Peters called early member engagement very promising four weeks in. (§ Netflix to Sell Streaming Services?: “We're literally four weeks in”) ^clm-f9bcbec715f2f7c65291
- Thompson argues Peters is signaling a possibly massive shift toward selling other streaming services while framing it, in classic Netflix style, as something the company has always done. (§ Netflix to Sell Streaming Services?: “they frame it as if it is something they have always done, and that nothing is changing”) ^clm-6873f118736c02ff372e
- Netflix has discussed bringing Peacock and Fox One onto its service with NBCUniversal and Fox, per the New York Times, though no deal is imminent and it is unclear whether it would absorb the content or act as a retailer. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “Netflix has had recent discussions about making other streaming services available as well, including Peacock and Fox One”) ^clm-d7f440f7448ff254a0c7
- Netflix reversed its stance: two years earlier it told shareholders it already operates as a go-to destination for entertainment, and its executives long balked at working with direct competitors. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “any move in that direction is a departure from what Netflix said just two years ago”) ^clm-d05c26cf5f19d1dc7f95
- Amazon has for years persuaded tens of millions of people to subscribe to services like HBO Max and Apple TV through its Prime Video app. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “Amazon has persuaded tens of millions of people to use its Prime Video app to subscribe to other services”) ^clm-9743205aba80ce69469d
- Thompson says it took him a long time to understand Amazon's model of selling third-party streaming services, which he was still describing tentatively as platform or aggregator in 2018. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “Amazon is acting as a platform/aggregator (still working on this!)”) ^clm-39a854bbfb5352958c0b
- Thompson argues Prime Video Channels works because Amazon was its own first customer for video content, so Prime members already had the app installed and Amazon owned the customer relationship for third-party providers. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “the company was its own first customer for video content for the Prime Video app”) ^clm-b8f83714a8fc08077528
- Thompson argues YouTube has made strikingly little progress selling other streaming services, despite his reading of its NFL Sunday Ticket deal as a bid to build such a marketplace. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “it's been striking how little progress YouTube has made”) ^clm-c6a0bc5176cad4b93dca
- YouTube struck a five-year deal in July 2026 to bring all of Peacock's content into YouTube Premium, its $16 a month ad-free tier. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “YouTube struck a major, five-year deal to bring all of Peacock's content to YouTube's paid tier”) ^clm-62370eb8b4dc2fe66f2e
- Thompson argues YouTube is bundling Peacock into Premium rather than selling it, building on its unified free-with-ads plus paid-no-ads model, and is free to experiment because Premium is not its core business, just as Prime Video was not Amazon's. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “YouTube is freer to explore bundling because Premium subscriptions aren't their core business either”) ^clm-f1d80d4f82b85d49bf1e
- Thompson drops his earlier Netflix end-game: that as the last big streamer standing it would buy content on its own terms. After Hollywood and Washington pushed back on the Warner Bros. bid, he calls distributing rival services for a cut a very rational pivot. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “if you can't buy the content, then distribute the streaming service and take a cut”) ^clm-d4a30229689b6de42169
- Thompson says the attempted Warner Bros. acquisition in late 2025 felt like Netflix trying to force his foreseen end-game into reality, and it met pushback from Hollywood and Washington. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “felt like Netflix trying to force that future into reality”) ^clm-421524787245032388f0
- Thompson argues studios such as Peacock and Fox run money-losing streaming services whose hardest problem is customer acquisition, which Netflix can supply on its own terms. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “the most difficult challenge in digital media, which is customer acquisition”) ^clm-2aaf9b2dfcaadda52ae2
- Thompson recommends Netflix follow the Amazon model: sell access to other services, own the customer relationship, and surface their content in the Netflix app. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “the model that makes the most sense is the Amazon model”) ^clm-b12c0ccaefd6985c5087
- Thompson argues the old fight for HDMI 1, the main TV input, has become a fight to be the default app people open first on their smart TVs. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “the fight is to be the default app that people go to first”) ^clm-79d8cdc19d40c62487c1
- Thompson argues Apple gave up its position: Apple TV is now just another app rather than a storefront, partly due to overpowered hardware hit hardest by Apple's memory-driven price increases. (§ Streamers as Aggregators: “Apple TV is now just another app, instead of a storefront”) ^clm-8e0e9c856b924a04b7a2
- New subscriptions sold through Roku's channels business rose 19 percent year over year in Q2 2026, according to Antenna. (§ Revisiting Roku: “rose 19 percent in the second quarter of this year compared with last year”) ^clm-332782fda6ca5059774e
- Roku began as an internal Netflix project, and Thompson argued in 2017 that spinning it out was smart because mixing horizontal and vertical business models is dangerous and Netflix needed to be on every device. (§ Revisiting Roku: “it is very dangerous to mix horizontal and vertical business models”) ^clm-2714ed6482aaf4248149
- Thompson revisits his Roku view: 2026 bears no resemblance to 2007, Netflix is everywhere, and the question now is whether it has saturated its addressable market. (§ Revisiting Roku: “it bears no resemblance whatsoever to 2007. Netflix is everywhere”) ^clm-93bdf091ff91258b1426
- Thompson argues Netflix has not saturated its market because it does not compete in free streaming, the fastest-growing area, which has synergy with pay TV as an extra window, marketing, and ad inventory. (§ Revisiting Roku: “there is one large and growing area of streaming that Netflix does not compete in: free”) ^clm-d52227919611c4f05214
- Thompson changes course on Netflix and Roku: while still backing Fox's purchase of Roku, he now says Netflix missed its pivot and should have answered the Warner Bros. letdown by moving into free ad-supported streaming and buying Roku. (§ Revisiting Roku: “the company should have responded to the Warner Bros. letdown by pivoting to FAST and buying Roku”) ^clm-822abce7f883c828c154
- Thompson judges that the Roku opportunity has passed, and that sharing Netflix's distribution with other streamers for a fee is the next best move. (§ Revisiting Roku: “leveraging the distribution they do have by sharing it with other streamers for a fee”) ^clm-27b60d5df1f169ce6d6b
