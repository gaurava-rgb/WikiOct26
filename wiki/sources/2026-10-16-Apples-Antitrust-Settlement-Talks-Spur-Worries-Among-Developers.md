# Apple’s Antitrust Settlement Talks Spur Worries Among Developers

Aaron Tilley, The Information, published 2026-10-16.

## Summary

The U.S. Department of Justice has engaged in settlement discussions to resolve its 2024 antitrust lawsuit against Apple, sparking concerns among app developers including PayPal and Epic Games that a deal will not dismantle anticompetitive restrictions. Government discussions prioritize iPhone hardware interoperability with rival smartwatches and payment apps. Meanwhile, interim antitrust division head Stanley Woodward has favored settlements to avoid prolonged court trials, even as Apple appeals a contempt referral in the Epic Games case to the Supreme Court.

## Key claims

- The U.S. Department of Justice is actively negotiating a settlement in its antitrust lawsuit accusing Apple of maintaining an illegal smartphone monopoly. (§ Developer alarms over potential DOJ settlement: “U.S. Department of Justice negotiates a settlement in its antitrust lawsuit against Apple, major software developers including PayPal and Epic Games have raised alarms”) ^clm-162d63d5867b93380f8c
- Justice Department officials have signaled a preference to settle the Apple antitrust case rather than take the dispute to trial. (§ Developer alarms over potential DOJ settlement: “Justice Department officials signaled an active preference for settling the lawsuit instead of pursuing a protracted court trial”) ^clm-cbfe50a186d3b8d4331d
- App developers PayPal and Epic Games warned the Justice Department that an Apple settlement would fail to resolve anticompetitive restrictions. (§ Developer alarms over potential DOJ settlement: “PayPal and Epic Games have raised alarms that an agreement will not adequately resolve anticompetitive conduct”) ^clm-34b41cb2918f5d40ab45
- Justice Department settlement negotiations with Apple focus primarily on iPhone interoperability with competing hardware and third-party services. (§ Core interoperability issues under negotiation: “Settlement negotiations center on iPhone interoperability with third-party devices and payment services”) ^clm-2b397b311c226dbe47f2
- Developers report that Apple's NFC tap-to-pay feature remains less smooth on rival payment apps like PayPal than on native Apple Pay. (§ Core interoperability issues under negotiation: “Apple opened NFC tap-to-pay hardware to third-party payment services like PayPal, developers report the feature remains less responsive and seamless”) ^clm-d3ec5f736f2a2c77d41b
- Government lawyers requested concrete proposals from app developers to improve iPhone technical interoperability with third-party devices. (§ Core interoperability issues under negotiation: “Government attorneys asked app developers to submit concrete technical proposals to improve iPhone interoperability with competing hardware”) ^clm-8389ec3018a30c82aa57
- Interim Antitrust Division head Stanley Woodward favors settlements over litigation to deliver faster consumer remedies and avoid lengthy trial expenses. (§ Leadership changes in the Antitrust Division: “Stanley Woodward assumed interim leadership in June 2025, advocating settlements on the grounds that negotiated agreements deliver consumer benefits faster”) ^clm-9bf98c2da06a447fc6b5
- A federal judge ruled that Apple willfully violated an injunction in the Epic Games antitrust lawsuit and referred the company for a criminal contempt investigation. (§ Parallel litigation and developer resistance: “Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers ruled last year that Apple willfully violated a 2021 injunction in the Epic Games case, referring the matter to federal prosecutors”) ^clm-49b2ba6683677d158ec7
- Apple is appealing to the Supreme Court a district judge's referral of the company for criminal contempt over its App Store injunction compliance. (§ Parallel litigation and developer resistance: “Apple is appealing that ruling to the Supreme Court”) ^clm-7ee780d9479abf54b59b
