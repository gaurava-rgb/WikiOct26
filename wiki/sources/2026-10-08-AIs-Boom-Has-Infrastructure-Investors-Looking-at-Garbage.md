# AI’s Boom Has Infrastructure Investors Looking at Garbage

Alex Eichenstein, The Information, published 2026-10-08.

## Summary

The AI data center boom pulled infrastructure investors' money and attention away from dull assets like landfills, rail terminals and regional airports. Now some investors see those assets as bargains with steady cash flows and less competition. At the same time, investors in AI assets are getting pickier because of construction delays, rising financing costs and regulatory pushback. Partners Group, BTG Capital and Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners are cited as moving into waste, transport and airports.

## Key claims

- Infrastructure investors are now keen on boring, cash-producing assets such as landfills and rail terminals, after years of AI drawing attention to data centers. (§ The core story: “investors are keen on boring assets that produce steady cash, such as landfills and rail terminals”) ^clm-8582c51826e09f007554
- Nicholas Pepper of Partners Group says the heavy focus on digital infrastructure leaves mature assets in utilities, transport and social infrastructure getting less attention, creating chances to buy them at attractive valuations. (§ Partners Group's view: “creating opportunities to acquire these assets at attractive valuations”) ^clm-00000626c154d3fd103e
- Partners Group managed $186 billion in assets at midyear 2026 and closed its fourth infrastructure fund at over $15 billion in July 2026. (§ Partners Group's view: “closed its fourth infrastructure fund at over $15 billion in July 2026”) ^clm-33f3ac2cdbed0fbeca6c
- Partners Group's data center deals include an initial $1 billion investment in AVK Power Solutions, a European data center power provider, announced in August 2026. (§ Partners Group's view: “initial $1 billion investment in AVK Power Solutions, a power provider for data centers in Europe”) ^clm-6a81710f9d70805b7c99
- In October 2025 a group including BlackRock's Global Infrastructure Partners bought Aligned Data Centers at an enterprise value of about $40 billion, the largest such sale at the time. (§ How much money AI has pulled in: “bought Aligned Data Centers at an enterprise value of around $40 billion”) ^clm-0b59289233d80a2586cb
- Blackstone has made AI a major theme across its infrastructure, credit, private equity and real estate businesses. (§ How much money AI has pulled in: “Blackstone has made AI a major theme across its infrastructure, credit, private equity”) ^clm-c73890a52dfcb929c1d6
- Investors in AI-related assets have grown more selective amid construction delays, rising financing costs and regulatory pushback, visible in delayed data center developer IPOs. (§ Signs investors are getting pickier on AI: “investors in AI-related assets have become more selective”) ^clm-0c37c22818209e3f6394
- Software fell out of favor with investors who feared a direct hit from AI, while traditional infrastructure has not suffered much from the AI focus. (§ Signs investors are getting pickier on AI: “software, which fell out of favor because investors feared AI would hit those businesses directly”) ^clm-11b51349f92d79af9ed5
- Brett Stevenson of BTG Capital argues big U.S. investors' minimum check sizes are so large they skip mid-sized deals, giving BTG a huge economic moat. (§ Where the money is going instead: “minimum check sizes are now so large that they skip this segment”) ^clm-3efa5e3be66d7c63335b
- BTG Capital bought the closed Stephenville International Airport in Newfoundland in June 2026 and reopened it on Oct. 2, 2026. (§ Where the money is going instead: “bought the then-closed Stephenville International Airport in Newfoundland”) ^clm-ec902c5d3d5e54165047
- Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners is focusing on waste management for its long-term municipal contracts and low commodity exposure, per a person familiar with the matter. (§ Waste management: “Morgan Stanley Infrastructure Partners, which has made several data center investments, is focusing on waste management”) ^clm-ecaf4d4e9891b30ae1bd
- U.S. waste management companies trade at about 15 times Ebitda versus 10 times for European ones, because U.S. firms control more of the chain from pickup to landfill. (§ Waste management: “U.S. waste companies trade at about 15 times Ebitda, versus 10 times for European ones”) ^clm-898e0f8865da81533330
- The median Ebitda multiple for strategic deals in transportation and logistics fell to 5 times in Q1 2026 from 12.7 times in 2025, per R.L. Hulett. (§ Transportation looks cheap: “fell to 5 times in the first quarter of 2026, from 12.7 times in 2025”) ^clm-c89f1a71e58a9ffb27c6
