# SpaceX and Anthropic, xAI’s Two Companies, Elon Musk and SpaceXAI’s Future

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-12.

## Summary

Anthropic agreed in May 2026 to rent more than 300 megawatts of compute at SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center in Memphis. Thompson calls the deal shocking but not surprising: Anthropic has the most demand and the most willingness to pay for scarce compute, so it outbids others, including xAI itself. He argues xAI is really two companies, a model maker and an infrastructure company, and that scarce AI hardware earns the most when sold to the highest bidder. He revives his 2022 view that Musk is at his best in the physical world, says the model fight does not suit xAI, and suggests Cursor makes more sense for Microsoft or Amazon.

## Key claims

- Anthropic signed a deal with SpaceX in May 2026 to access more than 300 megawatts of computing capacity at Colossus 1, SpaceX's large data center in Memphis; terms were not disclosed. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “access more than 300 megawatts of computing capacity from a large SpaceX data center in Memphis known as Colossus 1”) ^clm-d34e81a0d1085267078f
- Anthropic said the SpaceX deal would substantially increase its computing resources and let it raise usage limits for its Claude products. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “enable it to raise usage limits for its AI products”) ^clm-e81eea1520a6a17300ef
- Per Bloomberg, data center developers have recently locked in tenants at $1.5 million to $2 million per megawatt per year, so the Anthropic deal would be worth hundreds of millions of dollars a year to xAI. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “anywhere from $1.5 million to $2 million per megawatt per year”) ^clm-21b5326bc78f25df083d
- Dario Amodei said in May 2026 that Anthropic saw 80x growth in annualized revenue and usage in the first quarter of 2026 and is working as fast as possible to secure more compute. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “experiencing “80x growth” in annualized revenue and usage in the first quarter of this year”) ^clm-fc27167ab9f3ffa16168
- Anthropic also uses Google for chips and cloud services in addition to its SpaceX compute deal. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “Anthropic has also tapped Alphabet Inc.’s Google for chips and cloud services”) ^clm-c44fd2a48c3e6c6069a9
- Thompson argues the Anthropic and SpaceX deal is basic economics: when demand exceeds supply prices rise, and Anthropic has the most demand and the most willingness to pay, from both revenue and its ability to raise money. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “Anthropic has the most willingness to pay, not just because they are making meaningful revenue”) ^clm-c7144504032536a21ffb
- Thompson says the deal confirms his April 2026 view that Anthropic would not lose its position for lack of compute, and that OpenAI's compute was never a sustainable point of differentiation. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “didn’t see OpenAI’s compute as a sustainable point of differentiation”) ^clm-e501fb17fe58a1de1b69
- Thompson says OpenAI is almost certainly paying less per equivalent unit of compute than Anthropic, and that Anthropic getting more compute means someone else, here xAI, gets less. (§ SpaceX and Anthropic: “OpenAI is almost certainly paying less per equivalent unit of compute than Anthropic is”) ^clm-59f9878c8a4a74ccc7e2
- Thompson argues xAI has always been two companies in one, a model maker and an infrastructure company, with conflicting goals: software wants to run everywhere to spread R&D costs, while hardware wants maximum utilization at the highest price. (§ xAI’s Two Companies: “one is the model-making company, and one is the infrastructure company”) ^clm-72441aecfb34c3a05a0d
- Thompson argues that because AI hardware is in short supply, the best strategy for a hardware owner is to sell to the widest set of customers to find the highest willingness to pay, rather than differentiate it with its own software. (§ xAI’s Two Companies: “make itself available to the widest possible customer base in order to find the highest willingness to pay”) ^clm-ab4eb408d417e5a38ef6
- xAI's Colossus 1 has been reported to run at only 11% utilization, and Thompson argues that even full use by xAI's own models would earn less than opening the hardware to the market. (§ xAI’s Two Companies: “instead of the 11% utilization that has been reported”) ^clm-f20ddfeabdcb72b6c76d
- Prediction: Thompson argues Musk should also open Colossus 2 to outside customers, as he did with Colossus 1. (§ xAI’s Two Companies: “frankly, it’s what he should do with Colossus 2”) ^clm-3df9daf4769eedf6e9da
- Thompson argues the biggest driver of the deal was that Anthropic offered a price too good to turn down as SpaceX prepares for an IPO, turning Colossus 1 from an underused, depreciating asset into a revenue generator. (§ Elon Musk and SpaceXAI’s Future: “Suddenly Colossus 1 is a revenue generator, not a depreciating underutilized asset”) ^clm-9d852b1e1f7395864940
- Thompson revives his 2022 view that Tesla and SpaceX thrive because physics constrains hardware companies, while software like Twitter has no such limits, and says it fits xAI: praise goes to how fast Musk built Colossus 1 and 2, while the software has struggled. (§ Elon Musk and SpaceXAI’s Future: “That 2022 observation seems more pertinent than ever in light of xAI”) ^clm-1953ab48ed02fc0cae50
- Thompson calls Grok being used to undress women on Twitter the low point of xAI's software story, driven by a desperate push to raise usage and put idle GPUs to work. (§ Elon Musk and SpaceXAI’s Future: “Grok being used to undress females on Twitter”) ^clm-c113ce30e773a1788691
- Thompson argues that if Musk builds space-based data centers, selling to the highest bidder matters even more, since their whole rationale assumes demand will exceed earth-bound supply, so SpaceX should commit to it now. (§ Elon Musk and SpaceXAI’s Future: “in that world selling to the highest bidder is the optimal strategy”) ^clm-e400ace303c3479455a2
- Thompson now argues the model fight does not suit xAI temperamentally or economically, that Musk should stick to the physical world, and that Cursor makes more sense for Microsoft or Amazon, though the Cursor deal is not dead since Colossus 2 can still train Cursor's models. (§ Elon Musk and SpaceXAI’s Future: “Cursor makes a lot more sense for a Microsoft or Amazon”) ^clm-eddb00044f85599ce5d6
