# Nvidia's Growing Dependence On a Few Big Customers

Martin Peers and Qianer Liu, The Information, published 2026-09-13.

## Summary

Nvidia's sales lean more and more on a handful of buyers: three customers made up 44% of sales in the first half of its fiscal year, up from none above 10% in fiscal 2023. The biggest buyers likely include server makers Dell or Hon Hai and spenders like SpaceX, Meta and Microsoft, several of which are building their own chips. Nvidia hedges by investing in neoclouds and courting sovereign buyers. It is also stretching payment terms to as long as a year, which ties its cash flow to its customers' finances.

## Key claims

- In the first half of Nvidia's fiscal year ending July 2026, three customers that each made up more than 10% of sales together accounted for 44% of total sales. (§ Customer concentration, by the numbers: “three such customers made up 44% of total sales”) ^clm-0b8e1da6dff4bcf89596
- In Nvidia's last fiscal year, two customers made up 36% of sales; in fiscal 2023 no single customer reached 10%. (§ Customer concentration, by the numbers: “Last fiscal year: two customers made up 36%”) ^clm-34d07e2311799dbdd51a
- Nvidia's data center business grew from $15 billion in fiscal 2023 to $193.7 billion last fiscal year and is on track to double this year. (§ Customer concentration, by the numbers: “grew from $15 billion in fiscal 2023 to $193.7 billion last year”) ^clm-5cefa86586fc71d9bec2
- Investor Michael Burry has called Nvidia's customer concentration a major risk in recent months. (§ Customer concentration, by the numbers: “has called this concentration a major risk in recent months”) ^clm-74c760e3ec4f4f84a43f
- Nvidia's top three customers in its latest filing likely include Dell or Hon Hai, which build servers with Nvidia chips and resell them to many companies. (§ Who the big customers probably are: “likely include Dell or Hon Hai (Foxconn), which build servers with Nvidia chips”) ^clm-ddbe0ea1c035fd447020
- Dell said revenue from AI-optimized servers doubled in its second quarter to $16.4 billion, helping overall revenue grow 58%. (§ Who the big customers probably are: “doubled in Q2 to $16.4 billion, helping overall revenue grow 58%”) ^clm-722f864419455e322f73
- In February 2026, Nvidia CFO Colette Kress said the five biggest cloud firms and hyperscalers made up more than 50% of data center revenue. (§ Who the big customers probably are: “made up more than 50% of data center revenue”) ^clm-f95e63c6993118c148a5
- Microsoft, Meta and SpaceX are all making their own chips, which could shrink their Nvidia orders later; Google and Amazon already use their own chip designs. (§ Who the big customers probably are: “Microsoft, Meta and SpaceX are also making their own chips”) ^clm-aa6010cf1b944097de3e
- Nvidia invests in chip buyers such as CoreWeave and Nebius to seed new demand beyond its big US customers. (§ Nvidia's diversification push: “It invests in chip buyers such as CoreWeave and Nebius to seed new demand”) ^clm-c66f6dbb323b39e1eada
- Nvidia added several Australian companies to its AI Factory program and promoted similar build-outs in India and Armenia in 2026, aiming at sovereign customers and neoclouds. (§ Nvidia's diversification push: “aiming at "sovereign" customers (national, government-linked AI projects) and neoclouds”) ^clm-688e871d9a7871ecd55e
- In July 2026, 70% of Nvidia's accounts receivable came from five customers, up from three customers making up 56% in January. (§ Payment terms: the new risk: “July: 70% of receivables came from five customers”) ^clm-bbc1be27c092edecb8e3
- Nvidia's receivables rose 64% in the first half to $63 billion, which Nvidia attributed to extended payment terms on large multiquarter agreements with investment-grade customers. (§ Payment terms: the new risk: “Nvidia's receivables rose 64% in the first half to $63 billion”) ^clm-4f19608097778b161a04
- Dell's accounts payable jumped 48% in the first half to $49.7 billion at the end of July, and the article says Dell likely owes much of that to Nvidia. (§ Payment terms: the new risk: “jumped 48% in the first half to $49.7 billion at end of July”) ^clm-08395cb3ac90cff0dc52
- Nvidia stretched payment terms to 60 days from 45 days the prior quarter, and a filing says it may offer 90 days up to one year for big data center builds. (§ Payment terms: the new risk: “It stretched payment terms to 60 days from 45 the prior quarter”) ^clm-c0c4094293d315729ad5
- The notes argue that by giving buyers up to a year to pay, Nvidia is quietly acting like a lender, which ties its fate to its customers' finances. (§ Why it matters for the wiki: “Nvidia is quietly acting like a lender, giving buyers up to a year to pay”) ^clm-5f19f204e80f5ad7623d
