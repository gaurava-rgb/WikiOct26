# Why Blackstone Thinks AI Bottlenecks Could Work in its Favor

Alex Eichenstein, Nick Wingfield, The Information, published 2026-10-22.

## Summary

Blackstone leadership argued on its Q3 2026 earnings call that power bottlenecks and local opposition to data centers increase the scarcity value of existing infrastructure, allowing higher lease pricing on uncommitted capacity. While digital infrastructure leasing drove firm-leading returns, Blackstone faces mounting debt risks on billions loaned to CoreWeave and Firmus as both neoclouds encounter market turmoil. Blackstone AI investing head Jas Khaira warned that credit markets have not mapped out who will absorb trillions in infrastructure debt.

## Key claims

- Blackstone Chief Executive Steve Schwarzman argued on the firm's Q3 2026 earnings call that power availability constraints and local opposition make existing data centers more valuable. (§ Bottlenecks as pricing power: “power availability bottlenecks and rising local opposition to data centers make new capacity harder to build”) ^clm-78651b34895d6e36a47e
- Blackstone President Jon Gray stated the firm can demand higher lease returns on uncommitted data center projects to compensate for rising interest rates. (§ Bottlenecks as pricing power: “demand higher lease returns on uncommitted data center projects to compensate for rising interest rates”) ^clm-20ef18cea7817274e0ff
- Blackstone reported that its digital infrastructure assets experienced extraordinary leasing momentum in Q3 2026, significantly outperforming real estate and private equity. (§ Bottlenecks as pricing power: “digital infrastructure assets experienced extraordinary leasing momentum, significantly outperforming its traditional real estate”) ^clm-ecbcedea0790950d6420
- Blackstone President Jon Gray stated that AI computing demand continues to grow faster than the available supply of chips and data centers. (§ Bottlenecks as pricing power: “AI computing demand continues to outpace the supply of available compute chips”) ^clm-1bf1ac72a936c518031d
- CoreWeave shares fell another 10% in late October 2026 as financing fears mounted, increasing pressure on Blackstone's billions in loans to the company. (§ Neocloud exposure and funding uncertainty: “CoreWeave dropped another 10% on Thursday following steep declines earlier in October”) ^clm-c1c66c1cb0a4e421eb29
- Blackstone has provided both equity financing and loans to Australian GPU cloud provider Firmus, which cancelled its planned initial public offering in October 2026. (§ Neocloud exposure and funding uncertainty: “Blackstone has provided both equity financing and loans to Firmus”) ^clm-88d0b86f401126eb60cf
- Blackstone's joint venture with Google to build a 500-megawatt TPU cloud, backed by an initial $5 billion equity commitment, could require up to $25 billion in total capital and has faced site delays. (§ Neocloud exposure and funding uncertainty: “Google, announced in May 2026 with an initial $5 billion equity commitment to build a 500-megawatt TPU cloud”) ^clm-41ce280799f5b4fffbbe
- Jas Khaira, head of AI investing at Blackstone, cautioned at a conference that the firm has not identified where all the debt will come from to finance trillions in infrastructure. (§ Neocloud exposure and funding uncertainty: “cautioned at a conference that the firm has not identified where all the public and private debt will come from”) ^clm-62249cee87fe4d4e8e5c
- Meta Platforms shares fell 7% following its Q3 earnings release due to investor dilution concerns over its new Class C shares created to finance AI capital spending. (§ Market reactions: “Meta Platforms shares fell 7% on Thursday as investor optimism over third-quarter results gave way to dilution worries”) ^clm-229d9341a1f50c483f9a
- Google formed a partnership with energy software firm Voltus to pay residential and commercial customers to reduce electricity usage during peak demand.
