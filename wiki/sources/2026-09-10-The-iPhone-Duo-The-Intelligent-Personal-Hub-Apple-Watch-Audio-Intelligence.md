# The iPhone Duo, The Intelligent Personal Hub, Apple Watch Audio Intelligence

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-09-10.

## Summary

Thompson reviews Apple's September 2026 event. The foldable iPhone Duo shows Apple's hardware and software integration at its best, but developers must do real work to support it, just as they must to plug into Siri AI's 300,000-app integration. He accepts John Ternus' framing of the iPhone as an intelligent personal hub, but argues cloud agents like Meta's Muse are more capable, and that agents using the web may undercut Apple's app-based advantage; the true hub may not need apps. He expects the now functional Siri AI to be widely used, and is uneasy about the Apple Watch's new always-listening Audio Intelligence features.

## Key claims

- Apple unveiled its first foldable smartphone, the iPhone Duo, at its September 9, 2026 event, and new CEO John Ternus mocked rival foldables as two phones awkwardly stuck together. (§ The iPhone Duo: “unveiled the company’s first foldable smartphone during a wide-ranging event Wednesday”) ^clm-a264f00d3a9712b4d3ae
- The iPhone Duo unfolds to a 7.6-inch screen, the largest display ever on an iPhone, has an outer 5.4-inch screen for use when folded, and will work with the Apple Pencil later in 2026. (§ The iPhone Duo: “the largest display ever on an iPhone. There’s also an outer 5.4-inch screen”) ^clm-e7040cd0c5276fb15b10
- With the iPhone Duo, Apple aims to refine the foldable phone category pioneered by Samsung, with a sturdier feel and less of a crease in the middle of the screen. (§ The iPhone Duo: “Apple aims to show it can refine a category pioneered by Samsung Electronics”) ^clm-902813e4f6dcb40f32db
- Thompson calls the iPhone Duo Apple at its best, and says the most noteworthy part is not the hardware but how Apple redesigned iOS to fit the device, which is why software chief Craig Federighi appeared in the launch video. (§ The iPhone Duo: “what is most noteworthy is how the company redesigned iOS to fit the device”) ^clm-54df0895c3380e838d3a
- Thompson notes the irony that Apple's iPhone Duo video praised combining two products into one, when Tim Cook once mocked Microsoft's Surface for doing the same thing. (§ The iPhone Duo: “given how Tim Cook once mocked the Surface for doing the same thing”) ^clm-916c8a4f4e1c17da9207
- Craig Federighi said Apple made it easy for developers to adapt their apps for the iPhone Duo with new and updated APIs, showing Zoom, Slack, and Netflix apps built for the larger screen. (§ The iPhone Duo: “made it easy for developers to adapt their apps for iPhone Duo with new and updated APIs”) ^clm-5d0e159f9ee86f5d4954
- Thompson complains that the iPhone Duo puts its dock on the right so it stays the same open or closed, which makes it a right-handed device for anyone using one hand. (§ The iPhone Duo: “if you only use one hand this is a right-handed device”) ^clm-c21f5298831ec0c54629
- Thompson argues Netflix featuring in the iPhone Duo launch, after refusing to support the Vision Pro, shows app developers work with Apple purely out of self-interest, not affection, since Apple will sell far more Duos than Vision Pros. (§ The iPhone Duo: “app developers work with Apple purely out of self interest these days”) ^clm-478f5be0990a6fa45ee1
- Prediction: Thompson predicts most developers will do the work to support the iPhone Duo, even though apps that skip it will look terrible, because the iPhone is too important to ignore. (§ The iPhone Duo: “the iPhone is important enough that I expect most will”) ^clm-092ca97a31fa9d7e92d7
- At the September 2026 iPhone event, John Ternus described the ideal AI device as an intelligent personal hub, always with you and able to run on-device and cloud models, and said no product is better designed for that than the iPhone. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “there’s no product in the world better designed to be your intelligent personal hub than iPhone”) ^clm-81a8ee93fa885ce9f3cb
- Thompson accepts the intelligent personal hub framing but notes Ternus' description conveniently matches the iPhone, and argues an iPhone, however powerful, is fundamentally less capable than something running in the cloud. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “an iPhone, as powerful as it is, is just fundamentally less capable than something running in the cloud”) ^clm-f96fb0f453a2ef6dcc33
- Thompson argues that using powerful cloud models for everything will be more intelligent than Apple's approach of carefully rationed fallbacks to on-device models. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “using powerful models for everything, as opposed to carefully rationed fall-backs to on-device models”) ^clm-d2a9aefb5abab2f50f28
- Thompson argues the iPhone's all-day battery life would be tested if the intelligent personal hub were a real agent doing work while the user is not paying attention. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “that all-day battery life would be tested if the intelligent personal hub were actually an agent”) ^clm-5c4ba6d4c574164520ba
- Thompson argues Meta's Muse also fits the intelligent personal hub description, and compellingly so: it runs in the cloud on a capable dedicated virtual machine, uses Meta's nearly state of the art models by default, and runs continuously. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “Muse runs in the cloud on a very capable dedicated VM”) ^clm-5cefc48978ca5d387519
- Apple said in September 2026 that Siri AI can take action in third-party apps and already works with over 300,000 apps, such as texting in WhatsApp or drafting an email in Outlook. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “it already works seamlessly with over 300,000 apps”) ^clm-6aa6f94694915f42f731
- Thompson notes that Apple calling it easy for developers to adopt new Siri capabilities means developers still have to do the work, on top of updating their apps for the iPhone Duo. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “developers have to actually put in the work”) ^clm-4cdabbee02ad383f600d
- Thompson argues Apple's traditional advantage, developers plugging into its frameworks, may become a hindrance: agents that can simply use the web get an integration with nearly everything for free, while Apple depends on developers adopting its APIs. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “to the extent that agents can just use the web is the extent to which they get an integration”) ^clm-42a0f6deb1b9b929e0f3
- Apple Intelligence features announced in September 2026 include Notify Me in Safari to watch a webpage for changes, Shortcuts automations made by describing them, and surfacing details like a flight code when calling a business. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “In Safari, it powers Notify Me to monitor a webpage”) ^clm-92c77419c194f2a4fb3a
- Thompson argues that in Apple's vision AI augments your existing app workflow, but a better workflow may be to hand work straight to an agent, such as being reminded by an agent instead of checking a reminders app, and Apple is so married to apps it likely never considered that. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “Apple is so married to the app paradigm that they probably never even considered the alternative”) ^clm-13663f91e244b365f9a1
- Thompson concludes that Ternus described an iPhone whose vision assumes the primacy of apps, while the truly intelligent personal hub might not need apps at all. (§ The Intelligent Personal Hub: “the truly Intelligent Personal Hub might not need apps at all”) ^clm-bbe60a7a20dfe94c9572
- Prediction: Thompson argues being late to AI is not always bad for reaching normal users, and that Apple's now actually functional Siri AI, built on Google's Gemini, is likely to get a lot of usage. (§ Apple Watch Audio Intelligence: “the actually functional Siri AI is likely to get a lot of usage”) ^clm-7981992581333e72720c
- Thompson argues the real problem with Apple's initial Siri AI flop was that Apple announced it far too early, out of fear of being behind in AI. (§ Apple Watch Audio Intelligence: “Apple announced it way too early out of a fear of being behind”) ^clm-71e0097ec38ab2fd8578
- The Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 add Audio Intelligence: Siri Recap uses ambient listening to take high-level notes of the wearer's conversations all day, and Live Recap transcribes the last 15 seconds of a live conversation. (§ Apple Watch Audio Intelligence: “Siri Recap, which uses ambient listening to take high-level notes of a wearer’s conversations”) ^clm-792cf86b26d4358c6fd7
- Apple says each Apple Watch audio feature is opt-in, creates and stores no recordings, and deletes raw audio immediately after processing without Apple or other apps being able to access it. (§ Apple Watch Audio Intelligence: “raw audio is not accessible to Apple and other apps and will be immediately deleted after processing”) ^clm-f8c6783c8dba7c94275c
- Thompson says the Apple Watch's always-listening features make him uneasy: other wearables already do this, but Apple rolling it out at scale will make recording of everyday conversations spread far faster, and he wishes Apple were not early here. (§ Apple Watch Audio Intelligence: “Apple rolling out that capability at scale means it’s going to be everywhere far more quickly”) ^clm-951e56638baacc580e08
