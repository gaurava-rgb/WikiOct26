# An Interview with Ben Thompson at the MoffettNathanson Media, Internet & Communications Conference

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-14.

## Summary

Ben Thompson is the one being interviewed here, by analysts Michael Nathanson, Craig Moffett, and Michael Morton at their annual conference. He says the big change in AI is a hard limit on how much compute exists, not the cost of power, and that money still pulls compute to whoever wants it most, as Anthropic showed. He argues OpenAI erred by not building ads early, while Meta is well placed because consumers want fun, not productivity. He also says Apple was right to stay out of model building, that physical assets protect firms like Amazon and DoorDash, and that a compute glut would one day cause a deep chip down cycle.

## Key claims

- Thompson argues the key change with AI is not that power or chips cost more, but that there is a finite amount of compute in the world, which breaks the old zero marginal cost mindset. (§ The Compute Shortage: “what it really is, is a finite amount of compute in the world”) ^clm-6a80eb1831db137ddfd2
- Thompson argues the compute shortage makes the case for data centers in space stronger, since building on Earth is limited by power, zoning rules, and local pushback. (§ The Compute Shortage: “makes the case for data centers in space stronger, not weaker”) ^clm-b9aa34438967dc14a6fb
- Thompson argues the claim that OpenAI would win because Anthropic lacked compute was nonsense, since no one is more willing and able to pay for compute than Anthropic, and it then got a lot of compute. (§ The Compute Shortage: “there is no entity in the world with a greater willingness or capacity to pay for compute than Anthropic”) ^clm-6bfd6c5bd795ec4f467b
- Thompson says compute shifting to Anthropic while others like xAI lose out is the market working, and supports the Aggregation Theory idea that whoever controls demand pulls in supply, though with more friction in the physical world. (§ The Compute Shortage: “whoever controls demand will ultimately bring supply on their side”) ^clm-b2a565fe69377805503e
- Thompson names the big four US AI model makers as Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Meta, and warns not to set aside open source Chinese models. (§ The Compute Shortage: “the big four in the US are clearly those two plus Google and Meta”) ^clm-f3cd139a68e8fcdf6ef7
- Thompson says Google Cloud numbers were unbelievable last quarter and wonders what share of them is Anthropic compute. (§ The Compute Shortage: “I would really like to know what percentage of those numbers is Anthropic compute”) ^clm-aea040929117cd2e819f
- Thompson says TPU (Google's custom AI chip) supply made for Anthropic comes out of Google's own allotment at TSMC, so Google is choosing to earn more by selling to Anthropic than by keeping the compute. (§ The Compute Shortage: “We can make more money selling to Anthropic than saving all this compute for ourselves”) ^clm-8572a3ad48de70e32b7b
- Thompson says Google will probably succeed in AI no matter what, but the open question is its margin (profit per dollar of sales): selling infrastructure or owning models and apps. (§ The Compute Shortage: “it means they will probably succeed no matter what”) ^clm-acb1a2bdf306aeef6eb1
- Prediction: Thompson predicts a decent chance that OpenAI and Anthropic together reach $200 billion in revenue in 2026. (§ The Compute Shortage: “OpenAI and Anthropic do a combined $200 billion in revenue this year”) ^clm-b35ba478537b7a288c13
- Thompson says the top reason for today's compute shortage is that TSMC did not respond to ChatGPT, with its growth falling in the two years after ChatGPT launched. (§ The Compute Shortage: “The number one reason we have a computer shortage right now is”) ^clm-9b5e0e09e3ab48125983
- Thompson notes TSMC CEO C.C. Wei talked about agents on the last earnings call, a sign TSMC now sees the demand. (§ The Compute Shortage: “C.C. Wei was talking about agents on the last earnings call”) ^clm-c46758eb38b8d463df02
- Thompson says he has become bullish on AWS, partly because it does not compete with its customers, unlike Microsoft, which told analysts it missed Azure numbers by giving more compute to internal workloads. (§ The Compute Shortage: “they’re not competing with their customers is actually going to be pretty important”) ^clm-8eff4a38a3d77fc78ea6
- Thompson says all inference today, even coding agents, is answer inference with a human in the loop, so speed of response matters. (§ The Compute Shortage: “As long as there is a human in the loop, then inference time really matters”) ^clm-ef28a57d4b8cf90e91ca
- Prediction: Thompson predicts that in a truly agentic future, agentic inference will lean on large but slow memory hierarchies, making raw speed less important than it is for training or answer inference. (§ The Compute Shortage: “you’re going to lean more and more on high-capacity, but relatively slow memory hierarchies”) ^clm-60ad4ef21ec19951b88a
- Prediction: Thompson predicts agentic workloads will commoditize chips and memory, for example by swapping some HBM (costly stacked memory) for cheaper DRAM, and that China will play a big role in that. (§ Rapid Fire: “I think China’s gonna have a big role in that commoditization in the long run”) ^clm-46bd8b513cdbe0f5d710
- Thompson argues OpenAI's massive mistake in consumer AI was forgetting that consumers do not care about productivity; they want entertainment, while enterprises pay for productivity. (§ Consumer AI: “forgetting that consumers don’t care about productivity, no one actually wants to be more productive”) ^clm-ae3f128551aa89b31914
- Thompson argues OpenAI ran out of time on ads: had it started ads in 2023, it would now know which ad format works for AI and could fund R&D from an ad business in a virtuous cycle. (§ Consumer AI: “I think in some respects they ran out of time”) ^clm-193d561061f3e494b80b
- Thompson argues ChatGPT's large free consumer user base has been a massive negative for OpenAI, eating compute from users who pay nothing. (§ Consumer AI: “the consumer penetration of ChatGPT has been a massive negative for them”) ^clm-29f3cfe2e495cbf98955
- Thompson says Sam Altman chose to serve everyone to get the most leverage from huge model R&D costs, when nailing monetization in one market first would have been better. (§ Consumer AI: “he felt they had to get maximum leverage on their R&D spend”) ^clm-d6251a819c5fd632917e
- Thompson says being too online is the most accurate critique of OpenAI, and that consumers' revealed preference is they do not care about privacy or ads but do not want to pay. (§ Consumer AI: ““Too online” is the most accurate and consistently correct critique of OpenAI”) ^clm-5592ef2b633a7656af04
- Thompson argues Aggregation Theory does not apply to physical businesses, and Amazon's scarce warehouse and delivery network lets it capture value even if AI chatbots front the purchase. (§ Consumer AI: “I think that Amazon’s delivery capabilities are a pretty scarce resource”) ^clm-8716702558c0d8a63cae
- Thompson argues that for Meta the question is not whether it figures out AI but whether it keeps people's attention; as long as it does, it will be fine. (§ Consumer AI: “the question is, “Will you retain customer attention?””) ^clm-ede12b3bdfe23b697997
- Thompson says Meta must spend billions on AI because it is a purely digital business, which is a reason to be bearish, but also bullish because it will be focused on consumers like no one else and has no cloud business to fall back on. (§ Consumer AI: “they’re going to be insanely focused on the consumer in a way that no one else is going to be”) ^clm-c880db3bb94e35379928
- Thompson argues a core problem at Meta is that Mark Zuckerberg has never been into advertising, even though every pixel in Meta's apps can be monetized. (§ Consumer AI: “A big problem, I think a fundamental problem Meta has is Mark”) ^clm-5cf0442c64ec4aae4d44
- Thompson says that even with zero AI progress, Meta's runway to monetize what it already has is astronomical, and its ad ecosystem of businesses that exist only because of Facebook makes it anti-fragile. (§ Consumer AI: “we could have zero progress in AI tomorrow and their runway to monetize what they already have is astronomical”) ^clm-a8c718796b30f9c80d21
- Thompson says Apple, by choosing Gemini as the basis for Siri and naming its hardware chief as next CEO, has decided not to be in the frontier model building business, which he calls reasonable. (§ Apple: “the decision appears to be, “We’re not going to be in the model building business””) ^clm-710e75a5f4351db5ca48
- Thompson argues building quality devices is fundamentally different from building internet services that must fail gracefully, which is why Apple is bad at services and Google is brilliant at them. (§ Apple: “what it takes to build quality devices is fundamentally different than what it takes to build quality Internet services”) ^clm-9aa10ec718d4421b4af6
- Prediction: Thompson predicts physical players like DoorDash benefit from new tech like drones, and that Google Wing will likely work for DoorDash just as Google Cloud works for Anthropic. (§ Apple: “probably Google Wing is going to work for DoorDash just like Google Cloud works for Anthropic”) ^clm-98e43fd5be11b59fc5f1
- Thompson compares enterprise software to cable TV: likely stickier and longer lasting than theory says, but with real long-term risk to terminal value (what a business is worth far into the future) as AI-native firms undo their customers. (§ Rapid Fire: “I think the concerns about terminal value are real, not just for the software but for the companies using it”) ^clm-bea1d1411d256476eb60
- Thompson says he is always wrong about Google, that its ability to wring revenue out of Search appears infinite, and that Google wins fast human-in-the-loop answers, including AI answers. (§ Rapid Fire: “Their ability to wring revenue out of Search appears to be infinite”) ^clm-5c5505799464fec13b4a
- Prediction: Thompson predicts that if compute stops being scarce, the fixed costs in semiconductors will cause the semiconductor down cycle of all down cycles. (§ Rapid Fire: “it’s going be the semiconductor down cycle of all down cycles”) ^clm-159f3b40a30de6b946a7
- Thompson says he feeds rough drafts to both ChatGPT and Claude, which often attack his more novel ideas and speed up checking technical details. (§ Rapid Fire: “I always feed it to both, both ChatGPT and Claude”) ^clm-c71f7a1591e4ef0dd9f9
