# The OpenAI Super App, ChatGPT = Codex, Whither Chat

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-07-14.

## Summary

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work and GPT-5.6 on July 9, 2026, and turned its Codex coding app into the new desktop ChatGPT, with the old chat app renamed ChatGPT Classic. Critics like John Gruber and M.G. Siegler called the new app confusing and its branding clunky, while Thompson says Codex is one of the most incredible apps he has used and far more capable than chat. He argues OpenAI is deliberately trading its lead in consumer chat for a work-focused agent that acts like a self-contained operating system, a product Apple, Meta, and Google would not or could not launch. He doubts most people want a work tool in their free time, and asks why OpenAI still pushes advertising, a consumer business model, if ChatGPT is now about work.

## Key claims

- OpenAI launched ChatGPT Work on July 9, 2026, an agent that combines its ChatGPT chatbot with its Codex coding tool to create documents, presentations, and websites. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “The agent, called ChatGPT Work, combines OpenAI's popular chatbot with its AI coding tool, Codex”) ^clm-8146442b2c2c048de48a
- OpenAI's GPT-5.6 model debuted on July 9, 2026 to power ChatGPT Work; its launch had been delayed in June at the U.S. government's request over national security fears. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “That model's launch was delayed last month at the U.S. government's request over national security fears”) ^clm-449fb92c0a1b39d5205a
- OpenAI released GPT-5.6 in three sizes: Sol, the most advanced; Terra, the mid-sized model; and Luna, the smallest and fastest. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “Sol, the most advanced model, Terra, the mid-sized model, or Luna, the smallest and fastest model”) ^clm-fa3dcd5a622fbf0790ab
- Per Reuters, OpenAI's ChatGPT Work is a direct answer to Anthropic's Claude Cowork, an agent Anthropic launched in January 2026 that plans and carries out multi-step tasks on its own. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “ChatGPT Work is a direct answer to Anthropic's Claude Cowork, an agent it launched in January”) ^clm-a9af6fd1eb06ecd6cc24
- Per Reuters in July 2026, Anthropic and OpenAI are both preparing for possible public offerings (selling shares on the stock market) and are fighting for enterprise business, which pays more than consumers. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “both of which are preparing for possible public offerings soon”) ^clm-37551a42eab3a9b8f31e
- When the Mac apps updated in July 2026, OpenAI's Codex app renamed itself ChatGPT and the old ChatGPT app became ChatGPT Classic; downloading ChatGPT from OpenAI's website now gets the rebranded Codex. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “Upon updating, Codex renamed itself ChatGPT”) ^clm-48645291da315e3d0c6d
- The new Codex-based ChatGPT app opens to a menu choosing between ChatGPT Work and Codex, not chat; chat is a floating pane from the sidebar, which Thompson says feels like an afterthought. (§ The OpenAI Super App: “There is a dropdown menu that lets you choose between ChatGPT Work and Codex”) ^clm-01606f31506c6d566005
- John Gruber called OpenAI's new ChatGPT app an incredibly confusing sloppy mess whose UI only looks polished, and praised the old app as focused. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “The new app is an incredibly confusing sloppy mess”) ^clm-fd788085a2c8f717021f
- Gruber wrote that, per people near OpenAI, its senior executives are consumed by fear of missing out on Anthropic, and that real clout belongs to AI researchers, not product designers. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “the company's senior executives are singularly consumed with FOMO obsession regarding Anthropic”) ^clm-adcb802ede62ef38367a
- Per Gruber, the old focused ChatGPT app still ships on mobile, including iPad, so only the Mac desktop app became the Codex-based super app. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “That's the app that still ships for mobile”) ^clm-376e2e5cea1d3056cbf8
- M.G. Siegler called the ChatGPT Work name insanely clunky branding: it is meant to evoke Claude Cowork, but most people will read it as an office version of ChatGPT or think of Microsoft's enterprise suite. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “this is insanely clunky branding”) ^clm-07f3250078d01dfc9080
- Siegler suggested OpenAI may be betting on one AI to rule them all, where a person's personal AI is so ingrained that it also becomes their work AI, like bring-your-own-device in enterprise. (§ Whither Chat: “Is your personal AI going to be so ingrained in your life that it's also just most convenient”) ^clm-5708e1b98fe60ed41714
- Thompson runs OpenAI's Codex on a Mac Mini in his server rack and reaches it from his work laptop, a MacBook Air, or his phone, and argues the device at hand no longer matters as long as it reaches his AI. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “I run Codex on the Mac Mini in my server rack and access it from anywhere”) ^clm-35ac7acf2778dd8d1f4d
- Thompson argues Linux and LLMs go hand in hand: a pure text command line is a perfect match for a language model, giving far more control than a GUI, while a Mac server keeps throwing up GUI prompts and cannot run Docker natively. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “the extent to which Linux and LLMs go hand-in-hand”) ^clm-0f34e867abcad55f73ea
- When Thompson set up his GUI-centric Synology storage device, Codex opened the device's web GUI in its in-app browser, asked him to log in, and clicked around itself; it worked perfectly but much more slowly than a command line. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “it simply opened up the GUI in its in-app browser, asked me to log in, and started mousing around”) ^clm-42ff3586ff1101d0c0b1
- Per The Information in June 2026, OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux said Codex works better than ChatGPT for many tasks, including long-running multistep tasks, using outside tools, and writing code to edit complicated spreadsheets. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “Codex is better than ChatGPT at completing long-running, multistep tasks”) ^clm-b6b2d7aff37c9345bd1e
- In June 2026 OpenAI said its planned merger of ChatGPT, Codex, and its Atlas browser into one desktop superapp would launch within weeks, to focus on its most successful products, which lately means Codex. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “a previously announced combination of ChatGPT, Codex and its browser, Atlas, into a single desktop”) ^clm-bb84744cef71f81c343d
- Thompson argues Codex and ChatGPT are not really in the same category: comparing them is like comparing the calculator app to Excel, and few people know what they are missing. (§ ChatGPT = Codex: “It's like comparing the calculator app to Excel”) ^clm-41d95d40c3c6df089540
- Thompson argues the launch was shocking but not surprising, given his view a week earlier that consumer chats are mostly unverifiable and so make poor training feedback; it feels like OpenAI is killing chat. (§ Whither Chat: “while this app launch was shocking, it shouldn't be surprising”) ^clm-ff664e5c3aa6957d45d0
- Thompson argues the new ChatGPT app is in effect a self-contained operating system, with a built-in browser and chat in the corner, whose foundation is intelligence and whose offer is to do your work for you. (§ Whither Chat: “It is, for all intents and purposes, a self-contained operating system”) ^clm-bf429da10eb819a51c0e
- Thompson argues traditional ChatGPT chat is threatened by Google putting Gemini everywhere, Meta's Muse, and an Apple Siri that finally works for what people used a focused chat app for. (§ Whither Chat: “ChatGPT, traditionally understood, is threatened in a world of Google shoving Gemini everywhere”) ^clm-587a6ff7a88988a5b37d
- Prediction: Thompson argues Codex is the sort of product Apple will never launch, Meta will never gain user permission to launch, and Google seems incapable of launching. (§ Whither Chat: “Codex, meanwhile, is the sort of product that Apple will never launch”) ^clm-8409f8d323074997b66d
- Thompson argues OpenAI has gone all in, trading its huge head start in chatbot users for a chance to push AI's full capabilities on people, a deliberate rejection of the consumer opportunity that made it the accidental consumer tech company. (§ Whither Chat: “this release seems like a deliberate rejection of that opportunity”) ^clm-0a7198e722a2262cf2db
- Prediction: Thompson is not sure OpenAI's bet will work, because Codex is complicated and a bit intimidating, and it is about work, which he doubts most people want in their free time. (§ Whither Chat: “I'm also not sure if it's going to work”) ^clm-4b96695da705a7127306
- Thompson asks why OpenAI is pushing ahead with advertising, a consumer business model, if ChatGPT is now about work; maybe ads are a mobile story where ChatGPT stays a chatbot, or OpenAI is unsure what it wants to be. (§ Whither Chat: “why is OpenAI moving full steam ahead with their advertising product”) ^clm-da4436ded216a3b84208
- Thompson frames OpenAI's bet as a question: is it more valuable to have 1 billion consumers typing questions and not paying, or a fraction of them engaging so deeply that they pay for more? (§ Whither Chat: “1 billion consumers typing questions and not paying”) ^clm-ceefa0b36e04c373a670
- Tony Fadell told Thompson that Apple's lawsuit against OpenAI is Apple's typical tactic to scare current and former employees, and that he heard it was driven by the Apple board. (“I heard this lawsuit was driven by the Apple board”) ^clm-3ecaf6017390061c7f50
- Fadell says Steve Jobs once threatened to sue Nest for poaching 80 to 100 Apple employees; Fadell replied that retaining talent was Apple's job, and Nest kept hiring. (“Steve threatened to file a lawsuit against Nest for poaching 80-100 Apple employees”) ^clm-cb28f1099b176d39853b
- John Gruber argued on the Dithering podcast that Apple's lawsuit against OpenAI may have been driven by CEO John Ternus channeling his inner Steve Jobs. (“this lawsuit may very well have been driven by John Ternus channeling his inner Jobs”) ^clm-0681eb10bede3cb77bc7
