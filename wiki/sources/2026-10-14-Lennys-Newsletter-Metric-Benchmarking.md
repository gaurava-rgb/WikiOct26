# Metric Benchmarking

Lenny Rachitsky, Lenny's Newsletter, published 2026-10-14.

## Summary

Lenny Rachitsky compiles quantitative benchmark targets for good and great startup performance across engagement, retention, and cost efficiency. Metrics include user activation rates, multi-product expansion at firms like Datadog and CrowdStrike, net revenue retention, logo retention, gross margins across software, AI applications, and marketplaces, and customer acquisition payback periods.

## Key claims

- Lenny Rachitsky and Yuriy Timen define a good new user activation rate as 35% and a great rate as 50% or higher. (§ Engagement and activity metrics: “New user activation rate: Good is 35%, Great is 50%+”) ^clm-b014c863effa3320853d
- Datadog reports that 58% of its customer base uses four or more products, representing strong multi-product account expansion. (§ Engagement and activity metrics: “Majority of customers using multiple products. For example, Datadog has 58% of customers using 4+ products”) ^clm-017e35f87397c5843bea
- CrowdStrike reports that 51% of its customer base uses six or more modules, illustrating deep multi-product adoption. (§ Engagement and activity metrics: “CrowdStrike has 51% using 6+ products”) ^clm-471c3a414350ad1bf748
- Venture benchmarks establish that net revenue retention of 100% is considered good, while 120% or higher is considered great for software companies. (§ Retention metrics: “Net revenue retention: Good is 100%, Great is 120%+”) ^clm-7359db2021ebe5252d3a
- SaaS benchmarks define annual gross revenue retention of 90% as good and 95% or higher as top-quartile performance. (§ Retention metrics: “Gross revenue retention: Good is 90%, Great is 95%+”) ^clm-eb9f1c5c86ef137652cc
- Venture capital benchmarks define annual logo retention of 85% as good and 95% or higher as best in class. (§ Retention metrics: “Annual logo retention: Good is 85%, Great is 95%+”) ^clm-df0107f04639e7f9f036
- Lenny Rachitsky and Casey Winters define good 6-month user retention as 30% for marketplaces and 25% for social networks, and great retention as 50% and 45% respectively. (§ Retention metrics: “6-month user retention: Good is 30% for marketplaces and 25% for social; Great is 50% for marketplaces and 45% for social”) ^clm-6159ca9ef33295044b39
- Industrial cost efficiency benchmarks define a healthy cost decline per unit as 15% each time cumulative production volume doubles, with top-tier declines reaching 20% or more. (§ Cost efficiency metrics: “Cost per unit: decreases each time total volume doubles by 15% for Good, 20%+ for Great”) ^clm-e400277714dea0802da3
- Cloud software benchmarks establish that a gross margin of 75% is considered healthy, while 80% or higher is considered top quartile. (§ Cost efficiency metrics: “Gross margin for software: Good is 75%, Great is 80%+”) ^clm-7477c8b3cbdb633d58b9
- Venture benchmarks for artificial intelligence applications establish that a gross margin of 50% is considered good and 60% or higher is considered great, reflecting heavy inference costs. (§ Cost efficiency metrics: “Gross margin for AI apps: Good is 50%, Great is 60%+”) ^clm-58d64c8b1451e49fb6cd
- Online marketplace benchmarks define a gross margin of 70% as good and 80% or higher as great, while heavily managed marketplaces like DoorDash operate with lower margins. (§ Cost efficiency metrics: “Gross margin for marketplaces: Good is 70%, Great is 80%+”) ^clm-6825c42b75e3d2a422fd
- Software investor benchmarks define a customer acquisition cost payback period under 18 months as good and under 12 months as great. (§ Cost efficiency metrics: “CAC payback period: Good is under 18 months, Great is under 12 months”) ^clm-1789f238d7ab08df198b
