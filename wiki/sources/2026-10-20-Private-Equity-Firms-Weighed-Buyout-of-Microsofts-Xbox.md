# Private Equity Firms Weighed $100 Billion-Plus Buyout of Microsoft’s Xbox

Valida Pau, Aaron Holmes, The Information, published 2026-10-20.

## Summary

Private equity firms explored an acquisition of Microsoft's Xbox gaming division that could have valued it between $100 billion and $150 billion, though Microsoft declared the unit is not for sale. The division has struggled with falling revenue and razor-thin 3 percent profit margins despite acquiring Activision Blizzard for $75 billion. As Microsoft channels massive capital into AI data centers, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma is executing a 20 percent workforce reduction while grappling with AI-driven component inflation that could push next-generation console prices to $1,800.

## Key claims

- Private equity groups explored a potential acquisition of Microsoft's Xbox division that would have valued the business at $100 billion to $150 billion. - Silver Lake, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, and Affinity Partners completed a $55 billion leveraged buyout of Electronic Arts in early 2026, valuing the gaming publisher at roughly seven times annual revenue. - Microsoft's Xbox unit employs approximately 14,000 workers and includes Activision Blizzard, which Microsoft bought for $75 billion in 2023. - Xbox annual revenue fell 7 percent to $21.8 billion in the fiscal year ending June 2026, with its adjusted profit margin dropping to 3 percent. - Xbox CEO Asha Sharma implemented a restructuring plan in July 2026 to eliminate 3,200 jobs, or roughly 20 percent of staff, to prioritize core game franchises. - Prediction: AI hardware demand has inflated gaming console component costs, leading Xbox to internally forecast that its planned 2028 console could cost between $1,200 and $1,800.
