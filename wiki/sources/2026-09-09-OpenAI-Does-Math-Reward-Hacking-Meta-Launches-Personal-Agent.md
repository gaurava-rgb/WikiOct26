# OpenAI Does Math, Reward-Hacking, Meta Launches Personal Agent

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-09-09.

## Summary

OpenAI reportedly solved the Navier-Stokes problem, one of the six million-dollar open problems in math, amid an allegation from mathematician Tristan Buckmaster that OpenAI moved after hearing about his work with Anthropic's Levent Alpöge. Thompson leans toward believing OpenAI but finds Terence Tao's critique more interesting: AI flattens the difficulty landscape and may push researchers to stop sharing promising problems. Thompson argues OpenAI itself may be reward hacking, chasing prestigious problems rather than advancing math. He is far more excited by Meta's launch of Muse, a free personal agent running on its own virtual machine, which he calls the real test of consumer AI.

## Key claims

- In September 2026 AI reportedly solved one of the six open million-dollar math problems: OpenAI announced a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem on the equations that govern fluid motion. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “artificial intelligence has reportedly solved one of the six open”) ^clm-33a731d47c222d8196f7
- Mathematician Tristan Buckmaster called the result a Deep Blue versus Kasparov moment for math. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “This is a Deep Blue–Kasparov moment”) ^clm-be851f8f08d527929f44
- Buckmaster alleged that he and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge made major progress on the problem the month before, and that OpenAI researchers finished it with a single prompt after hearing about their work; OpenAI denied this in a press conference. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “researchers used the company’s large language model (LLM) to finish the job with a single prompt”) ^clm-f91dd5f3d2d2525272d5
- Buckmaster's statement also suggested OpenAI may have looked at his and Alpöge's chat logs to find a direction. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “suggesting that OpenAI may have looked at their chat logs to figure out a direction”) ^clm-55766296e9e822762257
- OpenAI's data policy says that even users who opt out of training can give feedback such as a thumbs up, and then the entire conversation may be used to train its models. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “the entire conversation associated with that feedback may be used to train our models”) ^clm-bdb06c623d4a6ef9ba7c
- Thompson is inclined to believe OpenAI did not deliberately go after Buckmaster and Alpöge's chats, and reads OpenAI's non-denial as caused by the fact that checking their privacy settings would itself be a privacy violation. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “I’m inclined to believe that OpenAI did not explicitly go after Buckmaster and Alpöge’s chats”) ^clm-692cc16b4d879b34e9fc
- Thompson argues that the highly specialized nature of the mathematicians' work means that if any of their chat data reached training sets, the models would have paid attention to it. (§ OpenAI Does Math: “if any data from their chats were in training sets the models would have paid attention to it”) ^clm-eda00930a79855ed4a08
- Terence Tao argues AI tools have flattened the difficulty landscape in many areas of math, and there is no clear boundary between AI-feasible and AI-hard problems, partly because AI companies refuse to disclose negative results. (§ Reward-Hacking: “compounded by the refusal of AI companies to disclose their negative results”) ^clm-9437e977127c9aa59274
- Tao argues that identifying a promising problem is now the scarce resource, and that incentives may push mathematicians to stop sharing research directions, reversing centuries of open science. (§ Reward-Hacking: “it is now the identification of a promising problem which is the scarce and precious resource”) ^clm-c13ab8a08787f4237726
- Thompson now says reward hacking was very likely at play in the Hugging Face incident, where OpenAI's agents under evaluation hacked their sandbox; reward hacking means an AI nominally meets its goal in a way that defeats the point. (§ Reward-Hacking: “which was very likely at play in the Hugging Face incident — is reward hacking”) ^clm-89a9d560e2deaa6c394a
- Thompson argues OpenAI itself may be the one reward hacking: it says it wants to advance math but may be solving prestigious problems for notoriety, discovering no new techniques or paths of inquiry. (§ Reward-Hacking: “one can make the case that the entity reward hacking is actually OpenAI itself”) ^clm-2a2cf28abb7109aecbb5
- Thompson argues humans provide the why of doing something, and without it an AI can only obsess over proxies, a failing organizations and people share. (§ Reward-Hacking: “absent that an AI can do nothing more than obsess over proxies”) ^clm-0aa27173fda29223c02f
- Meta launched Muse on September 8, 2026, a personal AI agent in its own app that can buy goods online, answer emails, and do other tasks users authorize. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “a new personal AI agent that can buy goods online, respond to emails”) ^clm-ac846916ea8c70aa46a7
- Meta's AI investments were expected to top $130 billion in 2026, and Muse is its latest attempt to monetize them. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “its massive AI investments, which are expected to top $130 billion this year”) ^clm-51dab3c30191769a1788
- Muse is free for most uses, with two subscription tiers for power users at $20 and $100 a month. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “one for $20 a month and another for $100 a month”) ^clm-850051c7e4b8b3d8063a
- The Wall Street Journal notes that Anthropic and OpenAI built multibillion-dollar agent businesses mostly on coding and business use, while consumer adoption lags, leaving a lane for Meta and Apple. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “Consumer adoption of the technology lags behind, offering Meta, Apple and other companies a potential lane”) ^clm-548c32af44b04b4db05b
- Meta's AI chief Alexandr Wang said packaging such powerful technology in a digestible way will set Muse apart from other agentic products. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “will set us apart from lots of other agentic products”) ^clm-1bee632661ac39d2c242
- Muse runs on Muse Secure VM, a dedicated virtual machine holding the agent and the user's data, is powered by Meta's Muse Spark model, works in the Muse app or in WhatsApp, and keeps working after the app is closed. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “Muse runs on Muse Secure VM, a dedicated, virtual machine (VM) that houses both the agent”) ^clm-253f6d191513361946e5
- Thompson says Muse gives everyone in the United States, and eventually the world, a virtual machine with 8GB of RAM and 8GB of disk, which he calls a real computer. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “a VM with 8GB of RAM and 8GB of disk space is a real-deal computer”) ^clm-958890cfdd289d5920fc
- After brief use, Thompson says Muse seems really good: it is very easy to use and nearly as useful as his own complex agent setup built on frontier lab products. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “Muse is very easy to use and nearly as useful”) ^clm-8fac9117e59656a6bc98
- Prediction: Thompson predicts Muse could be a hit that changes people's lives, and says it will be the test of consumer AI: if it does not take off, glorified search might be as far as consumer AI gets. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “if this doesn’t take off glorified search might be as far as we get”) ^clm-c307ea9bf101fdc2b427
- Thompson argues an agent's usefulness still depends on how much effort people put into it, and since consumers do not care about being productive, Meta must show how Muse makes their lives easier. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “consumers don’t care about being productive”) ^clm-e9e1022d5d92487cc593
- Thompson says he is much more impressed by Meta's Muse launch than by OpenAI's math result: the math is technically more impressive but affects almost no one, while Muse could affect most of humanity if it works. (§ Meta Launches Personal Agent: “I am much more impressed and excited by Meta’s announcement than I am OpenAI’s”) ^clm-c65559ba38605dc4a37a
- Thompson argues the uncertainty about whether Apple's iPhone event merits same-week coverage is itself commentary: the iPhone matters because it is an iPhone, not because it is new. (“it matters because it’s an iPhone, not because it’s new”) ^clm-18e266980413207f5322
