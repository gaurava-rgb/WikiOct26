# The Deployment Company, Back to the 70s, Apple and Intel

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-13.

## Summary

OpenAI formed a $4 billion-plus Deployment Company and bought the consultancy Tomoro, Google Cloud is hiring hundreds of forward deployed engineers, and Anthropic set up a private equity joint venture. Thompson says this matches his 2024 view that enterprise AI will look like the 1970s mainframe wave: executives buying software to replace work from the top down, with much of the effort going into reworking data. He then reads the reported Apple-Intel chip deal as mainly economic: Apple cannot get enough TSMC capacity because AI takes so much of it, which is costing it Mac and iPhone sales. TSMC's under-investment may hand Intel the customer that made TSMC the leader.

## Key claims

- OpenAI said on May 11, 2026 that it is setting up a new company, the OpenAI Deployment Company, with more than $4 billion in initial investment to help organizations build and deploy AI; OpenAI will majority own and control it. (§ The Deployment Company: “setting up a new company with more than $4 billion in initial investment”) ^clm-f024c0cdd2c6b6ad3e10
- OpenAI is buying the AI consulting firm Tomoro, which brings about 150 AI engineers and deployment specialists to the Deployment Company from day one; Tomoro was formed in 2023 in alliance with OpenAI and counts Mattel, Red Bull, Tesco and Virgin Atlantic as clients. (§ The Deployment Company: “will bring around 150 experienced AI engineers”) ^clm-39367fee6a49e90b767a
- Google plans to hire hundreds of forward deployed engineers (engineers placed at customer sites) in a new Google Cloud team to help customers adopt its business AI products, Thomas Kurian said on May 12, 2026, without giving the size. (§ The Deployment Company: “Google plans to hire hundreds of engineers to help customers”) ^clm-6ae12ffb15d6ad19fb42
- Google Cloud chief revenue officer Matt Renner said the forward deployed engineer team helps Google show up for customers with more technical resources instead of just an ocean of salespeople. (§ The Deployment Company: “more technical resources (vs just an ocean of salespeople)”) ^clm-b76c2fad76e1ca4bc4f3
- In early May 2026, Anthropic announced a joint venture with private equity firms to sell its AI to the PE firms' portfolio companies. (§ The Deployment Company: “Anthropic announced the creation of a joint venture with private equity firms”) ^clm-9afd88f138e6be37531e
- Thompson argues that OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic building human deployment teams is what he predicted, while joking that AGI is apparently not good enough to deploy AI. (§ The Deployment Company: “tempting to drop some snark about AGI apparently not being good enough to deploy AI”) ^clm-4a1f9babf7316bdf5c22
- Thompson recalls arguing in September 2024 that the right analogy for enterprise AI is not SaaS but the first wave of computing in the 1970s, the mainframe era. (§ Back to the 70s: “the proper analogy for AI in the enterprise was not SaaS”) ^clm-172084493075bf0e77b4
- Thompson argued in 2024 that agents are not copilots but replacements that do work in place of humans, starting with jobs like call centers, with software's advantages of always being available and scaling with demand. (§ Back to the 70s: “Agents aren’t copilots; they are replacements”) ^clm-98b095fe3febb48d6af7
- Thompson argued in 2024 that in the mainframe wave, accounting and ERP software replaced the workers who made reports, and the change was decided by executives, not by rank-and-file employees changing how they worked. (§ Back to the 70s: “did not depend on rank-and-file employees changing how they worked”) ^clm-3c08a96144e8ed2f7da4
- Thompson argued in 2024 that the biggest AI opportunity is top-down enterprise implementation, driven by buyers who want to raise revenue and cut costs, since only that justifies the compute for agents and the years of data work needed to replace humans. (§ Back to the 70s: “the biggest opportunity is in top-down enterprise implementations”) ^clm-d0247f21508e0f681a0c
- Thompson argues the OpenAI Deployment Company is not there to help employees use chatbots; he assumes the executive suite hires it to fundamentally rethink business processes in a way not done since the mainframe. (§ Back to the 70s: “hired by the executive suite to fundamentally rethink business processes”) ^clm-ddb6d515403cbc1aa7b0
- Prediction: Thompson predicts an ever-increasing number of deals where private equity buys software firms with reliable cash flows, makes significant layoffs, and has AI pick up the slack, which also solves stock-based compensation problems. (§ Back to the 70s: “PE buys software firms with reliable cash flows and conducts significant layoffs”) ^clm-240174cf350f00528cbb
- Thompson wonders how much of the deployment work will be reworking data, which is why he was bullish on Palantir in 2024. (§ Back to the 70s: “how much of the work ends up reworking data”) ^clm-9f9bdc75c88276332d52
- Thompson argued in 2024 that Palantir was the company most intriguing for the first wave of AI, because an effective agent needs access to all of a company's data in a form it can reason over, while Salesforce and Microsoft hold only scattered pieces. (§ Back to the 70s: “the company I am most intrigued by, for what I think will be the first wave of AI, is Palantir”) ^clm-89b3714903195967bf24
- Thompson argues AI is creating the need for new kinds of jobs, and the world is more dynamic than the most pessimistic forecasters assumed. (§ Back to the 70s: “it sure is interesting how AI is creating the need for new kinds of jobs”) ^clm-83737336eeb470490e95
- Thompson argues the rush of deployment teams comes down to this: OpenAI and Anthropic need the revenue, enterprises need the imagination, and Google needs to stay in the game. (§ Back to the 70s: “OpenAI and Anthropic need the revenue, enterprises need the imagination”) ^clm-993a370951eeb7dc38e5
- The Wall Street Journal reported in May 2026 that Apple and Intel reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to make some Apple device chips after more than a year of talks; which products is unclear. (§ Apple and Intel: “reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture some of the chips”) ^clm-5f33ad8219cd5034fb15
- Analyst Ming-Chi Kuo reported in late 2025 that Intel would make Apple's most basic M-series processor on its 18A process, without naming the generation. (§ Apple and Intel: “most basic M processor on its 18A process”) ^clm-14341c9d4cddcbd534c7
- Thompson argues the most obvious reason for an Apple-Intel deal is economic, more than Trump administration pressure or Apple's concentration risk on TSMC and Taiwan. (§ Apple and Intel: “the most obvious reason for a deal”) ^clm-0b2ea9c5036aeec35550
- On Apple's fiscal Q2 2026 earnings call, Tim Cook said the main supply limit in the March and June quarters is advanced chip nodes, not memory, that Apple undercalled demand, and that Mac mini and Mac Studio will take several months to balance. (§ Apple and Intel: “the primary constraint is the availability of the advanced nodes our SoCs are produced on”) ^clm-ef33afd2133a0a5c2e7c
- Thompson says new chips take about five months to make, so the A19 Pro lines Apple added only started producing partway through the March 2026 quarter, which held back iPhone 17 Pro sales. (§ Apple and Intel: “it does take five months or so to make new chips”) ^clm-3f5d9a2ee9d9c1e4e5e2
- Thompson argues Apple likely moved TSMC capacity from the Mac to iPhone chips and now lacks chips for the Mac mini and Mac Studio too. (§ Apple and Intel: “Apple took capacity away from the Mac to make more iPhone chips”) ^clm-c9ffffa332f565ab6766
- Thompson argues Apple no longer has flexible access to TSMC capacity because so much goes to AI, and this is costing Apple meaningful money across several product lines. (§ Apple and Intel: “flexible access to TSMC capacity anymore, because so much of that capacity”) ^clm-1106ba65787dcf8a47cc
- Thompson argued in TSMC Risk (2026) that TSMC customers' biggest risk is not China but TSMC's monopoly and reluctance to invest at the industry's pace, causing forgone AI revenue that could justify making Samsung or Intel viable rivals. (§ Apple and Intel: “TSMC’s monopoly and reasonable reluctance to risk a rate of investment”) ^clm-aec57bace360b2238660
- Prediction: Thompson argues TSMC's failure to invest aggressively enough will give Intel what it most needs to become a viable foundry: Apple, the customer that did more than any other to make TSMC the leader. (§ Apple and Intel: “the customer who did more than any other to make TSMC into the leader”) ^clm-833f2b6ef75ef3ec7683
