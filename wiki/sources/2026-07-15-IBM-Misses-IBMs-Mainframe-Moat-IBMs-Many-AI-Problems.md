# IBM Misses, IBM’s Mainframe Moat, IBM’s Many AI Problems

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-07-15.

## Summary

IBM's preliminary Q2 2026 revenue missed estimates and its stock fell 25%, its worst day since at least 1968. IBM blamed customers moving capex from new z17 mainframes to servers, storage, and memory ahead of price rises, which also hurt its usage-priced Transaction Processing software. Thompson argues this has little to do with seat-based SaaS firms like ServiceNow and Workday, and notes IBM gave the opposite explanation for weak mainframe software a year earlier. His bigger worry is that AI is well suited to porting mainframe code, so customers now see a way out, as United Airlines showed, and will hold off on new mainframes and bigger software deals.

## Key claims

- IBM's preliminary second-quarter 2026 revenue was $17.2 billion, below analyst estimates of $17.9 billion. (§ IBM Misses: “Preliminary second-quarter revenue totaled $17.2 billion, IBM said Tuesday in a statement, below analysts’ estimates of $17.9 billion”) ^clm-ff131f24f6fd9290c0ff
- Revenue in IBM's infrastructure division, which includes mainframes, fell 7% in the second quarter of 2026. (§ IBM Misses: “Sales from IBM’s infrastructure division, which includes mainframe computers, were especially hard hit, dropping 7%”) ^clm-8cd0647fd19cc1608a75
- IBM shares fell 25% to $217.07 on July 14, 2026, the biggest one-day loss since at least January 1968. (§ IBM Misses: “The shares plunged 25% to $217.07, the biggest single-day loss since at least Jan. 3, 1968”) ^clm-8ae1c910913cc3feb36b
- IBM's miss briefly pulled down other software stocks such as ServiceNow and Workday, which recovered part of the loss later that day. (§ IBM Misses: “stocks including ServiceNow Inc. and Workday Inc. slipping before making up some of the loss later in the trading day”) ^clm-56a36ae0d02e14cf5a5c
- Krishna says clients shifted their quarterly capex to servers, storage, and memory in late June to lock in scarce supply ahead of expected price rises, cutting mainframe purchases. (§ IBM Misses: “we saw clients shift their quarterly capex spend toward servers, storage, and memory purchases to secure supply-constrained infrastructure”) ^clm-d2167beceb0dd2891473
- IBM had guided in April 2026 that infrastructure revenue would fall low single digits for the year after the z17 launch, but the result was worse, driven by Z and its Transaction Processing software. (§ IBM Misses: “What played out was worse than our expectations, driven by a shortfall in our Z performance and the associated software stack”) ^clm-c0532491d28691e84b0a
- Krishna also says clients were distracted by fast-changing, industry-wide cybersecurity concerns during the quarter. (§ IBM Misses: “clients were distracted with rapidly-evolving, industry-wide cybersecurity concerns in the quarter”) ^clm-ca04517c51e64dc7fbbf
- Thompson argues the selloff in SaaS stocks was not clearly deserved, because SaaS is an opex seat license while IBM's miss came from capex on mainframes. (§ IBM Misses: “sell products that are operational expenses for their customers, i.e. opex, not capex”) ^clm-819e658be61cd352d828
- Thompson argues moving money within a capex budget is easier than cutting opex software licenses, which is bureaucratically harder. (§ IBM Misses: “it’s just more bureaucratically challenging than shifting capex budgets”) ^clm-a752c4a6ad37017d2ca4
- Thompson explains that IBM's mainframe software is tied to the hardware and priced more like usage than per seat, so skipped upgrades also mean skipped growth in software spending. (§ IBM Misses: “the pricing is much more akin to a usage-based model than it is a per-seat licensing model”) ^clm-d11ca606e438fd544657
- In Q3 2025, IBM said Transaction Processing revenue fell 3% because clients prioritized spending on z17 hardware. (§ IBM Misses: “Transaction Processing revenue declined by 3%, reflecting another quarter of z17 outperformance”) ^clm-c94b8bfb8f5b5d162168
- Prediction: In Q3 2025, Krishna predicted Transaction Processing software would grow low single digits into 2026 as new z17 capacity was deployed. (§ IBM Misses: “I expect to see TP grow, not quite in double digits to be clear, but let’s call it low single digits”) ^clm-619ab1dd5d76ef1afe81
- Thompson points out IBM reversed its story: a year ago it blamed strong z17 sales for weak Transaction Processing revenue, and now blames weak z17 sales for the same thing, so he suspects this quarter is the anomaly. (§ IBM Misses: “it sure seems to me that it’s this quarter that is the anomaly”) ^clm-0541ef1c6289294ee1f9
- Thompson argues IBM makes money coming and going: mainframe customers pay capex for the hardware and opex to IBM for the software that runs on it. (§ IBM’s Mainframe Moat: “you pay capex for the hardware and opex to IBM for the software that runs on it”) ^clm-e57f93597cc8f3fb2386
- Thompson argues almost no new company picks a mainframe; IBM sells to long-time customers like banks and airlines that are trapped because their transactions are too many and too complex to move. (§ IBM’s Mainframe Moat: “who IBM is selling to is existing customers, and those customers are trapped”) ^clm-c55e6e354b6010c6d783
- Scott Kirby says United spent several hundred million dollars rewriting code to get off its legacy mainframe systems, calling it a key unlock for everything else. (§ IBM’s Mainframe Moat: “we spent several hundred million dollars just getting off of our legacy mainframe systems”) ^clm-beb1dd6257f5d2d87a22
- Thompson notes Kirby may have overstated the move: United's old SHARES system ran on Unisys mainframes, and United still posts IBM mainframe developer jobs aimed at migrating to an Azure-based cloud. (§ IBM’s Mainframe Moat: “Kirby may have overstated things: SHARES was historically run on Unisys mainframes”) ^clm-99c4be20fdce143cc1b7
- Thompson argues moving off a mainframe removes IBM's combined hardware-and-software tax and lowers long-term costs, though the migration itself can be extremely expensive. (§ IBM’s Mainframe Moat: “Moving off a mainframe can eliminate IBM’s combined hardware-and-software tax and lower long-term marginal costs”) ^clm-8a86b304a86275eb65af
- Thompson argues most companies will keep paying IBM because they lack the developers or budget to port mainframe applications to the cloud. (§ IBM’s Mainframe Moat: “most companies will probably just pay IBM forever, because they don’t have the developers or budget”) ^clm-a3c584dfbd236307ac73
- Thompson calls IBM's short-term AI problem the crowd-out: mainframe upgrade money went to other hardware, most likely for AI projects. (§ IBM’s Many AI Problems: “mainframe customers repurposed spend that would have gone to upgrading their hardware to other hardware purchases”) ^clm-e6d1cd2ca2ee51dbd26a
- Thompson argues IBM's long-term AI problem is worse: porting code is one of the tasks AI suits best, and mainframe code is the most valuable code to port. (§ IBM’s Many AI Problems: “of all the things that AI is well-suited for, porting code has to be near the top of the list”) ^clm-9c0b26065d1060aa697b
- Thompson suspects customers did not move off mainframes this quarter, but became aware AI makes it possible, so they delay new mainframes and shift new usage elsewhere. (§ IBM’s Many AI Problems: “IBM customers just became aware that it was possible to do so with AI”) ^clm-9878d5ddce92ec2878d1
- Prediction: Thompson predicts IBM's high-margin mainframe business is in trouble: the longer customers wait without buying, the better AI porting will get. (§ IBM’s Many AI Problems: “the longer they wait to decide without making any purchases, the better the AI will become”) ^clm-0dddcd89296ddec1650c
- IBM's consulting business grew on demand for help adding AI, IBM framed Mythos as a boost to its cybersecurity business, and its other hardware lines gained from the capex shift. (§ IBM’s Many AI Problems: “Its consulting business grew, for example, thanks to companies looking for help in incorporating AI”) ^clm-ce6e0050f078f0b3eb41
