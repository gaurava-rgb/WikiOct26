# The SpaceX IPO and Data Centers in Space

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-27.

## Summary

Thompson reads the SpaceX S-1 and says no financial model justifies a $2 trillion valuation on $18.67 billion of 2025 revenue and a $4.9 billion loss driven by xAI. He argues Musk wins by changing the rules through scale, as Starlink has done to airlines, and sells a dream that capital markets turn into reality. He then makes the case that data centers in space are possible (a satellite as a rack), have a use case (agentic inference), and have a stick (local opposition to building on earth). He still thinks the IPO is nuts, but is glad it exists as a real bet on building more compute.

## Key claims

- Uber stopped allowing new Tesla Model Y cars onto its premium Uber Black tier in 2025. (“Uber finally stopped allowing new Model Y’s onto Black”) ^clm-ec61c248f1d1a2295412
- Tesla stopped production of the Model S and Model X in early 2026 to focus production resources on the CyberCab and robots. (“focusing production resources on the CyberCab and robots”) ^clm-6a5b009d55f03a1b3dc9
- American Airlines announced in May 2026 it will install Starlink Wi-Fi on more than 500 narrowbody aircraft beginning in Q1 2027. (§ Starlink and Airlines: “on more than 500 narrowbody aircraft beginning in Q1 2027”) ^clm-abd52413b578bd3e816d
- Thompson argues Starlink on planes has gone from a premium perk, as with United's 2024 deal, to table stakes, because airlines fear rivals will offer it and customers will choose flights by Internet quality. (§ Starlink and Airlines: “the stick is the prospect of everyone else having the same service”) ^clm-a396f50691ba5902909d
- Starlink, SpaceX's consumer-facing business, generated $8.7 billion in revenue and $4.4 billion in profit in 2025, helped by access to SpaceX's launch capacity. (§ Starlink and Airlines: “generating $8.7 billion in revenue last year and $4.4 billion in profit”) ^clm-45387c54ff89b732f21b
- SpaceX's launch capacity has put over ten thousand active Starlink satellites in low Earth orbit as of May 2026. (§ Starlink and Airlines: “That launch capacity has resulted in over ten thousand active satellites in low Earth orbit”) ^clm-78786b9892260e1bc52c
- Thompson argues Musk companies at their best change the rules of the game through scale, making all-in bets by starting from the desired end state and working backwards rather than making rational short-term decisions. (§ Starlink and Airlines: “Musk companies at their best don’t win the game; they change the rules through scale”) ^clm-3768b3f6d35cc437d27f
- SpaceX's S-1 claims a total addressable market of $28.5 trillion: $370 billion in space, $1.6 trillion in connectivity, and $26.5 trillion in AI, including $22.7 trillion in enterprise applications. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “We estimate that our quantifiable TAM is $28.5 trillion”) ^clm-4c715663244b440a5721
- Thompson calls the S-1's market numbers obviously absurd, noting the $26.5 trillion AI opportunity is more than 13 times the space and connectivity opportunity combined. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “the numbers are obviously absurd, but then again, everything about this IPO is absurd”) ^clm-be1939d2c76f2e84a6f4
- SpaceX is seeking a $2 trillion IPO valuation on $18.67 billion of 2025 revenue and a $4.9 billion loss, and its revenue growth slowed from 35% to 33%. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “SpaceX is seeking a $2 trillion valuation on a mere $18.67 billion in revenue”) ^clm-9f5785b80c9f4b3d798c
- Adding xAI, and with it X, tipped SpaceX from a small profit to a $4.9 billion loss in 2025, driven by $5.1 billion of AI research and development expense. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “tipped the company from a small profit to that massive loss”) ^clm-332414dcbacb351a3952
- Thompson notes xAI's $5.1 billion of R&D built a model in 5th place whose entire founding team has recently left the company. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “went towards building a model that is in 5th place”) ^clm-4f95ef9ff87f5c63e7b5
- Thompson argues Musk is the master of memes who sells a dream, such as Mars or a $28.5 trillion market, and uses his stock as access to it, so capital markets turn shared delusion into reality; he will not cover SpaceX's financials, as with Tesla's. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “transforms shared delusion into mass market reality”) ^clm-c0a07ab87f1957f03689
- Thompson argues Musk's track record with electric cars and landing rockets gives him the benefit of the doubt, so the question is whether the dream is possible, not whether today's numbers make sense. (§ SpaceX’s Silly S-1: “That Musk succeeded in both instances gives him the benefit of the doubt”) ^clm-27367e1c14dfa0cbb4f7
- The largest Starlink satellite, the V2 Mini Direct-to-Cell, measures about 7.4 by 2.7 by 0.3 meters and uses up to about 25kW, while an Nvidia NVL72 rack measures 2.2 by 1.1 by 0.6 meters, uses up to 135kW, and can fit a 1 trillion parameter model at FP4. (§ The Case for Data Centers in Space: “the largest Starlink satellite in orbit is the V2 Mini Direct-to-Cell”) ^clm-0c8743d1a3cb4c8bfbae
- Thompson argues data centers in space are possible if each satellite is treated as a rack rather than copying earth data centers, linked by lasers with its own solar panels and radiators, though 200+ square meters of radiators per rack will be a huge challenge. (§ The Case for Data Centers in Space: “think about an individual satellite as something akin to a rack”) ^clm-681e0c18b63a45e15b8f
- Thompson argues radiation and reliability problems for rack-satellites can be eased with larger, less efficient but lower-power chips, and by making the satellites disposable like Starlink; about 100kW might match an NVL72 since earth-style cooling and power distribution are not needed. (§ The Case for Data Centers in Space: “these rack-satellites will also be disposable, like Starlink satellites”) ^clm-179ec5b1f6235315a20c
- Thompson argues agentic inference, which can tolerate delays, is the workload that makes most sense for racks in space, and is also likely the largest market in the long run. (§ The Case for Data Centers in Space: “It’s agentic inference that makes the most sense for racks in space”) ^clm-2bf7683e167578cf2eb5
- Prediction: Thompson predicts compute demand may grow so large, and local opposition and zoning so binding, that there is eventually nowhere left to build on earth, making space not just an alternative but the only choice. (§ The Case for Data Centers in Space: “there eventually is nowhere left to build, making the vast expanses of space not just an alternative”) ^clm-ed07d4c153cf975c9145
- SpaceX is monetizing xAI's first data center, Colossus 1, at $15 billion a year for 300MW of capacity, which Thompson equates to 3,000 racks-in-space. (§ An IPO Worth Supporting: “to the tune of $15 billion/year for 300MW of capacity”) ^clm-a5ea26b5a4576a0ec89f
- Prediction: Thompson predicts Anthropic will probably make 3x SpaceX's revenue on the Colossus 1 capacity, and that xAI could earn more per rack if it gets back to the state of the art. (§ An IPO Worth Supporting: “Anthropic, meanwhile, will probably make 3x the revenue on that capacity”) ^clm-b71de43675140daf12e5
- Thompson argues that even without xAI, SpaceX could become a monopoly provider of marginal compute capacity through data centers in space. (§ An IPO Worth Supporting: “SpaceX has the potential to be a monopoly provider of marginal compute capacity”) ^clm-fb2407e212f738abcaa3
- Thompson says the case rests on many assumptions, including Starship working, chip supply, far larger compute demand, agentic inference unbundling current architectures, and data center opponents succeeding, so he still thinks the IPO is nuts. (§ An IPO Worth Supporting: “which is to say I still think this IPO is nuts”) ^clm-3aea8d0982f6cf68e87e
- Thompson says he is glad the SpaceX IPO exists because he worries about mustering enough compute for AI and fears a replay of nuclear power, where failure to build denied society a world of unlimited energy. (§ An IPO Worth Supporting: “am very worried about a replay of nuclear power”) ^clm-0b6d3aef603b499b6f6b
- Thompson argues the SpaceX IPO returns IPOs to their purpose, letting people fund the building of the business, so retail investors get to invest like a Series A venture capitalist, with big upside and big risk. (§ An IPO Worth Supporting: “It’s a testament to SpaceX’s ambitions that retail investors get to play VC”) ^clm-59c7743b0c96822a3aae
