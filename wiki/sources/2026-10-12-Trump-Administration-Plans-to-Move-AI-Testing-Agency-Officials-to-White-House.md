# Trump Administration Plans to Move AI Testing Agency Officials to White House

Leo Schwartz, The Information, published 2026-10-12.

## Summary

The Trump administration is moving three senior officials of CAISSI, the small Commerce Department agency that tests frontier AI models, to report to the White House. CAISSI has about 40 staff and $15 million a year, yet it works with the NSA to test models like OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Mythos before release under an August 2026 framework. The agency has had three names in under two years, constant turnover, and was left out of Trump's new super intelligence task force. Labs worry it lacks resources and have explored their own testing group.

## Key claims

- In September 2026 the Trump administration began restructuring CAISSI so that three of its senior officials report directly to the White House, per a White House official. (§ The core story: “restructuring CAISSI so that three of its senior officials report directly to the White House”) ^clm-13ecdcfeabef20299907
- After months of weighing a move of all of CAISSI into the White House, Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and other officials chose the smaller move to better fit CAISSI into White House AI decisions. (§ The core story: “settled on the smaller move to better fit CAISSI into White House decision-making on AI”) ^clm-be4fdc7fae67b48011db
- The three CAISSI officials will report to OSTP director Michael Kratsios and National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross; Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had invited both to co-manage CAISSI about six months earlier. (§ The core story: “Lutnick invited Kratsios and Cairncross to co-manage CAISSI six months before publication”) ^clm-17dbef951a7583fe44aa
- The White House and the Commerce Department have been confused about the CAISSI move; a White House official said Commerce's poor coordination shows why CAISSI leadership must move. (§ The core story: “Commerce's lack of coordination shows why CAISSI leadership needs to move”) ^clm-9b8de668807a39c6b1c8
- CAISSI recently renamed itself to match Trump's preferred term Super Intelligence, its third name in less than two years. (§ A troubled agency: “It is the agency's third name in less than two years”) ^clm-a7ad7028433604ba31d6
- Some officials want CAISSI moved out of Commerce for better resources; Michael Kratsios has argued for moving it to the Department of Energy to use the national laboratories. (§ A troubled agency: “Kratsios has argued for moving it to the Department of Energy”) ^clm-389fca409f8221e93d34
- As of October 2026 CAISSI has only about 40 staff and about $15 million in annual funding, yet plays a key role in testing new AI models for capabilities and safety. (§ A troubled agency: “CAISSI has only about 40 staff and about $15 million in annual funding”) ^clm-27549ac2f39a859244ea
- Under a White House framework finalized in August 2026, CAISSI works with the NSA to test frontier models such as OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Mythos before broader release, per four people familiar. (§ Pre-deployment testing: “test frontier models like OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Mythos before broader release”) ^clm-00d387e98c2774218f9b
- Google said in a September 30, 2026 blog post that it had submitted Gemini 4 Argon to the White House for voluntary testing; as of October 12 it still had not widely released the model. (§ Pre-deployment testing: “submitted its latest model, Gemini 4 Argon, to the White House for the voluntary testing process”) ^clm-0dfb9e274d88634f411b
- The White House still has not published its pre-deployment testing framework, and the split of duties between CAISSI and the NSA remains unclear, which worries the AI industry. (§ Pre-deployment testing: “The White House still has not published the testing framework”) ^clm-d54a21decbef6670f57b
- Google, OpenAI and Anthropic explored forming their own industry group to supplement CAISSI's model testing, partly because they worry the agency lacks resources. (§ Pre-deployment testing: “explored forming their own industry group to supplement CAISSI's model testing”) ^clm-8089045f8e9efad823b8
- In early October 2026 Trump named a new super intelligence task force led by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, with nobody from Commerce and no mention of CAISSI. (§ Left out of the new task force: “nobody from Commerce and no mention of CAISSI”) ^clm-9c39e9a431008b2ef9ba
- The Biden administration created the agency as the AI Safety Institute under a 2023 executive order; under AI czar David Sacks the Trump White House renamed it the Center for AI Standards and Innovation. (§ Political football: history: “the White House renamed it the Center for AI Standards and Innovation”) ^clm-59b79c19b74add32bc0c
- The Trump administration hired AI testing expert Collin Burns to lead the agency but fired him within days because he had worked at Anthropic, The Washington Post reported. (§ Political football: history: “fired him within days because he had worked at Anthropic”) ^clm-8ec869eb3465cbd84763
- The Trump administration imposed export controls on Anthropic in June 2026 over security concerns about Mythos and Fable, after which the White House leaned more on CAISSI's expertise. (§ Political football: history: “imposed export controls on Anthropic”) ^clm-d1cf9ab13f48fa77239f
- CAISSI director Chris Fall resigned unexpectedly in July 2026 after three months, and NIST director Arvind Raman is overseeing the agency while it seeks a new head. (§ Turnover: “director Chris Fall unexpectedly resigned after three months”) ^clm-be28767e965c1be199dc
- CAISSI cyber lead Andrew Fasano left for Anthropic in June 2026, and adviser Paul Christiano joined OpenAI's nonprofit board in September 2026. (§ Turnover: “Cyber lead Andrew Fasano left in June 2026 to join Anthropic”) ^clm-908ae93b1c6956e20ce1
