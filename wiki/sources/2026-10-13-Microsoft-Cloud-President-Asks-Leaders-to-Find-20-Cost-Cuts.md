# Microsoft Cloud President Asks Leaders to Find 20% Cost Cuts

Kevin McLaughlin, Aaron Holmes, The Information, published 2026-10-13.

## Summary

Azure president Girish Bablani asked leaders of his 11,500-person unit to find cost cuts of at least 20%, per one person, though Microsoft denies any such directive. The push comes even as Azure growth speeds up, showing how AI data center spending and talent retention costs weigh on Microsoft. Microsoft is also reorganizing the cloud unit around Arun Ulag. Azure made $101.9 billion in the year to June 2026, while Microsoft's free cash flow fell 20% year over year in the June quarter.

## Key claims

- Azure president Girish Bablani asked senior leaders in his roughly 11,500-person unit to find ideas to cut costs at least 20%, per one person; it is unclear if this includes layoffs. (§ The cost-cut ask: “told senior leaders in his group, about 11,500 employees, to find ideas to cut costs at least 20%”) ^clm-afb59ed60104461f9af8
- Microsoft denied the report, saying Azure leadership has not directed teams to develop cost-cutting plans and calling it inaccurate and careless reporting. (§ Microsoft denies it: “has not directed teams to develop cost-cutting plans”) ^clm-6e1c9006b82478cfc484
- Bablani's unit builds software for the cloud server business and is separate from the Azure data center team; Microsoft's broader cloud division has about 60,000 employees. (§ The cost-cut ask: “The broader cloud division has about 60,000 employees”) ^clm-1dc801bd930fcf95cd73
- The Information reads the Azure cost talks, which come as Azure growth accelerates, as a sign of how heavily data center spending weighs on Microsoft's finances. (§ The cost-cut ask: “a sign of how much data center spending weighs on Microsoft's finances”) ^clm-9a40b66aa244ed0f3e38
- Some Azure leaders cite talent retention as another reason for cuts, since Anthropic and Meta have hired or tried to hire Microsoft staff and Microsoft has had to match offers. (§ Talent costs and AI budgets: “Microsoft has had to match competing offers”) ^clm-8a780addc9d2a4f9575b
- Cloud chief Scott Guthrie has discussed raising AI spending budgets for existing Microsoft employees as the company automates more work, and Microsoft has been raising AI spend per employee in recent months. (§ Talent costs and AI budgets: “wanting to raise AI spending budgets for existing employees as the company automates more work”) ^clm-b0c57c0c383ce1da1e5d
- Microsoft's total headcount in June 2026 was slightly lower than a year earlier, after several waves of job cuts and voluntary buyouts. (§ Talent costs and AI budgets: “after several waves of job cuts and voluntary buyouts”) ^clm-23f013d0ccbffa751137
- Oracle and AWS have also made cuts, including layoffs, despite faster revenue growth, because rising AI data center capital spending hurt their free cash flow. (§ Other clouds are cutting too: “Oracle and Amazon Web Services have also made cuts, including layoffs”) ^clm-a9f9546b460cc79c4f7b
- Microsoft is reorganizing its cloud unit around Arun Ulag, who now also oversees Azure Core executives including Bablani and Mark Russinovich; Azure Core provides computing power to customers such as OpenAI. (§ Reorg around Arun Ulag: “which provides computing power to customers such as OpenAI”) ^clm-24b26ad26c1a91b28eea
- Scott Guthrie announced the Azure reorg internally on 2026-10-12, with Ulag still reporting to him; Charlie Bell joins Ulag's leadership team while still reporting to Satya Nadella. (§ Reorg around Arun Ulag: “Guthrie announced the changes internally on Monday (2026-10-12) and said Ulag will keep reporting to him”) ^clm-32c7de014fac85bd0081
- Microsoft began reporting Azure revenue in dollars in September 2026: $101.9 billion in the 12 months to June 2026, up 40% from a year earlier. (§ Azure's numbers: “Azure made $101.9 billion in the 12 months ending June 2026, up 40%”) ^clm-69849583cb98ac093963
- Azure revenue grew 43% in the June 2026 quarter, while AWS grew 37% that quarter and made nearly $150 billion over the 12 months to June. (§ Azure's numbers: “grew 43% in the June quarter alone”) ^clm-e672a838a0f31ca6e99e
- Azure's growth was held back by Microsoft's decision at the start of 2025 to pause many data center projects, per earlier reporting by The Information. (§ Azure's numbers: “pause many data center projects”) ^clm-971f4040b2b7a954d86f
- Azure is now about 31% of Microsoft's total revenue. (§ Azure's numbers: “Azure is now about 31% of Microsoft's total revenue”) ^clm-a19c8b5d7e1b1fd081e6
- Microsoft generated $20 billion of free cash flow in the June 2026 quarter, up more than $4 billion from the first quarter but down 20% from a year earlier, as AI data center spending takes a toll. (§ Cash flow squeeze: “down 20% from the same quarter a year earlier”) ^clm-108b71e0eb721138e521
