# Intel Earnings, Intel’s Differentiation?, Whither Terafab

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-04-29.

## Summary

Intel's Q1 2026 results and a June-quarter forecast far above estimates sent its stock to a record, but Thompson argues the main driver is structural: inference and agents need far more CPUs relative to GPUs. CEO Lip-Bu Tan dodged two analyst questions on how Intel's CPUs differ from AMD and Arm rivals, and Intel even sold shelved and de-specced chips, which shows demand, not differentiation. Thompson suggests Intel's real edge may be owning fabs that are not bound by TSMC capacity, which could flip the original foundry thesis. He reads Intel's role in Musk's Terafab as a speculative process-licensing deal where Intel's payoff is more in process learning than in cash.

## Key claims

- Intel guided June-quarter 2026 revenue to $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion, versus an analyst average of $13 billion, and its shares hit a record high. (§ Intel Earnings: “Revenue will be $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion in the June quarter”) ^clm-866257409fc67249ff14
- Thompson argues Lip-Bu Tan has had a real positive impact on Intel, but the number one factor driving Intel's performance is structural: AI. (§ Intel Earnings: “The number one factor driving Intel’s performance, however, is structural: AI.”) ^clm-adb67590538ca06829e9
- Tan says the CPU is reinserting itself as the foundation of the AI era, serving as the orchestration layer and control plane for the AI stack. (§ Intel Earnings: “the CPU is reinserting itself as the indispensable foundation of the AI era”) ^clm-b7c1835a8bf4c3a89340
- Tan says Xeon server CPU demand has strong and sustained momentum, with customers deploying CPUs alongside accelerators in a ratio moving back toward CPUs. (§ Intel Earnings: “Xeon server demand is seeing strong and sustained momentum”) ^clm-ddc64d6040be9797be0b
- Tan says the ratio of CPUs to GPUs used to be 1 to 8, is now 1 to 4, and is heading toward parity or better. (§ Intel Earnings: “the ratio of CPU to GPU used to be 1 and 8, and now it’s 1:4”) ^clm-e5b61f4133ce0abbaceb
- Prediction: Tan predicts the CPU to GPU ratio will move toward parity or even better, making the CPU franchise a growth engine for years, not just quarters. (§ Intel Earnings: “not just the quarters ahead”) ^clm-34615f505e07266cf728
- Thompson argues the CPU shift makes sense: training is GPU bound, but inference means many short jobs that need more orchestration, and agents add tool use on top, so they need still more CPUs. (§ Intel Earnings: “Once you get to inference, however, the jobs are much shorter but much more numerous”) ^clm-d82b30c00bc530806b9a
- Thompson argues the move from training to inference and agents helps AWS, easing worries about its training competitiveness: networking between GPUs matters less, while CPUs and access to data already on AWS matter more. (§ Intel Earnings: “so does that sort of horizontally scalable networking between GPUs”) ^clm-77ef3ae7f896d93b3984
- Asked whether Intel's server CPUs have competitive differentiation versus other x86 makers or Arm, Tan talked about ratios, packaging and foundry instead; Thompson says he completely dodged the question. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “he completely dodged the question about competitive differentiation within the CPU market”) ^clm-dc6737d5c4422059d2c9
- Asked about share versus AMD and competition from Arm chips such as Nvidia's Vera, Amazon's Graviton and Google's Axion, Tan cited Intel's roadmap, his friendship with Arm CEO Rene Haas, and his investment in Annapurna Labs, which Thompson calls a total dodge. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “Tan’s answer was again a total dodge”) ^clm-aacbf4fd2db497b8311f
- Zinsner says Q1 was Intel's lowest supply point of the year, and Intel beat expectations partly by selling de-specced and shelved legacy finished goods it had not expected to move. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “It was either de-specced product or it was legacy product we had shelved”) ^clm-fe9d15506089ae0182e2
- Prediction: Zinsner says Intel may not have the inventory boost again in Q2, so volume growth from Q1 to Q2 will rely on increasing supply. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “Q2 versus Q1 is going to be increasing supply”) ^clm-33e3396d9a3eeaa87732
- Thompson argues CPU demand is so high that Intel is scrounging through the couch cushions, and chips it sold that way are negatively differentiated beyond simply being available. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “Intel is basically scrounging through the couch cushions”) ^clm-3f35ffd305ddff7fc59e
- Thompson revisits his 2021 thesis that Intel needed outside foundry customers to fund the leading edge, asking whether Intel may instead consume all its own fab capacity because CPU demand is insatiable. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “what if Intel actually ends up consuming all of its fabs’ capacity”) ^clm-1bdd025625b7e38f3f54
- Thompson argues Intel's most important long-run advantage may be that it is the only CPU maker not constrained by TSMC capacity. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “the only CPU maker that is not constrained by TSMC capacity”) ^clm-df9f359bdc1b96ff81f6
- Thompson argues the foundry shift was not a mistake: it exposed how far behind Intel was on efficiency and yields, which Tan has improved and which drive near-term supply. (§ Intel’s Differentiation?: “The foundry model exposed how badly behind Intel was on both points”) ^clm-44c338582c0f1ec3efaa
- Tesla plans to spend about $3 billion on a research chip factory at Giga Texas producing a few thousand wafers a month, an early phase of Terafab that SpaceX will lead. (§ Whither Terafab: “Tesla Inc. plans to spend roughly $3 billion to build a research chip factory in Texas”) ^clm-2f90af6e49eaa04b690f
- Musk says Terafab plans to use Intel's 14A process, which is not yet complete but should be mature by the time Terafab scales up. (§ Whither Terafab: “we plan to use Intel’s 14A process, which is state-of-the-art and, in fact, not yet totally complete”) ^clm-a7b9faf596ec848ac3b2
- Tan says Intel has partnered with SpaceX, xAI and Tesla on Terafab because he and Musk believe global chip supply is not keeping pace with demand. (§ Whither Terafab: “global semiconductor supply is not keeping pace with the rapid acceleration in demand”) ^clm-a221e30c6b75379c99f7
- Tan says 14A is making great progress on yield and cycle time with multiple customers heavily engaged, but Intel will not announce customers unless they choose to. (§ Whither Terafab: “we are making great progress in terms of yield and the cycle time”) ^clm-53e05e1130ca60afc466
- Thompson reads the Terafab deal as a highly speculative process-licensing agreement, where Intel's payoff is less money than the process improvements Musk may find, a reasonable bet Tan seems unsure will pay off. (§ Whither Terafab: “it is a process-licensing agreement, but a highly speculative one”) ^clm-aff9ecd5e0ef58ad4d7b
- Thompson praises Tan for not claiming things that do not exist, whether Intel differentiation or concrete growth from Tesla. (§ Whither Terafab: “I appreciate how Tan doesn’t claim things that don’t exist”) ^clm-7d707471da35798418ce
