# Nvidia Earnings, Dollars Per Gigawatt, Open and Hugging Face

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-09-01.

## Summary

Thompson reads Nvidia's forecast of 70% growth for its next fiscal year as a supply number, not a demand number: Nvidia still sells everything it can make, as he argued in February 2024, so its results are both remarkable and boring. He compares Nvidia to the early iPhone, whose true demand was unknown until supply caught up. Nvidia's pitch on the call was dollars per gigawatt, its growing share of each data center's spending, and growth outside the hyperscalers, which Thompson says fits buyers who have too many other problems to worry about Nvidia's margins. He finds Jensen Huang's answer on OpenAI's own chip weak. He argues a reported Nvidia purchase of Hugging Face is simple: like Microsoft buying GitHub, it keeps the open model world healthy, and a fragmented world of many buyers protects Nvidia's margins.

## Key claims

- Nvidia projected 70% sales growth for its next fiscal year, far above the analyst average estimate of 45%, sending its shares on their biggest one-day rally since April 2025. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “projected sales growth of 70% for the next fiscal year”) ^clm-ffce9acf754bf7911efc
- Nvidia shares rose 8.7% to $227.98 the day after earnings, adding about $442 billion in market value and leaving the stock up 22% for 2026. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Nvidia shares rose 8.7% to $227.98 in New York on Thursday”) ^clm-8669962604c0cf0d7a03
- CFO Colette Kress said Nvidia would grow even faster with more supply, and that customers' forecasts point to Nvidia's growth doubling next year. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Customers’ forecasts point to our growth doubling next year”) ^clm-eb88bbda695f76346999
- Thompson recalls arguing in February 2024 that because Nvidia demand exceeds supply, its sales are in effect controlled by its suppliers from TSMC down, with price rises its only lever. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “the company’s sales are, in some respect, controlled by Nvidia’s suppliers, from TSMC on down”) ^clm-a18fdb0c93b5ebb83a93
- Thompson says that two and a half years after his February 2024 view, Nvidia is without question still limited by supply, and that is the subtext of its full-year forecast. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Nvidia is without question still limited by supply”) ^clm-18e11773680fa3420011
- Jensen Huang said Nvidia has never before guided a year ahead, and that although demand is much greater than 70% growth, its supply lets it confidently deliver 70%. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “even though our demand is much greater than 70%, our supply allows us to confidently deliver 70%”) ^clm-035fcc91e5a2f5df4cc1
- Huang said AI infrastructure now requires securing land, power, and shell two to three years out, so Nvidia works with power generation and land companies far upstream. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “You’ve got to go secure the land, power and shell, which oftentimes is a couple 2, 3 years out”) ^clm-d30566b0c8bf220f8217
- Thompson compares Nvidia to the early iPhone: revenue was set by how many units could be made, and true demand was unknown until supply was sufficient around the iPhone 6S. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “we didn’t truly know what iPhone demand was until the iPhone 6S generation”) ^clm-dbff6428f049805e4483
- Thompson argues Nvidia's earnings ought to be boring because top-line revenue is a function of component supply, and real demand will only be known when supply is abundant. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “we won’t know what demand truly is until supply is abundant”) ^clm-3caac4b6388d768085e1
- Nvidia grew 100% and forecasts 70% growth, which Thompson says is unquestionably a function of supply rather than demand, nearly four years after ChatGPT launched. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Nvidia grew 100% and is forecasting growth of “only” 70%”) ^clm-fcbd42125d3dac02d6d3
- Prediction: Thompson predicts Nvidia would still be accelerating if it could meet demand, and sees no sign any of this is slowing down. (§ Nvidia Earnings: “Nvidia would still be accelerating if it could actually meet demand”) ^clm-d5a0171ac68728fa9920
- Kress said Nvidia's revenue opportunity per gigawatt grew from about $18 billion with Hopper to $25 billion with Blackwell and $40 billion with Vera Rubin. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “roughly $18 billion per gigawatt to $25 billion with Blackwell, to $40 billion with Vera Rubin”) ^clm-4cd6b6eb00b099aa7d5a
- Vera Rubin spans the Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, NVLink, InfiniBand or Ethernet networking, and the Groq LPU announced the same week. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “Vera CPU, Rubin GPU, NVLink, InfiniBand or Ethernet and Groq LPU announced earlier this week”) ^clm-f24ec959d0d60288b54d
- Huang said sovereign AI, regional AIs, neoclouds, AI startups, and enterprises make up about half of Nvidia's business and are growing 100% a year. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “which represents about half of our business, and that’s growing 100% a year”) ^clm-eb2b80eacd90569f8604
- Prediction: Huang predicted the non-hyperscaler part of AI computing will likely become larger over time than the cloud part. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “likely to be larger over time than even what we’re currently experiencing in the cloud”) ^clm-fdd7df27e91454721974
- Thompson argues that when buyers face many hard problems, the integrated piece that solves several at once is especially valuable, so Nvidia's margins sit at the bottom of their worries. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “the integrated piece that solves multiple problems at once is particularly valuable”) ^clm-873a86c756e9bca461e4
- Thompson argues the exceptions are hyperscalers, whose scale makes it profitable to solve Nvidia's margins themselves, and frontier labs, whose demand and costs justify capturing more margin. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “the sort of scale that makes it possible and profitable to solve every problem you have”) ^clm-db09efaa32d1cd7e313a
- Asked about OpenAI's new chip, Huang said Nvidia is a platform for the whole AI life cycle in every cloud, unlike inference-specific XPUs for one cloud or service, and that OpenAI will stay a customer. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “many of these XPUs are inference-specific chips for one cloud or one service”) ^clm-a38c8566269d8bd2f5a0
- Thompson calls Huang's OpenAI chip answer about five different answers, none compelling if the frontier labs win everything, and notes Huang then reminded investors of Nvidia's big stakes in both labs. (§ Dollars Per Gigawatt: “That’s about five different answers, none of them particularly compelling”) ^clm-2688c7fb192c10387f14
- Huang said nearly all open models run on Nvidia because its footprint is the largest and its architecture the most fungible, and that frontier labs have fantastic margins and are limited only by compute. (§ Open and Hugging Face: “nearly all open models run on Nvidia”) ^clm-797c1f6feca42f8059af
- Thompson argues that as long as the world is fragmented Nvidia wins, and its margins come under pressure only if a few buyers can take part of its stack or replace it. (§ Open and Hugging Face: “As long as the world is fragmented Nvidia wins”) ^clm-c98c4720cec919a6ef79
- The Information reported that Nvidia agreed to buy Hugging Face, though the deal had not been announced. (§ Open and Hugging Face: “is Nvidia buying Hugging Face, the open model hosting service”) ^clm-7398f81ba8988e264408
- Nvidia said on its earnings call it had committed to $36 billion in cloud services contracts as credit support for customers, and could be responsible for that compute if customers cannot lease it out. (§ Open and Hugging Face: “it had committed to $36 billion in cloud services contracts as part of credit support agreements”) ^clm-5681f317fa52420e23d1
- Nvidia had committed $13 billion to rent back its own chips from cloud providers for DGX Cloud, a business it backed away from a year earlier, once thinking it could reach $150 billion in revenue. (§ Open and Hugging Face: “Nvidia had committed to spend $13 billion to rent back its own AI chips”) ^clm-0c42aa9d50efcf7af4d1
- Thompson argues Nvidia would buy Hugging Face, a Schelling point for open models as GitHub was for open source, to keep open weights thriving, which protects its long-term margins without needing profit from it. (§ Open and Hugging Face: “Hugging Face is a critical Schelling point for open source models”) ^clm-5995ca5a16578a448d43
