# Agents Over Bubbles

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-03-16.

## Summary

Thompson reverses his earlier view that AI is a bubble. Working agents (late 2025) multiply compute demand, need only a few people with initiative to drive that demand, and give companies a reason to replace staff with agents. Because agents need a model and a harness built together, the labs that integrate both (Anthropic, OpenAI) may be more profitable than the 'models are commodities' view expects. Microsoft's Copilot Cowork is his evidence.

## Key claims

- In March 2026 Thompson concluded the AI industry is not in a bubble, reversing his earlier stance that it was (and that bubbles can be good). (“I’ve come to a different conclusion: I don’t think we’re in a bubble”) ^clm-79fd8b2a6ea00f00568b
- The first LLM inflection point was the ChatGPT launch in November 2022. (§ LLM Paradigms: “The first LLM inflection point was the November 2022 launch of ChatGPT”) ^clm-35028dda6e2470f2ce0a
- The second LLM inflection point was OpenAI's o1 reasoning model in September 2024, which reasons over its answer before delivering it. (§ LLM Paradigms: “The second LLM inflection point was the release of OpenAI’s o1 model in September 2024”) ^clm-eb57b4a9f0a67c0dde88
- Anthropic released Opus 4.5 on November 24, 2025, and by December Claude Code with Opus 4.5 could do things never possible before. (§ LLM Paradigms: “Anthropic released Opus 4.5 on November 24, 2025, to relatively little fanfare”) ^clm-3fcad85d76a9aa335faf
- OpenAI released GPT-5.2-Codex on December 18, 2025, with similar agent capability. (§ LLM Paradigms: “OpenAI released GPT-5.2-Codex around the same time, on December 18”) ^clm-f0cb5e257c3d37f83d17
- A critical part of agentic workloads is the harness, the software that controls the model and can use deterministic tools to verify results. (§ LLM Paradigms: “a critical component of making agentic workloads work is the “harness”, i.e. the software that actually controls the model”) ^clm-cdffde2b3399c011ef38
- Each paradigm raised compute needs: chatbots were cheap at inference, reasoning multiplied tokens per answer, and agents call reasoning models many times and also need CPUs for tools. (§ The Decreased Need for Agency: “the agent itself needs compute, and that compute — and the tools the agent uses — is”) ^clm-f44be75ec4759b4c3e16
- Thompson argues one person can control many agents, so AI compute demand can soar without wide adoption by many people. (§ The Decreased Need for Agency: “any one single human can control multiple agents”) ^clm-67b5219852984749fdc4
- Thompson argues Anthropic got it right by focusing almost entirely on the enterprise market, because companies pay for productivity while most consumers do not want to pay for AI. (§ Enterprise Economic Imperatives: “Anthropic got it right by focusing almost entirely on the enterprise market”) ^clm-188f77eee1b4ab113e40
- Prediction: Thompson predicts companies will cut staff more rather than less and rebuild scale with agents, and that this is likely to get ugly. (§ Enterprise Economic Imperatives: “the most forward-looking and future-proof approach will likely be to cut more rather than less”) ^clm-c8168c0e045a56c171c5
- Thompson notes every hyperscaler says demand for compute exceeds supply and each is announcing capex plans above expectations. (§ Enterprise Economic Imperatives: “every single hyperscaler says that demand for compute exceeds supply”) ^clm-c580696b8b6d0e7a9cd4
- Horace Dediu argued Apple's choice to license Google's Gemini for about $1 billion a year, instead of building its own model, is brilliant because models are commoditizing. (§ Agents and the AI Value Chain: “It didn’t build its own AI model, it licensed Google’s Gemini for about $1 billion a year”) ^clm-a82f2b3bb42c6a2533fd
- Thompson argues the integration between model and harness is where agent differentiation is found, so profits flow to the integrators (Anthropic and OpenAI), not to a commodity model layer. (§ Agents and the AI Value Chain: “the integration between model and harness is where true agent differentiation is found”) ^clm-6aaf2e68f139fe5e83b7
- Thompson says Google's Gemini is a strong model but Google had not yet shipped a compelling harness as of March 2026. (§ Agents and the AI Value Chain: “Gemini is a strong model, but Google hasn’t yet shipped a compelling harness”) ^clm-41430cee6346602c5bd0
- Microsoft's new E7 enterprise bundle costs $99 per seat per month, twice the price of E5, and launched with Copilot Cowork. (§ Agents and the AI Value Chain: “which is going to cost twice as much — $99 per seat per month — as the formerly top-of-the-line E5”) ^clm-3866895e4d410890a57d
- Thompson argues Copilot Cowork, unlike the Copilot chatbot, is not model agnostic, which amounts to Microsoft admitting models may not be commodities. (§ Agents and the AI Value Chain: “Copilot Cowork — unlike the Copilot chatbot — is not model agnostic”) ^clm-7ddb4bc7f7953226f715
- Thompson questions Apple's plan to license Gemini and build its own harness in a new Siri, since Microsoft concluded that route could not produce a compelling product. (§ Agents and the AI Value Chain: “This certainly raises questions about Apple’s decision to merely license Gemini and build a harness themselves”) ^clm-8761556dd72f2a742013
