# An Interview with Michael Morton About E-Commerce in the Age of AI

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-06-18.

## Summary

Ben Thompson talks with MoffettNathanson analyst Michael Morton about how AI is changing online shopping. Morton says the fear that AI will cut stores off from their customers cannot be proven or disproven, and that fear has pushed his stocks' valuations down. His data shows AI is acting more like a referral channel than a distributor, and it sends much more traffic to brands' own sites, which helps Shopify. He argues companies with real-world delivery networks, like Amazon and DoorDash, are safest, while pure ad-funded marketplaces like Etsy are exposed. He also says Waymo will lead robotaxis for the next two years, though more players will matter over three to five years.

## Key claims

- Morton says the multiples on the e-commerce stocks he covers have fallen 30% year-to-date, because investors fear AI will put their growth and take rates under review. (A multiple is the price investors pay per dollar of earnings; the take rate is the cut a marketplace keeps.) (§ The Unfalsifiable Bear Case: “the multiples on the stocks have gone down 30% year-to-date”) ^clm-9312f7fd16010e5c7cc5
- Morton argues the AI bear case for e-commerce is hard to fight because it is amorphous and cannot be disproven with data today, unlike a margin worry that a good quarter can kill. (§ The Unfalsifiable Bear Case: “you can't convince people otherwise, because it's impossible to prove it with data today”) ^clm-40f4a8b6e6fff36016bf
- Morton says ChatGPT's in-chat checkout was deprecated soon after launch. (§ The Unfalsifiable Bear Case: “We've gone from ChatGPT checkout to it getting deprecated pretty quickly”) ^clm-e50f43991ebb836b03e0
- When Morton asked AI models which stores they prefer, they named price, shipping speed, and trust, and ranked Amazon first and Walmart second. (§ Shopify: “the clear favorite there was Amazon, then Walmart number two”) ^clm-1d4b95f11acf01417afa
- Morton's data shows that from traditional Google Search, brand.com sites (merchants' own stores, Shopify's base) grow traffic about 2x faster than the big marketplaces. (§ Shopify: “let's call it 2x the growth rate of the marketplaces coming from Google Search”) ^clm-0e077e1f66491acee4cc
- Morton's data shows that traffic from AI platforms to brand.com sites is growing 4 to 8 times faster than traffic to legacy marketplaces like Amazon, Wayfair, Chewy, eBay, and Etsy. (§ Shopify: “brand.com is outpacing what I'll call the legacy marketplaces 4-8x”) ^clm-fcbe54e74115146cd4a3
- Prediction: Morton upgraded Shopify to buy earlier in 2026, believing its GMV (total value of goods sold on the platform) will grow faster for longer than most expect. (§ Shopify: “GMV is going to grow higher for longer than most people think”) ^clm-fef98fcb9c83c294e2f1
- Morton argues merchants cannot vibe-code (build with AI coding tools) a cheaper copy of Shopify, because Shopify's partner deals, such as better Stripe fees and one-click Meta CAPI integration, give it bargaining power no merchant has. (§ Shopify: “It's a long list of reasons why you're not going to vibe-code your way to a Shopify stack”) ^clm-1a72d7c526e19f7171e2
- Morton says Shopify merchants get one-click Conversions API integration with Meta because Tobi Lütke and Mark Zuckerberg know each other and worked on the deal together. (§ Shopify: “are personally acquainted and worked on this deal together, so Shopify merchants get one-click CAPI integration”) ^clm-eacce248fa4765367651
- Morton says ChatGPT checkout struggled because ChatGPT needs Amazon more than Amazon needs ChatGPT, it never reached critical mass, and shoppers could tell inventory was missing. (§ Shopify: “ChatGPT needs Amazon more than Amazon needs ChatGPT. They also never got critical mass”) ^clm-6a989ca01592ef868140
- Shopify Catalog lets merchants plug their product feeds straight into AI platforms, and is now open even to non-Shopify merchants, because models do not like scraping raw HTML for shopping. (§ Shopify: “allow the Shopify merchant base to pull their product feeds and plug them directly into the AI platforms”) ^clm-9a599c6eddb948f1b935
- Morton says Google's StoreBot crawls e-commerce sites and places orders with fake credit cards to check delivery speed and inventory. (§ Shopify: “puts in orders with fake credit cards, and checks out to see what delivery speeds and inventory are”) ^clm-ec95f59387bff95c9981
- Morton says Shopify built Google's Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) together with Google, merchants can bring their own payment option, and a council now includes Amazon, Meta, Stripe, and Shopify. (§ Shopify: “Shopify developed this together with Google”) ^clm-af7b0fd4cac3ffa43fa9
- Morton argues UCP is good for incumbent players, because Google, which seems to be winning consumer AI, wants to move its ad business to the new form factor while keeping everyone intact. (§ Shopify: “I think this is a really good thing for the incumbent industry players”) ^clm-f58ea6ae65e4d53b4766
- Morton argues that attempts to make AI a distributor have fallen short, and AI now looks like a referral channel, a better Google Search that sends clearer intent, which should de-risk consumer internet stocks. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “we've seen the distributor attempts fall short, and AI is looking a lot more like a referral channel”) ^clm-56d0a4bee2c5b68ed6e3
- Morton cautions the risk is not gone: hyperscalers are not spending record capex (capital spending on data centers) just to build a better Google Search, and Google and Meta are reportedly building personal agents. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “We're not underwriting the next Industrial Revolution in CapEx spend from all the hyperscalers”) ^clm-b585c4253cc9f24c65d3
- Thompson argues Google and Meta learned that owning checkout does not work and advertising, where advertisers own the customer, is more profitable, so OpenAI's checkout failure may have helped it. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “they were going down the path that Google and Meta already figured out didn't work”) ^clm-5891bc9c99836ac4aa05
- Morton reports that growth in traffic sent from AI platforms to marketplaces has slowed since November 2025, partly because Amazon blocks most AI discovery platforms and more traffic goes to brand.com. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “We know Amazon is blocking most of the AI discovery platforms”) ^clm-692c1118fa6bfadbd822
- Morton says app terms of service let DoorDash, Uber, Instacart, and Amazon block AI agents that scrape or order through their apps, as with Amazon's legal fight against Perplexity, though a Meta-scale agent would be harder to block. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “the terms of service from the app providers prevent unauthorized access and scraping of their data”) ^clm-70f74d1ab8f04d152961
- Morton notes that in the early 1960s only 30% of US milk consumption came via home delivery, and it fell to zero, as evidence people do not want staples automatically delivered. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “you only had 30% of US milk consumption done via delivery”) ^clm-6aea0d29165cab76d34e
- Morton estimates that only 1% to 3% of Amazon's 2023 GMV was signed up for Subscribe & Save, backed out from Amazon's claim of a billion dollars in savings. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “it's somewhere between 1% and 3% of Amazon's GMV in 2023”) ^clm-883d68b9ad932506b859
- Morton argues automatic reordering fails because a household buys about 250 to 300 items a year, and if you do not solve for all of them you solve for nothing. (§ Referral vs. Distribution: “If you don't solve for all of them, you don't solve for anything”) ^clm-d5fc8be6b4caa68009db
- Morton is less worried about Amazon's on-site ad risk because AI adoption is slower than expected, and higher intent and conversion are lifting CPCs (cost per click) enough to offset fewer clicks, as Mercado Libre reported. (§ Ads and AI: “the greater intent and higher conversion is doing an incredible job lifting CPCs enough”) ^clm-07093efaf81674248bc1
- Morton argues marketplaces that earn all their profit from on-site ads and own no physical infrastructure are exposed to AI, while Amazon and DoorDash are protected because they also fulfill orders. (§ Ads and AI: “earn 100% of their profitability from on-site ads and don't have a physical infrastructure layer”) ^clm-0fc2f385fdc317b90282
- Morton estimates Etsy earns over 100% of its EBIT (operating profit) from on-site ads, and warns big Etsy sellers may leave its 25% take rate for Shopify stores found through AI. (§ Ads and AI: “Etsy, by our estimate, earns over 100% of EBIT from on-site ads”) ^clm-a2072cf96c976563d2b7
- Morton says Shopify GMV and Meta revenue have a near-perfect R-squared (a statistic for how closely two numbers move together), but he views the two stocks' weakness as separate. (§ Ads and AI: “the R-squared between Shopify GMV and Meta revenue is pretty much perfect”) ^clm-b41794bedd8e2ab7d1bd
- Amazon rolled out same-day grocery to over 2,000 US cities, and in Morton's basket pricing it jumped from middle of the pack to price parity with Walmart; Amazon says the business grew 40x year-to-date. (§ Grocery: “went from middle of the pack to price parity with Walmart overnight”) ^clm-85b498127c5f5565516a
- Morton says Amazon built its grocery business by putting refrigerators throughout its fulfillment network, but its SKU range (number of distinct products) is still limited. (§ Grocery: “Amazon put refrigerators in their whole fulfillment network, and they have a killer grocery product today”) ^clm-817cd2cf2d6decdd01f6
- Morton argues full grocery baskets still need a marketplace like DoorDash or Instacart, because the average grocery store carries 40,000 to 50,000 items to cover everyone's long-tail needs. (§ Grocery: “the average grocery store carries 40,000 to 50,000 items”) ^clm-c6e28d26f6fbfed373f4
- Morton says DoorDash took two-thirds of the US restaurant delivery market by starting in the suburbs, and its grocery business is growing 30% to 40%. (§ Grocery: “took two-thirds of market share in the US restaurant business”) ^clm-531cf27b043cf0195a86
- Morton says DoorDash has built over 100 DashMarts and a DashLink package service, in effect its own version of Fulfillment by Amazon, as local inventory grows more important. (§ Grocery: “they built out over 100 DashMarts”) ^clm-e83f755474e1cb4a0a1a
- Prediction: Morton predicts Waymo will dominate robotaxis for the next 18 to 24 months, while over 3 to 5 years multiple operators will need demand aggregation such as Uber. (§ Autonomous Vehicles: “In the next 18-to-24 months, this is Waymo's game”) ^clm-d5f79dd2da119129213f
- Morton says the Uber and Waymo partnership appears to have broken down, and he admits he got that call wrong. (§ Autonomous Vehicles: “The partnership between Uber and Waymo appears to have completely broken down”) ^clm-25a02db36cd314e1ebfe
- Thompson argues Google's asset-heavy robotaxi push looks strategically dumb right up until it works, like Berkshire moving cash into railroads, and could leave an impregnable moat. (§ Autonomous Vehicles: “it's actually totally dumb right up until it works, and then you have this impregnable moat”) ^clm-8e760ada9fa277f9eb8f
- Morton's model says robotaxis turn EBIT-per-mile positive at low-to-mid 30s trips per car per day, and Waymo's California cars are near high-20s. (§ Autonomous Vehicles: “low-30s to mid-30s trips per day gets you EBIT-per-mile positive”) ^clm-6793558427714fa8a345
- Using bill-of-lading shipping records, Morton found Waymo importing about 300 Zeekr-built Ojai vans a month despite 100% tariffs, putting it on pace to double its fleet in 12 months. (§ Autonomous Vehicles: “Waymo is bringing in roughly 300 Ojais a month”) ^clm-62a076d03ae13db28c23
- Morton calls Tesla the largely unknowable risk: if it can put working robotaxis on the road at $30,000 with its production scale, it could be game over for many AV operators. (§ Autonomous Vehicles: “it's kind of going to be game over for a lot of people”) ^clm-487b9210666d149f81f9
