# Netflix Earnings, Business or Franchise, Disney’s Deal

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-10-21.

## Summary

Netflix reported Q3 2026 revenue of $12.9 billion, up 12 percent year-over-year in its slowest quarterly revenue growth in three years, alongside net income of $3.66 billion. The company cut roughly 1,000 jobs and replaced its content leadership amid sluggish US engagement growth. Thompson applies Warren Buffett's distinction between an economic franchise and a business, arguing Netflix has transitioned into a business where continued growth requires operational excellence. However, a major licensing deal with Disney demonstrates Netflix's enduring distribution power across over 325 million subscribers.

## Key claims

- Netflix generated third-quarter 2026 revenue of $12.9 billion, up 12 percent year-over-year for its lowest quarterly percentage growth in three years, with net income rising 44 percent to $3.66 billion. (§ Netflix Earnings: “revenue of $12.9 billion, up 12% from the same period a year earlier. That is the company’s lowest quarterly-revenue percentage growth in three years.”) ^clm-450358e828faa87b7c93
- Netflix laid off approximately 1,000 employees, or 6 percent of its workforce, and replaced both its content chief and film division head following programming difficulties. (§ Netflix Earnings: “Netflix has cut its workforce by about 6%, or around 1,000 positions, and parted ways with its content chief and the head of its film unit”) ^clm-e7e16e3ca289bb3eae87
- Netflix stock fell over 40 percent in the year leading up to October 2026, pressured by slowing growth after its failed takeover bid for Warner Bros. Discovery assets. (§ Netflix Earnings: “Netflix’s stock is down more than 40% over the past year.”) ^clm-5c2e7f24c16fc044ff98
- Netflix co-CEO Greg Peters pointed to industry-leading low churn across all regions as proof that recent price hikes succeeded and customer value remained healthy. (§ Netflix Earnings: “The best evidence that we’ve got that we’re delivering that value is churn. We’ve got healthy retention in every region.”) ^clm-0720ecec18e1bf6c18ab
- Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos conceded that while international programming is driving growth, viewing engagement in the United States is not growing as fast as management desires. (§ Netflix Earnings: “Now the U.S. is also working. It’s just not growing as fast as it could or as fast as we’d like.”) ^clm-ae888b3856f6beb39938
- Thompson argues Netflix's content reputation remains excessively reliant on Stranger Things, which originally premiered ten years earlier without comparable widespread successors. (§ Netflix Earnings: “Stranger Things is by far Netflix’s most well-regarded show, at least as far as widespread acclaim for content quality goes, and it premiered ten years ago!”) ^clm-b0ec6582ba9bc5300e58
- Buffett defined an economic franchise as a business with no close substitutes, unregulated pricing, and an ability to absorb managerial mistakes without mortal injury. (§ Business or Franchise: “An economic franchise arises from a product or service that: (1) is needed or desired; (2) is thought by its customers to have no close substitute”) ^clm-62202b1424d10dff2614
- Buffett contrasted franchises with ordinary businesses, which earn superior returns only through low-cost operations or tight supply and remain vulnerable to poor management. (§ Business or Franchise: “In contrast, “a business” earns exceptional profits only if it is the low-cost operator or if supply of its product or service is tight.”) ^clm-f6a4ab22dd034ed1f899
- Thompson argues Netflix was previously viewed as an unstoppable franchise destined to aggregate all entertainment, but has transitioned into a business where further growth demands flawless execution. (§ Business or Franchise: “That doesn’t mean it’s a bad business, it just means it is in fact a business, not a franchise, at least when it comes to growth.”) ^clm-eea56ecc747554c0a6ca
- Disney licensed library titles and current television seasons to Netflix to leverage Netflix's global user base of more than 325 million subscribers. (§ Disney’s Deal: “Disney Entertainment and Netflix have entered into an expansive new content licensing agreement, which will bring an eclectic mix of library titles”) ^clm-fbc2222825b45ef6eed5
- Disney licensed Percy Jackson and the Olympians seasons to Netflix for a three-month promotional run ahead of its season premiere on Disney+. (§ Disney’s Deal: “Disney+ original series “Percy Jackson and the Olympians” Seasons 1-2 will be available on Netflix globally beginning Oct. 4”) ^clm-9de733a33514432cb9e5
- Disney licensed five Ice Age films to Netflix worldwide starting October 2026 as a promotional window before theatrical release of the sixth installment. (§ Disney’s Deal: “all five of 20th Century Studios’ “Ice Age” films will be available on Netflix worldwide beginning Oct. 4”) ^clm-f568a6a6e405f8e018d5
- Thompson argues Disney licensing major franchises to promote theatrical releases demonstrates that Netflix's massive distribution scale makes it the superior vehicle for monetizing entertainment content. (§ Disney’s Deal: “when money is actually on the line — specifically, an upcoming theatrical release — even Disney realizes that its optimal strategy is to build an audience on Netflix, not its own service.”) ^clm-b73ce5c322718c5e6f67
