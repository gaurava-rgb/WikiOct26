# How Amazon Rewired Its Data Centers After Mississippi Power Warning

Catherine Perloff, The Information, published 2026-10-08.

## Summary

Earlier in 2026 Mississippi utility Entergy warned Amazon it would start cutting its data centers off the grid at peak times. AWS responded with Project Falcon, a system that lets the utility itself switch AWS data centers to backup power. The article shows curtailment spreading across US grids in Texas, North Carolina and PJM, while Google instead moves workloads to regions with spare power. Amazon is also securing power directly, from a Constellation nuclear expansion to buying a Texas gas plant to run its first fully off-grid data center.

## Key claims

- Earlier in 2026, Entergy told Amazon it would have to start cutting Amazon's Mississippi data centers off the grid at peak times, per two people with knowledge. (§ The core story: “would have to start cutting the company's Mississippi data centers off the grid at peak times”) ^clm-069978eab4ddb584afa1
- AWS runs one data center near Jackson, Mississippi, which is being expanded, and is building two more facilities in the area. (§ The core story: “AWS runs one data center near Jackson, Mississippi”) ^clm-48d2db472b8ec3a60250
- AWS built Project Falcon, which lets the utility itself trigger the switch of AWS data centers from the grid to backup power without first contacting AWS, per one person. (§ The core story: “It lets the utility itself trigger the switch to backup power”) ^clm-1098b8f49f5a8f9ce9ce
- Amazon says utilities in multiple regions can already remotely prompt it to use backup generation. (§ The core story: “utilities in multiple regions can already remotely prompt it to use backup generation”) ^clm-d53ca2205dadf50dc7bc
- A 2025 Texas law lets grid operator ERCOT force large customers to curtail power use under certain conditions. (§ Curtailment spreads across U.S. grids: “letting grid operator ERCOT force large customers to curtail”) ^clm-f7bccad960c66578a579
- Duke Energy in North Carolina added contract terms letting it cut large customers off the grid when needed. (§ Curtailment spreads across U.S. grids: “Duke Energy added contract terms letting it cut large customers off the grid”) ^clm-0ae297ab8468385cf1b9
- PJM, the largest US grid operator, asked federal regulators for permission to cut large customers off first in emergencies to address a power capacity shortfall. (§ Curtailment spreads across U.S. grids: “asked federal regulators for permission to cut large customers off first in emergencies”) ^clm-0c07fc034ff42a8aee2f
- Google uses demand response, shifting workloads to regions with spare electricity, and in 2026 signed agreements with five utilities to give up as much as 1 gigawatt of grid power when needed. (§ How other clouds handle it: “committing to give up as much as 1 gigawatt of grid power”) ^clm-d080c2e114de10d976f9
- Constellation Energy said an Amazon deal would help fund a $3 billion, 190-megawatt expansion of its Maryland nuclear plant. (§ Amazon's hunt for power: “help fund a $3 billion, 190-megawatt expansion of its nuclear plant in Maryland”) ^clm-ee103f356949c58989b1
- Securing power has become one of the biggest bottlenecks of the AI data center buildout. (§ Amazon's hunt for power: “Securing power has become one of the biggest bottlenecks of the AI data center buildout”) ^clm-02e7e2ee307fd359fa4a
- Federal rules limit diesel backup generators to 100 hours per generator per year outside emergencies. (§ Backup power limits: “limit diesel backup generators to 100 hours per generator per year”) ^clm-85014de7971f30f1b1ed
- In 2025 the Minnesota Public Utilities Commission ruled against Amazon's plan to use 250 generators at a data center in Becker, Minnesota, and Amazon abandoned that data center. (§ Backup power limits: “ruled against Amazon's plan to use 250 generators”) ^clm-65f6dcf826990755cc96
- Amazon's battery backup talks with Dominion in Virginia went nowhere because Dominion fears AWS charging at peak times, per one person; Amazon says it would never charge at peak. (§ Backup power limits: “talks went nowhere because Dominion fears AWS charging at peak times”) ^clm-892720fc4e5b7a166d32
- Amazon bought GW Ranch, a power plant in Pecos County, Texas, to run its first data center fully off the grid; its permit allows 33 million tons of CO2 emissions, more than the largest US coal plant. (§ Owning the power plant: “its first data center fully off the grid”) ^clm-3cdd5557d6f6a4bd1f44
- Cleanview CEO Michael Thomas says that before ChatGPT siting a data center was standard, but now it is slower, harder and needs much more infrastructure. (§ Owning the power plant: “now it is slower, harder and needs much more infrastructure”) ^clm-1872ecf1cce682ab2e7f
