# John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future, SpaceXAI and Cursor

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-04-22.

## Summary

Thompson reads John Ternus's promotion to Apple CEO as the final step in Apple's shift from software to hardware as its main edge, a shift he first described in 2020 when Apple Silicon launched. He argues the choice of Ternus and the choice of Google's Gemini for Apple's AI are really one decision: Apple bets that AI will commoditize and that the best AI will run best on its devices. He calls it a reasonable bet, but still a bet. In the second half, he argues SpaceX's option to buy Cursor for $60 billion makes sense: SpaceXAI has huge compute but has lost its researchers, while Cursor has a real coding product, data, and distribution but is squeezed by paying frontier labs for model access.

- Thompson argues Tim Cook was the right CEO in 2011 because what Apple needed then was scale, and Cook was the master of scale. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “what Apple needed to do in 2011 was scale, and Cook was the master of scale”) ^clm-e0997244bd04a7fc8d45
- Thompson argues Apple could not pick an AI visionary as CEO because it has none in leadership, and its software leaders do not deserve promotion given how its software platforms, especially macOS, have slipped. - Thompson doubts that software will be the most important differentiator in the AI age. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “is software going to be the most important differentiator in the AI age?”) ^clm-1eab030c942144fbea19
- Thompson argues Ternus was the obvious internal pick because Apple's hardware, from the iPhone to the Mac and the Apple Silicon inside both, is in excellent shape. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “Apple’s hardware is in excellent shape, from the iPhone to the Mac and the Apple Silicon”) ^clm-4057e2ecceef0b6660d4
- Thompson revisits his 2020 thesis that Apple moved through three eras: Apple 1.0 won on software despite weaker hardware, Apple 2.0 was peak hardware-software integration (iPod, iPhone), and Apple 3.0 wins on chip performance as software reaches parity. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “the balance of which differentiates the other has shifted over time”) ^clm-01f694023539e20d5081
- In 2020 Thompson argued Apple's software edge was shrinking because of rough app parity with Android, the move to web apps on the desktop, and Microsoft's embrace of Linux. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “rough app parity with Android, the shift to web apps on the desktop, and Microsoft’s embrace of Linux”) ^clm-5d3c0d35cf2d2c039e53
- In 2020 Thompson called Apple's bet to take both share and margin with Apple Silicon reasonable, since buyers must purchase the whole integrated device to get the best chips. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “The company has the best chips in the world, and you have to buy the entire integrated widget to get them”) ^clm-97aa9842d5c925d9e465
- Thompson says the MacBook Neo is the peak of his 2020 hardware-differentiation thesis, and that naming Ternus CEO is its ultimate affirmation. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “Ternus’ elevation to CEO is the ultimate affirmation of this shift”) ^clm-81cc14fe5e40f2f97543
- Thompson argues the decision to base Apple's AI on Google's Gemini and the decision to make Ternus CEO are in many respects the same decision: compete in AI through devices and edge compute. (§ John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future: “that decision is in fact inseparable from the decision to make Ternus CEO”) ^clm-c557ba611f0b2d617480
- Prediction: Thompson says Apple is betting that the best hardware wins, that AI commoditizes, and that any differentiated AI will run best on Apple devices; he calls it a reasonable bet, but a bet. - The Cursor deal follows Musk saying xAI is behind rivals on coding tools, vowing to rebuild it, ordering layoffs in March 2026, and hiring engineers from Cursor. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “Musk said that xAI is behind on software coding tools compared with peers”) ^clm-98da006f6e8730b45a60
- All 11 original xAI co-founders had left by late March 2026, an exodus that sped up after SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “all 11 original co-founders of Elon Musk’s xAI have now departed the company”) ^clm-bb815ef416fb29aa9da8
- Thompson calls SpaceXAI a mess, a big step down from a year earlier when Grok 4 was briefly arguably the best model in the world. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “Grok 4 was, for a brief time, arguably the best model in the world”) ^clm-abb14331a2e31cb32d27
- xAI's Memphis Colossus site expanded in January 2026 to a planned 555,000 Nvidia GPUs across 2 gigawatts of power, with the GPUs alone estimated at $18 billion. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “expanding the total facility to 555,000 Nvidia GPUs across 2 gigawatts of power capacity”) ^clm-d957b419dcc2a7434ae8

- Thompson argues Musk excels at leveraging fixed-cost investments like car plants, rockets, and satellites but is weaker at software and open-ended exploration, so SpaceXAI has lots of compute and no one to use it. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “SpaceXAI has a ton of compute, and no one to use it, either for R&D or inference”) ^clm-70488a64b52e168a73ee
- Thompson argues Cursor is trapped: it pays frontier labs for model API access while those labs sell heavily subsidized agentic coding products by subscription, so it bleeds money and leading-edge customers. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “a startup with real traction that is both bleeding money and leading edge customers”) ^clm-6c40004ff34f664c8595
- Thompson says Cursor needs its own frontier model fully integrated into its own harness, plus compute to sell its product profitably. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “it needs its own frontier model that is fully integrated into its own harness”) ^clm-96cd48ceaa5f57056dbb
- In 2025 Cursor CEO Michael Truell argued his market resembles late-1990s search more than enterprise software, since distribution brings usage data that improves the product. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “the competitive dynamics of our market mirror search more than normal enterprise software markets”) ^clm-5ae626a1835ac9475167
- Thompson argues the SpaceXAI and Cursor tie-up makes a lot of sense: one side has compute, the other a product, proprietary data, and distribution in coding, the most important AI use case. (§ SpaceXAI and Cursor: “the former has compute, and the latter has a product, data, and a decent amount of distribution”) ^clm-7619a4798453adf6b37b
