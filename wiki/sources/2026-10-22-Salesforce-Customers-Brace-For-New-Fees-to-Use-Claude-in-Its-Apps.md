# Salesforce Customers Brace For New Fees to Use Claude in Its Apps

Laura Bratton, The Information, published 2026-10-22.

## Summary

Salesforce plans to require enterprise customers to register external AI agents around November 2026, preparing to bill each action as an interaction or charge for headless licenses when customers use Claude or OpenAI models. The move leads an industry-wide transition by SaaS vendors including Atlassian, Workday, ServiceNow, and Datadog to protect revenues as autonomous agents undermine per-seat pricing models.

## Key claims

- Salesforce plans to require enterprise customers to register external AI agents starting around November 2026, preparing to charge fees whenever outside agents analyze or update Salesforce records. (§ Tollgates for third-party AI agents: “register external AI agents starting around November 2026, preparing to charge fees”) ^clm-51de0eb2e03b9a67b65c
- Salesforce plans to bill each action an external AI agent takes inside its applications as an interaction. (§ Tollgates for third-party AI agents: “Each action an autonomous AI agent executes within Salesforce applications will be categorized and billed as an interaction”) ^clm-41721b8985ae2beefc94
- Salesforce offered enterprise customers the option to purchase unlimited AI agent usage through a headless license. (§ Tollgates for third-party AI agents: “option to purchase unlimited agent access via a headless license”) ^clm-15bce47a41a60d373554
- The rise of autonomous AI agents operating enterprise software more efficiently than humans threatens traditional per-seat SaaS licensing models. (§ Pressure on per-seat enterprise software pricing: “agents operate apps faster and more effectively than human workers, undermining traditional seat-based software licensing”) ^clm-379dca66d94b230713e0
- Enterprise software vendors Atlassian, Workday, ServiceNow, and Datadog are also establishing tollgates and outcome-based pricing models for external AI agents. (§ Pressure on per-seat enterprise software pricing: “Atlassian, Workday, ServiceNow, and Datadog are also establishing tollgates”) ^clm-e92906369f3e13409ed3
- Software consultant Nick Hamm reported that most of his 150 enterprise clients deploy Claude and OpenAI models across Salesforce applications for free. (§ Pressure on per-seat enterprise software pricing: “most of his 150 enterprise clients currently deploy Claude and OpenAI models across Salesforce applications for free”) ^clm-2f12b4b503d0fc237ceb
- Enterprise clients are reconsidering agent architectures and may transfer data out of Salesforce if third-party AI tollgate fees are set too high. (§ Pressure on per-seat enterprise software pricing: “transfer company data out of Salesforce into external repositories if tollgate fees are set too high”) ^clm-1376f1cfcaf929e2b763
- New Salesforce customers will receive a three-month grace period to register AI agents after formal pricing is published, while existing customers will see fee changes at contract renewal. (§ Pressure on per-seat enterprise software pricing: “three-month grace period to register AI agents after formal pricing is published, while existing customers will see fee changes at contract renewal”) ^clm-7e18fbf418cd6dde01cf
- Lumen deployed Salesforce native AI sales agents to automatically gather client requirements and generate pricing quotes for data center fiber connections. (§ Enterprise deployment at Lumen: “Lumen made significant investments over the past year deploying Salesforce native AI sales agents”) ^clm-8d6dccfedc5c56c9797d
- Salesforce allowed Lumen to use existing enterprise subscription fees to cover its usage of built-in AI sales agents. (§ Enterprise deployment at Lumen: “Salesforce has allowed existing enterprise subscription fees to cover usage of built-in AI agents”) ^clm-33b7a24b8eb7a2d37717
