# Chinese Open Models Win Over One in Five of The Information’s Subscribers

Amir Efrati, The Information, published 2026-10-08.

## Summary

A September 2026 survey of The Information's subscribers found that most organizations build AI apps and a third get returns that are a multiple of what they spend. Claude and Gemini gained on ChatGPT over the year, though ChatGPT still leads, and Claude Code now leads coding tools. 21% of subscribers use Chinese open-weight models such as DeepSeek and Kimi K3. The survey shows a big change from a year earlier, when 59% had never used an AI agent, while hiring has mostly not changed yet.

## Key claims

- In a September 2026 survey, nearly two-thirds of The Information's subscribers said their organizations build AI applications. (§ Returns from AI: “Nearly two-thirds of subscribers surveyed said their organizations build AI applications”) ^clm-d3d74b5dcd60f8b0cc33
- A third of surveyed subscribers said their AI return is a multiple of what they spend; 17% said returns were positive but below hopes, 30% said too soon to tell, and 10% called it a waste. (§ Returns from AI: “Another 17% said returns are positive but lower than hoped”) ^clm-e49fb4f86c14074a5a92
- The survey contrasts with a 2025 MIT study that found just 5% of custom-made enterprise AI tools reached production. (§ Returns from AI: “just 5% of custom-made enterprise AI tools reached production”) ^clm-9ba60f6ab801cc855bd9
- Over the past year subscribers' use of Anthropic's Claude and Google's Gemini rose at the expense of OpenAI's ChatGPT, though ChatGPT still leads and regained some ground versus Claude. (§ Chatbots: Claude and Gemini up, ChatGPT still first: “Claude and Google's Gemini rose at the expense of OpenAI's ChatGPT”) ^clm-0b10242c58f41ed6f8dc
- Efrati suggests Gemini's comeback is probably due to Google signing more enterprise deals or integrating Gemini with its core search engine. (§ Chatbots: Claude and Gemini up, ChatGPT still first: “Google signing more enterprise deals or tying Gemini into its core search engine”) ^clm-9292565290f12fdac514
- On Oct. 7, 2026, OpenAI launched visual answers in ChatGPT to catch up with a similar Gemini feature that shows videos or images in answers. (§ Chatbots: Claude and Gemini up, ChatGPT still first: “OpenAI launched visual answers in ChatGPT, to catch up with a similar Gemini feature”) ^clm-a3cbe73ac4f2868aa7b8
- Subscribers use Microsoft Copilot less, even as Microsoft signs up more corporate customers. (§ Chatbots: Claude and Gemini up, ChatGPT still first: “Subscribers use Microsoft Copilot less, even as Microsoft signs up more corporate customers”) ^clm-62b3706f7ffe2c975284
- Only 12% of subscribers regularly use the open-source agent OpenClaw, though Anthropic, OpenAI and others add OpenClaw-like features weekly for persistent agents. (§ Agents and coding tools: “Only 12% regularly use OpenClaw, the open-source AI agent technology”) ^clm-caaaf6e2a37d364df65e
- In coding assistants, the early GitHub Copilot and Cursor duopoly is gone: Claude Code now leads, OpenAI's Codex surged in 2026, and Cursor is close behind. (§ Agents and coding tools: “Claude Code now leads. OpenAI's Codex surged in 2026 as it improved”) ^clm-fcd74c1c7540f769cb89
- A quarter of subscribers use Perplexity and a quarter use DeepSeek models; about one-sixth regularly use Moonshot's Kimi K3. (§ Open and Chinese models: “About one-sixth regularly use Moonshot's Kimi K3”) ^clm-04295f76ab87fd003470
- 46% of subscribers use at least some open-source AI models for work and 21% use Chinese ones, implying some use U.S. open-weight models from Nvidia, Google or Thinking Machines. (§ Open and Chinese models: “46% use at least some open-source AI models for work; 21% use Chinese ones”) ^clm-1b11274ad3b9f8af510b
- Only 20% of subscribers use open-source models for a majority of their AI workloads; many still rely heavily on closed models like GPT and Claude. (§ Open and Chinese models: “Only 20% use open-source models for most of their AI workloads”) ^clm-4c5f23c2fcdd7969acb7
- Twelve months before the survey, 59% of The Information's readers said they had never used an AI agent. (§ How fast things changed: “12 months earlier, 59% of readers said they had never used an AI agent”) ^clm-da0a39956f58dbf54f34
- 22% of subscribers say they are hiring fewer workers because of AI versus 10% hiring more; nearly half report no hiring change. (§ Jobs: “22% say they are hiring fewer workers because of AI; 10% say they are hiring more”) ^clm-683f7cc14c8a974989e7
