# Uber's M&A Blitz

Martin Peers, The Information, published 2026-10-06.

## Summary

Peers says food delivery has entered a growth-by-acquisition era, shown by Uber's $2.3 billion purchase of business caterer ezCater and DoorDash's $300 million campus meal deal. Both companies dominate restaurant delivery, have rolled up European rivals, and find it harder to add subscribers, so they buy adjacent niches to keep bookings growing. Uber's Delivery Hero deal is big enough that its effect on Uber's growth rate will be more visible. The column also covers the close of the $110 billion Skydance purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery and its $79 billion debt load.

## Key claims

- Uber announced a $2.3 billion purchase of ezCater, a business catering company. (§ The core story: “Uber announced a $2.3 billion purchase of ezCater”) ^clm-801061771037cfd4e326
- A few weeks earlier, DoorDash bought a college campus meal-delivery business from Wonder for $300 million. (§ The core story: “bought a college campus meal-delivery business from Wonder for $300 million”) ^clm-d97bbdab090d2d243a10
- Peers argues neither deal will excite investors, but both show Uber and DoorDash doing whatever they can to keep growth going. (§ The core story: “neither deal will excite investors”) ^clm-26d27229dddd72d1748a
- DoorDash has bought Deliveroo and Wolt in Europe, and Uber is buying Delivery Hero, but only a handful of such targets exist. (§ Europe consolidation: “DoorDash bought Deliveroo and Wolt; Uber is buying Delivery Hero”) ^clm-cf55930d4ee101e3a802
- Uber and DoorDash dominate restaurant delivery and increasingly groceries, and growing by adding new subscribers keeps getting harder. (§ Europe consolidation: “Growing by adding new subscribers keeps getting harder”) ^clm-6c1a31b2294c0d2370d0
- DoorDash bookings growth slowed to 19% at the end of 2024 from 36% at the end of 2021, then sped back up to 36% over the past 18 months, much of it from Deliveroo. (§ Growth numbers: “slowed to 19% at end of 2024 from 36% at end of 2021”) ^clm-b150397aadcfedb1c705

- Peers reads both companies as throwing off lots of cash, so when a deal looks promising they just go for it. (§ Smaller deals: “when a deal looks promising, both just go for it”) ^clm-bf8e6c960152d14a43d5
