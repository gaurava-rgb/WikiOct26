# OpenAI ARR Snafu Highlights Flawed Metric

Martin Peers, The Information, published 2026-10-08.

## Summary

OpenAI's annualized revenue is closer to $50 billion, not the $70 billion widely reported the week before, and the correction knocked chip and neocloud stocks. Martin Peers argues the episode shows the industry leans too hard on annualized revenue, a one-month-times-12 figure that leaks irregularly and is counted differently by OpenAI and Anthropic. He expects the problem to fade once AI firms go public. The newsletter also doubts SpaceX's plan to offer full U.S. phone coverage after buying low-band spectrum.

## Key claims

- OpenAI generates closer to $50 billion in annualized revenue, not the $70 billion that The Information and others reported the week before. (§ The OpenAI number was wrong: “Instead of $70 billion in annualized revenue, as The Information and others reported”) ^clm-0752960285dba221ec7f
- After a Financial Times report of the lower OpenAI revenue figure, stocks of Nvidia, CoreWeave, Nebius and other chip and neocloud firms fell, per CNBC. (§ The OpenAI number was wrong: “Stocks of chip firms and neoclouds, including Nvidia, CoreWeave and Nebius, fell on the news”) ^clm-84d1ecc42f745796ef9f
- Peers argues everyone relies too much on annualized revenue, calling one month's revenue times 12 a poor substitute for a fuller picture of performance. (§ Why the metric is flawed: “a poor substitute for a fuller picture of a company's performance”) ^clm-5a46176230a40e5a2106
- Annualized revenue became standard for AI companies because their revenue grows so fast that quarterly reports feel backward-looking. (§ Why the metric is flawed: “quarterly reports feel backward-looking”) ^clm-3b32a5561d14e6689afe
- OpenAI and Anthropic count revenue differently, especially whether they include the cut paid to cloud firms, which makes ARR comparisons riskier. (§ Why the metric is flawed: “OpenAI and Anthropic count revenue differently, especially on whether they include the cut paid to cloud firms”) ^clm-3d151359edfb2322a012
- AI firms publish ARR only in occasional blog posts, and most ARR news comes from investor communications reaching reporters, sometimes with clumsy calculations. (§ Why the metric is flawed: “investors and news outlets sometimes do clumsy calculations that lead to errors”) ^clm-e5633b260fdf0c37a5f2
- Prediction: Peers expects the ARR problem to fade once AI firms go public and report real numbers. (§ What would fix it: “Peers expects the problem to fade when the AI firms go public”) ^clm-4869441a13a10a7fd54f

- Peers argues SpaceX will struggle to match mobile incumbents by combining its satellite-to-mobile constellation with the new spectrum, and must also build cell towers. (§ Elon Musk's phone ambitions: “SpaceX must build cell towers to use it”) ^clm-203e7cb2348547cb6706
- The U.S. government is limiting Microsoft's ability to sponsor visas for some immigrant workers, citing an investigation into claims it laid off Americans while hiring immigrants.
