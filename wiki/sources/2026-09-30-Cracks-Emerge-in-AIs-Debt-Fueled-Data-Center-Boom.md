# Cracks Emerge in AI's Debt-Fueled Data Center Boom

Dakin Campbell, The Information, published 2026-09-30.

## Summary

Financing for AI data centers is getting more expensive as bond investors demand discounts and some banks become more selective. Heavy borrowing by Amazon, Google and Microsoft, alongside rising government bond yields, is crowding the market and pushing up spreads. Recent high-yield data center deals, such as CleanSpark's bonds for a Meta facility, sold at discounts, and banks like MUFG are stepping back. No major deal has been pulled, but an Oracle force majeure notice is a warning that lending terms could tighten further.

## Key claims

- Some lenders, Societe Generale, SMBC and MUFG, are more selective on data center deals, and some planned data centers could struggle to get loans. (§ The core story: “some planned data centers could struggle to get loans, putting AI growth plans at risk”) ^clm-99bf2dcdcdd734b90735
- Amazon, Google and Microsoft together will spend about $700 billion on capital expenditures in 2026 and around that level for the next few years. (§ Pressure from big tech borrowing: “Amazon, Google and Microsoft together will spend about $700 billion on capital expenditures this year”) ^clm-aa970b50d3289beb99b4
- The hyperscalers have issued nearly $160 billion in investment-grade debt in 2026, and the spread on their debt rose about 0.25 percentage points versus 0.04 for the broader market. (§ Pressure from big tech borrowing: “They have issued nearly $160 billion in investment-grade debt this year”) ^clm-527815fdd7e3373d7e99
- About $55 billion of AI-related high-yield bonds have sold in 2026, including from developers building for Anthropic and OpenAI. (§ Pressure from big tech borrowing: “About $55 billion of AI-related high-yield bonds have sold this year”) ^clm-01eced8a33826c23beb8
- CleanSpark, building a data center for Meta, sold $2.3 billion in bonds at 98.5 cents on the dollar with a 7.875% coupon, one of the biggest discounts of the past year, despite Meta being investment grade. (§ Concessions in recent deals: “investors paid 98.5 cents per dollar of principal”) ^clm-e537e55371779e7f618e
- Per Morgan Stanley data, all four high-yield data center bond deals since July 2026 sold at a discount, versus three of the first 10 in the prior period. (§ Concessions in recent deals: “all four high-yield data center bond deals since July sold at a discount”) ^clm-c7466321d5a686ef162c
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said government yields climbed partly because heavy tech debt issuance is crowding out investors. (§ Macro pressure: “government yields climbed partly because heavy tech debt issuance is crowding out investors”) ^clm-6f3da913bba4a9d2f36d
- Anthropic's draft IPO paperwork reportedly shows a $42 billion net loss last year and over half a trillion dollars in compute and infrastructure commitments. (§ Macro pressure: “Anthropic's draft IPO paperwork reportedly shows a $42 billion net loss last year”) ^clm-a2241a6b09cf622f1b1a
- Societe Generale led a $7.1 billion financing for the first OpenAI-Oracle Stargate site, and SMBC helped lead $18 billion for Oracle's New Mexico project. (§ Banks pulling back: “SocGen led a $7.1 billion financing for the first OpenAI-Oracle Stargate site”) ^clm-b49f002b740ae7c5b7ae
- MUFG, a bigger data center lender in recent years, is stepping back, per two people with knowledge. (§ Banks pulling back: “MUFG, a bigger industry lender in recent years, is stepping back”) ^clm-28af42571690b6341b59
- Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the developer of its New Mexico data center project, owned by Blue Owl Capital, after power delays. (§ Banks pulling back: “Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the New Mexico project's developer”) ^clm-6925d7e55c68b02deb77
- No major data center deal has been pulled, and developers are still paying up to keep projects moving; a Manulife manager calls it still a story of concessions. (§ Banks pulling back: “No major deal has been pulled, and developers are still paying up to keep projects moving”) ^clm-1ce5f44e1d06bf5eae9e
