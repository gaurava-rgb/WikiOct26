# Muse Image, Grok 4.5, Alex Karp on CNBC

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-07-09.

## Summary

Thompson argues that the fight for verifiable data (usage where you can tell if the output worked) now shapes the AI race. Meta's new Muse Image model plugs into its ad tools, where every ad either converts or not, giving Meta a huge feedback signal no one else has. SpaceXAI's Grok 4.5, built with Cursor, shows the value of Cursor's coding data, while ChatGPT's consumer chats mostly give OpenAI data it cannot verify. Palantir's Alex Karp says enterprises distrust the frontier labs because sharing data may hand over their business edge. Thompson ties this to Anthropic's move to retain enterprise data and sees it as another reason to be bullish on Meta.

## Key claims

- Meta released Muse Image on July 7, 2026, its first image generation model since it spent billions rebuilding its AI lab under Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang; it rolls out in the Meta AI chatbot and will be embedded in Instagram and WhatsApp. (§ Muse Image: “its first such release since the company spent billions to rebuild its AI lab”) ^clm-c9f61c90bdbd5c6b1b92
- Muse Image lets users make images of friends or creators from their public Instagram posts; people who do not want their content reused or remixed can opt out in settings. (§ Muse Image: “Users can also make images featuring friends or creators based on their publicly available Instagram posts”) ^clm-2c5ce89045a7799a1eb2
- Meta says Muse Image borrows advances from large language models: tool use such as coding and search, self-refinement (reasoning that iterates on an image to fix mistakes), and test-time compute scaling, where more compute yields a better image. (§ Muse Image: “the more compute you spend on an image the better it is”) ^clm-d40f58fb8e603eb863ef
- Meta says Muse Image will power image generation in its Advantage+ creative ad tool in the coming weeks, and advertisers who tested it cited higher quality creative, with photorealism and product integrity standing out. (§ Muse Image: “will help power image generation in Meta Advantage+ creative”) ^clm-746cfb9f8d69984c1813
- Muse Image enables room restyling in Meta AI Shopping, letting people see products from a business's catalog in their own spaces. (§ Muse Image: “Muse Image now enables room restyling in Meta AI Shopping”) ^clm-223065585f9cfb21c00c
- Thompson argues the most compelling part of Muse Image is advertising: instead of testing 10 or 15 ads, advertisers can generate far more versions and rank them by whether they convert. (§ Muse Image: “instead of creating 10 or 15 pieces of creative and A/B testing which ones work best”) ^clm-734e6fe7c904ddd25403
- Eric Seufert argues that owning its own image model frees Meta from an outside model provider whose access, pricing, or availability could be cut by export controls, competition, or government action, citing Fable 5 and Mythos 5. (§ Muse Image: “Meta is not dependent on an outside model provider whose access, pricing, roadmap, or availability”) ^clm-6523c4b89c875e867add
- Seufert argues the deeper benefit is that Meta can train, tune, and evaluate Muse against advertiser performance data only it has, giving results more differentiated than fine-tuning a generic model. (§ Muse Image: “evaluate its own model against domain-specific advertiser performance data that only it possesses”) ^clm-1199c7eccf9e6f41007e
- Seufert argues the Muse integration into Advantage+ is the kind of visible development Meta should pursue to ease investor anxiety about its AI spending. (§ Muse Image: “exactly the kind of narrative-supporting development that Meta should be pursuing to assuage investor anxiety”) ^clm-bb84f0ec478c11ae7dab
- Thompson argues verifiability is one of the most important drivers of model improvement; coding advanced fastest because code either works or not, so the more verifiable a task, the sooner AI will do it. (§ Muse Image: “the more that a particular task is verifiable, the faster it will be done by AI”) ^clm-6742ac7268ee38a44e4b
- Thompson argues markets are verification mechanisms, and Meta ads, with trillions of ads that convert or not, are arguably the most liquid market in the world, so a model plugged into that signal will improve sooner. (§ Muse Image: “arguably the most liquid market in the world, with trillions of ads driving conversions or not”) ^clm-b60c090300eea28c4503
- SpaceXAI unveiled Grok 4.5, its first model built jointly with Cursor, aimed at finance, legal, and coding tasks, weeks after SpaceX agreed to acquire Cursor in a deal valuing it at $60 billion. (§ Grok 4.5: “SpaceX formally agreed to acquire Cursor in a deal that values the startup at $60 billion”) ^clm-78279daf8c324875573f
- Thompson says Grok 4.5 seems decent and cheap for its performance, but its headline benchmark is Cursor's own, so it deserves a grain of salt. (§ Grok 4.5: “The benchmark on the left is Cursor’s own benchmark, so take the performance with the appropriate grain of salt”) ^clm-72fb647df3119d28bdb7
- Prediction: Thompson argues Grok has a very long way to go to win usage, because people, himself included, rarely try other models once used to one, especially with its matching harness. (§ Grok 4.5: “Grok has a very long ways to go to actually win usage”) ^clm-cf35930b5a45c5dbee77
- Thompson argues the Cursor tie-up is paying off because Cursor captured a huge amount of usage traces around code, data that was always likely to be exceptionally valuable. (§ Grok 4.5: “captured a ton of traces around code in particular”) ^clm-e3b7f964ea8402bb0439
- Thompson argues OpenAI may have been harmed by ChatGPT's mass consumer adoption, absent an advertising business: consumers used many GPUs without subscribing enough, and their chats are mostly unverifiable, so they make poor training feedback. (§ Grok 4.5: “most consumer AI interactions aren’t particularly useful data for a feedback mechanism”) ^clm-23e73bc63fe4f03a2df4
- Palantir struck a deal to bring Nvidia's open source Nemotron model to U.S. government agencies. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “Palantir’s deal to bring Nvidia’s open source Nemotron model to the U.S. government”) ^clm-ee85389cfd98ffb09868
- On CNBC, Alex Karp said enterprises are unhappy with the frontier labs and fear paying for tokens that create no value while the labs get their IP and alpha (business edge). (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “I’m going to get no value and they’re going to get my IP”) ^clm-72166af3ba2a00505786
- Karp said technical customers want control over their compute, models, data stack, and alpha, which is what aligns Palantir with Nvidia. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “control over their compute, their models, their data stack and their alpha”) ^clm-f8547df6a2ff4609ec8e
- Karp said closed frontier models could also work if clients can ask basic questions such as whether the lab keeps their data or will enter their business. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “Are you keeping the data? Are you going to enter our business?”) ^clm-229825687d8e1240cd67
- Thompson argues Karp's point, also made by Satya Nadella, is that enterprises risk giving valuable data to labs that in the long run have an economic imperative to move up the stack and own the customer. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “have an economic imperative to move up the stack and own the customer directly”) ^clm-63d521979c0df340fd26
- Thompson calls Anthropic's Fable-era change to retain data from all customers, including enterprise, the most underrated part of the Fable release. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “the most underrated aspect of the Fable release was Anthropic changing the terms of service”) ^clm-82cd67bed7b32d756caf
- SemiAnalysis estimated that a $200 subscription plan gets $8,000 worth of Claude tokens and $14,000 worth of Codex tokens, which Thompson says the labs subsidize partly to get real usage data. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “a $200 plan gets you $8,000 worth of Claude tokens and $14,000 worth of Codex tokens”) ^clm-74003fe4dce8e62d3ace
- Prediction: Thompson predicts that if the retention policy does not cost Anthropic many customers, it is only a matter of time until Anthropic trains on that data, despite its pledge not to. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “I suspect it’s only a matter of time until they start using the data”) ^clm-8215bb9edb0b08cd828a
- Thompson argues the data fight is another reason to be bullish on Meta, which operates in markets where it is a given that it gets and acts on all the data, while Anthropic bets it can be good enough to get the data anyway. (§ Alex Karp on CNBC: “they operate in markets where it is a given that Meta gets and acts on all of the data”) ^clm-d6b84ffc44457c8225ac
