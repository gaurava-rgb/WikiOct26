# Nvidia Moves to Back GPU Cloud Provider Firmus After Investors Balk at Its IPO

Phoebe Liu, The Information, published 2026-10-20.

## Summary

Nvidia is in talks to invest hundreds of millions in Australian neocloud Firmus after it pulled a planned $5 billion IPO at a $30 billion valuation. Firmus has raised over $13 billion, including $10 billion in debt, and is seeking $2 billion privately. The talks reflect Nvidia's broader $170 billion in commitments to support GPU cloud buyers and counter hyperscaler custom chips. Nvidia has also adjusted its credit support terms, offering shorter-term guarantees for smaller cuts of revenue.

## Key claims

- Nvidia is negotiating to invest hundreds of millions of dollars in Australian cloud provider Firmus after Firmus aborted its initial public offering in October 2026. (§ Nvidia's rescue talks with Firmus: “Nvidia is negotiating to invest hundreds of millions of dollars in Australian GPU cloud provider Firmus”) ^clm-34d02f75755f6213b132
- Firmus sought a $30 billion valuation to raise $5 billion on the Australian Securities Exchange before canceling the listing due to investor resistance over valuation and revenue prospects. (§ Nvidia's rescue talks with Firmus: “Firmus had sought a $30 billion valuation to raise $5 billion on the Australian Securities Exchange”) ^clm-351154ac4717bcb07c6c
- Firmus has raised more than $13 billion across equity and debt, including approximately $10 billion in debt, with backing from Blackstone, Coatue, Jane Street, and Nvidia. (§ Nvidia's rescue talks with Firmus: “Firmus has raised over $13 billion to date, including roughly $10 billion in debt”) ^clm-d6807b6416604e1193e2
- Following its canceled public listing, Firmus is seeking to raise at least $2 billion in private financing with significant equity participation from Nvidia. (§ Nvidia's rescue talks with Firmus: “Firmus is now attempting to raise at least $2 billion privately, with Nvidia poised to contribute”) ^clm-8a49f234f044119f2a1e
- Nvidia has committed more than $170 billion in credit backstops, equity investments, and chip allocations to support neoclouds and large GPU customers. (§ Nvidia's $170 billion neocloud exposure: “Nvidia has committed more than $170 billion to support neoclouds and large customers”) ^clm-821da3409cbdabced334
- Nvidia agreed in June 2026 to guarantee Firmus' $15 billion purchase of server racks housing 170,000 Rubin and Blackwell GPUs for DayOne's facility in Batam, Indonesia. (§ Nvidia's $170 billion neocloud exposure: “backstop Firmus' purchase of server racks housing at least 170,000 Rubin and Blackwell GPUs valued at roughly $15 billion”) ^clm-0c6d2f7ba953dec5f4ac
- Nvidia supports neoclouds to defend GPU demand as its biggest cloud customers, including Google, Microsoft, and Amazon, develop in-house server processors. (§ Nvidia's $170 billion neocloud exposure: “major cloud hyperscalers like Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are developing proprietary server chips”) ^clm-6a64e76b682eedf78bbb
- An internal Nvidia team co-led by Nico Caprez assists neocloud partners in securing scarce data center locations with grid power connectivity. (§ Nvidia's $170 billion neocloud exposure: “co-led by Jensen Huang's son-in-law Nico Caprez helps neoclouds locate scarce data center sites”) ^clm-2bf00bf5de794edb75fc
- Larger neoclouds rejected Nvidia's initial credit support program because it required sharing a cut of future cloud computing revenue. (§ Adjusting credit support terms: “larger neoclouds balked at handing over revenue shares”) ^clm-2019997604a8f413cc99
- Nvidia modified its credit support structure for data center developers, providing shorter-term guarantees in exchange for a smaller share of cloud revenue. (§ Adjusting credit support terms: “offering shorter-term backstops in exchange for a smaller percentage of revenues”) ^clm-f8e4af21ff81574f5e44
