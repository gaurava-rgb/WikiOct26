# SpaceX Buys More Spectrum, Musk and Market Structure, Distributed Cellular

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-10-13.

## Summary

Thompson analyzes SpaceX's $8 billion cash purchase of 800-megahertz cellular spectrum licenses from Grain Management. While carrier shares sink on the news, Thompson connects this low-band purchase to SpaceX's earlier EchoStar spectrum deals and Gwynne Shotwell's comments on deploying femtocell base stations on customer Starlink dishes. Instead of spending billions building traditional cell towers, Starlink aims to build a distributed cellular mesh using rooftop dishes for space backhaul and low-band spectrum for indoor wall penetration. Thompson argues this fits Elon Musk's broader strategy of reshaping market structure to make his company's victory inevitable, even when competitors and partners like Delta Air Lines refuse to work with him personally.

## Key claims

- SpaceX agreed to buy U.S. cellular spectrum licenses from investment firm Grain Management for about $8 billion in cash to move into the wireless market. (§ SpaceX Buys More Spectrum: “SpaceX reached a deal to buy cellular spectrum licenses from investment firm Grain Management”) ^clm-4704bb519fd75ef01d8e
- The licenses SpaceX acquired from Grain Management are in the 800-megahertz band, tailored for wireless service from cellphone towers rather than satellites. (§ SpaceX Buys More Spectrum: “The U.S. licenses, which are in the 800-megahertz band, are tailored for wireless service from cellphone towers rather than satellites.”) ^clm-03cf344f410a7b42f764
- Following news of SpaceX's spectrum purchase, shares in AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile each fell more than 6% in post-close trading, while SpaceX shares gained roughly 3% to $165.10 after hours. (§ SpaceX Buys More Spectrum: “Shares in AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile all fell more than 6% in post-close trading.”) ^clm-2faad048137f0f8aeff6
- In September 2025 SpaceX acquired H-Block and AWS-4 spectrum from EchoStar, which faced coverage hurdles because AWS-4 was not approved for satellite-to-handset communication and EchoStar had configured uplink bands for downlink. (§ SpaceX Buys More Spectrum: “SpaceX acquired H-Block and AWS-4 spectrum from EchoStar”) ^clm-a79f59e1f2d38dab261f
- Rather than partnering with Starlink, AT&T and Verizon doubled down on AST SpaceMobile for satellite connectivity, while Apple allied with Amazon through its Globalstar purchase. (§ SpaceX Buys More Spectrum: “AT&T and Verizon doubled down on AST SpaceMobile”) ^clm-16b9f357b6f2a89c16ad
- Thompson argues major telecommunications carriers and tech companies avoided partnering with Starlink because none wanted to become beholden to Elon Musk. (§ SpaceX Buys More Spectrum: “none of the companies wanted to be beholden to Elon Musk.”) ^clm-ef44a14e0660782fa397
- In November 2025 SpaceX bought the AWS-3 spectrum block from EchoStar to secure expansive uplink capacity matching its AWS-4 purchase, awaiting satellite-to-handset regulatory approval. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “the company bought the AWS-3 spectrum block from EchoStar that provided much more expansive uplink capacity”) ^clm-5a5a5236fee6dcffd8fb
- Delta Air Lines CEO Ed Bastian stated that Delta tested Starlink in-flight internet but chose not to partner with it due to personal reluctance to work with Elon Musk. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “Ed also said that DL did test with starlink, but did not want to partner with Elon.”) ^clm-6225bc579eecd105c82a
- United Airlines had completed 41% of its Starlink in-flight internet installation rollout across its fleet as of October 2026. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “United rollout is only 41% complete.”) ^clm-889762c9db7130b2fa35
- Southwest Airlines began installing Starlink satellite internet on its aircraft as part of its passenger experience upgrades. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “Southwest has started its Starlink installation”) ^clm-ca2af16c592865cbddef
- American Airlines expanded its Starlink satellite internet commitment to cover its entire mainline jet fleet. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “American Airlines has upped its Starlink commitment”) ^clm-09a0a8d61c97ac5313bc
- Thompson argues Delta risks undermining its premium airline positioning by enduring inferior connectivity while awaiting Amazon Leo as competitors deploy fast Starlink WiFi. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “Delta has to endure the messy middle where some flights are awesome and some aren’t.”) ^clm-f4dbbd0618cc864fe610
- Thompson argues Elon Musk approaches industries by seeking to reshape market structure so that his companies' victory becomes inevitable regardless of customer or rival personal sentiment. (§ Musk and Market Structure: “his goal is to fundamentally reshape the market structure such that his companies’ victory is inevitable”) ^clm-c39aca871ca937bdba11
- SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell stated that SpaceX intends to build out a terrestrial mobile component by mounting cellular base stations directly onto the gear holding Starlink broadband dishes. (§ Distributed Cellular: “you could put a base station, a cellular base station on the — basically the gear that holds a Starlink broadband dish.”) ^clm-0d1100ec9d35fb841e67
- Shotwell stated that deploying femtocell base stations on customer Starlink dish mounts allows SpaceX to expand terrestrial mobile coverage as needed without spending billions upfront on low-band spectrum. (§ Distributed Cellular: “You don’t have to necessarily spend many billions of dollars in low-band spectrum all upfront before you deploy your system.”) ^clm-72aa97b9f9f622f569a9
- Elon Musk stated SpaceX plans to deploy small cellular base stations on rooftop Starlink antennas across homes and businesses, avoiding the expensive buildout of large cell towers. (§ Distributed Cellular: “Instead of having to deploy these very expensive and difficult-to-locate large cellular base stations, we feel reasonably confident that we can deploy a large number of sort of small stations.”) ^clm-71461a9375419d0cec84
- Thompson argues Starlink's satellite constellation gives its network ubiquitous backhaul everywhere, enabling a mesh network of small-scale rooftop access points rather than costly fiber-linked towers. (§ Distributed Cellular: “Thanks to the company’s satellite constellation its future network basically has backhaul everywhere; the way to leverage that is not with ultra-powerful nodes but rather what is basically a mesh network of smaller scale access points.”) ^clm-ec75e662d96d2296663b
- SpaceX's purchased 800-megahertz spectrum licenses were previously utilized for Sprint's old Nextel push-to-talk network, providing low frequencies that penetrate building walls. (§ Distributed Cellular: “This latest spectrum purchase was previously used for Sprint’s old Nextel push-to-talk network.”) ^clm-4a697ca4cf41027ad1eb
- Thompson argues low-band 800-megahertz spectrum fills coverage gaps between rooftop dish access points where SpaceX cannot mount antennas on every building. (§ Distributed Cellular: “still, it can’t have access points on every building, so this spectrum purchase fills in the gaps in letting those sites reach more places.”) ^clm-8183064b4ed24090c318
- SpaceX announced Starlink Mobile will combine its 2-gigahertz mid-band satellite constellation in space with low-band terrestrial spectrum to ensure indoor and outdoor cellular coverage. (§ Distributed Cellular: “Starlink Mobile will deploy a flexible architecture combining our satellite-to-mobile constellation in space with an advanced terrestrial deployment that can seamlessly connect devices everywhere.”) ^clm-63d732acc11f78c2c531
- SpaceX noted that most existing mobile handsets already support the underused 800-megahertz low-band spectrum without needing hardware alterations. (§ Distributed Cellular: “Critically, most existing mobile devices already support this underused band.”) ^clm-fa1bece621727eb9f35f
- Thompson argues SpaceX applies its low-orbit satellite swarm strategy to mobile networks by deploying large numbers of lower-capacity ground access points linked by software and space backhaul. (§ Distributed Cellular: “the company’s approach to mobile is in the same vein, leveraging its satellite advantage to build out massively more ground access points with relatively lower capacity”) ^clm-825d5c168203a681095b
- Thompson notes that unlike in-flight satellite broadband where airlines select providers, consumer mobile wireless requires SpaceX to win over individual retail customers from entrenched carriers. (§ Distributed Cellular: “when it comes to consumer cellular services, the decision makers are not airline executives but individual customers.”) ^clm-6fb25c8c33e2036deb83
