# Google I/O, World Models, I/O Spaghetti

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-05-20.

## Summary

Google I/O 2026 put AI into nearly every product: a redesigned search box, the Gemini Spark personal agent, Gemini 3.5 Flash, the Gemini Omni world model, Antigravity 2.0, and more. Thompson argues the sprawl shows Google is fully committed to AI but also unfocused, like a company shipping its org chart. He spends most of the piece on Demis Hassabis and world models: DeepMind thinks the gap in AI understanding is real-world data gathered through simulation, while Anthropic bets on coding and recursive self-improvement. Thompson suggests Google may trail in coding simply because its research leader has other priorities, then reminds himself that Google's lack of focus is exactly why it has endured.

## Key claims

- At I/O Google overhauled its search box to handle the longer, more complex queries people bring to chatbots, and plans search agents that track topics, make reservations, and monitor health, some limited at first to paying subscribers. (§ Google I/O: “Google overhauled the search box to better handle the longer, more complicated queries”) ^clm-de6d44502f9e7d9417fa
- Google began rolling out Gemini Spark, a personal agent that acts across a user's digital life, works across many Google products, and runs on Google's cloud infrastructure. (§ Google I/O: “Gemini Spark, a personal agent it says is capable of navigating a user's digital life”) ^clm-5c87b9cd80d50d0e66eb
- Google introduced Gemini 3.5 Flash, pitched for coding and agentic work and said to run four times faster than other frontier models, with the stronger but slower Gemini 3.5 Pro due within weeks. (§ Google I/O: “runs four times faster than other frontier models”) ^clm-61bd86f911f44f6eaf58
- Thompson argues I/O showed Google putting AI into everything, which is both the positive and the negative reading, and that the keynote fits the old critique that Google is unfocused and unmanageable. (§ Google I/O: “One of the enduring critiques of Google is that the company is unfocused and unmanageable”) ^clm-0db7e6f13e32a7663b82
- Although Sundar Pichai featured Demis Hassabis in his promotional tweet, Hassabis was barely in the keynote; he first appeared to present Gemini Omni, a world model whose first product, Omni Flash, targets video editing and generation. (§ World Models: “Hassabis didn't, however, feature for very much of the keynote”) ^clm-ed07533ad80f8b3ab4fb
- Prediction: Hassabis said at I/O that artificial general intelligence is just a few years away. (§ World Models: “artificial general intelligence is just a few years away”) ^clm-68fa5eb1e176df1e5d29
- Hassabis called Gemini Omni a step change in simulating physics like kinetic energy and gravity, starting with video but aiming to generate any output from any input. (§ World Models: “It's a step change in simulating things like kinetic energy and gravity”) ^clm-975cffd5f55e12297bef
- Hassabis argued in December 2025 that language models understand more of the world than he expected, but spatial and physical dynamics are hard to put into words, so robotics and universal assistants need world models. (§ World Models: “language models are able to understand a lot about the world”) ^clm-ea5dee3ab8a65678b18d
- Hassabis argues that generating realistic worlds is the test of world understanding, which is why he sees Veo and Genie as steps toward generalized world models. (§ World Models: “if you can generate it, then, in a sense, you must have understood”) ^clm-be83e43a9a63d7a4963f
- Thompson argues Chinese models are mostly distillations of frontier lab models, so their performance is jagged, with steep drops just outside where they look only six months behind. (§ World Models: “Chinese models, which are by-and-large distillations of frontier lab models”) ^clm-5e8e8a686faf69ccacf6
- Thompson argues frontier models are themselves distillations of human thought, since the internet holds only the final output of people's internal reasoning, which is why they too are jagged. (§ World Models: “Frontier models themselves, however, are also distillations, this time of human thought”) ^clm-29b38a8bffa4e4ad8d74
- Thompson says Anthropic focuses on coding partly because it believes an AI that recursively improves itself fills the generalization gap, and it recruited Andrej Karpathy to lead a team on that. (§ World Models: “creating an AI that can recursively self-improve is the key to solving this hole”) ^clm-d07d3de15c873ce94f9f
- Thompson says DeepMind instead believes the gap is real-world data best gathered through realistic simulation, which yields multimodal models, unlike text-only Claude, but much weaker coding. (§ World Models: “the best way to get that data is through realistic simulations”) ^clm-0b7a619ed4fb895493ae
- Thompson argues Google may trail Anthropic and OpenAI in coding, especially long-running agentic work, simply because its research team has other priorities, which is why the Antigravity team, not DeepMind, handled coding at I/O. (§ World Models: “simply comes down to their research team having other priorities”) ^clm-f862b5b438e4a71d52c7
- Hassabis closed the keynote saying people will look back and realize they were standing in the foothills of the singularity. (§ World Models: “we were standing in the foothills of the singularity”) ^clm-29ec5059c5517da9c1c0
- Prediction: Thompson suspects that if Google's AI leader does not care about prosaic matters like coding, video, and Search, Google may not go far in them, though if Hassabis reaches AGI first it may not matter. (§ World Models: “if the company's AI leader doesn't care about such prosaic matters”) ^clm-7bc33c35415c71216b58
- Thompson argues every part of Google has a mandate to add AI, and it is unclear how coordinated the launches were or how coherent they will be to users. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “every part of the company has a mandate to incorporate AI”) ^clm-7f6f053e726633cac31a
- Thompson calls the voice-powered Docs demo very underwhelming and the clearest sign that the keynote was a ship-the-org-chart event. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “this was a very underwhelming demo that, more than anything else in this list”) ^clm-e97538b0daf4644f784e
- OpenAI is joining Google's SynthID scheme for fingerprinting AI-generated content. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “OpenAI is joining Google's SynthID fingerprinting scheme for AI-generated content”) ^clm-cdc2fa5651371d7ed1c8
- Early online reaction to Gemini 3.5 Flash was fairly negative, especially on token usage, which makes its cost per task actually higher despite the speed claims. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “particularly in terms of token usage, which makes the cost-per-task actually higher”) ^clm-f55ece376c427c2ed641
- Google launched Antigravity 2.0, shifting its AI coding tool (IDE) toward being a manager of agents. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “shifting the company's AI IDE to be more of an agent manager”) ^clm-6f8798d2f1c10fc6a77c
- Google added an AI Mode button to the search box, but AI Mode still needs an explicit click; the default remains today's search experience. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “the default remains the search experience as it exists today”) ^clm-66ea672526403a9916a7
- Google announced a glasses partnership with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster to compete with Meta. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “A glasses partnership with Samsung, Warby Parker, and Gentle Monster to compete with Meta”) ^clm-79871d5821c65ec3ce0c
- Thompson previously wrote (2025) that he had come to appreciate Google's amorphous lack of strategy, since it handled one paradigm shift and was doing much better in AI than he originally expected. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “is doing much better than I originally expected with this one”) ^clm-82da147c966fd5dc6220
- Thompson concludes the keynote was a lot of cool things done with computers and a bit maddening, but that Google being Google has worked out well, so its lack of focus may be why it endures. (§ I/O Spaghetti: “But hey, Google being Google has worked out pretty well”) ^clm-92298a5570a30c7895da
