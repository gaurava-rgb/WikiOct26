# How OpenAI Beat Math

Stephanie Palazzolo, The Information, published 2026-10-08.

## Summary

OpenAI released 722 research papers with AI-created solutions to hard math problems, even though AI still makes mistakes on simple math. Palazzolo explains why: math answers can be checked, models can grind with brute force and stamina, track many variables, and lack human bias toward assuming hard problems need hard answers. A mathematician advising OpenAI warns models miss the forest for the trees, though AI has shown some ability to connect fields. A Deep Dive covers former OpenAI safety lead David Robinson's claim that trial-and-error safety guarantees growing failures, which Stuart Russell supports.

## Key claims

- OpenAI released 722 research papers with AI-created solutions to problems across many areas of math. (§ The core story: “OpenAI released 722 research papers with AI-created solutions”) ^clm-704a06783486a9f2b564
- AI can solve ultra-hard math problems even though it still makes mistakes on simple math. (§ The core story: “AI can solve ultra-hard math problems even though it still makes mistakes on simple math”) ^clm-8babcbdbc836fb1ed85f
- Math answers can often be checked, much like software code, which helps explain why AI is good at hard math. (§ Why AI is good at hard math: “Math answers can often be checked, much like software code”) ^clm-ebde4982d09641a7b877
- Math overlaps heavily with AI research work, which labs are trying to automate in pursuit of recursive self-improvement. (§ Why AI is good at hard math: “Math overlaps heavily with the work AI researchers do”) ^clm-6634c19891f98fdb034e
- A mathematician advising OpenAI said the group calls AI's ability to grind through years of number crunching without giving up brute force and stamina. (§ Why AI is good at hard math: “brute force and stamina”) ^clm-545716727f1cac9acce9
- In several cases OpenAI's models tried simple solutions that human mathematicians had overlooked because they assumed a hard problem could not have an easy answer, and found they worked. (§ Why AI is good at hard math: “OpenAI's models tried those simple solutions and found they worked”) ^clm-6d87bf3013bceb74c873
- The mathematician advising OpenAI says models miss the forest for the trees, since the point of math is often building new fields or general principles, not solving one hard problem. (§ Limits: “miss the forest for the trees”) ^clm-951fb282fe6e9cb6516c
- Palazzolo notes it is unproven that humans keep the edge in connecting ideas, since AI has already shown some ability to connect concepts across fields. (§ Limits: “AI has already shown some ability to connect concepts across fields”) ^clm-6315dc653546801a05e0
- David Robinson, who led writing of OpenAI's safety reports until his resignation, argued OpenAI's culture is broken because trial-and-error iterative deployment guarantees periodic failures that grow as systems get more capable.
