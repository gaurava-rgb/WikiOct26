# OpenAI’s Bug and Bumble’s AI-Powered Exit

Martin Peers, The Information, published 2026-10-13.

## Summary

Martin Peers' Briefing column covers an OpenAI engineer arrested for allegedly planting a recording device in a company conference room, and Ithaca Holdings' $1.3 billion purchase of Bumble, which Peers argues is a distressed sale dressed up as an AI bet. Character.AI's bankruptcy shows the end state of the 2024 license-and-hire deals, with Disney hiring its CEO and bidding for its IP. Shorter items: JPMorgan blocks outside AI agents such as Meta's Muse from client accounts, and Apple launches a $299 HomeView smart home screen.

## Key claims

- On October 13, 2026 the Justice Department said an OpenAI engineer, a Saudi national who has been arrested, allegedly planted a sophisticated recording device in a company conference room. (§ OpenAI's bug: “an OpenAI engineer allegedly planted a "sophisticated recording device" in a company conference room”) ^clm-745aaf6b50c190ff46a3
- The alleged bug was attached to the back of a TV in an OpenAI conference room where highly confidential technical, commercial and strategic information was often discussed. (§ OpenAI's bug: “highly confidential technical, commercial and strategic information”) ^clm-558740d8cccb9e3bbd34
- On October 13, 2026 investment firm Ithaca Holdings unveiled a deal to buy dating app Bumble at a $1.3 billion valuation. (§ Bumble's AI-powered exit: “Ithaca Holdings unveiled a deal to buy dating app Bumble at a $1.3 billion valuation”) ^clm-3dbc0fcd55ce9453fafa
- Ithaca co-CEO Ravi Gupta, a former Sequoia partner, said Bumble stood out among hundreds of companies Ithaca reviewed for which would become more valuable as AI advances. (§ Bumble's AI-powered exit: “would become more valuable as AI advances”) ^clm-aa7287d3f87b08606518
- Peers argues acquisitions of distressed companies are being dressed up as AI-driven purchases, and that Bumble could adopt AI without being sold, for example with forward-deployed engineers from Google or Palantir; the real reason it is selling is a troubled business. (§ Bumble's AI-powered exit: “acquisitions of distressed companies are starting to be dressed up as AI-driven purchases”) ^clm-f685252bd1afb529d325
- Bumble's revenue fell 10% in 2025 and another 15% in the first half of 2026. (§ Bumble's AI-powered exit: “Bumble revenue fell 10% in 2025 and another 15% in the first half of 2026”) ^clm-b6dac0988975b00ae582
- As of June 30, 2026, Bumble had $450 million of debt but only $154 million in cash. (§ Bumble's AI-powered exit: “Bumble had $450 million of debt but only $154 million in cash”) ^clm-c24df6be9280214f3330
- Bumble is selling at $6.05 a share, one-seventh of its $43 a share IPO price in 2021, far from the bright future founder Whitney Wolfe Herd promised in the prospectus. (§ Bumble's AI-powered exit: “Bumble is selling at $6.05 a share, one-seventh of its $43 a share IPO price”) ^clm-cf23b1f06a0f75fa4850
- Early AI startup Character.AI filed for bankruptcy on October 12, 2026, likely one of the final steps in its long decline. (§ Character.AI's slide into oblivion: “Character.AI filed for bankruptcy on Monday 2026-10-12”) ^clm-42aedb604891c026f41b
- In 2024 Google paid $2.7 billion to license Character.AI's models, letting investors cash out at a profit, and co-founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas rejoined Google; Shazeer later left for OpenAI. (§ Character.AI's slide into oblivion: “when Google paid $2.7 billion to license Character.AI's models”) ^clm-4d2ace177c08be2be859
- The Google and Character.AI deal was one of several 2024 license-and-hire deals, with Microsoft and Inflection AI and Amazon and Adept, structured as licenses rather than acquisitions to avoid antitrust scrutiny; many were investigated anyway. (§ Character.AI's slide into oblivion: “structured them as licensing deals, not acquisitions, hoping to avoid antitrust scrutiny”) ^clm-d3505e2e9e26cd7d984d
- What is left after license-and-hire deals varies: Inflection recently launched Inflection AI Labs, while Adept's website has not been updated since mid-2024, shortly after its Amazon deal. (§ Character.AI's slide into oblivion: “Adept's website has not been updated since mid-2024”) ^clm-e4acafcc08e30ba248e8
- In September 2026 Disney hired Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand, formerly of Meta, as chief technology officer along with other Character.AI technical staff, and it has bid $20 million to license Character.AI's IP in the bankruptcy. (§ Character.AI's slide into oblivion: “Disney has also bid $20 million to license Character.AI's intellectual property”) ^clm-4b1baec843a188664fc1
- JPMorgan said in October 2026 it is blocking external AI agents such as Meta's Muse and Instinct from accessing client accounts. (§ In other news: “JPMorgan said it is blocking external AI agents, such as Meta's Muse and Instinct”) ^clm-bd43255c7b9d8fad7333
- JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon said on the bank's third-quarter 2026 earnings call that letting any outside agent inside the company is too risky for clients and for JPMorgan. (§ In other news: “that's just too risky for clients and for JPMorgan”) ^clm-f7a84a419bf8107fc31b
- Apple is launching HomeView, a $299 tablet-like smart home device with a 7-inch screen for kitchen counters or walls, as part of a renewed push into the home. (§ In other news: “Apple is launching HomeView, a $299 tablet-like smart home device with a 7-inch screen”) ^clm-22c841cd9284d18829da
- Blackstone-backed Candle Media is selling Reese Witherspoon's Hello Sunshine back to Witherspoon with French firm Mediawan, per Bloomberg; Candle bought it at a $900 million valuation in 2021, seen as steep then. (§ In other news: “Candle bought it at a $900 million valuation in 2021, seen as steep then”) ^clm-4b9a9a846315e9bac26e
