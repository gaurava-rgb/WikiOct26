# Netflix and the Conservation of Attractive Profits

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2015-07-08.

## Summary

Thompson retracts his 2013 view that Netflix was just another TV network. He argues on-demand streaming commoditized time (there is no prime time), which makes Netflix a marketplace between viewers and creators, like Uber and Airbnb. He uses Christensen's Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits to explain it: when one stage of a value chain is modularized, profit moves to a new integration at an adjacent stage, here the customer relationship. He still caps his enthusiasm, because differentiated content creators keep real power and HBO remains the prestige leader, though its development backlog pushes talent toward Netflix and Amazon.

## Key claims

- In 2013 Thompson called Netflix just another network, HBO with a unique delivery system, and in 2015 he says he got this wrong in a big way. (“I got this one wrong, and in a big way.”) ^clm-60441fe4122f7f4b59b4
- In 2013 Thompson argued Netflix had pivoted from content delivery to being simply another network that funds new shows with reruns and old movies as filler. (“Pivot number two was their transformation from a content delivery provider to simply another network”) ^clm-616565b207dedf550281
- Prediction: A 2015 FBR Capital Markets analysis reported by Variety projected that Netflix's U.S. 24-hour audience would surpass each of ABC, CBS, Fox and NBC by 2016. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “Netflix U.S. Viewing to Surpass ABC, CBS, Fox and NBC by 2016”) ^clm-0de2e55e8f40063cffe0
- Netflix does not care about ratings of individual shows because it does not sell ads, and it discloses only general viewing data. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “Netflix doesn’t care about “ratings” of individual shows, given that it doesn’t sell ads”) ^clm-ca61ace03c344a09413b
- Thompson notes TV network revenue shifted over several years from advertising toward affiliate fees, which are more profitable, predictable and sustainable than chasing mass audiences. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “a little bit of money from every cable subscriber is much more profitable, predictable, and sustainable”) ^clm-36939613114d69b2a547
- Thompson argues the affiliate-fee model made must-see shows and events matter more than mass-appeal programming, since only fans basing their pay-TV decision on them count; sports fans are why ESPN is the most profitable network. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “it’s far more important to have “must-see” shows and events”) ^clm-b695bbcffb8da942df58
- Thompson argues on-demand streaming, and Netflix in particular, commoditized time: there is no prime time, so Sunday at 9pm is no different from Tuesday at 11am. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “the service has commoditized time”) ^clm-556764c67e1247076908
- Netflix releases original series all at once to spread an expensive up-front production cost over as many viewing minutes as possible. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “Thus Netflix will release original series all at once, because why not?”) ^clm-f3931fb35adc3fef56dc
- Thompson doubts binge release is optimal for any single show, suspecting appointment viewing creates valuable buzz, but says it signals Netflix is committed to connecting creators and viewers efficiently. (§ Why Netflix Isn’t a Network: “I’m actually not certain this strategy is optimal for any one given show”) ^clm-6662739abc86de08a0ec
- Thompson argues Netflix is less a network than a marketplace where consumers give their attention to creators, putting it in the same class as Uber and Airbnb. (§ The Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits: “Netflix isn’t that far off from Uber or Airbnb or any of the other market-makers”) ^clm-f275fc27699a4d497961
- Thompson lists three traits shared by Netflix and other market-makers, working in order: an infinitely scalable network, a commoditized former constraint, and positioning as chief beneficiary of the industry's transformation. (§ The Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits: “Positioned itself to be the chief beneficiary of industry transformation”) ^clm-a2f4a6722fd310910b94
- Christensen's 2003 book The Innovator's Solution states that when modularity and commoditization kill profits at one stage of a value chain, proprietary profits usually emerge at an adjacent stage. (§ The Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits: “the opportunity to earn attractive profits with proprietary products will usually emerge at an adjacent stage”) ^clm-38f8ae0d1485d7f06638
- Thompson argues Christensen explained in 2003, four years before the iPhone, why Intel was doomed in mobile and ARM would rise: a device that is not good enough needs a modular, conformable processor. (§ The Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits: “explaining why it was that Intel was doomed in mobile even as ARM would become ascendent”) ^clm-4baf3f2d38da1f85293b
- Thompson notes the iPhone is modular at the component level; its integration is between the finished phone and the software. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “the iPhone is in fact modular at the component level”) ^clm-64e37780809fbc6f2f6a
- Thompson argues that commoditizing and modularizing an integrated system destroys incumbent value while letting a new entrant integrate a different part of the value chain and capture new value. (§ The Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits: “destroys incumbent value while simultaneously allowing a new entrant to integrate a different part of the value chain”) ^clm-316a2b6ee181ec8060f5
- Thompson argues Airbnb commoditized trust by divorcing it from the physical property, freeing it to integrate trust and reservations across a worldwide network of hosts and guests. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “the service commoditized trust, divorcing it from the underlying physical property”) ^clm-3a9c7b728dc9c65fe517
- Thompson argues Uber commoditized dispatch and modularized cars, letting it integrate dispatch with hailing and payments. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “the service commoditized dispatch and modularized cars”) ^clm-37480d3a7d06f7f61577
- Thompson argues Netflix commoditized time and distribution and so integrated production with customer management (subscriptions and distribution), modularizing content production. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “by commoditizing time and distribution the company has integrated production and customer management”) ^clm-ded78f0f831759db16e1
- Thompson argues Airbnb, Uber and Netflix are all specialized customer-relationship companies that modularized production or delivery to move closer to the customer: Airbnb for travelers, Uber for commuters, Netflix for the bored. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “all of this new value is being created by specialized CRM companies”) ^clm-d14b7032921e3aa8b4a0
- Thompson limits his reversal: in the TV value chain highly differentiated content creators keep the most power, unlike Airbnb hosts and Uber drivers. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “highly differentiated content creators that retain the most power”) ^clm-61e59f3a72eb1674ad82
- Netflix faces strong competition for attention from traditional pay-TV, Amazon Prime, a revitalized Hulu, and especially HBO. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “Amazon Prime, a revitalized Hulu, and especially HBO”) ^clm-3c80aed15bd8cc78adcf
- A 2015 Hollywood Reporter profile found HBO remained the most desirable place for top producers because of its audience, budgets, promotion and experience. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “HBO remains the most desirable place to be”) ^clm-dced7e5b0780473a54aa
- HBO had more than 100 projects in development, and Netflix and Amazon lured talent frustrated by the queue; that speed reportedly won Netflix Bloodline and The OA. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “That swiftness is said to have been the key to Netflix, rather than HBO”) ^clm-e15d8956e4052126ba48
- Thompson argues a new entrant like Netflix is free to integrate around the customer while staying modular in content creation, and the most promising companies make the biggest breaks with the past. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “the most promising companies are the ones that create the biggest breaks with the past”) ^clm-81ae40c007f25ea51707
- Prediction: Thompson notes that if Uber gets self-driving cars it would become fully integrated, and that employee lawsuits are a real risk to Uber's modular driver model. (§ How Airbnb, Uber, and Netflix Capture Value: “when/if Uber has self-driving cars, they will be fully integrated”) ^clm-05270d9367fee7c6a8c7
