# Netflix Earnings, Is Netflix Washed?, Additional Notes

Ben Thompson, Stratechery, published 2026-07-21.

## Summary

Netflix's Q2 2026 results were in line with expectations: revenue of $12.56 billion, up over 13%, but a softer Q3 forecast sent shares down more than 8% after hours. Thompson, following Eric Seufert, now doubts his earlier benefit of the doubt on Netflix's shrinking disclosures, noting the company cuts metrics just as they are about to turn south and is now reporting engagement less often even though advertising depends on it. Drawing on Andrew Sharp's 'Is Netflix Washed?', he argues Netflix is a mature, boring company whose visible levers (subscribers, price increases, ads, buybacks) all work, and that the failed Warner Bros. bid looks like a last grab at adventure before growing up. New moves like live events, podcasts, and cloud TV games are sensible but marginal, so Netflix should tell investors exactly what those margins are.

## Key claims

- Netflix reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $12.56 billion, up more than 13% year over year, with net income up almost 9% to $3.4 billion, roughly in line with analyst expectations. (§ Netflix Earnings: “The company reported second-quarter revenue of $12.56 billion, up more than 13%”) ^clm-faf33e323ba282c2b81d
- Netflix forecast third-quarter 2026 revenue growth of 11.7%, its smallest year-on-year increase since late 2023, and shares fell more than 8% after hours. (§ Netflix Earnings: “its smallest year-on-year increase of any quarter since late 2023”) ^clm-99bafbfb26de25caf351
- Netflix attributed second-quarter gains partly to recent price increases and growth in advertising. (§ Netflix Earnings: “attributed the second-quarter gains in part to recent price increases and growth in advertising”) ^clm-50804d5a34913a0f0069
- Before the results, Netflix stock had already fallen more than 40% over the prior year, a slide that began when it explored bidding for Warner Bros. Discovery's studio and streaming service and continued after it lost the deal. (§ Netflix Earnings: “the streamer’s stock had already fallen more than 40% over the prior year”) ^clm-c87457a63b5db7ba1dee
- Viewers watched more than 97 billion hours on Netflix in the first half of 2026, up 1.5% from the second half of 2025 and 1.9% from the first half of 2025. (§ Netflix Earnings: “viewers watched more than 97 billion hours of content on its service in the first half of 2026”) ^clm-eb756a83ee6698d050e7
- Research firm Antenna puts Netflix's June 2026 churn at 2.11%, by far the best in the streaming industry. (§ Netflix Earnings: “by far the best in the industry at 2.11% for June”) ^clm-f94d38508caeeca48e2c
- Netflix will publish its What We Watched engagement report once a year instead of twice. (§ Netflix Earnings: “will shift to an annual release of its “What We Watched” report”) ^clm-664ff997257c4e870b5d
- Eric Seufert argues the engagement report cut continues a pattern of withheld metrics: forward subscriber guidance ended in 2023, and total subscriber numbers and regional revenue per member ended with Q1 2025. (§ Netflix Earnings: “the most recent disclosure rollback from Netflix in a steady, systematic practice of withholding metrics”) ^clm-ff3111741658504d7e28
- Seufert argues that if total view hours are a poor measure of engagement, Netflix should release more engagement data, not the same data less often. (§ Netflix Earnings: “it should release more engagement data, not less”) ^clm-ab367e97a06124fce96c
- Greg Peters says view hours do not map linearly to revenue: live events will be about 5% of Netflix's 2026 content budget but only about 1% of view hours, while kids and family animation is also about 5% of spend but about 8% of view hours. (§ Netflix Earnings: “live, we expect will be 5% of our content budget this year, but we think that will only be 1% of view hours”) ^clm-1d71bbe16d702073f920
- Peters says 6 of Netflix's top 10 new member sign-up days over the past five years came from live events. (§ Netflix Earnings: “6 out of top 10 new member sign-up days over the past 5 years have come from live events”) ^clm-875d83bdc1112ed75b4d
- In an earlier 2026 Stratechery interview, Peters explained Netflix dropped subscriber counts because extra members, widely varying revenue per member, and advertising made the subscriber number less complete, and Netflix wants to be judged on revenue and profit. (§ Netflix Earnings: “We’d love to people start to treating us like a business”) ^clm-70bfa1b36de6e7596c42
- Thompson changes his view: he had mostly given Netflix the benefit of the doubt on reduced disclosures, but now sees a propensity to hide metrics as soon as they are about to turn south. (§ Netflix Earnings: “Netflix has a propensity to hide metrics as soon as they are about to turn south on them”) ^clm-fff00f1b4fb8babc5813
- Thompson argues Peters' own example undercuts him: advertising is very much a function of engagement, yet Netflix is reducing the only real engagement numbers it reports. (§ Netflix Earnings: “advertising is very much a function of engagement, and now the company is reducing the only real engagement numbers”) ^clm-22d3f4ec9b14674f33d5
- Andrew Sharp argues Netflix is washed: not doomed, but almost certainly peaked as a cultural force, a business success story, and a Hollywood death star, with content that has never been worse. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “what’s interesting to consider is that Netflix has almost certainly peaked”) ^clm-2f8c08c5cd166d481135
- Sharp notes Netflix content reaches 85% of American viewers and Netflix has 325 million subscribers globally. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “Netflix content reaches a staggering 85% of American viewers and has 325 million subscribers globally”) ^clm-0554b5da02c66dbc896d
- Netflix lowered its third-quarter outlook below expectations but reaffirmed full-year guidance, implying a stronger fourth quarter. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “Netflix reaffirmed their full year guidance, suggesting that the fourth quarter will be better”) ^clm-7d0518f0f56747599526
- Excluding the Warner Bros. breakup fee, Netflix is set to generate $10.25 billion of cash flow in 2026, and it bought back $4.7 billion of shares in the quarter. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “the company bought $4.7 billion worth of shares this quarter”) ^clm-6a1663e0081d1a239c29
- Thompson argues Netflix is now pretty boring, and in retrospect the failed Warner Bros. acquisition feels like an inflection point. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “the failed Warner Bros. acquisition feels like an inflection point”) ^clm-50fff21230ca16331d9b
- Thompson revisits his earlier read that the Warner Bros. bid was a response to YouTube taking over the TV; after Sharp's piece he compares it to a last-ditch adventure before growing up. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “I assumed it was a response to YouTube’s continued takeover of the TV”) ^clm-e41fecc170337b4a9d0c
- Ted Sarandos frames Netflix's expansion from one prestige drama in 2013 to unscripted, local language, comedy, and competition shows as evolutionary, not revolutionary. (§ Is Netflix Washed?: “These expansions are evolutionary, not revolutionary”) ^clm-b1365b9b3e9f298cb6b5
- Netflix did not rule out a free tier eventually but said it is not coming soon because of cannibalization concerns, and that a thriving ad business is a prerequisite. (§ Additional Notes: “it wasn’t happening anytime soon, because of cannibalization concerns”) ^clm-d3be33c6d736b6794ab4
- Netflix views its podcast initiative as a success because it has driven more viewing during work hours and on mobile. (§ Additional Notes: “driven increased consumption during work hours and on mobile”) ^clm-3a369d15c1c9043fc340
- Thompson argues cloud-based TV games make far more sense for Netflix than app-based games; Seufert notes its Game Controller app regularly ranks in the App Store top ten. (§ Additional Notes: “This makes so much more sense for the service than app-based games”) ^clm-ccd147a461f0d420218a
- Netflix management did not dismiss partnering with other streamers or acting as a storefront for them. (§ Additional Notes: “potentially acting as a storefront, and did not dismiss it out of hand”) ^clm-e0ffa78403879c05daf9
