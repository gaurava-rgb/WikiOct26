# Uber

US ride-hailing and delivery app.

## Facts by source

### 2015-07-08 [[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits|Netflix and the Conservation of Attractive Profits]]

- Thompson argues Netflix is less a network than a marketplace where consumers give their attention to creators, putting it in the same class as Uber and Airbnb. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-f275fc27699a4d497961|cite]])
- Thompson lists three traits shared by Netflix and other market-makers, working in order: an infinitely scalable network, a commoditized former constraint, and positioning as chief beneficiary of the industry's transformation. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-a2f4a6722fd310910b94|cite]])
- Thompson argues that commoditizing and modularizing an integrated system destroys incumbent value while letting a new entrant integrate a different part of the value chain and capture new value. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-316a2b6ee181ec8060f5|cite]])
- Thompson argues Uber commoditized dispatch and modularized cars, letting it integrate dispatch with hailing and payments. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-37480d3a7d06f7f61577|cite]])
- Thompson argues Airbnb, Uber and Netflix are all specialized customer-relationship companies that modularized production or delivery to move closer to the customer: Airbnb for travelers, Uber for commuters, Netflix for the bored. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-d14b7032921e3aa8b4a0|cite]])
- Thompson limits his reversal: in the TV value chain highly differentiated content creators keep the most power, unlike Airbnb hosts and Uber drivers. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-61e59f3a72eb1674ad82|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson notes that if Uber gets self-driving cars it would become fully integrated, and that employee lawsuits are a real risk to Uber's modular driver model. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-05270d9367fee7c6a8c7|cite]])

### 2026-10-06 [[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz|Uber's M&A Blitz]]

- Uber announced a $2.3 billion purchase of ezCater, a business catering company. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-801061771037cfd4e326|cite]])
- Peers argues neither deal will excite investors, but both show Uber and DoorDash doing whatever they can to keep growth going. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-26d27229dddd72d1748a|cite]])
- DoorDash has bought Deliveroo and Wolt in Europe, and Uber is buying Delivery Hero, but only a handful of such targets exist. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-cf55930d4ee101e3a802|cite]])
- Uber and DoorDash dominate restaurant delivery and increasingly groceries, and growing by adding new subscribers keeps getting harder. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-6c1a31b2294c0d2370d0|cite]])
- Uber's food delivery bookings growth also sped up over the past 18 months without Delivery Hero, while its ride-hailing business slowed. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-21c79bde75577e1ed539|cite]])
- The parts of Delivery Hero that Uber is buying had gross bookings last year of roughly half of Uber Eats, so the deal's effect on Uber's growth rate will be more visible. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-44c8bdc338ae644f1010|cite]])
- Over roughly the past 12 months Uber bought parking app SpotHero, luxury travel firm Blacklane, and a data collection firm for a new AI data labeling business. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-4d8201c77b13c7ec7898|cite]])
- Peers reads both companies as throwing off lots of cash, so when a deal looks promising they just go for it. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-bf8e6c960152d14a43d5|cite]])
