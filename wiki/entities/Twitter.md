### 2026-05-21 [[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel|An Interview with Parallel Founder Parag Agarwal About Valuing Content on the Agentic Web]]

- Agarwal says he joined Twitter as its most junior software engineer, because he loved the product. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-8ae04bc583aee5e0a72d|cite]])
- Agarwal says Twitter's ads team grew revenue from almost nothing to a couple billion dollars in a couple of years after going public. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-cf8467de9bb5bd6e9be0|cite]])
- Agarwal says that after the IPO, Twitter's own projections showed it would stall at about 90 million daily active users (DAU, people who use it each day), so he moved from ads to the consumer product. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-230b9bacefff082fd8c5|cite]])
- Agarwal says bringing machine learning into Twitter's feed, against internal resistance, restarted DAU growth within months. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-aa7b14a235cb2e269076|cite]])
- Agarwal calls Bluesky, the decentralized protocol effort he worked on under Jack Dorsey at Twitter, a failure. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-534f32f4f7eeb176bbed|cite]])
- Agarwal says that as CEO during the Elon Musk takeover, he was already working with the board on a meaningful layoff, though smaller than the one Musk later made. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-016885738db9b30c3dc7|cite]])
- Agarwal says his biggest regret is not building a materially better Twitter business during his years as CTO, and he shares the blame with Jack Dorsey. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-45d2c43eab8d08da315c|cite]])
