# Prada

Italian luxury fashion house; its high-tech Manhattan flagship is a cautionary IS tale.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch02 Zara - Fast Fashion from Savvy Systems]]

- Prada's Rem Koolhaas-designed Manhattan flagship used RFID-tagged items and smart dressing rooms, but customers could not work the foot pedals, got stuck, and the inventory database often showed items falsely out of stock. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-d7704fd25996cda338b4|cite]])
- Prada staff said they no longer used the store PDAs, and the Manhattan location took in about one-third the sales needed to justify its tech spending (2004). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-92f516db48c5ad6b2d70|cite]])
- Gallaugher's lesson from Prada: an information system is hardware, software, data, procedures and people, and managers must forecast return on investment and test before rollout. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-2d768fe38a799ef0b02e|cite]])
- Zara has experimented with hybrid mirrors that read a garment's RFID tag to suggest outfits (2018), and H&M, Macy's and Sears have also tried smart mirrors. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-5b109ed4cb89a4fecb98|cite]])
