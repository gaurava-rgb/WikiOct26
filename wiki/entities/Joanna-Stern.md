# Joanna Stern

Tech columnist and video maker; at the Wall Street Journal 2013 to 2026, now runs New Things and is NBC News chief technology analyst.

## Facts by source

### 2026-05-07 [[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI|An Interview with Joanna Stern About Living With AI]]

- Stern's first book, I Am Not a Robot: My Year Using AI to Do (Almost) Everything, comes out on May 12, 2026. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-1cf5a494d011c34572dd|cite]])
- Stern started her AI year on January 1, 2025, going into the year with AI everywhere, and the book is organized by season. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-c10c5fb24764561de562|cite]])
- Stern says the book was meant from the start to cover AI beyond LLMs (large language models, the tech behind chatbots), such as Waymo robotaxis and AI radiology. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-04841967262a81dfd712|cite]])
- Stern argues AI will affect everyone's life whether or not they use chatbots, for example a mammogram may be read by AI without the patient knowing. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-024329807bf9e38ae1c5|cite]])
- Prediction: Stern predicts self-driving cars will take over roads in the next 10 years. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-419c6941155f557dec2d|cite]])
- Stern avoided naming specific AI models in the book because she did not think the model version made a difference, and details go stale fast. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-54fdf79467046e229d64|cite]])
- Stern says AI helped her ship the book fast through research, copy editing, and admin work, not by writing chapters; she hired a reporting assistant early but no longer needed her by year end because AI did many of those tasks. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-c8f29c64678035bd3487|cite]])
- Stern says that without AI she would still be writing the book instead of promoting it. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-482a0d3d1c1524904dc7|cite]])
- Stern admits heavy use of AI gadgets can cloud her view: she wore the Bee recording bracelet, which sends recordings to Amazon servers, and forgot to tell people she was recording them. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-38ddd79a493304829e04|cite]])
- Stern uses Meta's smart glasses so much she usually carries two pairs and forgets other people's concerns about them. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-da1b1fc90a0ba0d84738|cite]])
- Stern defines AI as machines that can think, see, learn, and act like humans, and Thompson notes the definition treats humans as the baseline. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-830c6ff5e0a7db035800|cite]])
- Stern lands in the middle on AI, neither doomer nor pure optimist, and says a one-sided ending would probably sell more books. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-f1336c2c598d5b579844|cite]])
- Stern says prompts to Claude and ChatGPT for her company's brand design looked generic, so she used a human illustrator instead, arguing great work still needs your own creativity and instincts. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-16ce7919f72c54440ce6|cite]])
- Stern left the Wall Street Journal and launched New Things, a newsletter, YouTube channel, and planned events, run by a three-person team. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-73555de8cdd50bbc997d|cite]])
- Stern partnered with NBC News, where she was already a contributor, to reach a mainstream audience beyond tech enthusiasts; NBC can use her videos, which also run on YouTube and her social platforms. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-c1901bff31cb8a7b12d0|cite]])
- Stern says she wanted a media partner mainly so she could still work within a newsroom around other journalists. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-9d6edafe38f44728e8bd|cite]])
- Stern says fake intimacy from AI companions is her number one fear, because company incentives, as with social media, push tech that tells users what they want and keeps them glued to their phones. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-60ad37548a361c0a5094|cite]])
- Stern tested AI companionship with a ChatGPT boyfriend it named Evan and with Replika, and was shocked how immersed she got even though she was not lonely. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-a81fcc49679bf8eafabe|cite]])
- Stern worries most about kids and teens forming their first relationships with chatbots that always talk back and tell them what they want to hear. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-ab457737fc9185513b95|cite]])
- Stern says seeing Claude Code fully code her personal website was a moment she realized how big AI would be. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-6a6b1d7583d337cc6418|cite]])
- Stern says that in the six months since she stopped writing, AI has gotten better and some of the job impact has already appeared. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-b75c28afddeaa840a478|cite]])
- Stern used ChatGPT, fed with notes from many conversations including her NBC deal, possible hires, and money runway, to help decide to quit the Journal; she says it was the only advisor honest with her. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-b81e225130c5403a3808|cite]])
- Stern joined the Wall Street Journal on New Year's Day 2013, at CES, after starting tech writing in 2008. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-3d809bde6950adc22547|cite]])
- Stern argues it is smart to change careers at the same time as a big technology change, because it lets you reshape what you do. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-13c0a066cc11ca94c9db|cite]])
- Stern's main lesson is to fact check AI: ChatGPT's live video said her son's praying mantis was pregnant, and it died days later. ([[2026-05-07-Interview-Joanna-Stern-Living-With-AI#^clm-903f19c78abd5efc2772|cite]])
