# Barnes & Noble

U.S. bookstore chain; launched the Nook to rival the Kindle.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Retailers like Amazon, Barnes & Noble, and Best Buy log incoming inventory as accounts payable and often hold goods a month or more before paying, while customers pay right away; the gap is the cash conversion cycle. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-9a973ef130ec341145ed|cite]])
- Barnes & Noble reported inventory sitting on shelves sixty-eight days on average before sale, and Best Buy has held inventory as long as seventy days. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-41e6d1456101baf8749e|cite]])
- Roughly 60 percent of books sold at Amazon are titles not available in even the biggest Barnes & Noble superstores, evidence for Chris Anderson's long tail. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-9eaef94564f5b3c48252|cite]])
- Barnes & Noble and other book retailers refused to carry titles from Amazon's publishing arm, a case of channel conflict. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-25e3cafc25ee3395f907|cite]])
- Jeff Bezos has joked that Barnes & Noble took only a year to launch a Kindle rival, while big tech rivals took years to build anything close to AWS. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-2d2e2a66840d07cebc30|cite]])
