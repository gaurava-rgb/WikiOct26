# Michael Morton

Senior research analyst at MoffettNathanson covering e-commerce and marketplaces; formerly on YouTube's strategy team.

## Facts by source

### 2026-06-18 [[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI|An Interview with Michael Morton About E-Commerce in the Age of AI]]

- Morton says the multiples on the e-commerce stocks he covers have fallen 30% year-to-date, because investors fear AI will put their growth and take rates under review. (A multiple is the price investors pay per dollar of earnings; the take rate is the cut a marketplace keeps.) ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-9312f7fd16010e5c7cc5|cite]])
- Morton argues the AI bear case for e-commerce is hard to fight because it is amorphous and cannot be disproven with data today, unlike a margin worry that a good quarter can kill. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-40f4a8b6e6fff36016bf|cite]])
- Morton says ChatGPT's in-chat checkout was deprecated soon after launch. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-e50f43991ebb836b03e0|cite]])
- When Morton asked AI models which stores they prefer, they named price, shipping speed, and trust, and ranked Amazon first and Walmart second. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-1d4b95f11acf01417afa|cite]])
- Morton's data shows that from traditional Google Search, brand.com sites (merchants' own stores, Shopify's base) grow traffic about 2x faster than the big marketplaces. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-0e077e1f66491acee4cc|cite]])
- Morton's data shows that traffic from AI platforms to brand.com sites is growing 4 to 8 times faster than traffic to legacy marketplaces like Amazon, Wayfair, Chewy, eBay, and Etsy. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-fcbe54e74115146cd4a3|cite]])
- Prediction: Morton upgraded Shopify to buy earlier in 2026, believing its GMV (total value of goods sold on the platform) will grow faster for longer than most expect. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-fef98fcb9c83c294e2f1|cite]])
- Morton argues merchants cannot vibe-code (build with AI coding tools) a cheaper copy of Shopify, because Shopify's partner deals, such as better Stripe fees and one-click Meta CAPI integration, give it bargaining power no merchant has. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-1a72d7c526e19f7171e2|cite]])
- Morton says ChatGPT checkout struggled because ChatGPT needs Amazon more than Amazon needs ChatGPT, it never reached critical mass, and shoppers could tell inventory was missing. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-6a989ca01592ef868140|cite]])
- Morton argues UCP is good for incumbent players, because Google, which seems to be winning consumer AI, wants to move its ad business to the new form factor while keeping everyone intact. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-f58ea6ae65e4d53b4766|cite]])
- Morton argues that attempts to make AI a distributor have fallen short, and AI now looks like a referral channel, a better Google Search that sends clearer intent, which should de-risk consumer internet stocks. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-56d0a4bee2c5b68ed6e3|cite]])
- Morton cautions the risk is not gone: hyperscalers are not spending record capex (capital spending on data centers) just to build a better Google Search, and Google and Meta are reportedly building personal agents. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-b585c4253cc9f24c65d3|cite]])
- Morton reports that growth in traffic sent from AI platforms to marketplaces has slowed since November 2025, partly because Amazon blocks most AI discovery platforms and more traffic goes to brand.com. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-692c1118fa6bfadbd822|cite]])
- Morton says app terms of service let DoorDash, Uber, Instacart, and Amazon block AI agents that scrape or order through their apps, as with Amazon's legal fight against Perplexity, though a Meta-scale agent would be harder to block. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-70f74d1ab8f04d152961|cite]])
- Morton notes that in the early 1960s only 30% of US milk consumption came via home delivery, and it fell to zero, as evidence people do not want staples automatically delivered. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-6aea0d29165cab76d34e|cite]])
- Morton estimates that only 1% to 3% of Amazon's 2023 GMV was signed up for Subscribe & Save, backed out from Amazon's claim of a billion dollars in savings. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-883d68b9ad932506b859|cite]])
- Morton argues automatic reordering fails because a household buys about 250 to 300 items a year, and if you do not solve for all of them you solve for nothing. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-d5fc8be6b4caa68009db|cite]])
- Morton is less worried about Amazon's on-site ad risk because AI adoption is slower than expected, and higher intent and conversion are lifting CPCs (cost per click) enough to offset fewer clicks, as Mercado Libre reported. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-07093efaf81674248bc1|cite]])
- Morton argues marketplaces that earn all their profit from on-site ads and own no physical infrastructure are exposed to AI, while Amazon and DoorDash are protected because they also fulfill orders. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-0fc2f385fdc317b90282|cite]])
- Morton estimates Etsy earns over 100% of its EBIT (operating profit) from on-site ads, and warns big Etsy sellers may leave its 25% take rate for Shopify stores found through AI. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-a2072cf96c976563d2b7|cite]])
- Morton says Shopify GMV and Meta revenue have a near-perfect R-squared (a statistic for how closely two numbers move together), but he views the two stocks' weakness as separate. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-b41794bedd8e2ab7d1bd|cite]])
- Amazon rolled out same-day grocery to over 2,000 US cities, and in Morton's basket pricing it jumped from middle of the pack to price parity with Walmart; Amazon says the business grew 40x year-to-date. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-85b498127c5f5565516a|cite]])
- Morton argues full grocery baskets still need a marketplace like DoorDash or Instacart, because the average grocery store carries 40,000 to 50,000 items to cover everyone's long-tail needs. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-c6e28d26f6fbfed373f4|cite]])
- Prediction: Morton predicts Waymo will dominate robotaxis for the next 18 to 24 months, while over 3 to 5 years multiple operators will need demand aggregation such as Uber. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-d5f79dd2da119129213f|cite]])
- Morton says the Uber and Waymo partnership appears to have broken down, and he admits he got that call wrong. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-25a02db36cd314e1ebfe|cite]])
- Morton's model says robotaxis turn EBIT-per-mile positive at low-to-mid 30s trips per car per day, and Waymo's California cars are near high-20s. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-6793558427714fa8a345|cite]])
- Using bill-of-lading shipping records, Morton found Waymo importing about 300 Zeekr-built Ojai vans a month despite 100% tariffs, putting it on pace to double its fleet in 12 months. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-62a076d03ae13db28c23|cite]])
- Morton calls Tesla the largely unknowable risk: if it can put working robotaxis on the road at $30,000 with its production scale, it could be game over for many AV operators. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-487b9210666d149f81f9|cite]])
