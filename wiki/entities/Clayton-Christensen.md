# Clayton Christensen

Late Harvard Business School professor known for disruption theory and the idea of integration versus modularity.

## Facts by source

### 2015-07-08 [[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits|Netflix and the Conservation of Attractive Profits]]

- Christensen's 2003 book The Innovator's Solution states that when modularity and commoditization kill profits at one stage of a value chain, proprietary profits usually emerge at an adjacent stage. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-38f8ae0d1485d7f06638|cite]])
- Thompson argues Christensen explained in 2003, four years before the iPhone, why Intel was doomed in mobile and ARM would rise: a device that is not good enough needs a modular, conformable processor. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-4baf3f2d38da1f85293b|cite]])

### 2026-08-24 [[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation|Autonomy and Innovation]]

- In 2023 Thompson asked in AI and the Big Five whether AI would be a sustaining or disruptive innovation, using Christensen's The Innovator's Dilemma. ([[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation#^clm-2319c50e3fffba359961|cite]])

### 2026-09-21 [[2026-09-21-Frontier-Overhangs|Frontier Overhangs]]

- Thompson argues the Fable episode shows Christensen's theory at work: current models are good enough that customers weigh data policy over pure performance, so pure capability is no longer a moat. ([[2026-09-21-Frontier-Overhangs#^clm-406c6b82d3479c0b0079|cite]])
