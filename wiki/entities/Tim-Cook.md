# Tim Cook

CEO of Apple, succeeded by John Ternus.

## Facts by source

### 2026-04-21 [[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing|Tim Cook’s Impeccable Timing]]

- Apple announced in April 2026 that Tim Cook will step up to Executive Chairman on September 1, 2026, with John Ternus becoming Apple's CEO. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-a816c64354ace11a9d83|cite]])
- Tim Cook became Apple CEO on August 24, 2011; over the 15 years to April 2026 Apple's revenue rose 303%, profit rose 354%, and its value went from $297 billion to $4 trillion, a 1,251% increase. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-d201b561795514e554c8|cite]])
- Steve Jobs died of cancer on October 5, 2011, six weeks after Tim Cook became Apple CEO; Cook had already served as interim CEO while Jobs was treated in 2009. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-4e348b3d2c38daba7791|cite]])
- Thompson argues the timing of Jobs' death made Tim Cook arguably the greatest non-founder CEO ever: founders usually stay past their peak, but Jobs left Apple only a few years after the iPhone, so Cook was the one who grew Apple's most revolutionary device. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-aef418d49a754f515fee|cite]])
- Thompson says that as an intern on Apple University's team in 2010 it was clear to him the program, launched in 2008 and credited publicly to Steve Jobs, was driven by Tim Cook. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-fe46a9fe9f114895ca1f|cite]])
- On Apple's January 2009 earnings call, during Steve Jobs' first leave, Tim Cook said Apple believes it must own and control the primary technologies behind its products and say no to thousands of projects to focus on a few. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-1e3b19c907f7d5a14b6e|cite]])
- Thompson calls the Cook Doctrine brilliant but argues it is ultimately about maintaining, nurturing, and growing what Steve Jobs built, not about creating new things. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-70b4c9046017294e6a6a|cite]])
- After joining Apple in 1998, Tim Cook shut down Apple's own factories and warehouses and moved its manufacturing to China, building a just-in-time supply chain of worldwide suppliers; Thompson says there was no significant product issue or recall under Cook. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-cb493ed725e5657dff84|cite]])
- Thompson argues AirPods and Apple Watch are derivatives of the iPhone, and that Tim Cook's signature new product, the Apple Vision Pro, is only half a new category. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-87b476651018b93d7e13|cite]])
- In a July 28, 2011 email, less than a month before Tim Cook became CEO, Phil Schiller wondered if Apple should lower its 30% App Store cut once it made $1 billion a year in App Store profit; Apple did not. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-04b2bf2003dc28cb546f|cite]])
- Thompson, who has criticized Apple's App Store policies since 2013, argues that from a shareholder perspective it is hard to argue with Tim Cook's uncompromising App Store approach. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-07fb5f50e601501b7255|cite]])
- Thompson argues it is ironic that Tim Cook built Apple's most important technology, making the best personal computers at huge scale, in a way that leaves Apple more exposed than anyone to worsening U.S.-China relations. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-c4d1d672aa09b2b029f1|cite]])
- Thompson argues a common thread in Tim Cook's record, on the App Store, China, and possibly AI, is favoring the financially optimal strategy over Apple's long-term sustainability. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-16c4f4448c53bbd66bd4|cite]])
- Tim Cook is stepping down right after Apple's best-ever quarter (the December 2025 quarter), with the iPhone selling at a record pace and Services still growing. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-10e99fb715dfc1e22986|cite]])
- Apple earned $436 billion in revenue and $118 billion in profit in 2025, which Thompson credits to Tim Cook taking Apple from Jobs' creation to that scale. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-afee2000dd0a2458d153|cite]])
- Thompson argues it is right that Tim Cook steps down now: Apple suffered no post-founder hangover, but only time will tell if Cook set up a crash by forgetting his own Cook Doctrine. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing#^clm-98158030d4dea9dc35f6|cite]])

### 2026-04-22 [[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor|John Ternus and Apple’s Hardware-Defined Future, SpaceXAI and Cursor]]

- Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports that Ternus was more cautious than Tim Cook on the Vision Pro, which flopped, and on the roughly $10 billion car project Apple scrapped, opposing both to varying degrees. ([[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor#^clm-65abac4d891e9c4d2c3d|cite]])
- Thompson argues Tim Cook was the right CEO in 2011 because what Apple needed then was scale, and Cook was the master of scale. ([[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor#^clm-e0997244bd04a7fc8d45|cite]])
