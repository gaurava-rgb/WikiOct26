# Netflix

Video streaming service; an AWS customer that competes with Amazon Prime Video.

## Facts by source

### 2015-07-08 [[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits|Netflix and the Conservation of Attractive Profits]]

- In 2013 Thompson called Netflix just another network, HBO with a unique delivery system, and in 2015 he says he got this wrong in a big way. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-60441fe4122f7f4b59b4|cite]])
- In 2013 Thompson argued Netflix had pivoted from content delivery to being simply another network that funds new shows with reruns and old movies as filler. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-616565b207dedf550281|cite]])
- Prediction: A 2015 FBR Capital Markets analysis reported by Variety projected that Netflix's U.S. 24-hour audience would surpass each of ABC, CBS, Fox and NBC by 2016. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-0de2e55e8f40063cffe0|cite]])
- Netflix does not care about ratings of individual shows because it does not sell ads, and it discloses only general viewing data. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-ca61ace03c344a09413b|cite]])
- Thompson argues on-demand streaming, and Netflix in particular, commoditized time: there is no prime time, so Sunday at 9pm is no different from Tuesday at 11am. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-556764c67e1247076908|cite]])
- Netflix releases original series all at once to spread an expensive up-front production cost over as many viewing minutes as possible. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-f3931fb35adc3fef56dc|cite]])
- Thompson doubts binge release is optimal for any single show, suspecting appointment viewing creates valuable buzz, but says it signals Netflix is committed to connecting creators and viewers efficiently. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-6662739abc86de08a0ec|cite]])
- Thompson argues Netflix is less a network than a marketplace where consumers give their attention to creators, putting it in the same class as Uber and Airbnb. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-f275fc27699a4d497961|cite]])
- Thompson lists three traits shared by Netflix and other market-makers, working in order: an infinitely scalable network, a commoditized former constraint, and positioning as chief beneficiary of the industry's transformation. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-a2f4a6722fd310910b94|cite]])
- Thompson argues that commoditizing and modularizing an integrated system destroys incumbent value while letting a new entrant integrate a different part of the value chain and capture new value. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-316a2b6ee181ec8060f5|cite]])
- Thompson argues Netflix commoditized time and distribution and so integrated production with customer management (subscriptions and distribution), modularizing content production. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-ded78f0f831759db16e1|cite]])
- Thompson argues Airbnb, Uber and Netflix are all specialized customer-relationship companies that modularized production or delivery to move closer to the customer: Airbnb for travelers, Uber for commuters, Netflix for the bored. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-d14b7032921e3aa8b4a0|cite]])
- Thompson limits his reversal: in the TV value chain highly differentiated content creators keep the most power, unlike Airbnb hosts and Uber drivers. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-61e59f3a72eb1674ad82|cite]])
- Netflix faces strong competition for attention from traditional pay-TV, Amazon Prime, a revitalized Hulu, and especially HBO. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-3c80aed15bd8cc78adcf|cite]])
- HBO had more than 100 projects in development, and Netflix and Amazon lured talent frustrated by the queue; that speed reportedly won Netflix Bloodline and The OA. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-e15d8956e4052126ba48|cite]])
- Thompson argues a new entrant like Netflix is free to integrate around the customer while staying modular in content creation, and the most promising companies make the biggest breaks with the past. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-81ae40c007f25ea51707|cite]])

### 2026-07-21 [[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes|Netflix Earnings, Is Netflix Washed?, Additional Notes]]

- Netflix reported second-quarter 2026 revenue of $12.56 billion, up more than 13% year over year, with net income up almost 9% to $3.4 billion, roughly in line with analyst expectations. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-faf33e323ba282c2b81d|cite]])
- Netflix forecast third-quarter 2026 revenue growth of 11.7%, its smallest year-on-year increase since late 2023, and shares fell more than 8% after hours. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-99bafbfb26de25caf351|cite]])
- Netflix attributed second-quarter gains partly to recent price increases and growth in advertising. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-50804d5a34913a0f0069|cite]])
- Before the results, Netflix stock had already fallen more than 40% over the prior year, a slide that began when it explored bidding for Warner Bros. Discovery's studio and streaming service and continued after it lost the deal. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-c87457a63b5db7ba1dee|cite]])
- Viewers watched more than 97 billion hours on Netflix in the first half of 2026, up 1.5% from the second half of 2025 and 1.9% from the first half of 2025. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-eb756a83ee6698d050e7|cite]])
- Research firm Antenna puts Netflix's June 2026 churn at 2.11%, by far the best in the streaming industry. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-f94d38508caeeca48e2c|cite]])
- Netflix will publish its What We Watched engagement report once a year instead of twice. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-664ff997257c4e870b5d|cite]])
- Eric Seufert argues the engagement report cut continues a pattern of withheld metrics: forward subscriber guidance ended in 2023, and total subscriber numbers and regional revenue per member ended with Q1 2025. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-ff3111741658504d7e28|cite]])
- Seufert argues that if total view hours are a poor measure of engagement, Netflix should release more engagement data, not the same data less often. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-ab367e97a06124fce96c|cite]])
- Greg Peters says view hours do not map linearly to revenue: live events will be about 5% of Netflix's 2026 content budget but only about 1% of view hours, while kids and family animation is also about 5% of spend but about 8% of view hours. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-1d71bbe16d702073f920|cite]])
- Peters says 6 of Netflix's top 10 new member sign-up days over the past five years came from live events. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-875d83bdc1112ed75b4d|cite]])
- In an earlier 2026 Stratechery interview, Peters explained Netflix dropped subscriber counts because extra members, widely varying revenue per member, and advertising made the subscriber number less complete, and Netflix wants to be judged on revenue and profit. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-70bfa1b36de6e7596c42|cite]])
- Thompson changes his view: he had mostly given Netflix the benefit of the doubt on reduced disclosures, but now sees a propensity to hide metrics as soon as they are about to turn south. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-fff00f1b4fb8babc5813|cite]])
- Thompson argues Peters' own example undercuts him: advertising is very much a function of engagement, yet Netflix is reducing the only real engagement numbers it reports. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-22d3f4ec9b14674f33d5|cite]])
- Andrew Sharp argues Netflix is washed: not doomed, but almost certainly peaked as a cultural force, a business success story, and a Hollywood death star, with content that has never been worse. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-2f8c08c5cd166d481135|cite]])
- Sharp notes Netflix content reaches 85% of American viewers and Netflix has 325 million subscribers globally. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-0554b5da02c66dbc896d|cite]])
- Netflix lowered its third-quarter outlook below expectations but reaffirmed full-year guidance, implying a stronger fourth quarter. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-7d0518f0f56747599526|cite]])
- Excluding the Warner Bros. breakup fee, Netflix is set to generate $10.25 billion of cash flow in 2026, and it bought back $4.7 billion of shares in the quarter. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-6a1663e0081d1a239c29|cite]])
- Thompson argues Netflix is now pretty boring, and in retrospect the failed Warner Bros. acquisition feels like an inflection point. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-50fff21230ca16331d9b|cite]])
- Thompson revisits his earlier read that the Warner Bros. bid was a response to YouTube taking over the TV; after Sharp's piece he compares it to a last-ditch adventure before growing up. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-e41fecc170337b4a9d0c|cite]])
- Ted Sarandos frames Netflix's expansion from one prestige drama in 2013 to unscripted, local language, comedy, and competition shows as evolutionary, not revolutionary. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-b1365b9b3e9f298cb6b5|cite]])
- Netflix did not rule out a free tier eventually but said it is not coming soon because of cannibalization concerns, and that a thriving ad business is a prerequisite. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-d3be33c6d736b6794ab4|cite]])
- Netflix views its podcast initiative as a success because it has driven more viewing during work hours and on mobile. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-3a369d15c1c9043fc340|cite]])
- Thompson argues cloud-based TV games make far more sense for Netflix than app-based games; Seufert notes its Game Controller app regularly ranks in the App Store top ten. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-ccd147a461f0d420218a|cite]])
- Netflix management did not dismiss partnering with other streamers or acting as a storefront for them. ([[2026-07-21-Netflix-Earnings-Is-Netflix-Washed-Additional-Notes#^clm-e0ffa78403879c05daf9|cite]])

### 2026-08-25 [[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku|Netflix to Sell Streaming Services?, Streamers as Aggregators, Revisiting Roku]]

- Netflix co-CEO Greg Peters, asked on the Q2 2026 earnings call about distributing third-party streaming services, did not rule it out and said Netflix would consider more deals that serve members, partners, and Netflix. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-89abadf6df22da654ed5|cite]])
- Netflix integrated French broadcaster TF1 into its service in France in June 2026, its first such move, and Peters called early member engagement very promising four weeks in. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-f9bcbec715f2f7c65291|cite]])
- Thompson argues Peters is signaling a possibly massive shift toward selling other streaming services while framing it, in classic Netflix style, as something the company has always done. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-6873f118736c02ff372e|cite]])
- Netflix has discussed bringing Peacock and Fox One onto its service with NBCUniversal and Fox, per the New York Times, though no deal is imminent and it is unclear whether it would absorb the content or act as a retailer. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-d7f440f7448ff254a0c7|cite]])
- Netflix reversed its stance: two years earlier it told shareholders it already operates as a go-to destination for entertainment, and its executives long balked at working with direct competitors. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-d05c26cf5f19d1dc7f95|cite]])
- Thompson drops his earlier Netflix end-game: that as the last big streamer standing it would buy content on its own terms. After Hollywood and Washington pushed back on the Warner Bros. bid, he calls distributing rival services for a cut a very rational pivot. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-d4a30229689b6de42169|cite]])
- Thompson says the attempted Warner Bros. acquisition in late 2025 felt like Netflix trying to force his foreseen end-game into reality, and it met pushback from Hollywood and Washington. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-421524787245032388f0|cite]])
- Thompson argues studios such as Peacock and Fox run money-losing streaming services whose hardest problem is customer acquisition, which Netflix can supply on its own terms. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-2aaf9b2dfcaadda52ae2|cite]])
- Thompson recommends Netflix follow the Amazon model: sell access to other services, own the customer relationship, and surface their content in the Netflix app. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-b12c0ccaefd6985c5087|cite]])
- Thompson argues the old fight for HDMI 1, the main TV input, has become a fight to be the default app people open first on their smart TVs. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-79d8cdc19d40c62487c1|cite]])
- Roku began as an internal Netflix project, and Thompson argued in 2017 that spinning it out was smart because mixing horizontal and vertical business models is dangerous and Netflix needed to be on every device. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-2714ed6482aaf4248149|cite]])
- Thompson revisits his Roku view: 2026 bears no resemblance to 2007, Netflix is everywhere, and the question now is whether it has saturated its addressable market. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-93bdf091ff91258b1426|cite]])
- Thompson argues Netflix has not saturated its market because it does not compete in free streaming, the fastest-growing area, which has synergy with pay TV as an extra window, marketing, and ad inventory. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-d52227919611c4f05214|cite]])
- Thompson changes course on Netflix and Roku: while still backing Fox's purchase of Roku, he now says Netflix missed its pivot and should have answered the Warner Bros. letdown by moving into free ad-supported streaming and buying Roku. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-822abce7f883c828c154|cite]])
- Thompson judges that the Roku opportunity has passed, and that sharing Netflix's distribution with other streamers for a fee is the next best move. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-27b60d5df1f169ce6d6b|cite]])
