### 2026-05-11 [[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift|The Inference Shift]]

- Thompson argues the surge in semiconductor stocks in May 2026 is driven by AI, and especially by the realization that agents will need a lot of compute. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-8047fb0392f4f0db1ca5|cite]])
- Thompson explains that inference decode is serial and limited by memory bandwidth: for every token generated, both the KV cache (the stored context) and the model weights must be read in full. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-a3ff8cc4c714c3f1af74|cite]])
- Thompson coins the split between answer inference (providing an answer) and agentic inference (doing a task), and says Cerebras targets answer inference while agentic inference will need an architecture unlike both Cerebras and GPUs. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-e7b3dba4d8c3a8965bd2|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson argues the true power of agents is not doing work for humans but doing work without any human involvement, so coding's need for a human in the loop is temporary. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-fae4622fde800995ef21|cite]])
- Thompson argues token speed matters most for answer inference, but memory matters most for agentic inference, since agents need context, state, and history held in KV cache, host memory, SSDs, databases, and object stores. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-3d24ff2bf45f6bc73d68|cite]])
- Thompson argues that with no human in the loop, delays are fine, so slower and cheaper memory such as traditional DRAM makes more sense than HBM, and chips need not be cutting edge. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-3114f56fe6122785b3c0|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson predicts agentic inference will be the largest market by far, because it is not limited by humans or time, and argues today's agents are just fancy answer inference. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-05cf088c008cfb451b3e|cite]])
- Thompson argues that scaling with compute has implicitly meant Nvidia bullishness, but Nvidia's edge comes largely from latency, so if latency is not the key constraint its approach seems less worth a premium. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-b213042c1a5b496169c1|cite]])
- Thompson argues China, despite lacking leading-edge compute, has everything agentic inference needs: good-enough GPUs and CPUs, DRAM, and hard drives; its gap is compute for training. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-ab7cb029d2f7cb3af2ac|cite]])
- Thompson argues slower chips make space data centers more viable: offloaded memory lets chips run simpler and cooler, older nodes resist radiation better, use less power, and are more reliable for satellites that cannot be repaired. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-ca5653e80fa9a630e15f|cite]])

### 2026-06-02 [[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company|The Google Capital Company]]

### 2026-07-20 [[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models|Who’s Afraid of Chinese Models?]]

- Thompson argues open weight models are only free in R&D terms; serving them still costs money, and AI inference is cost of goods sold (COGS) that rises with revenue, unlike most software. ([[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models#^clm-b0b54085b33c05a57b71|cite]])
