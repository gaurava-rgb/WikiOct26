# Prime Video

Amazon's streaming video service, bundled with Prime.

## Facts by source

### 2015-07-08 [[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits|Netflix and the Conservation of Attractive Profits]]

- Netflix faces strong competition for attention from traditional pay-TV, Amazon Prime, a revitalized Hulu, and especially HBO. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-3c80aed15bd8cc78adcf|cite]])
- HBO had more than 100 projects in development, and Netflix and Amazon lured talent frustrated by the queue; that speed reportedly won Netflix Bloodline and The OA. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-e15d8956e4052126ba48|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Gallaugher argues owning the TV matters because it gathers viewing data for profiling and targeted ads, and for Amazon the goal may be less winning TV than making Prime Video available everywhere. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-00a687716692029bebd4|cite]])
- Amazon pays $1 billion a year in a ten-year deal for exclusive NFL Thursday Night Football rights, bundled with Prime and free on Twitch. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-772c87c0a30659ac760f|cite]])
- Amazon paid over a quarter billion dollars for rights to a Lord of the Rings prequel and about three-quarters of a billion to make its first season, the most expensive series ever, yet only a little over a third of viewers finished it. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-a230653e2f9dad081c1d|cite]])
- Amazon's total bill for Prime content was $16.6 billion in 2022, and its push into ads on Prime Video and the free Freevee channel suggests it needs more cash to justify the spend. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-2ff68a32cd0ca92365e7|cite]])

### 2026-08-25 [[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku|Netflix to Sell Streaming Services?, Streamers as Aggregators, Revisiting Roku]]

- Amazon has for years persuaded tens of millions of people to subscribe to services like HBO Max and Apple TV through its Prime Video app. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-9743205aba80ce69469d|cite]])
- Thompson says it took him a long time to understand Amazon's model of selling third-party streaming services, which he was still describing tentatively as platform or aggregator in 2018. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-39a854bbfb5352958c0b|cite]])
- Thompson argues Prime Video Channels works because Amazon was its own first customer for video content, so Prime members already had the app installed and Amazon owned the customer relationship for third-party providers. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-b8f83714a8fc08077528|cite]])
- Thompson recommends Netflix follow the Amazon model: sell access to other services, own the customer relationship, and surface their content in the Netflix app. ([[2026-08-25-Netflix-to-Sell-Streaming-Services-Streamers-as-Aggregators-Revisiting-Roku#^clm-b12c0ccaefd6985c5087|cite]])
