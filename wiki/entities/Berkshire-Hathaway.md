# Berkshire Hathaway

Warren Buffett's holding company; invested $10 billion in Alphabet equity in June 2026.

## Facts by source

### 2026-06-02 [[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company|The Google Capital Company]]

- Warren Buffett said at the 2017 Berkshire Hathaway annual meeting that GEICO had paid Google about $10 or $11 per click and that you have almost never seen a business like it, yet Berkshire never invested in Google. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-e530baf078a265b4d8f8|cite]])
- Berkshire Hathaway bought See's Candies in 1972 for $25 million, when sales were $30 million and pre-tax earnings under $5 million; by 2007 sales were $383 million and pre-tax profit $82 million, with only $32 million reinvested since 1972. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-b85c0e45d8aecf44ce38|cite]])
- BNSF Railway, bought by Berkshire Hathaway partly with See's Candies profits, consumed $3.8 billion of capital in 2025 and earned net income of $5.5 billion on revenue of $23.4 billion. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-ca6f81f39e4c3511bf3e|cite]])
- Thompson estimates Berkshire Hathaway's total earnings from See's Candies are probably under $3 billion (the last disclosure, in 2019, was over $2 billion), less than capital-hungry BNSF Railway earned in one year. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-a2a62bfee43d1d85ca35|cite]])
- On June 1, 2026 Alphabet said it would raise $80 billion in equity: a $40 billion at-the-market share program starting in Q3, $30 billion of underwritten shares and mandatory convertible preferred stock, and a $10 billion deal with Berkshire Hathaway. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-4965a19ddf180c9f3553|cite]])
- Berkshire Hathaway invested in Google at only a slight discount to its all-time high, now that Warren Buffett no longer makes investment decisions and Greg Abel is his successor as CEO. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-151de28e0270cc2b3d0b|cite]])
- Berkshire Hathaway had $373 billion in cash at the end of Q1 2026 and generated $25 billion of free cash flow in 2025. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-3e0f9df826258012a3f5|cite]])
- Thompson argues Greg Abel is replaying Buffett's strategy, with Berkshire Hathaway now playing See's Candies (the cash machine) and Google playing BNSF Railway (the capital-hungry business that earns large absolute profits). ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-e83ea5215438a04a2692|cite]])
- Thompson argues the $10 billion Berkshire deal is small for both companies and is mainly a signal: Google expects demand far greater than anyone thinks and will fund supply by any means, including equity, and Berkshire's money endorses that view. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-740079675b73379fc161|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson asks what happens if there is not enough compute in the world to buy: the battle for compute may become who can bring the most cash to bear, compounding into more compute and more cash, and Berkshire Hathaway is betting that company is Google. ([[2026-06-02-The-Google-Capital-Company#^clm-bc216972430f231ad37b|cite]])
