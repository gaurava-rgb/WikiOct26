# Target

U.S. discount retailer.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): S17.7 Walmart - Data Asset in Action and the Rise of Walmart]]

- Walmart joined over a dozen retailers, including Best Buy, Target, Sears, and 7-Eleven, to create MCX, a pay-by-phone standard with coupons and loyalty programs. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-8f6f3483f001ddf2f63f|cite]])
- Walmart's data warehouse could not foresee the rise of Target and other up-market discounters. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-17c9bc27bab28eb50b90|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that historical data offers little help in adapting to radical changes in retail, so data is a vital tool for managers but not the only one, as Walmart's miss on Target shows. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-13482e342484d2624b6d|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Walmart warehouses ship big pallets of identical items to thousands of stores, while Amazon warehouses pack boxes of mixed single items for millions of homes, a difference that made it hard for traditional retailers to copy Amazon's model. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-c1850bd8983dcd240acf|cite]])
- Walmart and Target once sold the Kindle but stopped in 2012, fearing it would pull physical sales to Amazon. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-99851c7a3d28d6856d47|cite]])
- Target once relied on Amazon to power its website, left to build its own infrastructure, and three weeks later an unexpectedly popular promotion crashed its site. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-cb7b2168e70d11a0b104|cite]])
