# DoorDash

US food and goods delivery app.

## Facts by source

### 2026-05-14 [[2026-05-14-Interview-Ben-Thompson-MoffettNathanson|An Interview with Ben Thompson at the MoffettNathanson Media, Internet & Communications Conference]]

- Prediction: Thompson predicts physical players like DoorDash benefit from new tech like drones, and that Google Wing will likely work for DoorDash just as Google Cloud works for Anthropic. ([[2026-05-14-Interview-Ben-Thompson-MoffettNathanson#^clm-98e43fd5be11b59fc5f1|cite]])

### 2026-06-18 [[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI|An Interview with Michael Morton About E-Commerce in the Age of AI]]

- Morton says app terms of service let DoorDash, Uber, Instacart, and Amazon block AI agents that scrape or order through their apps, as with Amazon's legal fight against Perplexity, though a Meta-scale agent would be harder to block. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-70f74d1ab8f04d152961|cite]])
- Morton argues marketplaces that earn all their profit from on-site ads and own no physical infrastructure are exposed to AI, while Amazon and DoorDash are protected because they also fulfill orders. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-0fc2f385fdc317b90282|cite]])
- Morton argues full grocery baskets still need a marketplace like DoorDash or Instacart, because the average grocery store carries 40,000 to 50,000 items to cover everyone's long-tail needs. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-c6e28d26f6fbfed373f4|cite]])
- Morton says DoorDash took two-thirds of the US restaurant delivery market by starting in the suburbs, and its grocery business is growing 30% to 40%. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-531cf27b043cf0195a86|cite]])
- Morton says DoorDash has built over 100 DashMarts and a DashLink package service, in effect its own version of Fulfillment by Amazon, as local inventory grows more important. ([[2026-06-18-Interview-Michael-Morton-E-Commerce-AI#^clm-e83f755474e1cb4a0a1a|cite]])

### 2026-10-06 [[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz|Uber's M&A Blitz]]

- A few weeks earlier, DoorDash bought a college campus meal-delivery business from Wonder for $300 million. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-d97bbdab090d2d243a10|cite]])
- Peers argues neither deal will excite investors, but both show Uber and DoorDash doing whatever they can to keep growth going. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-26d27229dddd72d1748a|cite]])
- DoorDash has bought Deliveroo and Wolt in Europe, and Uber is buying Delivery Hero, but only a handful of such targets exist. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-cf55930d4ee101e3a802|cite]])
- Uber and DoorDash dominate restaurant delivery and increasingly groceries, and growing by adding new subscribers keeps getting harder. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-6c1a31b2294c0d2370d0|cite]])
- DoorDash bookings growth slowed to 19% at the end of 2024 from 36% at the end of 2021, then sped back up to 36% over the past 18 months, much of it from Deliveroo. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-b150397aadcfedb1c705|cite]])
- Peers reads both companies as throwing off lots of cash, so when a deal looks promising they just go for it. ([[2026-10-06-Ubers-MA-Blitz#^clm-bf8e6c960152d14a43d5|cite]])

### 2026-10-14 [[2026-10-14-Lennys-Newsletter-Metric-Benchmarking|Metric Benchmarking]]

- Online marketplace benchmarks define a gross margin of 70% as good and 80% or higher as great, while heavily managed marketplaces like DoorDash operate with lower margins. ([[2026-10-14-Lennys-Newsletter-Metric-Benchmarking#^clm-6825c42b75e3d2a422fd|cite]])
