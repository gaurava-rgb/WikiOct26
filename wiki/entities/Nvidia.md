### 2026-05-11 [[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift|The Inference Shift]]

- Thompson argues the surge in semiconductor stocks in May 2026 is driven by AI, and especially by the realization that agents will need a lot of compute. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-8047fb0392f4f0db1ca5|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson argues that while the AI compute story has been mostly about Nvidia GPUs, the future will look increasingly heterogeneous, with different chips for different jobs. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-393f3b125092dc1ee893|cite]])
- Thompson explains that Nvidia leads AI training because it secured high-bandwidth memory (HBM) ahead of rivals and invested in chip-to-chip networking, both needed because every GPU must share results before the next training step. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-c0420d16b846c9f5b195|cite]])
- Anthropic agreed in May 2026 to use all compute at SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center, over 300 megawatts and more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs, to raise capacity for Claude Pro and Max subscribers. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-ecf330fefff606b208d4|cite]])
- Thompson argues SpaceX can run both training and inference at Colossus 2 because xAI's models get little usage and because GPUs can do both jobs; the Colossus 1 GPUs Anthropic rents were first used for training, showing GPU flexibility is a big advantage. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-d22c5195160bdd9f365b|cite]])
- Cerebras lays wiring across the scribe lines between reticle exposures, turning a whole 300mm wafer into one chip with no slow chip-to-chip links, while Nvidia's B200 links two separate chips. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-eed97a821606823662fc|cite]])
- As of May 2026, Cerebras' WSE-3 has 44GB of on-chip SRAM at 21 PB/s of bandwidth, versus 80GB of HBM at 3.35 TB/s on Nvidia's H100: just over half the memory but 6,000 times the bandwidth. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-5326f7922809465cc3d2|cite]])
- Thompson coins the split between answer inference (providing an answer) and agentic inference (doing a task), and says Cerebras targets answer inference while agentic inference will need an architecture unlike both Cerebras and GPUs. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-e7b3dba4d8c3a8965bd2|cite]])
- Thompson argues that with no human in the loop, delays are fine, so slower and cheaper memory such as traditional DRAM makes more sense than HBM, and chips need not be cutting edge. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-3114f56fe6122785b3c0|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson predicts Nvidia's architecture will likely keep dominating training, and that answer inference will be a meaningful but relatively small market where fast chips like Cerebras or Groq are very useful. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-895cfc39d2ecf3238c9f|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson predicts agentic inference will gradually unbundle the GPU, which strands HBM during prefill and compute during decode, in favor of high-capacity, lower-cost memory hierarchies with good-enough compute, where CPU speed for tool use matters more than GPU speed. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-ff02535ae1e20260cd69|cite]])
- Thompson argues that scaling with compute has implicitly meant Nvidia bullishness, but Nvidia's edge comes largely from latency, so if latency is not the key constraint its approach seems less worth a premium. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-b213042c1a5b496169c1|cite]])
- Nvidia recognizes the shift: it launched Dynamo, an inference framework that splits up parts of inference, and ships standalone memory and CPU racks for larger KV caches and faster tool use, though Thompson expects hyperscalers to favor cost and simplicity. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-7fd1c661cf9d29d13017|cite]])
- Jensen Huang regularly says Moore's Law is dead, meaning speed-ups will come from systems innovation; Thompson argues agents without humans may make Moore's Law not matter, because the compute we have is already good enough. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift#^clm-d1c705e81458519544a6|cite]])
