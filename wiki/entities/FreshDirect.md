# FreshDirect

New York City online grocer that delivers from a single tech-heavy warehouse; owned by Ahold Delhaize then Getir.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch04 FreshDirect - A Tech-Heavy Online Grocer]]

- FreshDirect is a New York City online grocer whose co-founder David McInerney, a former chef who worked in Michelin Star restaurants, served as CEO during much of its growth. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-331b3e78a45991d2cbde|cite]])
- David McInerney's main goal in launching FreshDirect was to close the quality gap between store-bought produce and the food restaurants used. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-5d4cdfc171a146246940|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect solved the two biggest problems of New York grocery shoppers, limited selection and high prices, both caused by the city's high real estate costs. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-544f7b7143bc6a7867ad|cite]])
- FreshDirect grew to hold over 60 percent of the New York City grocery market while operating from just a single facility. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-0cbfe0e4111a4ba427b5|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues early online grocers like Webvan failed from a lack of product-market fit: suburban customers liked driving to the store on their own schedule and would not trust someone else to pick their produce. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-b3c25cf23a62059002cd|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect found product-market fit because New Yorkers were time-strapped, often lacked cars, and lived in walk-up apartments where carrying heavy grocery bags home was a burden. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-fa943f12fef393ce4342|cite]])
- FreshDirect's selection is several times larger than a conventional store's, and its website and app use collaborative filtering software to give personalized recommendations. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-0699bb5ab3a43e72d58b|cite]])
- FreshDirect pushes higher-margin semi-prepared foods such as "meals in four minutes," which appeal to New Yorkers with very small kitchens. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-2af7ef17019065dd5191|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that once a FreshDirect customer has past orders stored, re-creating them at a rival site is a switching cost that deters trying competitors. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-b8bc769e2171bb71b7ec|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect builds a data asset traditional grocers cannot match by tying each shopper's clickstream to an account profile used for recommendations, marketing, experiments, and machine learning. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-5eb8272157cb00dc2780|cite]])
- FreshDirect uses the third-party customer profiling package Selligent, adopted in 2020, a tool that rivals can also buy. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-84e40b7aa267798e6e4f|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that even though rivals can buy the same software as FreshDirect, its customer data collected over time is the barrier a new rival must overcome. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-d7e5a47b62f680b953f6|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect's advantages strengthen with time: more time yields more data, which yields stronger competitive advantage. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-429525fdd4fc2ab75ded|cite]])
- FreshDirect knows immediately when an item is nearing a stockout and can reorder, while a traditional grocer does not know when a shopper failed to find an item. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-7ceb88ec711828f0abc4|cite]])
- FreshDirect gives shoppers freshness ratings and guidance on uses, such as which apples are best for baking, information a produce aisle cannot provide. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-81bda10a4b69724e1474|cite]])
- By 2015 FreshDirect was so popular that New York apartment buildings began redesigning common areas with secure freezers to accept its deliveries when residents were out. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-c35dcf5b5d18178cb884|cite]])
- After sixteen years delivering from Queens, FreshDirect moved to a new South Bronx facility about eleven football fields in size, internally called the "Food Hive," that serves the New York suburbs, Connecticut, and New Jersey. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-2a5acfa0a1064a95ae01|cite]])
- FreshDirect's steady, predictable worker shifts avoid storefront lulls and rush spikes, giving it labor costs 60 percent lower than traditional grocers. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-75da8df0a2002e70bb2d|cite]])
- Traditional grocers often throw out one in seven truckloads of food delivered to stores, while FreshDirect's food waste is only about 1 percent because it buys and prepares what it sells. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-4a105d011f41abe3dda8|cite]])
- FreshDirect claimed in 2009 that its low food waste is "worth 5 percentage points of total revenue in terms of savings." ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-1047ad13315615347fad|cite]])
- FreshDirect's inventory turns 197 times a year versus 40 times a year at traditional grocers, so it sells goods faster and collects cash sooner than rivals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-0e75d7632e77cafaac62|cite]])
- The average grocer holds seven to nine days of seafood inventory, while FreshDirect's seafood stock turns every day, bought from the docks to fill orders placed less than 24 hours earlier. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-1fa47208273626eeb11b|cite]])
- FreshDirect keeps products in thirty-eight storage rooms with tuned temperature and humidity and ships in refrigerated trucks; executives estimate this adds about seven days of shelf life over a typical grocery product. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-893578a737fecad1557a|cite]])
- FreshDirect teams inspect and taste 400 to 500 items every day to set the one-to-five freshness ratings shown on its website. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-d6e37ca172639b3c44e6|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect's business is far harder than Amazon's non-food e-commerce because perishable food is easily damaged and a botched delivery sends hungry customers elsewhere. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-c3e32256139b13eaae08|cite]])
- FreshDirect's warehouse links staff and food through AI software, over nine miles of conveyor belts, and over seven miles of fiber-optic cable, verifying orders with 99.9 percent accuracy. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-1873e3ca60c4b246ffa8|cite]])
- In FreshDirect's warehouse, barcoded bins travel by conveyor to worker stations, each item is scanned into the bin, and operations data has been used to improve layout so items often packed together sit at one station. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-33f85fa9601724791fd8|cite]])
- FreshDirect's Bronx system cut packing time by 75 percent and lets an order be fulfilled in under thirty minutes. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-75208814955b3c67a65a|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect has a huge efficiency advantage over Instacart, which may send a human shopper to a store and deliver in a non-refrigerated car. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-7b566aeda82ff913eed5|cite]])
- FreshDirect saves energy because its climate-controlled warehouses avoid the open store refrigerators that fight a store's customer HVAC system. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-e360dd1d853d01e33536|cite]])
- As FreshDirect grew, each truck run served more customers in a smaller area; it now parks large trucks near customers with drivers delivering on foot, needing one-seventh the comparable fleet size. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-a636f93a74e1009fa9f5|cite]])
- FreshDirect's foot-delivery model cut traffic tickets, which had been a multi-hundred-thousand-dollar problem for the company. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-9622fb5fef765c7c24df|cite]])
- One 2013 study suggests delivery trucks produce 20 to 70 percent less emissions than shoppers' trips in personal vehicles. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-c48beec4dc6e160c5067|cite]])
- FreshDirect's three-tier model, "from farm or sea to our house to your house," cuts out three to four wholesaling and warehousing steps used by traditional rivals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-5f16da384745165b8575|cite]])
- FreshDirect buys directly from suppliers but builds partnerships instead of squeezing them, even co-developing specialty products such as staggered cantaloupe plantings and large sorted blueberries. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-3d4974c5a8f5632c30d9|cite]])
- In exchange for better terms, FreshDirect offers suppliers more shelf selection with no slotting fees, cobranding, payment in days rather than weeks, and shared data. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-709c4b80051408a1af98|cite]])
- FreshDirect's combined advantages let it undercut competitors' prices by as much as 35 percent. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-31f1c3590ff9087bd1b5|cite]])
- FreshDirect earns margins around 20 percent, up to 45 percent on many semi-prepared meals, versus razor-thin 1 percent margins at traditional grocers. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-94bb701ef82b328eae45|cite]])
- FreshDirect's customer base grew to over 600,000, roughly the population of metro Boston, served with no physical store. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-cf732e5b50c0400cfa75|cite]])
- Privately held FreshDirect has been consistently profitable since 2010, except for problems from the botched Bronx move, and raised capital for expansion, including $189 million in 2016. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-ac3bd696d5914f00042c|cite]])
- FreshDirect's move from Queens to the Bronx caused order mishaps, angry customers, brand damage, financial loss, and falling market share. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-10bef1223d3d074f6eb0|cite]])
- FreshDirect turned on the Bronx system without enough testing and switched 100 percent of orders from the old system to the new one at once. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-3c4184550eb9c0d19e5b|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues a phased rollout that ramped up orders and kept a fallback to the old reliable systems could have let FreshDirect catch and fix the Bronx problems. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-8e0fbe7903ac77cb7052|cite]])
- After the botched Bronx move, customers complained of broken eggs, spoiled fruit, and mispackaged or missed orders. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-9e2f6b22b7feb68aa7a8|cite]])
- After the Bronx move, FreshDirect's share of the New York grocery market fell from 66 percent to less than 46 percent. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-3f52993bf0b66ecf1bf6|cite]])
- During FreshDirect's Bronx troubles, Amazon's New York grocery market share rose from 8 percent to 22 percent and Instacart's delivery also grew. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-1ebe9895d39d7993e8b3|cite]])
- After the Bronx failure, FreshDirect's prior CEO stepped down and co-founder David McInerney became CEO to steer the transition. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-03a46f44b9997124d30d|cite]])
- FreshDirect's systems were an unwieldy mix of separately developed products, and a Splunk tool gathering log data into one central repository helped it find and fix problems across them. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-e5cc356240316a75e53a|cite]])
- To win back trust, FreshDirect launched a "Direct to David" line to the CEO and sent drivers, sometimes a worker in an Uber, to fix a flubbed order immediately instead of giving a credit. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-e6a216ff6771e80c168e|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect's costly error handling loses money short term but is far less damaging than losing a lifetime of orders from a churned customer who tells others a horror story. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-153662dbb8408670924d|cite]])
- When COVID-19 hit New York in 2020, FreshDirect's web traffic rose 800 percent and suburban orders doubled overnight. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-879664928a755fcdd80f|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues COVID-19 arrived after FreshDirect had fixed its Bronx problems, helping it regain lost customers and grow faster than it could keep up with. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-f018bb9adce253cac4d5|cite]])
- In March 2020 New York City deemed FreshDirect an "essential service" and its staff frontline workers. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-67b53af83359735dbd9f|cite]])
- During COVID-19 FreshDirect limited the variety of meat cuts so its butchers could keep up with faster order volume. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-e98123e691e26cf619ed|cite]])
- During the pandemic, an order that might require two Instacart shoppers inside a store could still be filled as a single FreshDirect delivery. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-985f6cc002826e12ed79|cite]])
- Through "Operation 5-Borough Food Drive" in 2020, FreshDirect took a $9 million contract with New York borough presidents to deliver 4.3 million pounds of food, creating forty jobs. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-5917480fac514ad0afb2|cite]])
- In 2020 FreshDirect partnered with Restaurant Associates to hire laid-off food service workers to cook ready meals, donating a second meal to city food pantries for each one bought. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-88d46a9285925c3abb40|cite]])
- In 2021 the Bronx Borough President honored FreshDirect employees for courage and service during the pandemic. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-55ec23669b3d0ebe3e19|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues the biggest threat to FreshDirect is better-funded rivals whose profitable non-grocery businesses can fund a costly fight for its customers. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-8a4e577d106902be63b8|cite]])
- Amazon has pushed into the $700 billion grocery retail market, buying Whole Foods and a large northern New Jersey distribution center formerly owned by PathMark. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-8a14a944ee5dea6fe736|cite]])
- Estimates suggest Amazon's former PathMark distribution center could move ten times the dollar volume of FreshDirect's Bronx facility. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-2b392fb61cfeeb996064|cite]])
- Because Amazon offers free same-day delivery to Prime members, FreshDirect rolled out same-day service in 2019 plus FreshDirect Express for a smaller curated selection. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-0a27adab7a2da581e180|cite]])
- By 2015 many items were cheaper from Amazon Fresh than from FreshDirect, which went from being known for low prices to being seen as a premium-price, premium-quality supplier. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-fa2849ac5d4e80a5d303|cite]])
- Walmart opened a Bronx-based food warehouse for its Jet.com division, later folded into Walmart.com, to compete with FreshDirect. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-bc47047edf1876c2a4e0|cite]])
- Trader Joe's has opened stores in some of New York's most lucrative neighborhoods, competing on FreshDirect's turf. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-78105a552fd8d33b392b|cite]])
- FreshDirect co-invested with Campbell Soup in 2017 in meal-kit firm Chef'd, while meal-kit services such as Hello Fresh offer another alternative to grocery shopping. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-fc2231bd892fba2a7cf8|cite]])
- Early reviews of Amazon Fresh in New York City criticized its poor online shopping experience and uneven selection. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-636efec312405cade3a6|cite]])
- FreshDirect's CEO said in 2014: "In a $700 billion industry, there's plenty of space for a lot of different people to play." ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-3303e5cc3d2eda57d334|cite]])
- Prediction: Gallaugher predicts FreshDirect may keep a segment valuing high-quality produce and semi-prepared meals while rivals take price-sensitive shoppers and packaged goods. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-4e3a69a45d91796c5fdd|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues technology alone is not FreshDirect's edge; it is the collective impact of its many differences from rivals, its tech-enabled strategic positioning, that delivers success. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-3c9246edd5a296fe478f|cite]])

- Gallaugher argues FreshDirect grew when New York's grocery market was fragmented among local players, but stronger chains like Trader Joe's and Whole Foods have since arrived. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-c402cd492d5ff8d1e97a|cite]])
