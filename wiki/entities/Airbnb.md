# Airbnb

Home-sharing marketplace that connects travelers with hosts renting out their own homes or rooms.

## Facts by source

### 2015-07-08 [[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits|Netflix and the Conservation of Attractive Profits]]

- Thompson argues Netflix is less a network than a marketplace where consumers give their attention to creators, putting it in the same class as Uber and Airbnb. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-f275fc27699a4d497961|cite]])
- Thompson lists three traits shared by Netflix and other market-makers, working in order: an infinitely scalable network, a commoditized former constraint, and positioning as chief beneficiary of the industry's transformation. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-a2f4a6722fd310910b94|cite]])
- Thompson argues that commoditizing and modularizing an integrated system destroys incumbent value while letting a new entrant integrate a different part of the value chain and capture new value. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-316a2b6ee181ec8060f5|cite]])
- Thompson argues Airbnb commoditized trust by divorcing it from the physical property, freeing it to integrate trust and reservations across a worldwide network of hosts and guests. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-3a9c7b728dc9c65fe517|cite]])
- Thompson argues Airbnb, Uber and Netflix are all specialized customer-relationship companies that modularized production or delivery to move closer to the customer: Airbnb for travelers, Uber for commuters, Netflix for the bored. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-d14b7032921e3aa8b4a0|cite]])
- Thompson limits his reversal: in the TV value chain highly differentiated content creators keep the most power, unlike Airbnb hosts and Uber drivers. ([[2015-07-08-Netflix-and-the-Conservation-of-Attractive-Profits#^clm-61e59f3a72eb1674ad82|cite]])

### 2015-07-21 [[2015-07-21-Aggregation-Theory|Aggregation Theory]]

- Airbnb and Uber digitized trust (and, for Uber, dispatch) to disrupt industries without a digital product. ([[2015-07-21-Aggregation-Theory#^clm-ac57be0b9bb05a7d7f18|cite]])

### 2026-10-14 [[2026-10-14-Lennys-Newsletter-Metric-Benchmarking|Metric Benchmarking]]

- Online marketplace benchmarks define a gross margin of 70% as good and 80% or higher as great, while heavily managed marketplaces like DoorDash operate with lower margins. ([[2026-10-14-Lennys-Newsletter-Metric-Benchmarking#^clm-6825c42b75e3d2a422fd|cite]])
