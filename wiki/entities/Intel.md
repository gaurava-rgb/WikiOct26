### 2026-04-20 [[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp|TSMC Earnings, New N3 Fabs, The Nvidia Ramp]]

- Thompson argued in 2024 that TSMC, once a trailing-edge foundry profiting from long-depreciated fabs, was shifting to an Intel-like model driven by leading-edge revenue and high prices, which was changing how it manages its fabs. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-e0c6be68c0fba6f81b34|cite]])
- Thompson argued in 2024 that one of Intel's challenges in becoming a foundry is that it lacks much depreciated trailing-edge capacity throwing off cash, because it repurposed old fabs for each new node. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-9fe0c1b069a4ba161dcb|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson predicts TSMC's new 3nm fabs will probably use an Intel-style copy-exact approach, cloning proven lines, so they should be reliable and fast, with large-scale production by 2027. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-335244167b7afd70ac2b|cite]])

### 2026-04-29 [[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab|Intel Earnings, Intel’s Differentiation?, Whither Terafab]]

- Intel guided June-quarter 2026 revenue to $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion, versus an analyst average of $13 billion, and its shares hit a record high. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-866257409fc67249ff14|cite]])
- Thompson argues Lip-Bu Tan has had a real positive impact on Intel, but the number one factor driving Intel's performance is structural: AI. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-adb67590538ca06829e9|cite]])
- Tan says the CPU is reinserting itself as the foundation of the AI era, serving as the orchestration layer and control plane for the AI stack. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-b7c1835a8bf4c3a89340|cite]])
- Tan says Xeon server CPU demand has strong and sustained momentum, with customers deploying CPUs alongside accelerators in a ratio moving back toward CPUs. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-ddc64d6040be9797be0b|cite]])
- Tan says the ratio of CPUs to GPUs used to be 1 to 8, is now 1 to 4, and is heading toward parity or better. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-e5b61f4133ce0abbaceb|cite]])
- Prediction: Tan predicts the CPU to GPU ratio will move toward parity or even better, making the CPU franchise a growth engine for years, not just quarters. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-34615f505e07266cf728|cite]])
- Thompson argues the CPU shift makes sense: training is GPU bound, but inference means many short jobs that need more orchestration, and agents add tool use on top, so they need still more CPUs. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-d82b30c00bc530806b9a|cite]])
- Asked whether Intel's server CPUs have competitive differentiation versus other x86 makers or Arm, Tan talked about ratios, packaging and foundry instead; Thompson says he completely dodged the question. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-dc6737d5c4422059d2c9|cite]])
- Asked about share versus AMD and competition from Arm chips such as Nvidia's Vera, Amazon's Graviton and Google's Axion, Tan cited Intel's roadmap, his friendship with Arm CEO Rene Haas, and his investment in Annapurna Labs, which Thompson calls a total dodge. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-aacbf4fd2db497b8311f|cite]])
- Zinsner says Q1 was Intel's lowest supply point of the year, and Intel beat expectations partly by selling de-specced and shelved legacy finished goods it had not expected to move. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-fe9d15506089ae0182e2|cite]])
- Prediction: Zinsner says Intel may not have the inventory boost again in Q2, so volume growth from Q1 to Q2 will rely on increasing supply. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-33e3396d9a3eeaa87732|cite]])
- Thompson argues CPU demand is so high that Intel is scrounging through the couch cushions, and chips it sold that way are negatively differentiated beyond simply being available. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-3f35ffd305ddff7fc59e|cite]])
- Thompson revisits his 2021 thesis that Intel needed outside foundry customers to fund the leading edge, asking whether Intel may instead consume all its own fab capacity because CPU demand is insatiable. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-1bdd025625b7e38f3f54|cite]])
- Thompson argues Intel's most important long-run advantage may be that it is the only CPU maker not constrained by TSMC capacity. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-df9f359bdc1b96ff81f6|cite]])
- Thompson argues the foundry shift was not a mistake: it exposed how far behind Intel was on efficiency and yields, which Tan has improved and which drive near-term supply. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-44c338582c0f1ec3efaa|cite]])
- Musk says Terafab plans to use Intel's 14A process, which is not yet complete but should be mature by the time Terafab scales up. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-a7b9faf596ec848ac3b2|cite]])
- Tan says Intel has partnered with SpaceX, xAI and Tesla on Terafab because he and Musk believe global chip supply is not keeping pace with demand. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-a221e30c6b75379c99f7|cite]])
- Tan says 14A is making great progress on yield and cycle time with multiple customers heavily engaged, but Intel will not announce customers unless they choose to. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-53e05e1130ca60afc466|cite]])
- Thompson reads the Terafab deal as a highly speculative process-licensing agreement, where Intel's payoff is less money than the process improvements Musk may find, a reasonable bet Tan seems unsure will pay off. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-aff9ecd5e0ef58ad4d7b|cite]])
- Thompson praises Tan for not claiming things that do not exist, whether Intel differentiation or concrete growth from Tesla. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab#^clm-7d707471da35798418ce|cite]])

- The Wall Street Journal reported in May 2026 that Apple and Intel reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to make some Apple device chips after more than a year of talks; which products is unclear. - Thompson argued in TSMC Risk (2026) that TSMC customers' biggest risk is not China but TSMC's monopoly and reluctance to invest at the industry's pace, causing forgone AI revenue that could justify making Samsung or Intel viable rivals.
