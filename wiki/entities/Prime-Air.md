# Prime Air

Amazon's cargo airline and drone delivery unit.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- A purchase of Boeing 767 passenger jets to be converted for cargo was to bring Amazon's Prime Air fleet to 85 planes. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-587dc871678343dfd69a|cite]])
- The chapter's Key Takeaways say Amazon has leased 85 cargo planes, funneling the most cost-effective shipments to its own fleet and taking next-day orders later. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-4ca2bcfbdfbffbc3ff6f|cite]])
- When FedEx flew for Amazon, its West Coast planes left by 9:00 p.m. to reach the Memphis hub, but Amazon's own point-to-point planes can leave as late as 2:00 a.m., so Amazon can take orders later and still promise fast delivery. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-024e715551fda67ce3d2|cite]])
- Amazon opened its own $1.5 billion, 900-acre air hub in northern Kentucky in 2021 so it could route air cargo without an air freight partner. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-b2d925412ab394abda68|cite]])
