# Zara

Spanish fast-fashion chain; flagship brand of Inditex.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch09 Shein and Temu - E-commerce Giants from Asia]]

- Gallaugher argues Shein has out-Zara'ed Inditex, using AI and analytics to automate design and information systems to unbundle much of Zara's vertically integrated supply chain in ways that skirt import restrictions and lower costs. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-1a51b9431972fd508f84|cite]])
- A TikTok with over 10 million views compared a $79 Zara dress with an indistinguishable one bought from Shein for $15, part of a wave of #zaravshein posts. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-06969642fed200857873|cite]])
- Shein can go from spotting a trend to shipping a product in as little as three days, faster than Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-78bd346ab58cdf05b517|cite]])
- A Key Takeaway in the chapter says Shein's data and AI operation lets it create thousands of new products a year, many more than even Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-490e4fdd8601e105b4dd|cite]])
- Shein is followed by over three times as many TikTok users as Zara, with ASOS and H&M further behind. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-eb47f303a808597ee3c0|cite]])
- Unlike Zara, which gathers in-store demand and mimics catwalk fashions, Shein ignores the runway and responds to consumer trends mined from social media, ads, and app use. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-178256e79d817026ce26|cite]])
- Nearly all products in the Shein app have been live three months or less, while at Zara about 53 percent of products are less than three months old. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-54dfb87288c91cd5fb19|cite]])
- An industry pundit says Zara cannot get around U.S. import duties because it sells to stores and imports in bulk rather than shipping to individuals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-2a29e459196ef4e1ed18|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch02 Zara - Fast Fashion from Savvy Systems]]

- Amancio Ortega first wanted to name his clothing shop Zorba, after the film Zorba the Greek, but a bar in town already had that name, and since the sign letter molds were already cast, his team rearranged them into Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-deabeaae742c54320079|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that technology, not the name, is what made Zara rise to dominate the fashion industry and become the largest pure-play fashion retailer in the world. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-2e40d68354a5e45ca227|cite]])
- Zara is the crown jewel of Inditex (Industrias de Diseno Textil), a group that has far outperformed its nearest global rival H&M. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-fd4203a8ddd74d7309de|cite]])
- Gap and Topshop, once high-fliers, were hurt even more than H&M by Zara's rise; Topshop was bought and then shed by ASOS. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-2ff16a2f87ea943c14d4|cite]])
- Inditex runs its business from The Cube, a futuristic headquarters in La Coruna (A Coruna) in northern Spain. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-804454a0b23d205fd12d|cite]])
- Unlike rivals, Zara shuns advertising, rarely runs sales, and keeps much of its production in-house while nearly every other major player outsources to low-cost countries. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f77d27c9462bff481944|cite]])
- Zara's success made founder Amancio Ortega the world's second wealthiest person, and at one point he briefly held the top spot (endnote dated 2017). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-3684016189dfce998cb5|cite]])
- Inditex supports eight brands, and Zara accounts for roughly two-thirds of the group's sales (cited to a 2008 article). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-09956923dcc19e18d059|cite]])
- Zara emerged from the COVID-19 retail downturn far stronger than its closest rivals while undergoing one of the fastest global expansions in fashion history. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-a707f43c7a3c5f368d32|cite]])
- The fashion director of luxury group LVMH called Zara the most innovative and devastating retailer in the world. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-6c621e09b5a553001d9d|cite]])
- Zara's average item price is about $27, though prices vary by country; a Goldman analyst called the chain Armani at moderate prices. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-6c35fbf6ea0ac5077cae|cite]])
- Zara stores get a Z-day delivery of new clothing lines twice a week, which fans eagerly await. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8952f29e2a18da61ca1f|cite]])
- While Neiman Marcus, H&M, Gap and Macy's shrink or close stores, Zara mostly closes stores only to open bigger ones to meet demand (cited to a 2015 article). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-c96ed6f7110e633f43ec|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that Zara's efficient model yields competitive prices, higher margins, fewer markdowns and write-offs, faster inventory turnover, lower risk and less advertising. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-5a53d7f622849db46a90|cite]])
- Gap was not implicated in Rana Plaza but became a protest target in 2013 as one of the largest importers of Bangladeshi clothing and for refusing to sign a safety accord backed by H&M and Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-071644700e9ed80efadd|cite]])
- Zara store managers lead intelligence gathering with custom apps on mobile devices, first Windows PDAs and now iPads, chatting with customers about what they want and sending the answers to headquarters. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-52a76e125d0e8fefdc6d|cite]])
- Zara managers have skin in the game: up to 70 percent of salaries can come from store sales performance (2008), and 10 percent of the firm's profit increase is shared across all employees (2015). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8a4c1155150f0ab29d89|cite]])
- At Zara, the POS system tells the firm what is selling, while PDA and related data tell it what customers at each store want to see, so styles and rebuys follow feedback rather than hunches. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-49005bee29e9a7d78452|cite]])
- Zara's chairman and CEO said its business model is the opposite of the traditional one: instead of designing a collection long before the season, Zara first learns what customers like, then designs and produces it. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-95c1b4b2850247a0e0f9|cite]])
- About 300 Zara designers at The Cube produce 30,000 items a year (2014), versus 2,000 to 4,000 items at big chains like H&M and Gap. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ced18144998f9d1659a4|cite]])
- Zara designers regularly contact store managers and sometimes fly them to Spain to review mockups, on the idea that those closest to customers know best what they want. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-fc45ec02ee41c50853ce|cite]])
- Zara CEO Pablo Isla said the idea from the start was to understand what the customer wants first, then have integrated manufacturing and logistics to deliver it quickly. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-90b0bf7e5e3eadc850aa|cite]])
- A Zara concept takes an average of fifteen days from idea to store, and small tweaks like a round neck instead of a V neck can reach stores in ten days (2003), while rivals get new styles once or twice a season. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f0d45c8c6e4cc661dce6|cite]])
- Zara is twelve times faster than Gap while offering roughly ten times more unique products (2002). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ef2dd82f518e081599e6|cite]])
- H&M takes three to five months from creation to delivery and is considered one of the best; other retailers average six months to design and three to manufacture, VF Corporation can take nine months to design jeans, and J.Jill a year. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-022a8d74808be35be7f0|cite]])
- Most products in Zara stores did not exist three weeks earlier, not even as sketches, and an analyst likened Zara to a speedy chameleon that adapts instantly to trends. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-7341cc56b03b54476da3|cite]])
- Zara's responsiveness comes from combining vertical integration with technology-coordinated suppliers, just-in-time manufacturing and finely tuned logistics. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f6eab38485961820d962|cite]])
- H&M has 900 suppliers and no factories, while nearly 60 percent of Zara's merchandise is produced in-house. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-62bd5106b32cfa302d08|cite]])
- Zara uses inventory optimization models to decide how many of which items in which sizes go to each store in its twice-weekly shipments. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-07e6dfb4e47fff2844f0|cite]])
- Near its La Coruna distribution center, Zara cuts and dyes fabric with robots in twenty-three highly automated factories, makes 40 percent of its own fabric and buys most dyes from its own subsidiary. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-bcce9703cc72c5c261db|cite]])
- About half of Zara's cloth arrives undyed so it can react to midseason taste shifts; when cotton hit record prices, Zara moved away from costly fabrics and kept margins, while H&M's profits fell 10 percent (2011). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-0c9be467da0a6b539d42|cite]])
- After cutting and dyeing, many Zara items are sewn by local cooperatives that have worked with Inditex so long they operate without written contracts. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8266935d9e029c556daa|cite]])
- Zara's contract manufacturers for trendy clothes are nearby in Portugal, Morocco and Turkey, because for Zara speed beats manufacturing cost and drives profits. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-339c30ec5c5bc5739207|cite]])
- Zara uses distant contractors in Asia and Latin America only for long-shelf-life staples like T-shirts and jeans, about one-eighth of dollar volume, which also limits exposure to partner labor abuses. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-7837f90c1d13194c7b6f|cite]])
- All Zara items pass through a five-million-square-foot distribution center in La Coruna (about ninety football fields) or a similar one in Zaragoza. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-87aaac3f257cf30d8dd8|cite]])
- Zara's distribution centers move over 17 million items a week (2014), with no item staying more than seventy-two hours, using parcel-style sorting machines and Toyota-designed logistics. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8448aeb039cf2a5a1a55|cite]])
- Zara builds RFID into its custom security tags so scanners can track every item through the distribution center and stores, letting staff with iPod Touch apps find an item in store, at a nearby store, at the distribution center or on Zara.com. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-0f6f628b4dd8b20b6f43|cite]])
- Walmart and JCPenney struggled with RFID, but Zara's vertical integration helps: all items flow through two warehouses it controls, so Zara can tag every product before shipping. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-064d3b405f91b8938e3e|cite]])
- Zara stores remove RFID tags at purchase, avoiding privacy concerns, and send them back for reuse; tags cost ten cents each, and at launch Zara bought one in every six tags made for the whole apparel industry that year (2014). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-4d28ced612bbaa595ccc|cite]])
- RFID lets Zara staff take in-store inventory four times as often as before, more accurately, with one-fourth the staff (2014). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-e8c401304f0c79867e9e|cite]])
- Zara trucks reach destinations that can be reached overnight, and chartered cargo flights on Air France-KLM Cargo and Emirates serve farther stores within forty-eight hours, returning with raw materials. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-a99f1f768b98ac4d0461|cite]])
- Zara makes most products in limited runs, so each store carries items tailored to local tastes, unlike Gap, where a Los Angeles store carries nearly the same line as one in Milwaukee. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-a57f3e2094186f160734|cite]])
- Bain & Company estimated the industry average markdown ratio at 50 to 70 percent, and less than 1 percent of JCPenney revenue came from full-price items, while 85 percent of Zara products sell without discount. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-78b81580dd304c9a14d3|cite]])
- The average Zara customer visits seventeen times a year, compared with three annual visits to competitors (2006). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-290fd3a3c87f7c7e3cd9|cite]])
- Zara's founder has called advertising a pointless distraction, and during Gap's collapse Gap raised ad spending while sales dropped. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-b804462d5118d64e1b45|cite]])
- Fashion retailers like H&M spend as much as 4 percent of revenue on promotion, while Inditex ad spending ranges from 0.3 to 0.05 percent (2015). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-55f7aa98812656a551a0|cite]])
- Zara's CEO said limited production runs reduce to a minimum the risk of making a mistake, admitting that Zara does make mistakes with its collections. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8590dae401349f03c183|cite]])
- Failed product introductions at Zara are about 1 percent versus an industry average of 10 percent (2006), and unsold items are under 10 percent of inventory versus as high as 20 percent industry-wide (2013). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-555a65c1b50f734fb770|cite]])
- Even with higher manufacturing costs than rivals, Inditex gross margins are nearly 60 percent, compared with 39 percent at Gap (2012 results). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-396b981524697bf4e007|cite]])
- Inditex was a latecomer to e-commerce, but online sales reached 14 percent of revenue by year-end 2019, and Inditex does not report online sales as a separate category. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-105eea6218e59f645846|cite]])
- About two-thirds of Zara online returns are made in-store and one-third of online orders are picked up in-store (2016), with no evidence that online shopping cannibalizes store sales. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-00992f367344e2cc7caa|cite]])
- E-commerce is estimated to make up about a quarter of Zara sales (January 2023). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-aeada5e552cba4de1028|cite]])
- Zara deployed fifty-two special e-commerce stockrooms globally, each able to deliver to stores or homes. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ac32c0093820945e5575|cite]])
- Primark quit its e-commerce experiment after finding shipping costs for commodity shirts were similar to their sale price, while Zara's unique products give it price cushioning. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-6441915b8014c2a04288|cite]])
- With 88 percent of its stores closed in spring 2020, Inditex saw a 44 percent quarterly sales drop and its first-ever loss as a public company. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-e12f30630bd992582a62|cite]])
- RFID let Inditex locate stock in closed stores and use it for online orders in 2020; an executive said that without RFID it would not have been possible. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-1e06195549d2ed271551|cite]])
- Researchers Donald Sull and Stefano Turconi found Zara's IT spending, measured by IT staff share or spending as a share of sales, is less than one-fourth the fashion industry average (2008). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8814c3f792581c94da5b|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues Zara excels by aiming technology spending at the points in its value chain with the biggest impact, not by spending more. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-cc3b695b40c8853e8802|cite]])
- Zara has experimented with hybrid mirrors that read a garment's RFID tag to suggest outfits (2018), and H&M, Macy's and Sears have also tried smart mirrors. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-5b109ed4cb89a4fecb98|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues Zara has delivered a sustainable competitive advantage for two decades through its tech-centric demand-to-design-to-sourcing value chain. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-5bbd13abd764faee49fe|cite]])
- In June 2020 Inditex planned up to 1,200 permanent store closures, about 16 percent of its outlets, covering its weakest stores with about 5 percent of sales, some to be replaced by larger marquee stores. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-0d51fd31d7890b33e3f2|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that routing all deliveries through two sites in Spain leaves Zara hostage to any disruption in northern Spain, with no fallback. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-5d3d777e4a5d5cd6b73d|cite]])
- Zara's Spain-centric cost base makes it vulnerable when the euro strengthens or fuel prices rise, raising the cost of twice-weekly deliveries; it can offset some by pricing items up to 40 percent higher abroad than in Spain. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-c32e362f8703318f2d2d|cite]])
- China passed France to become Zara's second-largest market after Spain (2014), so some clothing travels from Asia to Spain and back to China; management has considered but not built an Asian logistics center. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-4b7cf48a790e5f984550|cite]])
- Experts doubt Zara can replicate its flexible capacity half a world away, and an advisor worried about having two brains, one in Spain and one in China. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-608e4970d2757286be19|cite]])
- Zara has over eighty-five U.S. stores and plans to keep expanding there, though one industry watcher calls the U.S. a graveyard of European retailers, citing coastal fashion buyers and different American sizing. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-aac39b056879c9ab2c75|cite]])
- As fast fashion's biggest player, Zara is a prime environmental target: the U.S. throws away about 11.3 million tons of textiles a year, and fashion produces 10 percent of global CO2, more than international flights and shipping combined (2022). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-389d001caf7b949dbc5e|cite]])
- Dell's manufacturing edge from vertical integration faded as contract partners supplying its rivals copied its techniques, a precedent for rivals closing the gap on Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ce526577d5cf348985e6|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues clothing is more complex than computing in the number of new models offered, so Zara's value chain may be harder to copy than Dell's. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f2975141b00b0e6d1345|cite]])
- H&M has increased how often new items reach stores and is eyeing moving some contract manufacturing from Asia closer to Europe (2017). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-54a218ddfbd99b591c32|cite]])
- Forever 21 and Uniqlo get new looks within six weeks, and Brazil's Renner rolls out mini-collections every two months (2008). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-b4da02a223abfcea82e9|cite]])
- Shein's U.S. sales exceeded Zara's and H&M's combined, and in 2022 investors valued the private Shein above the combined market cap of Inditex and H&M. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f9e6623b8fe1cc39f607|cite]])
- Many of Zara's tech initiatives were led by Jose Maria Castellano, a technophile business professor who became Amancio Ortega's right-hand man in the 1980s. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-98e33754446992619816|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues Zara's value chain is hard to copy but not invulnerable, so management must keep scanning for new threats and opportunities. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-4cc2f6aac8098e37460b|cite]])
