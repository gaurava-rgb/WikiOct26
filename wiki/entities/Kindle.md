# Kindle

Amazon's e-reader line, launched in 2007.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Gallaugher argues Dash Carts are clearly version 1.0, too small for a big family's haul, but likens them to the first Kindle, which was clunky and expensive until a few iterations made it a hit. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-65d3cf3f932f980da2b6|cite]])
- Amazon began its push into consumer electronics when it launched the Kindle in 2007. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-f9abe26ab348788611de|cite]])
- Gallaugher rates Amazon's device results as mixed: category-monopolizing (Kindle), dominant but not money-making (Echo and Alexa), and an utter flop (Fire Phone). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-ee3c25532de5371c3f0b|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues the Kindle is less about reading than about putting an always-on Amazon store in customers' hands that locks them into Amazon's standard and opens a new ad channel. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-2fc23ef5f6f78d8ed7f1|cite]])
- Amazon built the Kindle because media made up well over 20 percent of its revenue, and Jeff Bezos saw that media was going digital and that whoever controlled the platform would dominate, since readers want one library on one device. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-ce7ea73c8412103d3f7d|cite]])
- Kindles launched at $399, and thanks to Moore's Law a low-end model sold for $69 five years later. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-935230f49ad7a1d59213|cite]])
- By the 2009 holiday season, the Kindle's second year, it was Amazon's top-selling product by both dollars and units. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-52ae1f3334017c58eb20|cite]])
- Amazon owned roughly 83 percent of the eBook market. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-e22b5ab1fcf86b2234ec|cite]])
- Amazon does not try to profit from Kindle and Fire hardware; entry-level models are regularly sold at or below cost, and each arrives linked to the buyer's Amazon account. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-1a5fb0a037aee3312240|cite]])
- RBC Capital estimated that each Kindle Fire, though sold at a loss, generates over $136 in operating income, with lifetime operating margins above 20 percent. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-ffc0017bfa763a82e623|cite]])
- Kindle owners buy three to four times more books than before, and by 2012 Amazon sold more eBooks than print books. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-e1e28043c8109b1d6118|cite]])
- SmartMoney reported that Amazon customers without a Kindle spent $87 a month, Kindle owners $136, and Kindle Fire owners over $150. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-bc7ca2ba05ae793e3784|cite]])
- The Kindle accounts for two-thirds of e-readers sold and fuels over 80 percent of the eBook market, which Gallaugher calls winner-take-most platform dynamics. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-d8399d0648ff9f32a9c1|cite]])
- Amazon became a book publisher with its own imprints, offering authors royalties of 35 to 70 percent, and controls over 90 percent of the self-publishing market. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-5e395012a945635fb979|cite]])
- Walmart and Target once sold the Kindle but stopped in 2012, fearing it would pull physical sales to Amazon. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-99851c7a3d28d6856d47|cite]])
- Under wholesale pricing Amazon paid publishers for eBooks and set its own prices; Jeff Bezos said trade eBooks should not cost more than ten dollars, and Amazon sold many near or below cost to build the Kindle standard. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-32a441324f5780f3bd9c|cite]])
- Because Apple charges 30 percent on in-app purchases, Amazon removed the purchase button from its iOS Kindle app, so users must buy eBooks on the web or a Kindle. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-057b878c7a89acd02bd6|cite]])
