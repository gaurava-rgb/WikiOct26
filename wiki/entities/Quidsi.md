# Quidsi

New Jersey operator of Diapers.com and Wag.com; bought by Amazon, later shut down.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): S17.7 Walmart - Data Asset in Action and the Rise of Walmart]]

- Jet.com's founders had earlier founded Quidsi, the Diapers.com parent that Walmart tried to buy but lost when Amazon outbid it. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-f772085daff1853be9b6|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Bloomberg Businessweek ran a cover story, 'What Amazon Fears Most', implying Quidsi's Diapers.com could build brand and scale in staples and draw customers away; Amazon then bought Quidsi for over half a billion dollars. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-53461a54c1e71e02b026|cite]])
- Quidsi never turned a profit, and Amazon eventually shut down Diapers.com, Wag.com, and other Quidsi brands. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-03644f6463c4fcba809c|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues buying Quidsi removed one of the biggest threats in high-growth markets Amazon did not dominate and kept Walmart, a rival bidder, from gaining that expertise. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-432739e3365add0b333c|cite]])
- Quidsi's founders went on to start Jet.com and sold it to Walmart for $3 billion a short time later. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-64b99ccecac84c3d435a|cite]])
