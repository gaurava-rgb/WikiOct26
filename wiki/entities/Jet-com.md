# Jet.com

E-commerce startup from Quidsi's founders, sold to Walmart.

## Facts by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): S17.7 Walmart - Data Asset in Action and the Rise of Walmart]]

- Walmart bought Jet.com in 2016 for $3.3 billion, a young and still unprofitable Amazon competitor, to gain e-commerce expertise. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-56b7665f8d4694156dd8|cite]])
- Jet.com's founders had earlier founded Quidsi, the Diapers.com parent that Walmart tried to buy but lost when Amazon outbid it. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-f772085daff1853be9b6|cite]])
- In the year after buying Jet.com, Walmart's US e-commerce sales rose 63 percent, and the items sold on its website grew from 10 million to over 67 million. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart#^clm-9930ce3618b6b95ffb6f|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch04 FreshDirect - A Tech-Heavy Online Grocer]]

- Walmart opened a Bronx-based food warehouse for its Jet.com division, later folded into Walmart.com, to compete with FreshDirect. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-bc47047edf1876c2a4e0|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Quidsi's founders went on to start Jet.com and sold it to Walmart for $3 billion a short time later. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-64b99ccecac84c3d435a|cite]])
