# Parag Agrawal

Founder and CEO of Parallel; former CTO and then CEO of Twitter until Elon Musk bought it. Also spelled Agrawal.

## Facts by source

### 2026-05-21 [[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel|An Interview with Parallel Founder Parag Agarwal About Valuing Content on the Agentic Web]]

- Parallel, founded by Parag Agarwal in 2023, is a search engine for AI agents that mixes compute, models, and search, and delivers results based on how much the customer is willing to spend. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-c173324ef5a87661668d|cite]])
- Agarwal says he joined Twitter as its most junior software engineer, because he loved the product. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-8ae04bc583aee5e0a72d|cite]])
- Agarwal says Twitter's ads team grew revenue from almost nothing to a couple billion dollars in a couple of years after going public. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-cf8467de9bb5bd6e9be0|cite]])
- Agarwal says that after the IPO, Twitter's own projections showed it would stall at about 90 million daily active users (DAU, people who use it each day), so he moved from ads to the consumer product. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-230b9bacefff082fd8c5|cite]])
- Agarwal says bringing machine learning into Twitter's feed, against internal resistance, restarted DAU growth within months. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-aa7b14a235cb2e269076|cite]])
- Agarwal calls Bluesky, the decentralized protocol effort he worked on under Jack Dorsey at Twitter, a failure. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-534f32f4f7eeb176bbed|cite]])
- Agarwal says that as CEO during the Elon Musk takeover, he was already working with the board on a meaningful layoff, though smaller than the one Musk later made. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-016885738db9b30c3dc7|cite]])
- Agarwal says his biggest regret is not building a materially better Twitter business during his years as CTO, and he shares the blame with Jack Dorsey. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-45d2c43eab8d08da315c|cite]])
- Agarwal explains Shapley values with a gloves example: with one seller of a left glove and two sellers of a right glove, the Shapley math pays about 4x more for the scarce left glove. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-f36d1a0b6c73fdf47279|cite]])
- Prediction: Agarwal says that three years ago he became convinced agents will use the web 1000x more than humans ever have. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-3ba8477483de4dcadd8f|cite]])
- Agarwal argues the web runs on three business models, subscriptions, advertising, and transactions, and the first two depend on human attention, which agents do not supply, so a new model is needed. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-86a9b1378b74a6c9cb3d|cite]])
- Agarwal argues ads are so efficient because they allow fine-grained differential pricing (charging each user close to their value), where the top few percent of queries or users fund the rest. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-2760185f5abea2639e1c|cite]])
- Agarwal argues much ad spend now counted as consumer is really B2B, such as a startup buying Google ads to reach business buyers, and in an agent world that spend will flow through agents doing high-value work. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-06797853c0a1226ab48b|cite]])
- Agarwal argues the open web will not stay open if its business model fails, and that paying content owners is a business argument, not a moral one. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-0c38de769e038d04b50a|cite]])
- Prediction: Agarwal predicts that if models and intelligence keep getting cheaper, data becomes more valuable as the complement, so a larger share of spend may go to content over time. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-daafe3ac30f13cb3ac12|cite]])
- Agarwal says the 1000x agent usage forecast from two and a half years ago now looks too low, and demand is growing very fast. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-d53aa5589f963ee3a8a3|cite]])
- Prediction: Agarwal predicts that if demand keeps growing as forecast, within two years people will change behavior and create content aimed at gaps agents need filled. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-171d726d35fdf0ed75f7|cite]])
- Agarwal says agents so far do not care about the quality of writing, and Thompson argues agents ignore the storytelling that makes content work for humans. ([[2026-05-21-Interview-Parag-Agarwal-Parallel#^clm-ed8119e57dd3d93b3c4a|cite]])
