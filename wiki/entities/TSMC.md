# TSMC

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company, the largest contract chip maker; makes chips for Apple, Nvidia, and others.

## Facts by source

### 2026-04-20 [[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp|TSMC Earnings, New N3 Fabs, The Nvidia Ramp]]

- TSMC raised its 2026 revenue outlook in April 2026 to growth of more than 30% in US dollar terms, up from earlier guidance of below 30%. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-5f348cc5d9c62b6bef01|cite]])
- TSMC said in April 2026 that its 2026 capex (capital spending on fabs and equipment) would trend toward the high end of the $52 to $56 billion range it gave the prior quarter. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-c47f0ea0a27f70e26951|cite]])
- Thompson recalls finding TSMC's fourth quarter 2025 earnings alarming because the leading foundry was not investing nearly enough to meet current AI demand, let alone demand over the 3 to 5 years it takes to build and ramp a new fab. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-758511e1da6bcdc35818|cite]])
- Thompson says TSMC's first quarter 2026 earnings brought no big capex news beyond landing at the top of the range, which he calls trending in the right direction. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-d794b0694613b38c7e0f|cite]])
- TSMC CEO C.C. Wei said in April 2026 that the shift from generative AI in query mode to agentic AI in command-and-action mode is driving another step up in tokens consumed and in demand for leading-edge chips. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-6073f85642bdb7b2bdec|cite]])
- C.C. Wei said in April 2026 that TSMC's customers and their customers, mainly cloud providers, keep sending very strong signals, so TSMC's conviction in a multi-year AI megatrend remains high. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-ca8f3d28b95f0bb10688|cite]])
- Thompson says that in years of reading TSMC earnings calls he does not remember a TSMC executive being this specific about end-user applications as Wei was about agentic AI. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-01c6dd56d5349ed653e8|cite]])
- TSMC said in April 2026 it cannot tell conventional server CPUs apart from AI server CPUs, so it still leaves CPUs out of its AI accelerator revenue count, though it might include them later. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-95fb4619ec6c06734323|cite]])
- Thompson argues Wei and his team were convinced, perhaps by Jensen Huang, that agents are real and will drive a massive expansion of compute demand, which matches Thompson's own thesis but matters far more for the industry if it is Wei's too. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-08ee611359fa50cdf5b2|cite]])
- Wei said TSMC historically does not add capacity to a node once it reaches its target, but in April 2026 TSMC is stepping up capex to expand 3nm (N3) capacity worldwide to meet strong AI demand. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-0c7b769229a0f28e1f13|cite]])
- TSMC announced in April 2026 a new 3nm fab in its Tainan GIGAFAB cluster in Taiwan with volume production in the first half of 2027, and said its second Arizona fab, already built, will run 3nm with volume production in the second half of 2027. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-c4014ba552170c54068e|cite]])
- TSMC said in April 2026 it now plans to use 3nm technology in its second fab in Japan, with volume production scheduled for 2028. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-a360d387b499f1853f08|cite]])
- In an update Thompson quotes from 2024, N3 was already 15% of TSMC revenue, followed by 35% from N5 and 17% from N7, with N3 mostly Apple and lowering margins in its early years. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-cb4b0ebed76dab64ba5c|cite]])
- Thompson argued in 2024 that TSMC, once a trailing-edge foundry profiting from long-depreciated fabs, was shifting to an Intel-like model driven by leading-edge revenue and high prices, which was changing how it manages its fabs. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-e0c6be68c0fba6f81b34|cite]])
- Thompson argued in 2024 that one of Intel's challenges in becoming a foundry is that it lacks much depreciated trailing-edge capacity throwing off cash, because it repurposed old fabs for each new node. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-9fe0c1b069a4ba161dcb|cite]])
- Thompson argues that TSMC building brand-new 3nm fabs years after the node began ramping in 2022, rather than reworking 5nm fabs as in 2024, could not be more different from its traditional model, meaning AI is transforming TSMC completely. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-cb1fedeeaed66d156d7a|cite]])
- Thompson says Apple has long been TSMC's most important customer for each new process, paying and suffering early defects to be first, as with the N3B process for the A17 Pro and M3, which he calls a dead end that was still worth it to Apple. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-403ee54b9f51f80a63e4|cite]])
- Thompson describes TSMC's usual node order: Apple ramps first and moves on, then Qualcomm, then AMD, and in the pre-AI era Nvidia and AMD GPUs came last. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-46531074f8272357d711|cite]])
- Thompson explains Nvidia has always come late to new TSMC processes because its datacenter GPUs are as big as the reticle allows, so one defect ruins a whole GPU instead of 1 of 8 or 9 Apple A-series chips, making early yields too punishing. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-39787932c25e0251a8ef|cite]])
- Nvidia's Blackwell is made on N4P, a variant of TSMC's 5nm process, and Rubin will be Nvidia's first chip on TSMC's 3nm process, as of April 2026. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-30401694f635c927ca44|cite]])
- Thompson argues Nvidia used to be able to take over Apple's old capacity on a node, but its needs are now so large that TSMC's initial 3nm capacity is not enough, so the new 3nm fabs are basically Rubin fabs. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-201742be86edacc8f0b1|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson predicts TSMC's new 3nm fabs will probably use an Intel-style copy-exact approach, cloning proven lines, so they should be reliable and fast, with large-scale production by 2027. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-335244167b7afd70ac2b|cite]])
- Thompson says C.C. Wei had been the person most worried about an AI bubble, for good reason, but as of April 2026 is not only saying AI is real but meaningfully changing TSMC's approach to capitalize on it. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp#^clm-83daf64063fbe047a9fb|cite]])

### 2026-05-13 [[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel|The Deployment Company, Back to the 70s, Apple and Intel]]

- Thompson argues the most obvious reason for an Apple-Intel deal is economic, more than Trump administration pressure or Apple's concentration risk on TSMC and Taiwan. ([[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel#^clm-0b2ea9c5036aeec35550|cite]])

- Thompson says new chips take about five months to make, so the A19 Pro lines Apple added only started producing partway through the March 2026 quarter, which held back iPhone 17 Pro sales. ([[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel#^clm-3f5d9a2ee9d9c1e4e5e2|cite]])
- Thompson argues Apple likely moved TSMC capacity from the Mac to iPhone chips and now lacks chips for the Mac mini and Mac Studio too. ([[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel#^clm-c9ffffa332f565ab6766|cite]])
- Thompson argues Apple no longer has flexible access to TSMC capacity because so much goes to AI, and this is costing Apple meaningful money across several product lines. ([[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel#^clm-1106ba65787dcf8a47cc|cite]])
- Thompson argued in TSMC Risk (2026) that TSMC customers' biggest risk is not China but TSMC's monopoly and reluctance to invest at the industry's pace, causing forgone AI revenue that could justify making Samsung or Intel viable rivals. - Prediction: Thompson argues TSMC's failure to invest aggressively enough will give Intel what it most needs to become a viable foundry: Apple, the customer that did more than any other to make TSMC the leader.
