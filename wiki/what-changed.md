# What changed

This page lists the places where a writer, a company, or the market changed its mind, or where two sources disagree. Each entry puts the older claim next to the newer one, both cited, so you can see what moved and why it matters.

Groups run by topic. Inside each group, the newest change comes first.

## AI hardware and AR glasses

**2026-09-28: Meta's glasses become a delivery truck for an agent**
- Before: Thompson predicted in 2024 that generative AI is building a bridge to wearables that are full computers in their own right, not phone add-ons, and he called that bullish for Meta. ([[2024-12-02-The-Gen-AI-Bridge-to-the-Future|The Gen AI Bridge]]) He also said the weakest part of the 2024 Orion demo was its app launcher, because it just put the phone's way of working in front of his face. ([[2024-12-02-The-Gen-AI-Bridge-to-the-Future|The Gen AI Bridge]])
- Now: In April 2026 Thompson argued the Ray-Ban Display should not be an app platform, and that AI is the killer feature for these glasses. ([[2026-04-27-AI-Hardware-Meta-Display|AI Hardware, Meta Display]]) By September 2026 he argued Meta's glasses and other devices are best seen as delivery mechanisms for Muse, Meta's agent, not as AR, VR, or AI glasses. ([[2026-09-28-Apps-Agents-and-Aggregation|Apps, Agents, and Aggregation]])
- Why it matters: The 2024 bet on wearables held up, but the prize turned out to be the agent, not the glasses or apps on them.

**2026-04-27: Orion goes from true AR to a dead end**
- Before: In 2024 Thompson said the best thing about Meta's Orion glasses was seeing the real world directly, so its 70 degree field of view felt huge even though the Vision Pro offers 100 degrees. ([[2024-12-02-The-Gen-AI-Bridge-to-the-Future|The Gen AI Bridge]]) He said Orion was not shipping because the hardware was not ready, especially on cost, and because the apps had to be built on today's devices first. ([[2024-12-02-The-Gen-AI-Bridge-to-the-Future|The Gen AI Bridge]])
- Now: Thompson argues Orion is virtual reality (a device that takes over your attention), not augmented reality (digital things laid over the real world), because its eye-tracking controls demand the user's focus. ([[2026-04-27-AI-Hardware-Meta-Display|AI Hardware, Meta Display]]) He calls Orion a dead end and says he cannot imagine preferring it to the Ray-Ban Display. ([[2026-04-27-AI-Hardware-Meta-Display|AI Hardware, Meta Display]])
- Why it matters: Thompson now judges AR glasses by how you control them, not by whether you can see through them.

**2026-04: The wristband prototype ships**
- Before: As of 2024, Orion's wristband used electromyography (EMG, reading the tiny electric signals in your arm muscles) to read hand gestures with no cameras at all. ([[2024-12-02-The-Gen-AI-Bridge-to-the-Future|The Gen AI Bridge]])
- Now: The Meta Ray-Ban Display has a small display in the right lens and uses a Neural Band to control it. ([[2026-04-27-AI-Hardware-Meta-Display|AI Hardware, Meta Display]]) Thompson predicts the Neural Band will become a way to control many real-world devices beyond glasses. ([[2026-04-27-AI-Hardware-Meta-Display|AI Hardware, Meta Display]])
- Why it matters: A lab demo became a product you can buy, so the "how do you control it" problem for wearables is closer to solved.

**2025-12: Meta's true AR glasses slip a year**
- Before: Meta's Phoenix AR glasses, pitched for work and entertainment with help from a separate puck device, had been slated for the second half of 2026. ([[2026-09-15-Meta-to-Launch-Camera-Free-Smart-Glasses|Camera-Free Glasses]])
- Now: In December 2025 Reality Labs staff were told Phoenix would slip to 2027, and this fall Meta plans Luna, glasses with no camera at all, built around voice access to Meta AI and Muse. ([[2026-09-15-Meta-to-Launch-Camera-Free-Smart-Glasses|Camera-Free Glasses]])
- Why it matters: Meta's near-term glasses bet moved from showing you things to listening to you, with the agent as the main interface.

## Chips and compute economics

**2026-10-15: Anthropic IPO valuation target resets to $1.4 trillion to $1.5 trillion**
- Before: In early 2026 bankers floated private IPO valuation targets up to $2 trillion for Anthropic, and secondary market expectations anticipated $1.5 trillion or more. ([[2026-10-04-Anthropics-Big-Charity-Bill-for-Shareholders|Anthropic's Charity Bill]]; [[2026-10-14-Anthropics-Rapid-Slowing-Growth-Weigh-IPO-Valuation|Anthropic IPO Valuation]])
- Now: Following disclosures that third-quarter revenue growth slowed to 45% sequentially, Manhattan Venture Partners managing partner Jared Carmel projected the IPO will need to price to sell at $1.4 trillion to $1.5 trillion; simultaneously, fund managers at Morgan Stanley's tech conference warned that debt-heavy neoclouds like Nscale cannot complete public offerings at requested valuations ($35 billion-plus). ([[2026-10-15-Did-Anthropic-Miss-Its-Peak-IPO-Moment|Did Anthropic Miss Peak IPO]]; [[2026-10-15-The-Party-Is-Still-Going-Strong-for-Private-Tech|The Party Is Still Going Strong for Private Tech]])
- Why it matters: Frontier lab valuations are getting re-anchored to near-term public market reality rather than speculative growth multiples.

**2026-10-14: Neocloud debt meets rising rates and distressed bond levels**
- Before: CoreWeave expanded rapidly on heavy borrowing, including issuing $4.2 billion in convertible notes in September 2026 to buy chips and data centers, while co-founder Brannin McBee argued operators must achieve massive scale and hoped to tap unlimited capital from insurance firms. ([[2026-10-14-CoreWeave-Co-Founders-Stock-Sales-Arent-Helping-Investor-Confidence|CoreWeave Stock Sales]])
- Now: On October 14, CoreWeave shares plunged 15.5% to $65 (down from $128 in May and $86 in September) as bad inflation data and rate hike expectations sparked a sell-off in AI neoclouds, market chatter cited distressed bond levels, and filings revealed CEO Michael Intrator sold stock every week from April through late September 2026; simultaneously, smaller neoclouds Nscale and SB Energy struggled with public offerings, Firmus Grid cancelled its Australian IPO, and AirTrunk postponed its Singapore REIT listing. ([[2026-10-14-CoreWeave-Co-Founders-Stock-Sales-Arent-Helping-Investor-Confidence|CoreWeave Stock Sales]]; [[2026-10-14-Anthropics-Rapid-Slowing-Growth-Weigh-IPO-Valuation|Anthropic IPO Valuation]])
- Why it matters: The AI infrastructure build-out is running into the limits of debt financing; rising borrowing costs and distressed bonds punish leveraged neoclouds even as compute demand remains high.

**2026-10-14: Anthropic's growth slows as distillation revenue declines**
- Before: In the second quarter of 2026, demand for Claude Code propelled Anthropic's revenue up more than 140% from the first quarter, pushing annualized revenue past $65 billion by July and prompting bankers to float private IPO valuation targets up to $2 trillion. ([[2026-10-14-Anthropics-Rapid-Slowing-Growth-Weigh-IPO-Valuation|Anthropic IPO Valuation]]; [[2026-10-05-Microsoft-Slashes-Internal-Claude-Spending-by-a-Third|Microsoft Cuts Claude]])
- Now: In an October 14 meeting with prospective IPO investors, Anthropic reported third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, a 45% sequential gain that marked a slowdown from Q2; the company revealed 5% to 7% of its revenue came from model distillation, which is expected to decline as it fights unauthorized copying, leading attendees to expect bankers to lower the planned November 2026 IPO valuation below $1.5 trillion while Anthropic withheld an updated annualized revenue figure. ([[2026-10-14-Anthropics-Rapid-Slowing-Growth-Weigh-IPO-Valuation|Anthropic IPO Valuation]])
- Why it matters: Even the fastest-growing frontier lab faces growth deceleration as enterprise software pricing faces headwinds and model distillation is restricted, bringing valuation expectations down ahead of its public debut.

**2026-10-14: Software gross margins vs the AI app penalty**
- Before: Traditional software business models enjoyed gross margins of 75% (good) to 80%+ (great) because serving digital code has near-zero marginal distribution cost. ([[2026-10-14-Lennys-Newsletter-Metric-Benchmarking|Metric Benchmarking]])
- Now: Benchmark data across venture capital firms reveals that AI applications run at significantly lower gross margins, with 50% considered good and 60%+ considered great, because every user query incurs real-time inference compute costs. ([[2026-10-14-Lennys-Newsletter-Metric-Benchmarking|Metric Benchmarking]])
- Why it matters: Software companies trading at software valuation multiples face margin compression as they add AI features, unless inference costs fall or pricing shifts to consumption.

**2026-10-08: Nvidia's rivals stop trying to replace it**
- Before: Big buyers built their own chips to need Nvidia less, and Amazon's Bedrock moved to running mostly on Trainium in late 2025, easing its need for scarce Nvidia chips. ([[2026-09-13-Nvidias-Growing-Dependence-On-a-Few-Big-Customers|Nvidia's Few Big Customers]]; [[2026-09-08-How-Six-AWS-Engineers-Rebuilt-Bedrock-to-Challenge-Microsoft|Bedrock Rebuilt]])
- Now: Rivals that could not dent Nvidia's lead increasingly work alongside its GPUs instead of replacing them; in August 2026 Amazon said Trainium would use Nvidia's networking and memory hardware, and Nvidia plans to invest in rival d-Matrix. ([[2026-10-08-Nvidia-to-Invest-in-Chip-Rival-d-Matrix-as-Its-Challengers-Choose-Partnership|Nvidia and d-Matrix]])
- Why it matters: Even custom chips now feed Nvidia's networking business, so going custom no longer means leaving Nvidia. ([[2026-10-08-Nvidia-to-Invest-in-Chip-Rival-d-Matrix-as-Its-Challengers-Choose-Partnership|Nvidia and d-Matrix]])

**2026-10-07: Nvidia's OpenAI pledges shrink**
- Before: Nvidia had planned, but never finalized, a $100 billion investment in OpenAI tied to OpenAI building 10 gigawatts of Nvidia data centers. ([[2026-02-27-What-Amazon-and-Microsoft-Will-Win-or-Lose-From-OpenAIs-New-Deals|OpenAI's New Deals]]) For OpenAI's Ohio data center, Nvidia first discussed $250 billion of credit support. ([[2026-10-07-Where-Nvidias-100-Billion-Dealmaking-Juggernaut-Will-Go-Next|Nvidia's Dealmaking]])
- Now: Nvidia invested $30 billion in OpenAI's 2026 funding round instead, with the last $10 billion closing October 1, 2026. ([[2026-02-27-What-Amazon-and-Microsoft-Will-Win-or-Lose-From-OpenAIs-New-Deals|OpenAI's New Deals]]; [[2026-10-07-Where-Nvidias-100-Billion-Dealmaking-Juggernaut-Will-Go-Next|Nvidia's Dealmaking]]) It guaranteed $105 billion for the Ohio site's first phase only, after the price of insuring against an Nvidia default (its credit default swap spread) widened in August. ([[2026-10-07-Where-Nvidias-100-Billion-Dealmaking-Juggernaut-Will-Go-Next|Nvidia's Dealmaking]])
- Why it matters: Even Nvidia, AI's biggest winner so far, pulled back once investors signaled worry about its risk.

**2026-09-21: Is AI demand safe, or running on borrowed money?**
- Before: In August 2026 Nvidia's Jensen Huang said frontier labs have fantastic margins (the share of each sale kept as profit) and are limited only by compute. ([[2026-09-01-Nvidia-Earnings-Dollars-Per-Gigawatt-Open-and-Hugging-Face|Nvidia earnings, September]]) Thompson predicted Nvidia would still be speeding up if it could meet demand, and saw no sign of a slowdown. ([[2026-09-01-Nvidia-Earnings-Dollars-Per-Gigawatt-Open-and-Hugging-Face|Nvidia earnings, September]])
- Other view: On 2026-09-21 Thompson argued the labs, hyperscalers (the giant cloud companies), and neoclouds (newer firms that rent out AI chips) all need AI revenue to rise sharply, because capital (money from investors and lenders) is limited, and hitting that limit would end badly for anyone relying on outside funding. ([[2026-09-21-Frontier-Overhangs|Frontier Overhangs]])
- Why it matters: Demand looks endless from Nvidia's seat, but the buyers of its chips must earn enough to keep paying.

**2026-09-09: Meta's AI bill grows again**
- Before: In its first-quarter 2026 report, Meta said it would spend more on AI data centers in 2026 than it had forecast. ([[2026-05-04-Google-Earnings-Meta-Earnings|Google and Meta earnings]])
- Now: By September 2026, Meta's AI investments were expected to top $130 billion for the year, and Muse is its latest try at earning money from them. ([[2026-09-09-OpenAI-Does-Math-Reward-Hacking-Meta-Launches-Personal-Agent|OpenAI Does Math]])
- Why it matters: The bigger the bill, the more Muse has to pay off.

**2026-08-26: Nvidia's growth speeds up, and supply is the limit**
- Before: Nvidia's sales for the April 2026 quarter reached $81.6 billion, up 85% from a year earlier. ([[2026-05-26-Nvidia-Earnings-The-AI-Stack-Nvidias-New-Reporting|Nvidia earnings, May]])
- Now: In its August 2026 report Nvidia grew 100% (sales doubled in a year) and forecast 70% growth, which Thompson says is plainly set by supply, not demand. ([[2026-09-01-Nvidia-Earnings-Dollars-Per-Gigawatt-Open-and-Hugging-Face|Nvidia earnings, September]])
- Why it matters: Nvidia is not selling all that buyers want; it is selling all it can make.

**2026-08-26: OpenAI gets its own chip**
- Before: As of May 2026, all of OpenAI's training and inference (running the model to answer) ran on Nvidia chips, even though OpenAI had announced a deal to use Amazon's Trainium chip, so more OpenAI use meant more Nvidia use. ([[2026-05-26-Nvidia-Earnings-The-AI-Stack-Nvidias-New-Reporting|Nvidia earnings, May]])
- Now: Asked in August 2026 about OpenAI's new chip, Huang said Nvidia serves the whole AI life cycle in every cloud, unlike chips built for one cloud or service, and that OpenAI will stay a customer. ([[2026-09-01-Nvidia-Earnings-Dollars-Per-Gigawatt-Open-and-Hugging-Face|Nvidia earnings, September]])
- Why it matters: Nvidia's biggest buyers now make their own chips, so Nvidia has to argue for its place.

**2026-08-26: The half of Nvidia's business outside the big clouds grows fast**
- Before: As of May 2026 the hyperscalers made up about 50% of Nvidia's data center revenue, and Thompson called them frenemies, since they most want, and are best able, to turn Nvidia into just a chip maker. ([[2026-05-26-Nvidia-Earnings-The-AI-Stack-Nvidias-New-Reporting|Nvidia earnings, May]])
- Now: Huang said sovereign AI (country-backed AI), regional AIs, neoclouds, AI startups, and enterprises make up about half of Nvidia's business and are growing 100% a year. ([[2026-09-01-Nvidia-Earnings-Dollars-Per-Gigawatt-Open-and-Hugging-Face|Nvidia earnings, September]])
- Why it matters: The other half growing this fast makes Nvidia less exposed to its frenemies.

**2026-05-20: Does agent work favor Nvidia?**
- Before: Thompson argued Nvidia's edge comes largely from latency (how fast one answer comes back), so if speed is not the key limit for agent work, Nvidia's chips seem less worth their premium price. ([[2026-05-11-The-Inference-Shift|The Inference Shift]])
- Other view: Huang said in May 2026 that demand has gone parabolic (rising faster and faster) because the era of agentic AI is here. ([[2026-05-26-Nvidia-Earnings-The-AI-Stack-Nvidias-New-Reporting|Nvidia earnings, May]])
- Why it matters: If agents can wait for answers, cheaper chips can do the work; if not, Nvidia keeps its price edge.

**2026-05-13: TSMC's spending looks too cautious again**
- Before: Thompson said TSMC's first-quarter 2026 earnings brought no big capex (money spent on factories and machines) news beyond landing at the top of the range, which he called trending in the right direction. ([[2026-04-20-TSMC-Earnings-New-N3-Fabs-The-Nvidia-Ramp|TSMC earnings]])
- Now: By May 2026 Thompson argued TSMC's failure to invest hard enough will give Intel what it most needs to become a real foundry (a factory that makes chips others design): Apple, the customer that did more than any other to make TSMC the leader. ([[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel|The Deployment Company]])
- Why it matters: If TSMC builds too little, Apple has a reason to move work to Intel.

**2026-05: Apple may become Intel's outside customer**
- Before: In April 2026 Thompson revisited his 2021 view that Intel needed outside foundry customers to pay for its most advanced factories, and asked whether Intel may instead use all its own capacity because demand for its CPUs is endless. ([[2026-04-29-Intel-Earnings-Intels-Differentiation-Whither-Terafab|Intel earnings]])
- Other view: The Wall Street Journal reported in May 2026 that Apple and Intel reached a preliminary deal for Intel to make some Apple device chips, after more than a year of talks; which products is unclear. ([[2026-05-13-The-Deployment-Company-Back-to-the-70s-Apple-and-Intel|The Deployment Company]])
- Why it matters: A customer as big as Apple would change how Intel pays for its factories.

## xAI, SpaceX and Cursor

**2026-10-15: Cursor finds agent testing captures enterprise client data without rights**
- Before: In April and July 2026, Ben Thompson argued SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor was exceptionally valuable because Cursor captured huge volumes of user usage traces around coding, proprietary data that gave it an edge in training frontier models. ([[2026-07-09-Muse-Image-Grok-4-5-Alex-Karp-on-CNBC|Muse Image, Grok 4.5]]; [[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor|John Ternus and Apple]])
- Now: In late July 2026, Cursor engineers investigated whether internal testing of Grok Bot (code-named Sand) sent confidential enterprise customer records from sales and recruiting to SpaceXSI for training, prompting a Cursor lawyer to warn that Cursor arguably lacked legal rights to train on that customer data and risked a major trust crisis; the company barred employee usage data from training, following earlier leaks of client data (Verizon, Bosch, Coupa) embedded in public Vercel marketing sites. ([[2026-10-15-Cursor-Staff-Investigated-Confidential-Data-SpaceX-AI-Training|Cursor Confidential Data Investigation]])
- Why it matters: Autonomous agents with broad system access turn internal usage logs into compliance and security liabilities, showing why large companies demand strict zero data retention.

**2026-10-13: How SpaceX enters mobile without cell towers**
- Before: On October 8, Martin Peers argued SpaceX will struggle to match mobile incumbents by combining its satellite-to-mobile constellation with the new spectrum, and must also build cell towers. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric|OpenAI ARR Snafu]]) Earlier, Thompson doubted SpaceX could rival traditional telecom providers and assumed it aimed only to be their satellite partner. ([[2026-10-13-SpaceX-Buys-More-Spectrum-Musk-and-Market-Structure-Distributed-Cellular|SpaceX Buys Spectrum]])
- Now: On October 13, Thompson showed how SpaceX bypasses cell towers: President Gwynne Shotwell and Elon Musk revealed plans to mount cellular base stations directly on customer Starlink dish hardware on roofs of houses and businesses, using satellite constellation backhaul and $8 billion of low-band 800-megahertz spectrum from Grain Management to penetrate building walls in a distributed mesh network. ([[2026-10-13-SpaceX-Buys-More-Spectrum-Musk-and-Market-Structure-Distributed-Cellular|SpaceX Buys Spectrum]])
- Why it matters: Cell towers and fiber backhaul are the largest expense for traditional carriers; mounting base stations on existing satellite dishes turns customer equipment into a low-cost mobile network.

**2026-09-10: xAI trades speed for reliability**
- Before: In 2024 xAI brought its first data center online with 100,000 GPUs in 122 days, putting speed and capacity ahead of reliability. ([[2026-09-10-SpaceX-Overhauls-Data-Center-Build-Out-Potentially-Slowing-Expansion|SpaceX Overhauls Build-Out]]) SpaceX pitched fast construction as a key edge ahead of its June 2026 IPO. ([[2026-09-01-SpaceX-Shakes-Up-Data-Center-Leadership-After-Aggressive-Build-Out|SpaceX Shakes Up Data Centers]])
- Now: A new team of SpaceX rocket engineers runs the data centers, adding and testing backup power and cooling before sites go live, which may slow new campuses. ([[2026-09-10-SpaceX-Overhauls-Data-Center-Build-Out-Potentially-Slowing-Expansion|SpaceX Overhauls Build-Out]])
- Why it matters: Once SpaceX rented its chips to Anthropic and Google, an outage hurt paying customers, so uptime beat speed. ([[2026-09-10-SpaceX-Overhauls-Data-Center-Build-Out-Potentially-Slowing-Expansion|SpaceX Overhauls Build-Out]])

**2026-07-09: The Cursor deal flips twice**
- Before: In April 2026 Thompson argued the SpaceXAI and Cursor tie-up makes a lot of sense: one side has compute, the other has a product, its own data, and reach in coding, the most important AI use. ([[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor|Ternus and SpaceXAI]])
- Then: In May 2026 he argued the model fight does not suit xAI, that Musk should stick to the physical world, and that Cursor fits Microsoft or Amazon better, though the deal was not dead since Colossus 2 could still train Cursor's models. ([[2026-05-12-SpaceX-and-Anthropic-xAIs-Two-Companies-Elon-Musk-and-SpaceXAIs-Future|SpaceX and Anthropic]])
- Now: In July 2026 he argued the tie-up is paying off because Cursor captured a huge amount of usage traces (records of how people write code), data that was always likely to be very valuable. ([[2026-07-09-Muse-Image-Grok-4-5-Alex-Karp-on-CNBC|Muse Image, Grok 4.5]])
- Why it matters: The prize in the deal turned out to be Cursor's data, not its app.

**2026-07-08: xAI ships a model built with Cursor**
- Before: Thompson noted that xAI's $5.1 billion of R&D (research and development spending) built a model in 5th place, and its whole founding team had recently left. ([[2026-05-27-The-SpaceX-IPO-and-Data-Centers-in-Space|The SpaceX IPO]])
- Now: SpaceXAI unveiled Grok 4.5, its first model built jointly with Cursor, aimed at finance, legal, and coding work, weeks after SpaceX agreed to buy Cursor in a deal valuing it at $60 billion. ([[2026-07-09-Muse-Image-Grok-4-5-Alex-Karp-on-CNBC|Muse Image, Grok 4.5]])
- Why it matters: Buying Cursor gave xAI a new path after a weak model and lost founders.

**2026-05-12: Idle compute finds a renter**
- Before: In April 2026 Thompson argued Musk excels at big fixed-cost projects like car plants, rockets, and satellites but is weaker at software, so SpaceXAI had lots of compute and no one to use it. ([[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor|Ternus and SpaceXAI]])
- Now: In May 2026 Thompson argued the biggest driver of the Anthropic deal was a price too good to turn down as SpaceX prepares for an IPO (its first sale of shares to the public), turning Colossus 1 from an underused, aging asset into a source of revenue. ([[2026-05-12-SpaceX-and-Anthropic-xAIs-Two-Companies-Elon-Musk-and-SpaceXAIs-Future|SpaceX and Anthropic]])
- Why it matters: Chips that sit idle lose value, so renting them out turns a cost into income.

## Apple

**2026-10-15: Apple's capital-light AI strategy outshines hyperscaler capex**
- Before: Investors criticized Apple for slow revenue growth and lacking an in-house frontier model, while Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft poured tens of billions into AI data center capital expenditures.
- Now: Apple shares gained 26% year to date through mid-October 2026, more than double the 10% to 12% returns of Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft, and stood as the only mega-cap tech company to expand its valuation multiples in 2026. ([[2026-10-15-Did-Anthropic-Miss-Its-Peak-IPO-Moment|Did Anthropic Miss Peak IPO]])
- Why it matters: Avoiding the massive, debt-heavy capex race to build frontier models protected Apple's margins and made its partner-led AI strategy the healthier market bet.

**2026-09-16: Apple eyes the server business again**
- Before: Apple sold Xserve servers from 2002 to 2011, and the line failed largely from Apple's lack of interest in business customers. ([[2026-09-16-Apple-Considers-Return-to-Server-Market-Has-Talked-With-Nvidia-to-Use-Network-Tech|Apple Servers]]) Apple builds servers for its own Private Cloud Compute but has refused partners' requests to use them. ([[2026-09-16-Apple-Considers-Return-to-Server-Market-Has-Talked-With-Nvidia-to-Use-Network-Tech|Apple Servers]])
- Now: Apple is planning an enterprise server built on its own M8 Ultra chips for AI developers, businesses and governments, and has talked with Nvidia about using its networking. ([[2026-09-16-Apple-Considers-Return-to-Server-Market-Has-Talked-With-Nvidia-to-Use-Network-Tech|Apple Servers]])
- Why it matters: AI labs buying Macs by the tens of thousands showed demand Apple had walked away from. ([[2026-09-16-Apple-Considers-Return-to-Server-Market-Has-Talked-With-Nvidia-to-Use-Network-Tech|Apple Servers]])

**2026-09-10: Siri on Gemini goes from doubtful to working**
- Before: In March 2026 Thompson questioned Apple's plan to license Google's Gemini and build its own harness (the software that steers the model) for a new Siri, since Microsoft had concluded that route could not make a compelling product. ([[2026-03-16-Agents-Over-Bubbles|Agents Over Bubbles]]) In April he called Apple's bet, that the best hardware wins and AI becomes a commodity, reasonable, but a bet. ([[2026-04-22-John-Ternus-and-Apples-Hardware-Defined-Future-SpaceXAI-and-Cursor|Ternus and SpaceXAI]])
- Now: In September 2026 Thompson argued being late to AI is not always bad for reaching normal users, and that Apple's now actually working Siri AI, built on Gemini, is likely to get a lot of use. ([[2026-09-10-The-iPhone-Duo-The-Intelligent-Personal-Hub-Apple-Watch-Audio-Intelligence|The iPhone Duo]])
- Why it matters: Being late, and borrowing Google's model, may cost Apple less than Thompson first thought.

**2026-09-10: Is the iPhone the center of AI?**
- Before: In April 2026 Thompson argued Apple's device growth in the AI era depends on delivering on AI, because more devices make the phone less central. ([[2026-04-27-AI-Hardware-Meta-Display|AI Hardware, Meta Display]])
- Other view: At the September 2026 iPhone event, John Ternus described the ideal AI device as an intelligent personal hub, always with you and able to run models on the device and in the cloud, and said no product fits that better than the iPhone. ([[2026-09-10-The-iPhone-Duo-The-Intelligent-Personal-Hub-Apple-Watch-Audio-Intelligence|The iPhone Duo]])
- Why it matters: Apple's future rests on the phone staying the hub as glasses and agents spread.

**2026-09-10: On the device or in the cloud?**
- Before: In the first quarter of 2026 Tim Cook pitched the Mac as the best platform for AI, citing Apple Silicon's ability to run advanced models locally (on the machine itself). ([[2026-05-06-Microsoft-Earnings-Apple-Earnings|Microsoft and Apple earnings]])
- Other view: Thompson argues an iPhone, however powerful, is at root less capable than something running in the cloud. ([[2026-09-10-The-iPhone-Duo-The-Intelligent-Personal-Hub-Apple-Watch-Audio-Intelligence|The iPhone Duo]])
- Why it matters: Apple's AI pitch leans on its own chips; if the cloud wins, that edge shrinks.

**2026-09-09: Ternus takes the stage as CEO**
- Before: Apple announced in April 2026 that Tim Cook would become Executive Chairman on September 1, 2026, with John Ternus becoming CEO. ([[2026-04-21-Tim-Cooks-Impeccable-Timing|Tim Cook's Timing]])
- Now: At the September 9, 2026 event Apple unveiled its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, and new CEO Ternus mocked rival foldables as two phones awkwardly stuck together. ([[2026-09-10-The-iPhone-Duo-The-Intelligent-Personal-Hub-Apple-Watch-Audio-Intelligence|The iPhone Duo]])
- Why it matters: The handoff is done, and the first Ternus launch leads with hardware.

**2026-10-15: OpenAI accelerates search ad monetization at steep discount to Google**

- Why it matters: The old trade of content for clicks breaks when the AI answers the question itself.
