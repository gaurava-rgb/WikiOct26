# Dynamic Pricing

Changing prices on the fly as conditions change, such as raising a price when an item is scarce or when rivals sell out, and cutting it to match a rival. Data and software let an online seller do this constantly. It can lift profit, but shoppers get angry if they feel cheated.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- The Wall Street Journal reported that where rivals sold out of items, Amazon raised its prices an average of 10 percent. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-43fd93b0b7c3dd13544b|cite]])
- Amazon has been accused of constantly monitoring competitor prices with data analytics and raising or lowering its own, a dynamic pricing practice that can anger consumers who feel taken advantage of. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-520f1d5f8108f8f8ca9a|cite]])
