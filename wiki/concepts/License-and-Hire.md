# License-and-Hire Deal

A deal where a big tech company pays an AI startup to license its technology and hires its founders and key staff, instead of buying the company outright. The startup's investors get paid and a shell company is left behind. Buyers use it to get talent fast and to try to avoid the antitrust review a formal acquisition would trigger, though regulators have probed many of these deals anyway.

## Evidence by source

### 2026-10-13 [[2026-10-13-OpenAIs-Bug-and-Bumbles-AI-Powered-Exit|OpenAI’s Bug and Bumble’s AI-Powered Exit]]

- In 2024 Google paid $2.7 billion to license Character.AI's models, letting investors cash out at a profit, and co-founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas rejoined Google; Shazeer later left for OpenAI. ([[2026-10-13-OpenAIs-Bug-and-Bumbles-AI-Powered-Exit#^clm-4d2ace177c08be2be859|cite]])
- The Google and Character.AI deal was one of several 2024 license-and-hire deals, with Microsoft and Inflection AI and Amazon and Adept, structured as licenses rather than acquisitions to avoid antitrust scrutiny; many were investigated anyway. ([[2026-10-13-OpenAIs-Bug-and-Bumbles-AI-Powered-Exit#^clm-d3505e2e9e26cd7d984d|cite]])
- What is left after license-and-hire deals varies: Inflection recently launched Inflection AI Labs, while Adept's website has not been updated since mid-2024, shortly after its Amazon deal. ([[2026-10-13-OpenAIs-Bug-and-Bumbles-AI-Powered-Exit#^clm-e4acafcc08e30ba248e8|cite]])
- In September 2026 Disney hired Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand, formerly of Meta, as chief technology officer along with other Character.AI technical staff, and it has bid $20 million to license Character.AI's IP in the bankruptcy. ([[2026-10-13-OpenAIs-Bug-and-Bumbles-AI-Powered-Exit#^clm-4b1baec843a188664fc1|cite]])
