# Fast Fashion

Making clothes in small batches that follow current trends and reach stores in weeks instead of months. Sell what customers are buying now, make less of what they are not, and restock often. Zara built the model; Shein pushed it further online with test batches of a hundred or so pieces.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch09 Shein and Temu - E-commerce Giants from Asia]]

- Shein posted $23 billion in sales in 2022, making it the world's number two fashion firm, just ahead of H&M ($22.25 billion) and behind only Inditex ($35.2 billion). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-eb1020c431c142eb4992|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues Shein has out-Zara'ed Inditex, using AI and analytics to automate design and information systems to unbundle much of Zara's vertically integrated supply chain in ways that skirt import restrictions and lower costs. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-1a51b9431972fd508f84|cite]])
- Temu, an upstart launched in China, mimics large portions of the Shein model; it began with a wide variety of goods rather than clothing but has started a money-losing assault on fast fashion. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-074680dc6a55869cba08|cite]])
- Shein can go from spotting a trend to shipping a product in as little as three days, faster than Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-78bd346ab58cdf05b517|cite]])
- Shein introduces some 10,000 new styles every single day, an outrageous figure even for fast fashion, and may have as many as 600,000 items on its site at a time. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-d0558a1c1ca88da1cc96|cite]])
- A Key Takeaway in the chapter says Shein's data and AI operation lets it create thousands of new products a year, many more than even Zara. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-490e4fdd8601e105b4dd|cite]])
- Shein's produced lots are tiny, sometimes fewer than 100 goods, and with no stores it holds little warehouse stock and very little unsold inventory, in an industry whose saying is 'inventory = death'. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-30ed95db5c207a7fb133|cite]])
- Unlike Zara, which gathers in-store demand and mimics catwalk fashions, Shein ignores the runway and responds to consumer trends mined from social media, ads, and app use. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-178256e79d817026ce26|cite]])
- Andreessen Horowitz partner Connie Chan sees Shein as a new phase of fast fashion where consumers care less if Vogue thinks a piece is cool. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-e42acf6be463fc28732d|cite]])
- Shein calls its model of real-time data, trial test, then more units if warranted the large-scale automated test and reorder (LATR) model. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-9bf55c96b797b393b8ff|cite]])
- Nearly all products in the Shein app have been live three months or less, while at Zara about 53 percent of products are less than three months old. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-54dfb87288c91cd5fb19|cite]])
- Only about one-third of all fast fashion sales happen online, suggesting upside for Shein, Temu, and rivals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-6f7b5db450891b9d5b18|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch02 Zara - Fast Fashion from Savvy Systems]]

- Unlike rivals, Zara shuns advertising, rarely runs sales, and keeps much of its production in-house while nearly every other major player outsources to low-cost countries. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f77d27c9462bff481944|cite]])
- Zara stores get a Z-day delivery of new clothing lines twice a week, which fans eagerly await. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-8952f29e2a18da61ca1f|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that Zara's efficient model yields competitive prices, higher margins, fewer markdowns and write-offs, faster inventory turnover, lower risk and less advertising. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-5a53d7f622849db46a90|cite]])
- Zara's chairman and CEO said its business model is the opposite of the traditional one: instead of designing a collection long before the season, Zara first learns what customers like, then designs and produces it. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-95c1b4b2850247a0e0f9|cite]])
- About 300 Zara designers at The Cube produce 30,000 items a year (2014), versus 2,000 to 4,000 items at big chains like H&M and Gap. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ced18144998f9d1659a4|cite]])
- A Zara concept takes an average of fifteen days from idea to store, and small tweaks like a round neck instead of a V neck can reach stores in ten days (2003), while rivals get new styles once or twice a season. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-f0d45c8c6e4cc661dce6|cite]])
- Zara is twelve times faster than Gap while offering roughly ten times more unique products (2002). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ef2dd82f518e081599e6|cite]])
- H&M takes three to five months from creation to delivery and is considered one of the best; other retailers average six months to design and three to manufacture, VF Corporation can take nine months to design jeans, and J.Jill a year. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-022a8d74808be35be7f0|cite]])
- Most products in Zara stores did not exist three weeks earlier, not even as sketches, and an analyst likened Zara to a speedy chameleon that adapts instantly to trends. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-7341cc56b03b54476da3|cite]])
- Zara makes most products in limited runs, so each store carries items tailored to local tastes, unlike Gap, where a Los Angeles store carries nearly the same line as one in Milwaukee. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-a57f3e2094186f160734|cite]])
- Bain & Company estimated the industry average markdown ratio at 50 to 70 percent, and less than 1 percent of JCPenney revenue came from full-price items, while 85 percent of Zara products sell without discount. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-78b81580dd304c9a14d3|cite]])
- Failed product introductions at Zara are about 1 percent versus an industry average of 10 percent (2006), and unsold items are under 10 percent of inventory versus as high as 20 percent industry-wide (2013). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-555a65c1b50f734fb770|cite]])
- As fast fashion's biggest player, Zara is a prime environmental target: the U.S. throws away about 11.3 million tons of textiles a year, and fashion produces 10 percent of global CO2, more than international flights and shipping combined (2022). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-389d001caf7b949dbc5e|cite]])
- H&M has increased how often new items reach stores and is eyeing moving some contract manufacturing from Asia closer to Europe (2017). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-54a218ddfbd99b591c32|cite]])
- Forever 21 and Uniqlo get new looks within six weeks, and Brazil's Renner rolls out mini-collections every two months (2008). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-b4da02a223abfcea82e9|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues the biggest threat to Inditex and all fast-fashion firms is direct-to-consumer retailer Shein and similar rivals like Temu, which use technology to squeeze out costs. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-9bb2512ac9d9ad584524|cite]])
- Shein has added up to 10,000 new items a day, and its sales grew from $10 billion in 2020 to $100 billion in 2022. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-b6a258b872f4961cd52e|cite]])
