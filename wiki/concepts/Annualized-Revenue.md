# Annualized Revenue (ARR)

A guess at a full year's sales, usually one month's revenue multiplied by 12. Fast-growing AI firms quote it because quarterly results look out of date, but it can mislead: one month may not be typical, firms count revenue differently, and the figures often leak through investors rather than formal reports.

## Evidence by source

### 2026-10-08 [[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric|OpenAI ARR Snafu Highlights Flawed Metric]]

- OpenAI generates closer to $50 billion in annualized revenue, not the $70 billion that The Information and others reported the week before. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric#^clm-0752960285dba221ec7f|cite]])
- Peers argues everyone relies too much on annualized revenue, calling one month's revenue times 12 a poor substitute for a fuller picture of performance. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric#^clm-5a46176230a40e5a2106|cite]])
- Annualized revenue became standard for AI companies because their revenue grows so fast that quarterly reports feel backward-looking. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric#^clm-3b32a5561d14e6689afe|cite]])
- OpenAI and Anthropic count revenue differently, especially whether they include the cut paid to cloud firms, which makes ARR comparisons riskier. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric#^clm-3d151359edfb2322a012|cite]])
- AI firms publish ARR only in occasional blog posts, and most ARR news comes from investor communications reaching reporters, sometimes with clumsy calculations. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric#^clm-e5633b260fdf0c37a5f2|cite]])
- Prediction: Peers expects the ARR problem to fade once AI firms go public and report real numbers. ([[2026-10-08-OpenAI-ARR-Snafu-Highlights-Flawed-Metric#^clm-4869441a13a10a7fd54f|cite]])
