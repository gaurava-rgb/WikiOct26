# De Minimis Rule (Import Loophole)

A U.S. customs rule under which shipments worth $800 or less pay no import duty and skip most customs checks; many other nations have similar rules. Retailers that ship each order straight from a foreign factory to a single consumer, like Shein and Temu, stay under the limit, so their goods arrive cheaper and faster than bulk imports that stores bring in.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch09 Shein and Temu - E-commerce Giants from Asia]]

- Prediction: Gallaugher argues it is hard to imagine the U.S. legislature, with its bipartisan critique of China, allowing the de minimis loophole that speeds Shein's direct-to-consumer shipments to continue. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-10e09c8989fc1907bcb3|cite]])
- Under the U.S. de minimis provision, imports are subject to duty only when a shipment is worth over $800, so a Shein order under $800 arrives with no import duty assessed. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-50acc65ac8608b62ac57|cite]])
- China has not taxed exports by Chinese direct-to-consumer companies since 2018. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-b71228bd395d3606e189|cite]])
- An industry pundit says Zara cannot get around U.S. import duties because it sells to stores and imports in bulk rather than shipping to individuals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-2a29e459196ef4e1ed18|cite]])
- Under the U.S. Tariff Act of 1930, orders of $800 or less also skip customs checks, so Shein goods almost always arrive in under two weeks and many in four days. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-1c2410be385b2d234ba5|cite]])
- Lawmakers in the U.S. and other nations are exploring legislation to close the factory-to-consumer de minimis provision, and the lighter inspections raise concern about banned Xinjiang cotton. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-75e5184bf2e51398aad1|cite]])
- Shein is building warehouses in the United States, likely partly in anticipation of having to change its strategy once de minimis laws change. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-a1bbe79c2fe57994a29a|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that because Shein's direct shipments face almost no import testing, the number of dangerous products in circulation could be far larger than recalls suggest. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-fa59b34939203838e2f4|cite]])
- Prediction: Temu uses the same de minimis loophole as Shein, which Gallaugher expects will close at some point, raising shipping costs and lengthening ship times. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu#^clm-a43b1aee2ff96653dc53|cite]])
