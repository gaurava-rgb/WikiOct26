# Channel Conflict

When a firm's would-be partners start to see it as a threat, because it sells competing products through other channels or works closely with their rivals. The partners may stop carrying the firm's goods. Gallaugher argues the side that offers the partners more total value usually wins.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Gallaugher defines channel conflict as potential partners seeing a firm as a threat, because it sells competing products through other channels or works closely with threatening rivals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-de1716543bb0221473ec|cite]])
- Barnes & Noble and other book retailers refused to carry titles from Amazon's publishing arm, a case of channel conflict. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-25e3cafc25ee3395f907|cite]])
- Walmart and Target once sold the Kindle but stopped in 2012, fearing it would pull physical sales to Amazon. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-99851c7a3d28d6856d47|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues the winner in channel conflict is whoever offers more value to the conflicted partners, so as Amazon's scale grows, authors may not care that working with Amazon cuts off other channels. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-396423cff5aec9fc5934|cite]])

- OpenAI told some business partners it will no longer take ChatGPT ads for products that generate images or audio, which compete with ChatGPT's own features. ([[2026-09-09-OpenAI-Restricts-Adobe-and-Other-Competitors-From-Advertising-in-ChatGPT#^clm-31c73ece6850b39caaac|cite]])
