# Capex vs Opex

**In one sentence:** Capex (capital spending) is a big up-front payment for something used for years, like a mainframe, while opex (operating spending) is an ongoing payment, like a software license; Thompson argues moving money within capex is easier than cutting opex. ([[2026-07-15-IBM-Misses-IBMs-Mainframe-Moat-IBMs-Many-AI-Problems|IBM Misses]])

## Why it matters
The AI build-out is capex on a huge scale: Amazon, Google and Microsoft will spend about $700 billion on it in 2026. ([[2026-09-30-Cracks-Emerge-in-AIs-Debt-Fueled-Data-Center-Boom|Data Center Debt]]) That squeezes cash. Microsoft's free cash flow (cash left after running costs and new buildings and gear) fell 20% from a year earlier to $20 billion in the June 2026 quarter. ([[2026-10-13-Microsoft-Cloud-President-Asks-Leaders-to-Find-20-Cost-Cuts|Azure Cost Cuts]]) The trade also runs the other way: one home GPU owner says owning the machine beat paying monthly cloud fees. ([[2026-10-10-GPUs-in-the-Wine-Cellar-Why-Techies-Are-Hoarding-AI-Compute-in-Their-Homes|GPUs in the Wine Cellar]])

## Worked example: IBM's mainframe miss, July 2026
Krishna says clients shifted their quarterly capex to servers, storage and memory in late June to lock in scarce supply, cutting mainframe purchases. ([[2026-07-15-IBM-Misses-IBMs-Mainframe-Moat-IBMs-Many-AI-Problems|IBM Misses]]) Thompson calls this the crowd-out: mainframe money went to other hardware, most likely for AI. ([[2026-07-15-IBM-Misses-IBMs-Mainframe-Moat-IBMs-Many-AI-Problems|IBM Misses]]) So he argues the selloff in SaaS stocks (software sold by subscription) was not clearly deserved, because SaaS is an opex seat license. ([[2026-07-15-IBM-Misses-IBMs-Mainframe-Moat-IBMs-Many-AI-Problems|IBM Misses]])
