# Omnichannel Retail

Running stores, website, and apps as one joined system, so customers can buy online and pick up or return in a store, or find an item online while standing in a shop. The goal is more sales and a smoother experience, and it depends on knowing where every item is.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch02 Zara - Fast Fashion from Savvy Systems]]

- Inditex was a latecomer to e-commerce, but online sales reached 14 percent of revenue by year-end 2019, and Inditex does not report online sales as a separate category. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-105eea6218e59f645846|cite]])
- About two-thirds of Zara online returns are made in-store and one-third of online orders are picked up in-store (2016), with no evidence that online shopping cannibalizes store sales. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-00992f367344e2cc7caa|cite]])
- E-commerce is estimated to make up about a quarter of Zara sales (January 2023). ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-aeada5e552cba4de1028|cite]])
- Zara deployed fifty-two special e-commerce stockrooms globally, each able to deliver to stores or homes. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-ac32c0093820945e5575|cite]])
- Inditex online sales, 14 percent of sales before the pandemic, rose 95 percent in April 2020 and 50 percent that quarter. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-147568aa5d1b9fe87203|cite]])
- Prediction: In 2020 Inditex expected online sales to reach one-quarter of revenue within two years. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-d392e3e47745e8b65c65|cite]])
- Inditex planned to spend over 1 billion euros on e-commerce infrastructure and another 1.7 billion euros to integrate online with stores (2020), largely on homegrown proprietary systems. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara#^clm-dbbf145eeb42e61b1aaa|cite]])
