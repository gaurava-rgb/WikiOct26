# A/B Testing

Showing two random groups of users two versions of something (option A and option B) and measuring which one does better. Online firms can run thousands of these a year because every click and delay is recorded. It lets data, not rank, settle arguments.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- At Amazon data wins arguments: when a senior vice president shot down coder Greg Linden's 'customers who bought this also bought' idea, Linden ran an A/B test that showed recommendations would drive revenue. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-eee368606425bb3d56df|cite]])
- One Amazon test found that a tenth of a second delay in page loading meant a 1 percent drop in customer activity, a clear return on investment for scalable server capacity. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-e23584e45a2a366354f0|cite]])
- Amazon runs some 12,000 experiments a year, which helps make it one of the best converting e-commerce sites. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-0464abcc14c1551b669d|cite]])

### 2026-07-09 [[2026-07-09-Muse-Image-Grok-4-5-Alex-Karp-on-CNBC|Muse Image, Grok 4.5, Alex Karp on CNBC]]

- Thompson argues the most compelling part of Muse Image is advertising: instead of testing 10 or 15 ads, advertisers can generate far more versions and rank them by whether they convert. ([[2026-07-09-Muse-Image-Grok-4-5-Alex-Karp-on-CNBC#^clm-734e6fe7c904ddd25403|cite]])
