# Winning a Commodity Market on Cost

**In one sentence:** In a commodity market (where buyers see every seller's product as the same), the price is set by the costliest seller still needed, so the cheapest sellers keep the gap as profit. ([[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models|Who's Afraid of Chinese Models]])

## Why it matters
Thompson argues that AI "intelligence" for many everyday jobs, like building a basic app, is fast becoming a commodity. ([[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models|Who's Afraid of Chinese Models]]) If so, profit will come from lower costs, not higher prices. ([[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models|Who's Afraid of Chinese Models]]) The costliest seller is the one at risk of going broke from its fixed costs and debt. ([[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models|Who's Afraid of Chinese Models]])

## Worked example: AWS, April 2026
Amazon invented cloud computing and saw it would be a commodity market won on cost. ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability|Amazon's Durability]]) Its home-made Nitro chips took server chores off Intel and AMD chips, so each server could run more customers' virtual machines (rented slices of one computer). ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability|Amazon's Durability]]) In April 2026 Andy Jassy said Trainium would save Amazon tens of billions of dollars of capex (money spent on data centers and machines) each year. ([[2026-04-30-Amazon-Earnings-Trainium|Amazon earnings, Trainium]]) He also expected an operating margin edge of several hundred basis points (a few cents more profit per dollar of sales). ([[2026-04-30-Amazon-Earnings-Trainium|Amazon earnings, Trainium]]) Thompson agrees that if Trainium makes AI output more cheaply than Nvidia chips, AWS profits grow. ([[2026-04-30-Amazon-Earnings-Trainium|Amazon earnings, Trainium]])

## Where analysts disagree
- In July 2026 Thompson called the Chinese price threat overblown, since shortages let Anthropic and OpenAI charge well above a full-supply price ([[2026-07-20-Whos-Afraid-of-Chinese-Models|Who's Afraid of Chinese Models]]). In September Bloomberg reported cheaper Chinese open models were pushing Anthropic on price ([[2026-09-02-Fable-5-1-Enterprise-Frontier-Safeguards|Fable 5.1]]).
- Jensen Huang argued power is the real limit, so output per watt beats chip cost; Thompson gives three reasons this may not hold for Amazon ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability|Amazon's Durability]]).
