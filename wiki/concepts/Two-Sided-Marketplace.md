# Two-Sided Marketplace (Platform)

**In one sentence:** A business that connects two groups, like shoppers and outside sellers, so that more of one side attracts more of the other. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.243)

## Why it matters
A marketplace lets a firm offer a huge range of goods without the risk of owning stock that may not sell. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.254) Selling other firms' goods earns far better margins (the share of each sale kept as profit) than buying and reselling them. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.238) That is why Shein opened to outside brands, trading less revenue per item for usually far greater profits. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu|Gallaugher, Shein and Temu]], p.322)

## Worked example: Amazon Marketplace, 2023
Amazon has over 2.1 million active outside sellers, who make up over 60 percent of goods sold on Amazon. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.253) Because they buy so many must-have services from Amazon, roughly one of every two dollars of their sales goes to Amazon. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.253) Gallaugher argues sellers who skip Amazon miss first look in more than half of online product searches. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.257) The power has a cost: the FTC (the US consumer-protection regulator) says Amazon buries sellers who offer lower prices elsewhere. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Amazon]], p.300)
