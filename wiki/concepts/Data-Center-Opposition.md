# Local Opposition to Data Centers

Growing local pushback against building data centers, especially for AI. Neighbors worry about power bills, water, noise, and land, and also about AI taking jobs. Because a data center needs local permission to be built, towns and counties can slow or block the AI build-out.

## Evidence by source

### 2026-05-18 [[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent|Data Center Discontent, Understanding the Opposition, Fixing the Problem]]

- Hill County, Texas, voted 3-2 to put a one-year moratorium on data center and power plant construction in unincorporated areas, citing up to eight planned data centers. A moratorium is a temporary ban. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-c447b57d4c1518d18911|cite]])
- In Missouri, a small town unhappy with its city council's approval of a data center voted out all four incumbents running for reelection. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-1d16b3c93fe7f91fdaca|cite]])
- North Carolina Governor Josh Stein says sales tax exemptions for data centers cost the state up to $57 million a year. A sales tax exemption means the company skips the state's tax on things it buys. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-7c42ffed24eae01acf4c|cite]])
- Texas has hundreds of data center sites operating or in development, second only to Virginia among US states. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-210e100263a362871521|cite]])
- Some state officials are having second thoughts about data centers and are looking to roll back tax incentives. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-d350912e9e998d417326|cite]])
- A Gallup poll found 71% of Americans oppose building AI data centers in their area, 48% strongly, while only 7% strongly favor them. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-9067680c25cd99433361|cite]])
- In the same Gallup survey, 53% oppose a local nuclear plant, and nuclear opposition has never been above 63% since Gallup began asking in 2001. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-34aa796c9d5daec53f88|cite]])
- Majorities of every party oppose a local data center, but Democrats are more likely to be strongly opposed than Republicans (56% vs 39%), with independents at 48%. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-cbf1b169c46aeac38884|cite]])
- Thompson argues that not building out nuclear power fifty years ago was a huge mistake that feeds data center opposition today, through worries about energy bills. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-9ab6aee4d22fca8360ea|cite]])
- Thompson argues there is a lot of misinformation about data center water use, but fixing it through education or calling people stupid will not work, because people believe what they already want to believe. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-5c9fbd4e861f9e4e5aa6|cite]])
- Thompson argues there was little opposition when data centers were built for the general Internet, so today's opposition cannot be separated from fear of AI taking jobs. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-578d8d714b8640c48be0|cite]])
- Thompson contrasts AI with globalization: towns had no say when factories moved to China, but data centers need permission, so people now get a say and use it to block them. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-6bd4d53fc4261fd9dd4b|cite]])
- Thompson argues tech has no constituency in partisan politics: the left opposes Big Tech, and the right's base hates Big Tech almost as much, after what it saw as censorship during COVID. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-77f751bff91fc9aadf00|cite]])
- Thompson argues tech's messaging is hard to fix: many at leading AI labs truly believe most jobs will go away, future benefits are hard to describe, and tech has always been bad at relating to the rest of society. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-3a9e55b05944e3eb9cb9|cite]])
- Thompson notes TikTok is a major vector for data center opposition, and its algorithm is still controlled by a Chinese company, while China has an interest in the US not building more AI. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-43df69af8d2bd17403cd|cite]])
- Thompson argues Meta has learned not to overtly censor misinformation again, and so will not defend its own interests on data centers. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-2b048fa2087d899e0306|cite]])
- X is now owned by SpaceX, and Thompson argues X has an interest in being anti-datacenter because SpaceX's upcoming IPO pitch includes building data centers in space. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-6dbd4e53f39759adf4d3|cite]])
- Meta goes beyond stressing local economic benefits of its data centers, including funding workforce training, but Thompson argues this is not concrete enough to offset AI anxiety. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-a1d8d2298a88e82c2e2b|cite]])
- Thompson argues the best fix is to pay nearby residents directly, such as a yearly check, rather than promise tax benefits that local governments may not spend well. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-c61d7909eb527396d987|cite]])
- The planned DeForest, Wisconsin data center was expected to be 1.6 GW and could bring in about $3 billion a year in operator revenue; the village has about 11,500 people. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-4080c2c93e2d388e3eb8|cite]])
- Thompson calculates that paying every DeForest resident $10,000 a year would cost 3.8% of the data center's annual revenue, and says operators could pass that cost on to users. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-61e929d1ee7ff87830a6|cite]])
- Thompson calls QTS's $50 million community commitment for its Dane County data center relatively pathetic next to that math. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-231051d37e6a5dcb42c3|cite]])
- Prediction: Thompson predicts that once Big Tech pays people off for data centers, the US will have a kludgy, AI-funded Universal Basic Income, which will get data centers built and drive growth. Universal Basic Income means regular cash paid to everyone with no strings. ([[2026-05-18-Data-Center-Discontent#^clm-dc56479a88543fddf5b6|cite]])
