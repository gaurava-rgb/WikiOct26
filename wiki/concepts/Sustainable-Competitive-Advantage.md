# Sustainable Competitive Advantage

**In one sentence:** An edge rivals cannot easily copy, which usually comes from many parts of a business working together rather than from technology alone. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect|Gallaugher, FreshDirect]], p.126)

## Why it matters
Rivals can buy the same software, so the edge must come from something harder to get, like customer data built up over years. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect|Gallaugher, FreshDirect]], p.119) Walmart custom builds much of its own systems to keep competitors off its trail. ([[2025-Gallaugher-S17-7-Walmart|Gallaugher, Walmart]], p.643) The foil is Shein: Gallaugher doubts its edge lasts, since Temu shows the model can be copied. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch09-Shein-and-Temu|Gallaugher, Shein and Temu]], p.325)

## Worked example: Zara, 2012 to 2025
Gallaugher argues Zara has held a lasting edge for two decades through a tech-heavy chain that runs from customer demand to design to making the clothes. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Zara]], p.85) In 2012 a Gap executive said he would love to organize Gap like Zara's owner, Inditex, but would have to knock the company down and rebuild it from scratch. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Zara]], p.74) Clothing has far more new models than computers do, so Zara's system may be harder to copy than Dell's. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Zara]], p.87) Still, Gallaugher warns it is hard to copy but not invulnerable, so managers must keep watching for threats. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch02-Zara|Gallaugher, Zara]], p.88)
