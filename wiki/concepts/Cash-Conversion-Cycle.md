# Cash Conversion Cycle

The number of days between paying suppliers for goods and collecting cash from customers for them. A negative cycle means the firm gets paid by customers before it pays suppliers, so suppliers in effect fund its growth with free short-term loans. Fast-selling inventory and slow supplier payment terms push the cycle down.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch04 FreshDirect - A Tech-Heavy Online Grocer]]

- FreshDirect's inventory turns 197 times a year versus 40 times a year at traditional grocers, so it sells goods faster and collects cash sooner than rivals. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-0e75d7632e77cafaac62|cite]])
- In exchange for better terms, FreshDirect offers suppliers more shelf selection with no slotting fees, cobranding, payment in days rather than weeks, and shared data. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch04-FreshDirect#^clm-709c4b80051408a1af98|cite]])

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Retailers like Amazon, Barnes & Noble, and Best Buy log incoming inventory as accounts payable and often hold goods a month or more before paying, while customers pay right away; the gap is the cash conversion cycle. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-9a973ef130ec341145ed|cite]])
- Barnes & Noble reported inventory sitting on shelves sixty-eight days on average before sale, and Best Buy has held inventory as long as seventy days. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-41e6d1456101baf8749e|cite]])
- Costco and Walmart sell goods quickly but still pay for inventory before it sells; among peers, Amazon alone consistently reports a negative cash conversion cycle, collecting from customers weeks before paying suppliers. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-7658105bf0c043a304c8|cite]])
- Amazon's negative cash conversion cycle gives it an extra pool of cash for expanding operations and investing; its goal is to keep inventory turns high and pay suppliers later. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-17d6df585ac0fb0232e2|cite]])
- When Amazon switched to deep learning models for demand forecasting, its forecast accuracy improved fifteen-fold, which helps its cash conversion cycle by reordering stock at the right time. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-c09528e083386cf7b49a|cite]])
- Amazon's cash conversion cycle for Q4 2020 was almost negative 25 days, while Macy's was positive 71 days, paying for goods that sat about two and a half months. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-18c773f21986205b3f04|cite]])
