# First and Best Customer (Amazon Primitives)

Amazon's pattern: build a basic service (a primitive) for its own use, so Amazon is the first and best customer that justifies the cost, then sell the same service to other companies to gain scale. AWS, Amazon's logistics, and the Leo satellite network follow this path.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Amazon provides warehousing and inventory services to its own sellers through Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) and began offering them to third-party clients in the United Kingdom, a threat to the logistics businesses of FedEx and UPS. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-435e2ec243175d6cdef9|cite]])
- Prediction: Some analysts believe Amazon could grow a third-party logistics business into its next AWS. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-0bf65194d0de54bb21a2|cite]])
- Amazon's own e-commerce and Kindle operations use the products AWS builds, so they also benefit from the AWS effort. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-bfaf75c8ae80f7bdfb86|cite]])

### 2026-05-05 [[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability|Amazon’s Durability]]

- Amazon unveiled Amazon Supply Chain Services (ASCS) bundling its freight and distribution offerings. ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability#^clm-3fdba35f04dc8c462986|cite]])
- Amazon Supply Chain Services packages air and ocean freight, trucking, and last-mile delivery for other companies, and sent FedEx and UPS shares lower. ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability#^clm-9af9229107d6d938e48d|cite]])
- Thompson says ASCS fulfills his 2016 'The Amazon Tax' prediction that Amazon would sell its logistics network to third parties, the same way it sells AWS. ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability#^clm-c3f9f5f964575520904f|cite]])
- Thompson argues Amazon works on decade-long timeframes, converting marginal costs into capital costs and then selling that capacity to other businesses. ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability#^clm-7cbe960648324f6453cd|cite]])
- Andy Jassy said Amazon Leo has over 20 launches planned in 2026 and over 30 in 2027, and could be a many-billion-dollar business with AWS-like economics. ([[2026-05-05-Amazons-Durability#^clm-249e7fd5715485171fa8|cite]])
