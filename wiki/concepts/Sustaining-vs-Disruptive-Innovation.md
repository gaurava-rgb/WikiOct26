# Sustaining vs Disruptive Innovation

Clayton Christensen's split from The Innovator's Dilemma. A sustaining innovation makes existing products better in the ways current customers already value, so incumbents tend to get stronger. A disruptive innovation starts out worse for mainstream customers but is cheaper, simpler, or more convenient for a few new ones, so startups tend to capture the value.

### 2026-08-24 [[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation|Autonomy and Innovation]]

- Prediction: Thompson predicts the winners in each category will be AI-native startups driven fully by AI, not incumbents using AI as a productivity enhancer. ([[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation#^clm-afd6ade34ca2f9d87db4|cite]])
- In 2023 Thompson asked in AI and the Big Five whether AI would be a sustaining or disruptive innovation, using Christensen's The Innovator's Dilemma. ([[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation#^clm-2319c50e3fffba359961|cite]])
- Thompson argues AI is set up to be both sustaining and disruptive; for software developers using it is increasingly a necessity rather than a choice. ([[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation#^clm-923d022039c56f5a4429|cite]])
- Thompson argues that in established companies risk-avoidance will limit AI to a sustaining role and nothing more. ([[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation#^clm-b5a6e989fec161addf67|cite]])
- Thompson argues startups, whose base case is failure, gain from fully leaning into AI, acting like offensive hackers against incumbents who keep humans in the loop too long. ([[2026-08-24-Autonomy-and-Innovation#^clm-8651bbeae13aecaa7f8d|cite]])
