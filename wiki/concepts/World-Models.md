# World Models

AI models that learn how the physical world works (how things move, fall, and push on each other) so they can simulate it, not just describe it in words. Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind argues much of this knowledge is hard to put into text, so language models miss it. He sees generating realistic video and interactive worlds as proof of that understanding, and as a path to robots, everyday assistants, and AGI.

## Evidence by source

### 2026-05-20 [[2026-05-20-Google-IO-World-Models-IO-Spaghetti|Google I/O, World Models, I/O Spaghetti]]

- Although Sundar Pichai featured Demis Hassabis in his promotional tweet, Hassabis was barely in the keynote; he first appeared to present Gemini Omni, a world model whose first product, Omni Flash, targets video editing and generation. ([[2026-05-20-Google-IO-World-Models-IO-Spaghetti#^clm-ed07533ad80f8b3ab4fb|cite]])
- Hassabis called Gemini Omni a step change in simulating physics like kinetic energy and gravity, starting with video but aiming to generate any output from any input. ([[2026-05-20-Google-IO-World-Models-IO-Spaghetti#^clm-975cffd5f55e12297bef|cite]])
- Hassabis argued in December 2025 that language models understand more of the world than he expected, but spatial and physical dynamics are hard to put into words, so robotics and universal assistants need world models. ([[2026-05-20-Google-IO-World-Models-IO-Spaghetti#^clm-ea5dee3ab8a65678b18d|cite]])
- Hassabis argues that generating realistic worlds is the test of world understanding, which is why he sees Veo and Genie as steps toward generalized world models. ([[2026-05-20-Google-IO-World-Models-IO-Spaghetti#^clm-be83e43a9a63d7a4963f|cite]])
- Thompson says DeepMind instead believes the gap is real-world data best gathered through realistic simulation, which yields multimodal models, unlike text-only Claude, but much weaker coding. ([[2026-05-20-Google-IO-World-Models-IO-Spaghetti#^clm-0b7a619ed4fb895493ae|cite]])

### 2026-10-19 [[2026-10-19-Sonia-Joseph-on-Interpretability-and-Physical-AI|Interpretability Is the Key to Physical AI, Says World Mechanics CEO Sonia Joseph]]

- Joseph contrasts an East Coast definition of world models centered on neuroscience and internal physical representations against a West Coast definition centered on photorealistic video generation and robot control. ([[2026-10-19-Sonia-Joseph-on-Interpretability-and-Physical-AI#^clm-347df0685ca2a4d51d6c|cite]])
- Joseph argues accurate and safe world models require internal neural net representations that are disentangled, causal, and map functionally to physical mechanics. ([[2026-10-19-Sonia-Joseph-on-Interpretability-and-Physical-AI#^clm-9870e3c983358df90583|cite]])
- Prediction: Joseph predicts physical AI will diverge from language models because multi-sensor environments require diverse, specialized models rather than a single dominant foundation model. ([[2026-10-19-Sonia-Joseph-on-Interpretability-and-Physical-AI#^clm-3a2ffc172e9bd3c0ea07|cite]])
- Physical AI differs fundamentally from language AI because the physical world possesses mathematical and physical ground truth like Newtonian mechanics. ([[2026-10-19-Sonia-Joseph-on-Interpretability-and-Physical-AI#^clm-42043d39c9b109853573|cite]])
- Meta developed the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with Yann LeCun to predict abstract object representations in latent space instead of predicting raw pixels. ([[2026-10-19-Sonia-Joseph-on-Interpretability-and-Physical-AI#^clm-dc8609ae59941df3b25a|cite]])
