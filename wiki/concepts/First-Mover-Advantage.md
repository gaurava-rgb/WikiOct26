# First-Mover Advantage

The edge a firm can gain by entering a market before rivals. An early lead with a good product can bring big market share, customer lock-in, lessons learned inside the firm, and network effects from partners and skilled workers who build around it. Being first only pays if the firm uses the head start to build assets rivals cannot quickly copy.

## Evidence by source

### 2025 [[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon|Gallaugher, Information Systems v10 (2025): Ch08 Amazon - An Empire from Cardboard Box to Kindle to Cloud]]

- Google's rival App Engine did not arrive until 2008 and Microsoft's Azure cloud platform came out in 2010, giving AWS a multi-year head start. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-bd0a33cfd7b85b11f8ff|cite]])
- Jeff Bezos has described AWS as having a seven-year head start before facing serious, like-minded competition. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-344effddeb146ff7f8b3|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues AWS's early lead as a platform provider gave it big share, customer lock-in, internal learning, and a network effect from complements such as a growing pool of AWS-skilled developers and software built for the platform. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-abe600c67a2a9979bce0|cite]])
- Gallaugher argues that many firms had tried utility computing, but Amazon was the first to get the cloud model right and deploy it at large scale. ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-73cd72f9d8940978b120|cite]])
- Warren Buffett, praising Jeff Bezos for building two major industries at once, said: "You do not want to give Jeff Bezos a 7-year head start." ([[2025-Gallaugher-Ch08-Amazon#^clm-644bdcebe50f8b37b198|cite]])
