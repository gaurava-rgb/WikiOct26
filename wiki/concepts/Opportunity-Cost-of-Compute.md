# Opportunity Cost of Compute

Thompson's idea that AI compute is scarce, so every chip a company uses for one purpose is a chip it cannot rent to someone else. Firms with a cloud or enterprise business give up paying customers when they spend compute on free consumer products. A firm with no cloud business, like Meta, gives up nothing, so it can focus all its compute on consumers.

- Thompson argues the Anthropic and SpaceX deal is basic economics: when demand exceeds supply prices rise, and Anthropic has the most demand and the most willingness to pay, from both revenue and its ability to raise money. - Thompson argues that because AI hardware is in short supply, the best strategy for a hardware owner is to sell to the widest set of customers to find the highest willingness to pay, rather than differentiate it with its own software. ([[2026-05-12-SpaceX-and-Anthropic-xAIs-Two-Companies-Elon-Musk-and-SpaceXAIs-Future#^clm-ab4eb408d417e5a38ef6|cite]])
- xAI's Colossus 1 has been reported to run at only 11% utilization, and Thompson argues that even full use by xAI's own models would earn less than opening the hardware to the market. - Prediction: Thompson argues Musk should also open Colossus 2 to outside customers, as he did with Colossus 1. ([[2026-05-12-SpaceX-and-Anthropic-xAIs-Two-Companies-Elon-Musk-and-SpaceXAIs-Future#^clm-3df9daf4769eedf6e9da|cite]])
