# Notes: Six OpenAI Rivals Google and Microsoft Are Watching

The Information, Jon Victor, 2023-01-09 (exclusive). Amir Efrati contributed.

**Old article.** All facts below are as of January 2023, not today.

## The setup

- Microsoft's bet on OpenAI was starting to look very smart. Microsoft planned to use OpenAI's tech to improve Bing search and to help Word and Outlook users draft documents and emails from simple prompts.
- But OpenAI was not the only startup building language-understanding software that Google, Amazon or Meta might want to license, partner with or buy.
- Top researchers from OpenAI, DeepMind and Google had left to start or join six rival startups. Together they had raised more than $1 billion in equity funding (money from investors in exchange for ownership).
- They build their own large language models (AI trained on huge amounts of text). Training can cost millions of dollars in computing. They plan to license the models, as OpenAI does, or build chatbot apps.
- Excluded from the list: Hugging Face (hosts open-source models but does not build them) and Neeva (a paid search engine not investing as much in frontier models).

## Who might buy, and why

- Vin Vashishta (founder, AI consulting firm V Squared): companies like Salesforce or VMware could buy one of these startups to sell AI apps alongside their products. Netflix or Disney could buy one and train models on their film and TV libraries to generate scripts or animation. Netflix and Disney did not respond.
- Stanford's Center for Research on Foundation Models found models from AI21 Labs, Cohere and Anthropic were less likely to produce toxic answers than OpenAI's GPT-3 (released June 2020). OpenAI had since released GPT-3.5, which was not in the test.
- Cristóbal Valenzuela (CEO, Runway ML) said if language models become a commodity (a product where every seller's version is about the same), winning comes down to cost: "Whoever can run the cheapest and most effective model will win."
- Open-source models were a threat, since any developer could use them without licensing.

## The six startups

- **Adept AI Labs** (founded 2021, San Francisco). A browser extension that does tasks from plain-language instructions, like building a list of apartment listings. $66M raised (Greylock, Addition), 20 staff, nominal revenue. CEO David Luan led Google's large-model work and was earlier a VP of engineering at OpenAI. Pitch: automate tedious office work in Excel and similar tools. Luan wants Adept to be "the interface to your computer." Update Jan. 13: two co-founders left to start their own company.
- **AI21 Labs** (2017, Tel Aviv). Products: AI21 Studio (developers customize models), Wordtune (copywriting), Wordtune Read (summaries). $128M raised (Walden Catalyst, Ahren). Valued at $750M in July 2022. 160 staff. Over $20M in annual recurring revenue (yearly subscription revenue run rate); the CMO said it ended 2022 in the "tens of millions." Chair Amnon Shashua co-founded Mobileye (Intel bought it for $15.3B in 2017). Co-CEO Ori Goshen says its models are smaller and cheaper to run. Customers include Ubisoft and Capgemini.
- **Anthropic** (2021, San Francisco). General-purpose models with a focus on safety (avoiding bias and toxicity). $704M raised. Backers included Sam Bankman-Fried, Caroline Ellison and Jaan Tallinn. Valued at $4B in April 2022 (per PitchBook). 80 staff, nominal revenue. Siblings Dario Amodei (CEO) and Daniela Amodei (President) were OpenAI VPs during GPT-3. Registered as a public benefit corporation (a company legally bound to a public mission as well as profit). Would not say how FTX's collapse affected those investments. Planned to open API access (a way for other software to use its models) with "a small set of partners" in 2023, and was looking to raise hundreds of millions more (per one person).
- **Character** (2021, Palo Alto). Users create and chat with role-playing bots (for example Draco Malfoy or Sigmund Freud). Funding unknown; angels include Elad Gil, Paul Buchheit, Nat Friedman, SV Angel. 18 staff, no revenue, free product. Founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas built Google's LaMDA and left over red tape. Planned to raise more in early 2023.
- **Cohere** (2019, Toronto). An API for summarizing, copywriting and search. $170M raised (Radical, Index, Tiger Global). Valued at $800M to $900M in February 2022 (per one person). 175 staff. Has a strategic partnership with Google and reportedly talked with Google and Nvidia about investment (per The Wall Street Journal). CEO Aidan Gomez co-invented the transformer (the core design behind GPT-style models). YouTube finance chief Martin Kon joined as president and COO.
- **Inflection** (2022, Palo Alto). Said little publicly and declined interviews. $225M raised (Greylock). Valued at $1.23B in April 2022 (per PitchBook). 20 staff, no product yet. CEO Mustafa Suleyman co-founded DeepMind (Google bought it for over $500M in 2014); WSJ reported in 2021 he lost most management duties after bullying complaints, then became a Google VP. Co-founders: Karén Simonyan and LinkedIn co-founder Reid Hoffman.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- A starting-line snapshot for the model labs vs clouds theme: in Jan 2023, Anthropic was an 80-person lab worth $4B with nominal revenue.
- Early sign of the AI economics argument: models as a commodity, where the cheapest good model wins.
- Agents were already a pitch in 2023 (Adept's browser automation), years before the 2024 to 2026 agent push.
