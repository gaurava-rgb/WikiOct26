# Notes: Apple’s Antitrust Settlement Talks Spur Worries Among Developers

The Information, Aaron Tilley, 2026-10-16.

## Developer alarms over potential DOJ settlement

- As the U.S. Department of Justice negotiates a settlement in its antitrust lawsuit against Apple, major software developers including PayPal and Epic Games have raised alarms that an agreement will not adequately resolve anticompetitive conduct.
- In private discussions with developers, Justice Department officials signaled an active preference for settling the lawsuit instead of pursuing a protracted court trial.
- Developers and antitrust attorneys fear the government will grant Apple lenient terms that impose modest interoperability requirements on iPhone software and hardware integrations.

## Core interoperability issues under negotiation

- Settlement negotiations center on iPhone interoperability with third-party devices and payment services.
- The 2024 government lawsuit alleged Apple maintained an illegal smartphone monopoly by restricting rival devices and apps, pointing out that Apple Watch connects to iPhones more seamlessly than competing smartwatches.
- While Apple opened NFC tap-to-pay hardware to third-party payment services like PayPal, developers report the feature remains less responsive and seamless than Apple Pay.
- Government attorneys asked app developers to submit concrete technical proposals to improve iPhone interoperability with competing hardware.
- Apple previously adopted Rich Communication Services messaging to ease cross-platform messaging between Android and iPhone users following the 2024 lawsuit.

## Leadership changes in the Antitrust Division

- Following Donald Trump taking office in January 2025, aggressive antitrust enforcement advocate Gail Slater led the division until stepping down in February 2025 after clashes over dealmaking policies.
- Stanley Woodward assumed interim leadership in June 2025, advocating settlements on the grounds that negotiated agreements deliver consumer benefits faster and avoid years of expensive litigation.
- The White House nominated Adam Candeub to direct the antitrust division permanently, with confirmation pending in the Senate.

## Parallel litigation and developer resistance

- In parallel litigation, U.S. District Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers ruled last year that Apple willfully violated a 2021 injunction in the Epic Games case, referring the matter to federal prosecutors for potential criminal contempt proceedings.
- Apple is appealing that ruling to the Supreme Court.
- Privacy-focused online service maker Proton, which sued Apple in 2025 over App Store rules, expressed concern that the Justice Department may finalize an inadequate settlement before Candeub assumes office.
