# Notes: China's AI Model Race Is Just Getting More Crowded

The Information, Juro Osawa (Jing Yang contributed), 2026-10-09.

## The core story

- Three big Chinese tech names from outside AI are now building their own large language models (LLMs, the text AI behind chatbots) from scratch.
- The author's analogy: imagine Epic Games, Apple and DoorDash each building LLMs to fight OpenAI and Anthropic.
- miHoYo, the Shanghai game maker behind Genshin Impact, is building LLMs and wants to be a top-tier lab in China within the next couple of years.
- Xiaomi, a top smartphone maker that also sells electric cars, released its latest LLM two weeks before publication.
- Meituan, China's biggest food delivery and local services app, unveiled a stronger LLM in June 2026, a step up from the one it launched last year.
- The new entrants will further intensify a domestic race where incumbents and upstarts fight hard for talent and on price.
- That competition has already produced models that have significantly narrowed the gap with the best U.S. models.

## How China differs from the U.S.

- In the U.S., OpenAI and Anthropic hold a clear technical lead over a few followers, including Meta, SpaceX and Google.
- In China, nobody is giving up, yet no one has pulled meaningfully ahead.
- The three giants, ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent, still race to build models, paid for by profits from e-commerce, social media and other businesses.
- Five startups earn recurring revenue from their own models.
- Two of them, Zhipu (also called Z.ai) and MiniMax, went public in Hong Kong in January 2026.
- The other three, DeepSeek, Moonshot and StepFun, are preparing to go public soon.
- Model leaderboards often reshuffle whenever any of these eight companies releases a new model.

## miHoYo

- miHoYo plans to invest up to 100 billion yuan ($14.9 billion) in AI development over the next three years.
- It is trying to get access to Nvidia's advanced Blackwell chips to train more competitive models, like leading Chinese AI firms do, per a person with direct knowledge.
- Co-founder Cai Haoyu, its largest shareholder and the mind behind its hit games, oversees the foundation model team, per the same person. A foundation model is the big general model other products are built on.

## Xiaomi

- Founder and CEO Lei Jun has talked publicly about putting the company's MiMo models into its gadgets and cars.
- Xiaomi made a splash last year (2025) by poaching a star researcher from DeepSeek.
- Unlike Apple, which partnered with Google for the models behind Apple Intelligence, Xiaomi builds AI for its devices on its own.
- Its newest flagship, MiMo-V2.6-Pro, released in September 2026, ranks ninth on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (a public model leaderboard).
- That puts it on par with xAI's Grok 4.7 and ahead of the flagship models Alibaba and Z.ai launched several weeks earlier.
- Xiaomi declined to comment; miHoYo and Meituan did not reply.

## Why no consolidation

- China is less crowded than in 2023, when countless startups copied ChatGPT in what local media called the "war of 100 models."
- But the consolidation many predicted then has not happened yet.
- Chinese tech firms deeply distrust each other, and executives see building their own AI as cheaper than buying rivals.
- Examples of rivalry: Meituan and Alibaba fight in food and grocery delivery; Alibaba and ByteDance build AI devices that could encroach on Xiaomi; miHoYo and Tencent are videogame rivals.
- Meituan co-founder and CEO Wang Xing said on a 2025 earnings call the only sensible AI strategy is to "play offense" by investing in its own models, not defend its existing business.
- Wang said LongCat 2.0, released in June 2026, gives Meituan a structural advantage in long-term cost control. LongCat 2.0 still trails many Chinese models.
- Bernstein senior analyst Robin Zhu: Chinese founders worry that lacking their own models could leave them vulnerable if they lose access to outside platforms.

## Distillation levels the field

- Another reason so many can compete is distillation: training your own model on outputs from frontier models such as those of Anthropic and OpenAI, per employees of Chinese AI labs.
- U.S. export controls on advanced chips pushed many Chinese labs to use distillation as a shortcut.
- Relying on distillation could make it harder for labs to stand apart, but well-known techniques and resources also level the playing field for every Chinese competitor.

## No big M&A in China

- The lack of mergers and acquisitions (M&A, one company buying another) also keeps the field crowded.
- Huge deals such as AMD's recent $8 billion purchase of Fei-Fei Li's World Labs, or SpaceX's $60 billion takeover of Cursor, are unheard of in China's AI boom.
- Alibaba and Tencent have taken minority stakes in many Chinese AI labs but have not bought any.
- Rui Ma, angel investor and founder of the Tech Buzz China newsletter: Chinese tech firms have been less willing than U.S. Big Tech to pay large strategic premiums to absorb startups. A premium is paying above market value.
- Ma says China developed "an IPO-or-nothing mentality," while in Silicon Valley acquisitions are a normal growth path often rewarded by investors.
- Ma: that makes it hard for a buyer to offer a price good enough to sell, and it reinforces itself, since buyers who never expect sellers never build acquisition and integration skills.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: China's model race widens rather than consolidates, with non-AI firms (a game maker, a phone maker, a delivery app) joining in.
- OpenAI and Anthropic journeys: Chinese labs keep closing the gap partly by distilling OpenAI and Anthropic model outputs.
- Nvidia journey: even a game maker like miHoYo wants Blackwell chips despite U.S. export controls.
- Apple journey: Xiaomi builds its device AI in-house, the opposite of Apple's partnership with Google for Apple Intelligence.
