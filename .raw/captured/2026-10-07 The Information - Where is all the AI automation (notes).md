# Notes: AI's Simmering Question: "Where the F--- Is All the Automation?"

The Information, Cory Weinberg, 2026-10-07 (The Briefing newsletter, opinion-style column).

## The framing

- AI labs feel a new age of discovery and automation has arrived. Ordinary people may not feel it.
- Hook: OpenAI's late Tuesday announcement that an unreleased model solved, or made major progress on, hundreds of significant math and computer science problems.
- Weinberg finds it hard to separate real technical wins from announcements that keep the AI money train going.
- Context he lists: the math news landed amid reports of OpenAI raising more cash, and a week after its developer day mostly underwhelmed.
- Consumer agents, the latest craze, are hitting websites that want to block bots.
- Economists do not yet see AI making people more productive in major statistics.
- Central question: how do companies turn raw intelligence into useful, provable results?

## The optimist: Scott Wu

- Scene: the Midway, a San Francisco waterfront venue, where nearly 800 developers attended a conference hosted by Modal, a highly valued seller of compute and developer tools.
- Headliner Scott Wu, a former math competition star, founded coding agent startup Cognition, valued at $48 billion last month.
- His slide: "It's time to be ambitious." He described companies full of virtual employees that work on outcomes, not tasks, given enough data centers, memory and sandboxes (safe, isolated spaces where code can run).
- He said agents keep getting more capable and that is the whole trend.
- His turning point was the AIME moment last year, when AI began beating humans on top math contests. "That's when I knew it was all over," meaning humans lost their monopoly on intelligence.

## The skeptic: Diogo Almeida

- Former OpenAI researcher, now runs TypeSafe AI, which launched Jev, a popular alternative to large language models.
- On stage he asked where all the automation is.
- His claim: all of today's LLMs are tuned for assistance through RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback, training a model to please human raters).
- So, in his view, LLMs are built to please a human in the loop: great at helping, poor at running unattended.

## The practitioners

- Cat Wu, head of product for Claude Code at Anthropic, pushes staff to ask which repeated tasks they are still stuck doing.
- She said much automation stalls at a semi-automated stage: Claude does 80% of the work and the person does 20%, over and over.
- Dax Raad, open-source developer behind OpenCode, says his bigger problem is people: engineers are not paying enough attention to AI-written code.
- He wants his team to ask the right questions and feel bad when unchecked code ships. He called it the age-old problem of getting people to care.

## In other news (brief items)

- Elon Musk said SpaceX's AI unit will use some competitor models to power Grok Bot, moving away from only in-house models.
- Microsoft unveiled a push to run AI on Windows PCs instead of the cloud, which it says cuts customer costs.
- Anjney Midha (ex-Andreessen Horowitz general partner) and former Google, Apple and Nvidia executives launched a company to make compute cheaper and easier for smaller firms and startups.
- The U.S. Treasury fined the parent of accelerator Plug and Play Tech Center, the first penalty under an outbound investment rule aimed at China's tech sector.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Agents theme: a clear statement of the gap between agent hype (virtual employees) and reality (80/20 semi-automation, bots blocked by websites).
- AI economics: no productivity signal yet in major statistics, while labs keep raising money on breakthrough news.
- Model labs: the RLHF critique argues today's chat-tuned models are structurally built for assistance, not autonomy.
