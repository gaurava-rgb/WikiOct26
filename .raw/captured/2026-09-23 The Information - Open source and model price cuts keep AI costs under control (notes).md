# Notes: Open Source, Model Price Cuts Keep AI Costs Under Control

The Information Staff, 2026-09-23. Session write-ups from The Information's AI Agenda Live conference in San Francisco.

## The core story

- Speakers said open-source models (models anyone can download and run) and cheaper releases from Anthropic and OpenAI help businesses cut AI costs while getting the same or more out of AI.
- Replit CEO Amjad Masad: open-source models add price pressure on the labs, which he called great.
- But value is unclear. Dan Diasio, EY's global AI leader, said only one in ten companies can show AI return on investment in its profit and loss statement (the report of revenue minus costs). He said most scaling has run on "enthusiasm as opposed to that evidence."

## Uber holds token spend flat (Tiffany Li)

- Uber VP Mattie Toia said token spending (tokens are the text chunks AI is billed by) stayed mostly flat lately despite wider use, after AI coding tools blew through its annual AI budget within months.
- Fixes over three to four months: memory optimization, Uber-specific data, and stronger open-source models.
- Nebius chief revenue officer Marc Boroditsky said firms are moving from "token-maxxing" (using as much AI as possible to look modern) to "value-maxxing."
- Nebius is open to charging by outcome instead of per GPU hour, and has tried spot pricing (a changing price for spare capacity) and auctions.
- Nvidia's Dion Harris said Nvidia drives efficiency mostly through software, citing its open-source Nemotron models.

## Replit: OpenAI price cuts slow open-source use (Kevin McLaughlin)

- Masad said Replit uses open-source models "probably less" now than in January because OpenAI cut prices hard.
- OpenAI this week released GPT-6 Sol and Luna, its second- and third-strongest new models, priced 50% below the same tiers of the prior model.
- Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, which it says costs 40% less to run than its prior top models, Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, while matching or beating them.
- Replit pays OpenAI, Anthropic and others whenever users generate code, so these cuts matter a lot to it.
- Atlassian's chief product and AI officer Tamar Yehoshua warned open-source models carry a non-zero serving cost plus hidden costs in the in-house expertise needed to run them.

## OpenAI: customers barely scratch the surface (Aaron Tilley)

- Alexander Embiricos, product lead for OpenAI's enterprise products, said customers are nowhere near the limit of value; even if models stopped improving, much product work remains.
- OpenAI ships open-ended products and adds features to what sticks; this worked with its $200-a-month Pro plan.
- It has leaned into business sales over the past year as Anthropic's coding tools soared; big firms adopt coding agents first.

## Blackstone may buy AI labs' debt (Dakin Campbell)

- Jas Khaira, head of Blackstone's AI investment team, said Blackstone's venture that buys Google TPUs (Google-designed AI chips) and leases them to developers plans to buy more, given the shortage.
- Three expected funding buckets: public debt markets for investment-grade companies (firms with strong credit ratings), private credit (loans from non-bank investors), and debt from OpenAI and Anthropic after they go public.
- Khaira: "We have not mapped out who's going to buy all the debt." Blackstone may buy lab debt at the right price.

## Block and Cloudflare on AI-driven layoffs (Michael Roddan)

- Block cut nearly half its staff in February. Its agent Builderbot fixes bugs and can build up to 80% of a new feature, said business lead Owen Jennings. The new bottleneck was code review, with too few reviewers for the flood of code.
- Cloudflare cut about 20% of staff in May. Strategy chief Stephanie Cohen said month-end finance decks went from nearly a week to an hour, but sales staff wasted time all vibe-coding (building software by prompting AI) the same tool.

## Neolabs: AI needs reliability (Rocket Drew)

- Jerry Tworek, Core Automation CEO and ex-OpenAI research VP, said models ace math and coding contests but are not much more reliable at everyday work, so people do not trust agents.
- Eric Zelikman, CEO of Humans&, said OpenAI and Anthropic are in a "battle to the death" to automate tasks. Renting Humans&'s two owned chip clusters would have cost over $1 billion.

## Lab economics (Phoebe Liu)

- OpenAI and Anthropic are racing to lock in more than $1 trillion of compute contracts.
- Volta CEO Ricard Boada: frontier labs earn $75 million per megawatt a year versus a levelized cost of compute of $15 million to $20 million. Levelized cost means the full lifetime cost spread evenly per year.
- Volta signed a $10 billion Norway compute deal with a frontier lab, reportedly Anthropic.
- Crusoe's Chris Dolan: a gigawatt site costs $15 billion to $20 billion to build plus $30 billion to $40 billion in chips; payoff needs power and chips delivered on time together.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics beat: the shift from token-maxxing to value-maxxing, with only one in ten firms proving ROI, marks spending discipline arriving.
- Model labs vs clouds beat: OpenAI and Anthropic price cuts (50% and 40%) undercut open source, and labs claim roughly 4x revenue over compute cost.
- Agents beat: agents now write most of a feature at Block, but reliability and code review limit how far firms trust them.
