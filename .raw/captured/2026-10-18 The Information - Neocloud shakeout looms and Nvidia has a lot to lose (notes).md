# Notes: Neocloud Shakeout Looms, and Nvidia Has a Lot to Lose

The Information, Phoebe Liu, 2026-10-18 (AI Infrastructure Newsletter).

## Looming neocloud consolidation and investor skepticism

- Neocloud and data center executives warn of an impending shakeout among specialized AI cloud providers, driven by heavy debt loads and unproven business models.
- Two executives predict that only seven to 10 neoclouds will remain in a few years, compared to hundreds attempting to operate today.
- Research firm Gartner predicts that 80% of independent neoclouds will cease independent operations by 2030.
- Bernstein analysts estimate that only 35% of announced data center capacity in North America will ever actually be built.
- Public market sentiment souring hit the sector: Firmus abruptly pulled its $5.5 billion IPO, while shares of CoreWeave and smaller rivals fell roughly 20%, and Oracle shares declined.
- Neoclouds face cost pressures as each must generate rapid revenue growth to pay off debt borrowed to buy Nvidia hardware.

## Divergence between facility lessees and facility builders

- Not all neocloud business models carry equal risk for Nvidia and the broader market.
- CoreWeave primarily leases data center facilities; if it were to fail, landlords could find replacement tenants for the powered shells.
- In contrast, neoclouds like Nebius, IREN, and Nscale build and own facilities; capital trouble at builders directly halts data center construction and freezes Nvidia chip deliveries.
- Cirrascale chief executive David Driggers noted consolidation is inevitable, but pointed out Cirrascale has rented Nvidia servers for over a decade, produces positive free cash flow, and generates more than $200 million in annual revenue.
- CoreWeave chief executive Michael Intrator criticized rivals Firmus, Lambda, and Nscale in an employee memo, stating they seek massive capital without the execution, scale, or software that CoreWeave delivers.

## Nvidia deep financial entanglement and credit interventions

- Nvidia depends heavily on neoclouds because hyperscalers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Oracle, Meta) are building in-house custom silicon to reduce dependence on Nvidia GPUs.
- In the July 2026 quarter, Nvidia data center revenue from non-hyperscalers (primarily neoclouds and enterprises) hit $40 billion, or 45% of total data center sales, more than doubling year-over-year.
- Nvidia has taken on $20 billion in its own data center leases to sub-lease to neoclouds and customers, as of the July quarter.
- An internal Nvidia team led by Nico Caprez and Raj Mirpuri assists neoclouds in finding data center sites with secured grid power.
- Nvidia modified its credit support terms: after large neoclouds rejected initial offers demanding a share of future revenues, Nvidia now offers shorter-term credit backstops for smaller revenue cuts.
- In Stockholm, Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang tried to soothe market fears, stating CoreWeave will not need further credit backstops beyond an existing agreement to rent back GPUs worth $6.3 billion in rental revenue.
- One power investor noted Anthropic intentionally over-commits to data center lease contracts in case sites fail due to power, labor, or local permitting obstacles.

- Neocloud shakeout and AI infrastructure story: highlights how debt loads, rising rates, and power bottlenecks trigger a culling from hundreds of players to fewer than ten.
- Nvidia journey: reveals Nvidia massive balance sheet exposure ($20 billion in leases, credit backstops, and 45% revenue concentration in non-hyperscalers) as customer hyperscalers design custom silicon.
