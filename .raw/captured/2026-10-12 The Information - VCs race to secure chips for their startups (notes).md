# Notes: Andreessen Horowitz, General Catalyst Race to Secure the Chips Their Startups Need

The Information, Julia Hornstein, Dakin Campbell and Phoebe Liu, 2026-10-12 (exclusive). Laura Bratton contributed.

## The core story

- More venture capital firms are lining up AI compute for their portfolio startups, as startups scramble to get access to AI servers.
- Andreessen Horowitz set up a cluster of 20,000 GPUs (graphics processing units, the main AI chips) for its startups two years ago. It is now in talks to expand that offering, possibly through two data center companies it has backed, per people familiar with its plans.
- General Catalyst is building a data center to supply compute for its startups, per two other people.
- Several other VC funds have used brokers to find scarce chips for their companies.

## Why VCs are doing this

- Jeff Jewett, chief operating officer of Shadeform, said more and more VC firms want to deploy compute across their portfolios. Shadeform rents server capacity from various cloud providers and resells it to customers including AI startups.
- Jewett said compute access will be "absolutely going to be a requirement" to attract the hottest startups.
- The push reflects a shortage of chips built for training and running AI.
- Earlier in 2026, those constraints led Microsoft and other large cloud providers to divert GPUs to their internal teams or bigger customers, so smaller startups found supply harder to get.
- Even for mature startups, compute can be a big share of operating costs.
- VC firms usually supply cash plus softer help like executive introductions and hiring advice. Now more are wading into hardware.
- Daniel Gross and Nat Friedman were among the first, in 2023. They bought advanced Nvidia chips, pooled them, and offered them to portfolio companies at a discount. Their Andromeda Cluster has since pivoted to matching buyers and sellers of compute.

## Andreessen Horowitz: Switch and Volta talks

- Last year Andreessen Horowitz expanded its cluster, first called Oxygen, into multiple private clusters, including chips rented from different cloud providers.
- In recent months the firm discussed partnering with Switch, a data center developer and operator, and Volta, a platform that helps build, finance and operate data centers, to expand the cluster, per people familiar with the talks.
- It has ties to both. It co-led a $300 million round in Volta in August 2026 at a $2.4 billion valuation. It reportedly was in talks in July 2026 to lead a $2 billion round in Switch.
- These deals are part of the firm's bigger bet on hardware and AI infrastructure companies, led by general partner Martin Casado.
- How big the expansion will be is unclear. If portfolio companies do not use all the capacity, the firm could resell the rest to other buyers.
- A spokesperson said the firm helps founders "navigate finding compute, negotiating contracts and obtaining desirable or special pricing."

## General Catalyst: its own data center

- General Catalyst, a sprawling investment firm that previously bought a hospital system, is now moving into chips.
- In May 2026 it hired Alexis Black Björlin as chief strategy officer. She formerly helped run Nvidia's cloud computing division. She focuses on infrastructure projects, what she calls "the efficiency frontier of large-scale AI systems" for portfolio companies.
- Earlier in 2026, CEO Hemant Taneja surveyed founders about their access to compute. The Information reported in April that the firm was weighing a shared capacity pool or negotiating on startups' behalf.
- Lately the firm has been working to secure a data center for this compute, per someone with knowledge of the project.
- It will serve portfolio companies from early stage to growth stage, per another person. That suggests the cluster could be big enough for mature companies that process more tokens (units of AI text processed) than young startups.

## Other firms

- Coatue Management launched a data center development company, Next Frontier, last year. It has at least two joint ventures underway with neocloud Fluidstack. A neocloud is a newer cloud company that mainly rents out AI chips. The effort partly aims to secure scarce compute for Coatue's investments, per someone who spoke with a senior member of the firm.
- How the biggest VC firms will pay for bigger chip and data center pushes is unclear, and they could profit from them.
- Options: many large funds have side vehicles (separate investment pools) to finance such projects. They could also guarantee a loan or sign a lease commitment that lets a partner raise the money.

## Venture payback

- Early-stage investor Crucible Capital began building its own cluster last year, mostly as an experiment. Its cluster has 576 Nvidia B300 chips.
- Crucible now sells part of that capacity to a frontier lab, while portfolio companies use it to test products or run pilots, per a person with knowledge of the firm.
- Evan Conrad founded San Francisco Compute (SF Compute), a three-year-old startup that builds AI chip clusters and runs a marketplace to rent GPUs without long-term contracts.
- Conrad said "every major fund is attempting to do this" and they want to own clusters to secure access for their portfolios.
- Conrad is working with a few VC firms to set up clusters they will control and parcel out to startups. Until startups need it, SF Compute plans to sell the excess to other buyers.
- Sriram Krishnan, co-founder and general partner at early-stage firm Kearny Jackson, posted on X in early October 2026 that he had "hundreds of AMD mi350s" for lease or purchase. These are AMD's high-end AI server chips. He set up the cluster with a supplier of AI servers.
- Krishnan said founders flooded him with requests: "It was a bit of a hurricane." Some founders compared GPU access to "having oil during an oil shortage."
- Krishnan will give the GPUs to portfolio companies and does not plan to profit. He expects fast compute access will draw new founders to the firm.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: the compute shortage now reaches down to startups, and VCs are turning into compute suppliers, not just cash suppliers.
- Nvidia journey: demand for its chips is strong enough that VC funds hoard and resell clusters of B300s; an ex-Nvidia cloud leader now runs General Catalyst's infrastructure push.
- Anthropic journey: Fluidstack, Anthropic's data center partner, also builds joint ventures with Coatue's Next Frontier; Volta (reportedly Anthropic's Norway compute partner) now gets a16z money.
- Microsoft: diverting GPUs to internal teams in 2026 squeezed smaller startups, echoing its missed Azure numbers.
