# Notes: Apple Considers Return to Server Market, Has Talked With Nvidia to Use Network Tech

The Information, Aaron Tilley, Qianer Liu and Phoebe Liu, 2026-09-16 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Apple is planning an enterprise server built on its own chips, possibly with Nvidia networking gear inside (per people familiar with the project). An enterprise server is a machine sold to businesses to run software in their own data rooms.
- Buyers would be AI developers, other businesses and governments.
- Two versions under consideration: a smaller one with two planned M8 Ultra chips linked together, and a bigger one with four. M-series Ultra chips are Apple's top chips and power the Mac Studio.
- Not expected on sale until 2029. It could still be cancelled or ship without Nvidia tech.
- New CEO John Ternus, who took over earlier this month, backed the project from its start about a year ago when he ran hardware engineering (per some of the people).

## The Nvidia piece

- Apple has looked at linking the M8 chips with **NVLink Fusion**, an Nvidia package of switches, chiplets and software that lets chips in a data center talk to each other fast enough to act as one.
- NVLink was first built for Nvidia's own chips; Nvidia has since opened it to other companies' chips. Some Apple staffers think it may be the best option.
- Apple's current in-house connection tech for its servers is too slow and costly at large scale (per the people).
- Networking gear can be 10% to 15% of AI data center hardware costs, and was a fifth of Nvidia's data center revenue in the April quarter.
- Nvidia's Dion Harris: "We would always rather sell something than nothing." He would not say if Apple is a customer but called Apple a great customer and partner.
- Last month Amazon's cloud unit said it will expand use of NVLink Fusion in next-generation AI servers.

## A long cold relationship, thawing

- Apple put Nvidia graphics chips in Macs from 2001; Steve Jobs called it a "great relationship."
- Tensions followed, including Jobs's belief that Nvidia products held technology copied from Pixar (previously reported).
- Recently: part of Apple's rebuilt Siri runs on Nvidia chips in Google data centers. Apple and Nvidia have since talked about Nvidia tech and its open-source AI models.

## Why Apple sees demand

- Over the past year AI developers rushed to buy Mac mini and Mac Studio (box computers sold without keyboard or screen). Large AI labs bought tens of thousands.
- Dragonfruit AI uses them for security camera analysis for retailers. Startup Mount Thor is building a cloud business on Apple hardware.
- Result: an extreme shortage. Mac was Apple's fastest-growing segment last quarter, up nearly 29% to $10.4 billion.
- Macs lack enterprise features such as remote management software.
- Apple already builds servers for Private Cloud Compute, its internal system for AI tasks too big for phones and Macs. It has refused partners' requests to use them.
- Apple must lock in components years ahead to sell servers outside.
- Target market: AI inference (running models) for customers who want their own servers instead of public clouds.

## Hurdles

- Apple sold Xserve servers from 2002 to 2011, using IBM and then Intel chips. It failed largely from Apple's indifference to enterprise and weak customer support (per former staff).
- Former Apple enterprise marketing manager Todd Dailey said Apple has no enterprise-focused engineering team or enterprise developer relations.
- Apple held a "Business at the Park" event on June 23 to court corporations and AI players.
- One AI hardware investor said networking is Apple's biggest missing piece; Nvidia gear may be a stopgap while Apple builds its own.
- Apple would need developer software like Nvidia's CUDA (the toolkit that makes Nvidia chips easy to program). Its own tool, MLX, has a small team, and its leader Awni Hannun left for Anthropic this year.
- Competition moves fast: OpenAI plans to deploy its custom chip, Jalapeno, later this year.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Apple journey beat: a consumer-first company eyeing enterprise AI servers, with a failed Xserve history and a new CEO who backs the idea.
- Compute scarcity beat: hunger for compute is so high that AI labs are buying Mac minis by the tens of thousands.
- Model labs vs clouds beat: Apple bets on buyers who want private, on-premises inference rather than public clouds.
