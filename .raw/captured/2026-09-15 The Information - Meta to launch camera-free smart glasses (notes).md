# Notes: Meta to Launch Camera-Free Smart Glasses Amid Mounting Privacy Concerns

The Information, Jyoti Mann, 2026-09-15 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Meta plans to release smart glasses with no camera this fall (per people familiar with the matter). The goal: widen its hardware lineup while camera glasses face privacy criticism.
- Internal code name: **Luna**.
- Features (per one person):
  - Six built-in microphones for talking to Meta AI and Muse, Meta's recently launched consumer AI agent (an assistant that can carry out tasks, not just chat).
  - A side button to quickly start Meta AI.
  - Open-ear speakers like current models, aimed at the ears without covering them.
  - Slimmer temple arms than earlier Meta glasses such as the Ray-Ban models, so they look more like normal eyewear.
- Meta could unveil Luna at its annual Connect developer conference next Wednesday and Thursday. Shipments targeted for October.
- Two styles: Clubmaster and Burbank.
- Luna is part of a broader wearables plan Meta set earlier this year, per earlier reporting.
- Meta hopes Luna broadens appeal and offers an alternative to its flagship Ray-Ban glasses (per two people).

## The privacy problem

- The Ray-Ban glasses are popular, but some people have used them to secretly record and harass strangers for viral videos, per multiple reports.
- The glasses have an LED that blinks while recording, but some users cover or remove it.
- Last month Meta pushed a software update that blocks recording if the LED is covered.
- Meta estimates less than 0.1% of glasses sold have been tampered with, per Alex Himel, Meta's VP of wearables, speaking to Semafor.
- Without a camera, Luna sidesteps those concerns but keeps voice access to Meta AI, like the Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta models. It may also feel less intrusive where people dislike being filmed.

## Money context

- Meta keeps pushing hardware despite heavy losses in Reality Labs, its hardware and wearables unit.
- Reality Labs posted an operating loss (loss from running the business, before taxes and interest) of $4.62 billion last quarter alone.

## Phoenix: AR glasses

- Meta also plans to unveil augmented reality glasses (digital images laid over the real world), code-named **Phoenix**, at Connect (per two people).
- Pitched for productivity and entertainment: show your work computer screens on the glasses, or watch films.
- Comes with a "puck," a separate device that helps power the glasses. Lighter than an earlier prototype, with longer battery life (per one person).
- Timeline: Phoenix had been slated for the second half of 2026. In December, Reality Labs staff were told it would slip to 2027 "in order to get the details right."

## Hologram calls

- Earlier this month, CEO Mark Zuckerberg and CTO Andrew Bosworth reviewed work on hologram-like video calls (per one person).
- They decided to show a full-body, photorealistic hologram call during a live Phoenix demo in the Connect keynote, with Bosworth appearing as the hologram, barring changes.
- Reality Labs has worked on lifelike avatars, called Codec Avatars, for more than a decade. Meta says these 4D avatars can copy a user's facial expressions and be animated with text prompts.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Meta journey: Meta keeps betting on wearables as its AI hardware, despite a $4.62 billion quarterly loss in Reality Labs.
- Agents: Luna is a voice-only device built around Meta AI and the Muse agent, a sign that AI assistants are becoming the main interface, not the camera or screen.
- Trust as a product constraint: privacy misuse is pushing Meta to remove features (the camera) rather than add them.
