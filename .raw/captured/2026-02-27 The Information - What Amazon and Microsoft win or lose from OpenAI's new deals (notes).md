# Notes: What Amazon and Microsoft Will Win or Lose From OpenAI's New Deals

The Information, Aaron Holmes, Catherine Perloff, Stephanie Palazzolo, Anissa Gardizy, Valida Pau and Shane Burke, 2026-02-27 (Feb 2026).

## The core story

- On Friday, Feb 27, 2026, OpenAI announced a wide partnership with Amazon as part of a larger set of funding deals. The round values OpenAI at $730 billion before the new money ("pre-money valuation": what the company is worth before investors add cash).
- Two of the three biggest clouds (Amazon, Microsoft) will now sell OpenAI's services.
- Nvidia is putting in $30 billion as part of an expanded chip deal.
- OpenAI is also talking with financial investors, such as UAE fund MGX, about at least $10 billion more, expected to close by end of March (per a person with knowledge).

## Amazon: gains the most, pays the most

- Amazon invests $15 billion up front, due by end of March, and commits another $35 billion either when OpenAI goes public or hits certain unspecified milestones.
- OpenAI's cloud deal with AWS, announced last fall at $38 billion over seven years, grows to $138 billion over eight years.
- OpenAI commits to Amazon's Trainium AI chips (Trainium3 and the next-gen Trainium4, expected next year). That commitment uses about 2 gigawatts of power. For scale: last year OpenAI had about 2 GW total across all its cloud providers. (A gigawatt here is a shorthand for how big a data center fleet is, measured by the power it draws.)
- Amazon becomes the only outside provider of OpenAI's Frontier AI agent management tool.
- The two will jointly build customized models for Amazon's customer apps. No shopping deal was announced, but Amazon could use this to put OpenAI models into its Rufus shopping chatbot or Alexa. Amazon now relies on Anthropic models for some products, but Anthropic limits how much customers like Amazon can customize them.
- OpenAI models will complement Amazon's own Nova models.
- Amazon also raised its capital expenditures (spending on long-lived things like data centers) by 50% to $200 billion this year, mostly for AWS capacity.

## OpenAI: an IPO may be closer

- The Amazon terms hint OpenAI could go public as soon as this year. Amazon said the $35 billion could come within months, depending on conditions, including a public listing.
- OpenAI aims to be ready for an IPO (initial public offering: first sale of shares on a stock market) by Q4 2026, depending on markets (per the person with knowledge).
- But bankers and lawyers who talk with OpenAI executives say only early steps have been discussed, making 2027 more likely.
- The Amazon agreement expires at end of 2028, so OpenAI could wait longer and still collect the $35 billion.
- OpenAI's nonprofit shareholder, whose stake is valued at more than $180 billion in this round, might sell some shares worth low-to-mid single-digit billions to avoid dilution (having its ownership percentage shrink), per the person.
- Sam Altman told CNBC the round gives OpenAI a long runway, but he still expects to need more capital.
- What else triggers Amazon's $35 billion was unclear. The Information had earlier reported the milestones were reaching AGI (AI on par with human abilities), but the announcement did not mention it, and the SEC-filed contract redacted parts that might cover it. Altman told CNBC that OpenAI sees AGI as near-term and is no longer signing deals that end when AGI arrives.

## SoftBank

- SoftBank will invest three installments of $10 billion, the last on Oct. 1, bringing its total to $64.6 billion and about a 13% stake. Nearly doubles its stake.

## Microsoft: keeps the money, loses exclusivity

- Microsoft keeps much of its OpenAI business but loses the exclusivity that helped Azure.
- Parts of OpenAI's agents, such as memory holding a customer's name, company, internal file connections and usage history, can be stored on AWS.
- Microsoft still has exclusive rights to host stateless (generic, no memory of the user) versions of OpenAI models. So some OpenAI-powered agents on AWS will still generate revenue for Microsoft's cloud. Microsoft and OpenAI restated this in a blog post Friday.
- Microsoft will likely still capture most revenue from developers using OpenAI models, a business worth several billion dollars a year, per prior reporting.
- Unclear if Amazon's custom models will eat into that. Altman told CNBC the Microsoft tie stays strong.

## Nvidia

- Nvidia's $30 billion appears to replace an earlier plan to invest $100 billion. That earlier plan (OpenAI building 10 GW of data centers with Nvidia chips, Nvidia investing in phases) was never finalized.
- Besides Hopper and Blackwell chips rented from Microsoft, Oracle and CoreWeave, OpenAI will use an extra 5 GW of Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips, no timeline given: 3 GW for dedicated inference capacity (running models for users) and 2 GW for training.
- Provider not named; the AWS deal also includes renting GPUs. Nvidia CFO Colette Kress said this week that nearly 9 GW of Blackwell chips have been deployed.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Model labs vs clouds: OpenAI breaks Microsoft's exclusivity and plays clouds against each other, while Microsoft keeps a toll on stateless model hosting.
- Compute scarcity: OpenAI commits to 2 GW of Trainium plus 5 GW of Vera Rubin, when it had about 2 GW total the year before.
- Amazon journey: a $50 billion bet, a $200 billion capex year, and custom OpenAI models that could power Rufus and Alexa (retail agents).
- AI economics: a $730 billion valuation and IPO talk for Q4 2026 or 2027.
