# Notes: Nvidia to Invest in Chip Rival d-Matrix as Its Challengers Choose Partnership

The Information, Phoebe Liu and Valida Pau, 2026-10-08 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Nvidia plans to invest in d-Matrix, a seven-year-old maker of AI server chips that aims to compete with Nvidia, per three people with knowledge of the deal.
- The investment is part of Nvidia's effort to make its technology work with rival chip designers and pull them into its dominant hardware ecosystem, even as Nvidia has recently gained market share against nearly all of them.
- It is Nvidia's first deal with an AI inference chip startup since its $20 billion licensing agreement with Groq in December 2025. Inference means running an already-trained AI model to answer requests.
- That shows Nvidia is not content to bet on just one such firm.
- Three months before publication, Nvidia said it was working with d-Matrix so both companies' chips could handle different parts of the same AI inference workloads, to make that computing more efficient.

## Rivals choose partnership

- Rivals have found it hard to dent Nvidia's lead, so they increasingly try to work alongside Nvidia GPUs (graphics processing units, its main AI chips) instead of replacing them.
- Bringing rivals into its ecosystem could help Nvidia's networking hardware, which links server racks so they work as one system on heavy AI tasks. This is called parallel processing.
- In the past 12 months Nvidia took multibillion-dollar stakes in more established rivals Intel and Marvell, partly to support integrating Nvidia GPUs with their chips. When these integrations happen at scale is unclear.
- Nvidia has pitched several other chip startups on making their chips work with GPUs that use Nvidia's networking, per people at those startups.
- In August 2026 Amazon raised the prospect of a similar setup: Nvidia GPUs and Amazon Trainium chips in the same data center with similar server racks. Amazon also said Trainium would use Nvidia's networking and memory hardware.
- In June 2026 chip startup SambaNova Systems showed a way to connect its chips with Nvidia GPUs to run AI models together.
- Separately, Nvidia is building a system that combines its GPUs with Groq's specialized inference chips. Nvidia hired Groq's founders and other staff in the $20 billion licensing deal.

## Antitrust probe

- The Department of Justice is investigating whether Nvidia structured the Groq deal as a license-and-hire to avoid a formal DOJ review of its effect on the chip market, per people familiar with the probe. The New York Times first reported the probe.

## Disaggregated inference

- Using chips from different designers on the same AI workload is a fairly new idea, sometimes called disaggregated inference.
- Nvidia still says publicly that its own chips, alone, are the most efficient for nearly all AI workloads.
- Cloud providers and big AI developers such as Anthropic and OpenAI do not want to depend on one chip vendor.
- Anthropic and OpenAI are each developing their own inference chips and using other firms' chips to rely less on Nvidia.

## What d-Matrix does

- d-Matrix focuses on speculative decoding, a trick that speeds up an AI model.
- In the planned system, d-Matrix chips run a small AI model that guesses a bigger model's answer, and the bigger model, on an Nvidia GPU, checks and accepts the guesses.
- The Information earlier reported d-Matrix was seeking new funding at a $5 billion valuation. It is unclear if Nvidia's money is part of that round.
- d-Matrix last raised $275 million at a $2 billion valuation in November 2025.
- It began producing its first chips in summer 2026 and is now testing second-generation chips.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Nvidia journey: a clear move from fighting chip rivals to absorbing them as partners, which protects its networking business and its lead.
- AI shift 2024-2026 story: inference, not training, is where chip rivals now compete, and splitting one workload across chips is a new pattern.
- Amazon journey: Trainium is being designed to sit next to Nvidia GPUs and use Nvidia networking, not only to replace them.
- Anthropic and OpenAI journeys: both building their own inference chips to cut dependence on Nvidia.
