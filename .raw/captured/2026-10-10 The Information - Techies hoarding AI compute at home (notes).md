# Notes: GPUs in the Wine Cellar: Why Techies Are Hoarding AI Compute in Their Homes

The Information, Nick Wingfield and Aaron Tilley, 2026-10-10.

## Gary Flake's basement rig

- Gary Flake, a former Microsoft executive and startup founder, keeps a custom computer in the basement of his Bellevue, Wash., home with seven large Nvidia GPUs (graphics processing units, the chips that run AI) hanging off its frame.
- He built it to train custom AI models on terabytes of EEG data (tests that measure electrical activity in the brain) for a startup project he has since left.
- He says building his own machine from discounted parts cost under $6,000, far cheaper than paying Amazon Web Services or Microsoft cloud fees every month.
- That holds even after $10,000 to $20,000 he spent on electrical upgrades and a new HVAC (heating and cooling) system to handle the heat the rig gives off.
- Flake: "I want to own. I don't want to rent." He plans to use the machine again for a new AI project in the coming months.

## A local AI movement

- Running AI in attics, basements and home offices is an unexpected outcome of the AI boom, since leading AI firms train and run models in remote data centers with tens of thousands of GPUs.
- A growing local AI movement of small developers and tinkerers wants to run AI on their own computers for cost, privacy and convenience.
- Some libertarian-minded developers believe local AI ensures governments cannot take away their access to the technology.
- Autonomous agent platforms like OpenClaw, which can do complex tasks with a user's local apps and files, also fueled the movement.

## Tobi Lütke's wine cellar

- In September 2026 Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke went viral with a photo of three racks of computer gear in his Toronto home, lit by pink LEDs.
- The gear included a Dell computer with a powerful Nvidia GPU that Nvidia markets as the "ultimate deskside supercomputer."
- He cooled it by using the house's glassed, climate-controlled wine cellar, since his family does not drink much wine.

## Computer makers benefit

- Home AI rigs have been a pleasant surprise for computer makers.
- Apple is seeing booming sales of its Mac mini and Mac Studio, both favorites of agent developers.
- Dell also says it sees huge growth from the home AI community.
- Marc Hammons, senior distinguished engineer at Dell, called it "one of these zeitgeist moments" and said Dell saw explosive growth after OpenClaw appeared earlier in 2026.

## Homelabs have a long history

- Powerful home computers go back decades; a "homelab" community connects on Discord and Reddit.
- In the early 2000s, hobbyists built home servers with lots of disk storage for MP3 and video libraries, often from file-sharing services.
- Later, crypto mining drove demand for powerful home GPUs.

## Hiten Shah: from crypto to AI

- Hiten Shah, CEO of software startup Crazy Egg, bought eight GPUs in a server rack in 2020 to mine Ethereum.
- He has since moved to AI development and has 18 computers in his Redwood Shores, Calif., home, including 14 Mac minis and Mac Studios.
- He says local compute gives him more control over the performance of his website optimization software, because he expects outages and slowdowns from cloud AI models.
- Shah: "Every time there's a new model in the cloud, you see models get janky for days."
- The cost is comfort: his home office sometimes hits 85 degrees Fahrenheit (about 29 Celsius) from the machines. "I should put some AC in there," he said.

## YouTubers with home data centers

- An anonymous Austin-based YouTuber who goes by his channel name, Digital Spaceport, runs a "home datacenter" in his garage with five AMD GPUs plus networking and storage gear.
- He currently runs the Chinese open-source model Qwen 3.8 27B on it to edit and analyze his YouTube videos.
- Another anonymous tech consultant and YouTuber, 0xSero, based in Poland, owns four Nvidia 6000 GPUs.
- They used to sit in his home office but were noisy and ran so hot they dried out the room, which he blames for making him sick last year; he moved them to the attic.
- He first used cloud AI models, but a turning point came in late 2024 when AI coding tool maker Cursor raised its prices: "That was the point that I needed to figure out local AI."
- 0xSero believes people should own and control their AI because governments may one day try to restrict access. "I really like AI and I want to keep it," he said.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: a home "own, don't rent" counter-trend to cloud AI, driven by cost, privacy, reliability and fear of losing access.
- Apple journey: Mac mini and Mac Studio demand from agent developers keeps booming, a follow-up to the earlier Mac shortage.
- Nvidia journey: Nvidia GPUs now sell into homes, not only data centers, including its "deskside supercomputer" via Dell.
- Local inference concept: real home setups and their hidden costs, such as heat, power upgrades and cooling.
