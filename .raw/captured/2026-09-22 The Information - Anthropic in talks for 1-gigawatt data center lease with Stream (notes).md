# Notes: Anthropic in Talks for 1-Gigawatt Data Center Lease With Apollo-Backed Developer

The Information, Valida Pau and Amir Efrati, 2026-09-22 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Anthropic is in early talks to lease up to 1 gigawatt of compute capacity from **Stream Data Centers**, a developer majority-owned by investment firm Apollo Global Management. A gigawatt is a unit of power; here it measures how much electricity the data centers can draw for chips.
- The goal is to rely less on cloud providers.
- Anthropic would be a direct tenant of Stream sites and fill them with TPUs (tensor processing units, AI chips designed by Google and Broadcom) (per people with knowledge of the talks).
- Anthropic has also discussed Google giving a credit guarantee for the project (per some of the people). A credit guarantee means Google promises to cover payments if Anthropic cannot. Which part it would cover is unclear. Anthropic has sought Google backstops for other leases before.
- Nvidia GPUs could also go in the sites (per one person). Nvidia has been investing widely so its chips, not TPUs, end up in big AI facilities.
- Developers say a gigawatt of capacity needs at least $40 billion of capital investment.
- The deal is months from done and could fall apart. Capacity and structure are still being discussed. Anthropic would need financial guarantees for the leases and financing for the chips.

## Why leases instead of clouds

- Today Anthropic gets most of its compute by renting chip servers from AWS, Google and SpaceX.
- It already agreed to lease smaller sites from former bitcoin miners Hut 8 and TeraWulf. That gives it direct control over servers and a way to cut costs by skipping cloud providers, which want their own profit.
- Stream could be its biggest lease yet. Rival OpenAI is pursuing the same strategy.
- Leasing a data center usually means buying and running the servers inside. That could give the labs more control over chip prices and supply, plus racks and networking gear.
- The tradeoff: the labs carry the cost of the equipment and the staff. Anthropic is hiring dozens of people, some in Canada and Australia, for roles like securing power and facility design.

## Anthropic's other compute deals

- Leasing chips directly: earlier this year, a deal with AMD for 2 GW worth of chips.
- A $35 billion agreement to lease TPUs from a special purpose vehicle set up by Apollo and Blackstone. A special purpose vehicle is a separate company created just to hold and finance specific assets.
- Broadcom said it wants to work with those financial firms to finance more than 20 GW of compute, implying about $700 billion in chip purchases.
- Anthropic signed $531 billion of compute deals in the last 11 months, mostly with AWS, Google and Microsoft.

## The race with OpenAI

- Only a limited number of developers have power supplies and permits lined up.
- Last month OpenAI agreed to lease the first phase of a planned 10 GW data center in Ohio, with Nvidia credit support covering up to $105 billion. The site sits on federal land and has U.S. government support.

## About Stream

- Dallas-based, 27 years old, has developed more than 20 U.S. data center sites.
- Apollo bought a majority stake last year; Principal Asset Management also owns a stake.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Model labs vs clouds beat: Anthropic is moving from renting cloud servers to leasing whole data centers, cutting out the clouds' profit margin. Margin here means the extra the cloud charges above its own cost.
- AI economics beat: the scale is huge ($531 billion of deals in 11 months; $40 billion per gigawatt), and it depends on outside guarantees from Google, Nvidia and private finance firms.
- Compute scarcity beat: the real limit is developers who already hold power and permits, so labs fight over them.
