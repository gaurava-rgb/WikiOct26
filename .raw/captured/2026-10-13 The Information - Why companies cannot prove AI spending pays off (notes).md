# Notes: Why Companies Can't Prove Their AI Spending Pays Off

The Information, Laura Bratton, Applied AI newsletter, 2026-10-13.

## The measurement problem

- The big question of the AI boom is whether business spending on AI pays off, but measuring that return is often uncertain, confusing or incomplete.
- It takes time for AI to show a return on investment (ROI, the gain compared with the cost) that shows up in a company's income statement (the profit and loss report).

## The Wharton study

- A new study by the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School and consultancy GBK Collective surveyed 1,400 U.S. employees online at firms with at least 1,000 staff and more than $50 million in annual revenue.
- Most companies still track AI ROI by worker efficiency, not by new revenue.
- 40% of managers measured AI's impact by "hours saved per employee" and 37% tracked how AI cut worker errors.
- Only about 27% of managers said they track revenue from AI features, apps and tools they offer customers.
- 44% of managers said AI's main role over the next one to two years will be to improve productivity; just 8% said it will be to drive new revenue or create new products.
- Productivity gains still matter, especially when AI replaces labor such as outside contractors, customer service or IT support tickets.

## What the author of the study says

- Study author Jeremy Korst said it was "very difficult" to find "actual, materially measurable financial impacts" from AI spending.
- He said measuring returns today is more about vibes and managers' "perception" of a return than hard numbers.
- He called this level of return "normal" for an early rollout phase, in line with past technology adoption cycles.

## Who reports returns

- Some 80% of managers reported positive returns from AI; many work in IT, finance or HR.
- The 28% who said returns were "strongly positive" were more likely to work at companies that measure AI by growth outcomes such as new revenue, not just cost savings, Korst said.
- For comparison, in a recent survey of The Information's subscribers (mostly tech and investment firms), 35% said they get a "multiple" return on AI spending, but only 52% said they get a positive return overall.

## Few revenue examples outside tech

- In The Information's reporting, non-tech firms with revenue gains from AI are few and far between.
- Novo Nordisk built AI agents to audit and coordinate clinical trials, and expects extra revenue in the tens or hundreds of millions of dollars a year.
- Walmart CEO David Guggina said at the Goldman Sachs technology conference in September 2026 that customers using its AI assistant Sparky spent 40% more per order on average.
- Most other examples the column has cited over the years are labor and time savings.

## Spending outlook

- The study suggests corporate AI spending will keep rising fast only if companies show strong returns tied to growth outcomes like new revenue and higher output, not cost cutting.
- About 45% of managers said their companies will spend $10 million or more on AI in 2026, up from 39% in 2025.
- Big budget jumps may be rarer in 2027: just 10% of managers expect their AI spending to rise "substantially" in the next 12 months, down from 19% who said so in 2025.
- Companies may be getting better at tracking AI spending: 40% of managers were "very" confident in their company's ability to track it, 39% "fairly confident" and 17% "somewhat confident."

## Workday joins Salesforce in going headless

- On Tuesday, October 13, 2026, Workday unveiled tools that let customers use AI agents from firms like Anthropic, Microsoft and OpenAI to automate tasks in their Workday accounts.
- The tools resemble Salesforce's SIforce, which was called AIforce a month earlier and Headless 360 before that.
- Some employees and investors fear these tools are a capitulation to the new AI providers, turning Workday and Salesforce into "dumb" infrastructure, basically glorified databases. Their leaders say they will still make plenty of money.
- CEO Aneel Bhusri said "we're giving customers choice." Workday's own experience runs through Sana, its AI platform, but if a customer prefers headless (using another company's AI to reach Workday), that is fine.
- Bhusri said "headless will be equally good for Workday," though he would always prefer customers use Workday's interface.
- Starting in February 2027, after a grace period with no extra fees, Workday will charge customers based on how often AI agents tap connectors and take actions in Workday.
