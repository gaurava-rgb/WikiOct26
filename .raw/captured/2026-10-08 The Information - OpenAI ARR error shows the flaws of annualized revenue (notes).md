# Notes: OpenAI ARR Snafu Highlights Flawed Metric

The Briefing newsletter, Martin Peers (columnist and co-executive editor), 2026-10-08.

## The OpenAI number was wrong

- Revenue figures for OpenAI that spread widely the week before were way off.
- Instead of $70 billion in annualized revenue, as The Information and others reported, OpenAI makes closer to $50 billion in annualized revenue.
- Annualized revenue (often shortened to ARR) is a guess at a full year's sales, usually one month's revenue times 12.
- A Financial Times report of the lower number on Thursday, Oct. 8, 2026 shocked investors. Stocks of chip firms and neoclouds, including Nvidia, CoreWeave and Nebius, fell on the news, per CNBC. Neoclouds are newer cloud companies that mainly rent out AI chips.

## Why the metric is flawed

- Peers says the cause of the mistake is complicated, but it shows everyone relies too much on annualized revenue to judge a company.
- It became standard for AI companies because their revenue grows so fast that quarterly reports feel backward-looking.
- Peers calls the one-month-times-12 method "a poor substitute for a fuller picture of a company's performance." He notes this is not a new problem and points to a 2020 story on it.
- No one knows enough about what happens in that single month to know whether multiplying it by 12 is accurate.
- The risk grows because OpenAI and Anthropic count revenue differently, especially on whether they include the cut paid to cloud firms.
- Companies publish these figures only in occasional blog posts, not on a regular schedule.
- Much of the time, ARR news comes from communications with investors that reach reporters.
- As with the mistaken $70 billion figure, investors and news outlets sometimes do clumsy calculations that lead to errors.

## What would fix it

- Peers expects the problem to fade when the AI firms go public.
- They may keep issuing annualized-revenue estimates, as some public companies have begun to do, but the real reported numbers will also be visible.
- For now, apart from occasional leaks of detailed financial statements, outsiders are in the dark.

## Elon Musk's phone ambitions

- Peers asks what SpaceX is: a cloud firm, a rocket firm, or a broadband operator, perhaps all three.
- On Thursday, Oct. 8, 2026, SpaceX said it struck a deal to buy nationwide low-band spectrum (radio frequencies licensed for mobile service).
- Musk said on X it is the last piece SpaceX needs to provide complete phone coverage in America.
- Shares of Verizon, T-Mobile and AT&T each fell 6% to 7% in after-hours trading.
- Peers argues reliable cell service is very hard. He bets SpaceX will struggle to match the incumbents by combining its satellite-to-mobile constellation with the new spectrum.
- Beyond the spectrum cost, SpaceX must build cell towers to use it. More competition is welcome, but winning will not be easy, he says.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- OpenAI journey: its real run-rate is about $50 billion, not $70 billion, a key correction for any revenue claim from late September 2026.
- AI shift 2024-2026 story: the market is so tied to OpenAI's growth that one revenue correction moved Nvidia, CoreWeave and Nebius stock.
- Anthropic vs OpenAI comparisons need care, since they count cloud resale revenue differently.
- SpaceX keeps widening its scope (cloud, rockets, broadband, now phone service).
