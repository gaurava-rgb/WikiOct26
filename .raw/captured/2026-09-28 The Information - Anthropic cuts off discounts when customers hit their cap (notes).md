# Notes: Anthropic Moves to Cut Off Discounts When Customers Hit Their Cap

The Information, Kevin McLaughlin, 2026-09-28 (exclusive). Laura Bratton contributed.

## The core story

- Both Anthropic and OpenAI give discounts to companies that commit to spending millions a year.
- Anthropic takes an unusual hard line: once a customer uses up the tokens (units of AI usage) in its contract, the discount ends. The customer must sign a new deal or pay higher prices (per managers at three software firms that buy from both labs).
- One of them described immediate calls and emails from Anthropic sales once the cap is hit, urging a new deal to avoid overages (charges for use beyond the contract). "Otherwise you're on your own."
- Anthropic's spokesperson had no comment.

## How others handle it

- Jeff Muscarella (chief innovation officer, NPI Financial, which helps Fortune 500 firms negotiate software deals): Amazon, Microsoft and Google usually keep discounted prices on spending above the commitment for the rest of the contract term.
- OpenAI is softer (per licensing consultants). When customers hit their commitment, OpenAI gives the rest of the calendar month plus one more month to negotiate before switching to list prices (per a person close to OpenAI).

## The numbers

- Anthropic's discounts can be around 15% off list price (per the three software firms).
- More than 100 firms spent over $10 million each with Anthropic in the 12 months ending June; more than 1,000 spent over $1 million each.
- Big customers include Meta and Cursor. Many enterprises pay for Claude Code or Claude Cowork, both of which got pricier as Anthropic changed pricing.
- Anthropic told at least one partner it earns about a 75% gross margin (share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving it) on its existing models.

## OpenAI's push to catch up

- Anthropic zoomed far ahead of OpenAI in revenue this year, and widened that lead between May and August. OpenAI is still growing fast.
- OpenAI cut prices of new models 50% for customers buying through intermediaries like OpenRouter and Vercel.
- At Tuesday's model launch, Sam Altman tweeted the new models are "half the price per token, and even less per task."
- Fredrik Filipsson (co-founder, Redress Compliance): OpenAI is more aggressive than Anthropic on discounts to win enterprise business.
- OpenRouter said the discounting led more of its customers to pick OpenAI models.
- Example: CodeRabbit (code-review service). Six months ago CEO Harjot Gill said Anthropic was its main supplier; now OpenAI is. He would not discuss the discount but said the three-year-old company spends tens of millions a year on AI. Better OpenAI model quality helped.

## OpenAI sales shake-up

- The executive leading OpenAI's enterprise push left abruptly in August.
- OpenAI is merging pre-sale and post-sale roles: the people who demo products must also act as customer success managers (staff who keep clients happy after signing) (per an employee).

## Share of wallet and trust

- Both labs now add "share of wallet" clauses asking big customers to send most of their AI spend to them (per Gill and another software manager). Consultants say it is symbolic since the labs cannot verify it.
- Worries that the labs could learn from customers' intellectual property have led Palantir, Nvidia and Booz Allen Hamilton to demand new guarantees or reduce or stop using the most advanced models.
- Still, evaluations show the two labs have the best models for automating white-collar work and research, so businesses find them hard to ignore.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics: Anthropic's 75% margin and strict discount policy show pricing power from being the revenue leader.
- Model labs vs clouds: Anthropic's terms are tougher than the cloud giants' norms; OpenAI competes on price.
- Agents and coding tools (Claude Code, Cursor, CodeRabbit) are where enterprise AI spend concentrates.
