# Notes: Why the SaaSpocalypse Isn't Over For Workday

The Information, Laura Bratton and Anita Ramaswamy, 2026-10-06 (exclusive).

## The core story

- AI is unlikely to replace Workday's core HR and finance records, but it may cap Workday's growth.
- Some customers now use outside agents (Anthropic, Microsoft and others) to pull data out of Workday, so nobody visits the app. Many who bought Workday's own AI tools barely use them (per five consultants and partners serving 2,000+ Workday customers).
- The risk, in the reporters' framing: Workday becomes "dumb" infrastructure, a trusted database that agents call, while the valuable work happens above it.

## Key facts and numbers

- At one consultancy with 400+ Workday customers: about half signed deals with usage-based AI access, but only 10% to 20% actively use Workday AI; none use Sana Enterprise (launched March 2026). Some were shocked by bills (per a consulting executive).
- Workday incentives: free AI credits; some big customers offered a free year of Sana Enterprise.
- Toll booth plan: Workday will charge when outside AI reads or changes its data, but it waived API overage fees from May 2026 to February 2027 (Workday says to give customers time to plan).
- Customers using Workday's agents: 5,500 of about 11,500, up 35% quarter on quarter (July 2026 quarter, company). Unclear how many pay.
- AI agents plus Sana on pace for $600 million a year, three times a year earlier (Workday, August 2026).
- Overall revenue growth slowed to 12.8% in the July 2026 quarter; analysts expect 9.7% in the January 2027 quarter (Koyfin). Market cap about $45 billion.
- Reuters reported in August 2026 that Silver Lake considered taking Workday private.
- Founders Duffield and Bhusri hold 69% of voting power. Layoffs: about 400 in February 2026, another 500 (2.5%) announced late September.
- Customer wins cited by Workday: FlexGen avoided $35 million in tariffs; Chipotle cut hiring time 75%; Great Wolf Lodge saved $700,000 a year.

## Named people and roles

- Gabe Monroy, Workday CTO (ex-Google): focus is "adoption, adoption, adoption"; leading work to make its in-house XpressO code language usable by AI coding tools.
- Aneel Bhusri, co-founder, back as CEO since February 2026.
- Yasmeen Ahmad, SVP data cloud (ex-Google): regulated firms do not want big model labs touching their data.
- Raphaëlle D'Ornano, adviser: "Workday is called, not visited, and the toll is waived."
- Joel Carusone (NinjaOne) and Jaspreet Singh (Druva CEO): built their own agents instead.
- Phill Pearson, Kainos: customers anxious about AI on sensitive data.

## Change versus earlier views

- The earlier fear that AI kills enterprise apps outright has faded, and shares partly recovered. The new worry is slower growth and lost pricing power.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Who owns the customer: the agent layer (Claude, Copilot) takes the user relationship; Workday keeps the record. Classic Aggregation Theory pressure on suppliers.
- Switching Costs: the system of record stays sticky, but stickiness no longer guarantees growth.
- Who pays: usage-based AI pricing plus bill shock pushes buyers to consolidate AI spend with one provider.
