# Notes: Software Firms Discount AI to Keep Customers From Anthropic, OpenAI

The Information, Laura Bratton, Aaron Holmes and Catherine Perloff, 2026-09-22 (exclusive). Updated with AWS partner funding and Microsoft's steeper discounts.

## The core story

- Software firms and clouds, including Amazon, Microsoft, Figma and Workday, are offering new discounts on AI products to clients and consulting partners tired of pricing changes.
- Customers raised budgets for tools like Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's Codex, cut other costs to pay for them, and are now choosier.
- Many vendors are moving to usage-based pricing (pay per use or per completed task) instead of per-seat pricing (a flat fee per employee). That can push costs up, and customers are grumbling.
- Four years into the AI boom, traditional software makers are still working out how to compete with Anthropic and OpenAI, each on pace to make tens of billions this year. OpenAI discounting its own models adds pressure.
- Those same software makers pay Anthropic and OpenAI to power their AI features, which is costly and part of why prices changed.
- Snowflake shows rising AI revenue growth. Workday, HubSpot, Salesforce and Adobe have not shown a notable acceleration.

## Microsoft

- A global manufacturer worth more than $40 billion got at least a month's "grace period" to keep paying per seat for GitHub Copilot (a coding assistant) after Microsoft moved to usage pricing. The change would add roughly $150,000 a month to its bill.
- Microsoft also gave it tens of thousands of dollars in free credits per month for three months. The team is still weighing a move to Claude Code and SpaceX's Cursor.
- Copilot costs $30 per user per month, plus new usage charges for features like Copilot Cowork.
- In recent months Microsoft began offering 5% to 10% per-seat discounts to buyers of at least 2,000 seats. Before, only giants like KPMG or PwC with hundreds of thousands of seats got them (per a Microsoft employee). Savings can top hundreds of thousands of dollars a year.
- Later Tuesday, The Information reported Microsoft plans 30% to 50% discounts on corporate Copilot in coming months to win subscribers who will also pay usage fees.

## Other software firms

- FICO CIO Mike Trkay: dozens of its vendors added AI at no extra cost at renewal this year (unnamed). FICO has 3,000+ employees and uses Salesforce, Oracle and Workday. He said he would not sign a contract that does not heavily discount or bundle the AI.
- Workday: this summer began a year of unlimited free access to its Sana Enterprise AI platform for top customers. About 20 large enterprises take part, getting agents for HR and finance tasks like onboarding. President of technology Gerrit Kazmaier on a late-August call: "our focus is really on adoption, adoption, adoption." He said the dollar impact is hard to answer yet.
- Figma: CEO Dylan Field said at a Goldman Sachs conference this month it cut AI costs by as much as half. It moved to usage pricing in March and in late August gave more credits for the same price. Goal is to drive volume over high margins on tools like Figma Make.
- Thoughtworks chief data and AI officer Shayan Mohanty: in the past six months vendors typically give 30% to 35% discounts on software and AI and longer trials. Before, discounts were usually under 20% and harder to get.
- HubSpot: CEO Yamini Rangan said in April it began free trials of up to 30 days for its AI agents while starting to charge per completed task on top of subscriptions. HubSpot's revenue growth slowed in the June quarter.
- Adobe: gives basic AI tools free and charges for advanced ones ("freemium"). That boosted Firefly use, but executives said this month it weighs on backlog and annual recurring revenue growth. Annual recurring revenue (ARR) is the yearly value of ongoing subscriptions.

## Amazon (AWS)

- AWS offered consultancy Rhythmic Technologies about four months free of Kiro's top tier (an AI coding assistant), per co-founder Cris Daniluk. He declined because his firm already uses Claude Code.
- Daniluk says the discounts aim to win share where customers "already made their bets." Neither Rhythmic nor most clients are adopting Kiro, though some developers like it.
- AWS says Kiro developer numbers doubled from Q1 to Q2 2026.
- AWS is finalizing a program to pay consulting partners up to $75,000 for client demos and about $400,000 for pilots (per a participant).

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Model labs vs clouds and SaaS: incumbents are paying the labs for models while discounting to stop customers going straight to them.
- AI economics: the shift from seats to usage pricing is squeezing buyers, and vendors trade margin for adoption.
- Amazon journey: AWS is still struggling to get Kiro adopted against Claude Code, despite free tiers and partner cash.
