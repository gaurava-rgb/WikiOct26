# Notes: OpenAI’s Bug and Bumble’s AI-Powered Exit

The Information, The Briefing newsletter, Martin Peers, 2026-10-13.

## OpenAI's bug

- Peers jokes OpenAI deserves a TV show: swarms of rogue AI agents causing havoc across the internet, plus periodic corporate drama.
- On 2026-10-13 the Justice Department said an OpenAI engineer allegedly planted a "sophisticated recording device" in a company conference room.
- The engineer has been arrested. The Justice Department identified him as a Saudi national.
- The bug was attached to the back of a TV in a conference room where "highly confidential technical, commercial and strategic information" was often discussed.

## Bumble's AI-powered exit

- Peers says acquisitions of distressed companies are starting to be dressed up as AI-driven purchases.
- On Tuesday 2026-10-13 investment firm Ithaca Holdings unveiled a deal to buy dating app Bumble at a $1.3 billion valuation.
- Ithaca co-CEO Ravi Gupta, a former Sequoia partner, said on X that Bumble stood out from hundreds of companies Ithaca reviewed to see which "would become more valuable as AI advances."
- Peers argues AI could improve Bumble, but Bumble could adopt AI without being sold. It could hire help from Google or Palantir, which send "forward-deployed engineers" (consultants embedded with a customer) to help businesses adopt AI.
- Peers argues the real reason Bumble is selling is that its business is troubled.
- Bumble revenue fell 10% in 2025 and another 15% in the first half of 2026.
- As of June 30, 2026, Bumble had $450 million of debt but only $154 million in cash.
- Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky congratulated founder and outgoing CEO Whitney Wolfe Herd on X, which Peers finds hard to take at face value.
- Bumble is selling at $6.05 a share, one-seventh of its $43 a share IPO price (initial public offering, its 2021 stock market debut).
- Herd's IPO prospectus had promised "a very bright future" that would drive great value for shareholders.
- Gupta and co-CEO John Hegeman, a former Meta Platforms executive, could still turn Bumble around. Herd said "the best is yet to come." Peers says public market investors who got burned will not share in that.

## Character.AI's slide into oblivion

- Early AI startup Character.AI filed for bankruptcy on Monday 2026-10-12, likely one of the final steps in its long decline.
- Its investors probably did not mind much: they cashed out at a decent profit in 2024 when Google paid $2.7 billion to license Character.AI's models.
- In that deal, co-founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, former Google engineers, rejoined Google. Shazeer later left for OpenAI.
- The Google deal was one of several 2024 license-and-hire deals, also including Microsoft with Inflection AI and Amazon with Adept, to get talent and technology from AI startups.
- The tech giants structured them as licensing deals, not acquisitions, hoping to avoid antitrust scrutiny (government review of deals that could hurt competition). Many were investigated anyway.
- What is left sometimes survives: Inflection recently launched Inflection AI Labs. Adept's website has not been updated since mid-2024, shortly after its Amazon deal.
- In mid-2025 Character hired Karandeep Anand, formerly of Meta, as CEO. A few months later The Information reported it was considering raising money.
- Last month, September 2026, Walt Disney Co. hired Anand as chief technology officer, along with other members of Character.AI's technical team.
- Disney has also bid $20 million to license Character.AI's intellectual property in the bankruptcy process, Character said on 2026-10-13.

## In other news

- Candle Media, the Blackstone-backed media rollup run by former Disney executives Kevin Mayer and Tom Staggs, is selling Reese Witherspoon's Hello Sunshine back to Witherspoon, along with French firm Mediawan, per Bloomberg. Candle bought it at a $900 million valuation in 2021, seen as steep then.
- Apple is launching HomeView, a $299 tablet-like smart home device with a 7-inch screen for kitchen counters or walls, as part of a renewed push into the home. Apple says it is for watching and listening to content and managing the smart home.
- JPMorgan said it is blocking external AI agents, such as Meta's Muse and Instinct, from accessing client accounts.
- CEO Jamie Dimon said on the third-quarter earnings call on 2026-10-13: "We're not going to allow just any outside agent to come in inside the company because that's just too risky for clients and for JPMorgan."

## Why it matters for the wiki

- OpenAI journey: on top of rogue agents and fired safety researchers, an alleged insider spying case shows how much its secrets are worth.
- AI shift 2024-2026 story: the 2024 license-and-hire deals (Character.AI, Inflection, Adept) now show their end state, and the same pattern sits behind Nvidia's Groq deal.
- Retail stores, boxes, agents story: a big bank blocking outside AI agents like Meta's Muse is a real limit on agentic commerce.
- Apple journey: a new $299 HomeView device marks a renewed smart home push.
- Meta journey: Muse is now blocked by at least one major bank.
