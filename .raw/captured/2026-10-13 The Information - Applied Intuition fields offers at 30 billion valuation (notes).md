# Notes: Applied Intuition Fields Offers at $30 Billion Valuation

The Information, Stephanie Palazzolo, 2026-10-13 (Dealmaker newsletter).

## The core story

- For three years AI labs like OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI took most investor attention and the highest valuations.
- Now more startups that build apps, agents or other AI-powered software are reaching valuations of $20 billion or more.
- Applied Intuition, which makes software to train self-driving vehicles, has received investment offers valuing it at least $30 billion, per people who have spoken to company leaders.
- The offers will likely push the company to raise at least $1 billion in the next few months, the same people said.

## Why the number matters

- A $30 billion valuation would double its June 2026 valuation.
- It would back the nine-year-old company's choice to focus on the software side of self-driving instead of also building and running the cars.
- At the end of 2024 it had $400 million in annual recurring revenue (ARR, yearly revenue from ongoing contracts). It has doubled revenue every year in recent years, investor Andreessen Horowitz said in July 2026.
- The round would add to the group of startups worth tens of billions, such as coding agent Cognition and Perplexity.

## What the company does

- Founders: Qasar Younis, former chief operating officer of Y Combinator, and Peter Ludwig, a former Google product manager. Younis is CEO.
- It sells software to develop, test and validate driver-assistance and automated driving systems, plus operating systems for vehicles.
- Customers include Japan's Isuzu, which uses its software to run self-driving heavy trucks, Volkswagen, and Saudi Arabia's Humain.
- It is expanding into tech for autonomous military vehicles, which sets it up to compete with defense tech startups Anduril and Saronic.
- This summer it launched Dana, an AI agent tool that employees use to automate building systems for physical AI and software-controlled vehicles.
- Last week it announced deals to supply vehicle operating systems and other tech to Honda and Nissan.

## Investors and backdrop

- Google's Waymo has expanded commercial service across the U.S., and its success is encouraging investors to back self-driving startups.
- Applied Intuition is based in Sunnyvale, Calif. It has raised $1.2 billion from investors including Andreessen Horowitz, General Catalyst, Lux Capital and Kleiner Perkins.
- It has not used most of that money, Andreessen Horowitz said in July 2026.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: big valuations are spreading from model labs to application and physical AI companies.
- Google journey: Waymo's success is lifting the whole self-driving sector, while Applied Intuition bets on the opposite model, software only, no owned fleet.
- Ties to existing Waymo claims on asset-heavy versus asset-light robotaxi strategy.
