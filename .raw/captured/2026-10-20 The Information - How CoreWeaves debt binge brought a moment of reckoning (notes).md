# Notes: How CoreWeave's Debt Binge Brought a Moment of Reckoning

The Information, Dakin Campbell, 2026-10-20 (deep dive).

## The market selloff and rising debt yields

- CoreWeave shares fell roughly 20 percent in one week, dropping from nearly $80 to just over $61, leaving the public company valued at around $35 billion.
- The decline far exceeded the broader market fall of less than 1 percent, and dragged down fellow neoclouds Nebius (down 15 percent) and IREN (down 17 percent).
- Yields on CoreWeave's existing debt climbed above 15 percent, reflecting sharp investor anxiety over debt service as benchmark bond yields rose and September inflation data increased the odds of interest rate hikes.
- CoreWeave CEO Mike Intrator told employees in a memo that the market was acting irrationally, while securities filings showed Intrator sold shares every week from April through late September 2026.
- Venture capitalist Ben Horowitz called CoreWeave a Rube Goldberg financial machine on the All-In podcast, explaining why Andreessen Horowitz largely avoided debt-heavy neocloud investments.
- Investors have used CoreWeave stock and credit default swaps to hedge broad exposure to private AI foundation labs.

## The debt model and Blackstone origin

- CoreWeave began in 2017 as Atlantic Crypto, founded by commodities traders mining cryptocurrency with Nvidia GPUs, before pivoting to AI cloud compute after the 2018 crypto downturn.
- In early 2023, Blackstone CEO Steve Schwarzman approved a $2.3 billion loan to CoreWeave despite serious internal misgivings about backing a firm that generated only $16 million in revenue the prior year.
- The loan was anchored by Microsoft's compute contract after OpenAI's ChatGPT launch left Microsoft short of GPUs, pioneering a structure where debt was collateralized directly by AI chips.
- CoreWeave structures each project by borrowing against long-term customer contracts, estimating it takes two and a half years of cash flow to repay project debt while continuing to collect payouts.
- The company layers non-project corporate debt on top to fund operations, piling debt on top of debt.
- In 2025, CoreWeave generated $5.1 billion in revenue across contracts with Microsoft, Meta, and OpenAI, having gone public in March 2025 at $40 per share before peaking above $183.

## Wall Street ties and insurance capital

- Blackstone helped CoreWeave tap insurance companies seeking yield, including American General Life Insurance, Fidelity & Guaranty Life, and Security Life of Denver, who bought over $740 million of debt tied to a Meta contract.
- Nvidia invested $221 million in CoreWeave in April 2023 and another $2 billion in January 2026 to foster cloud alternatives to Amazon, Google, and Microsoft.
- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang publicly defended CoreWeave, saying its strength was improving daily and that it would not need an additional Nvidia credit backstop.
- Rosenblatt Securities analyst John McPeake noted CoreWeave raised hourly GPU rental prices by an additional 10 percent at a recent customer conference.
- Meanwhile, credit strategists note rising rates and heavy debt supply could force neoclouds to dilute shareholders with equity sales.
