# Notes: Wall Street and Silicon Valley Split Over AI's Price Tag

The Information, Cory Weinberg and Valida Pau, 2026-09-30.

## The core story

- Silicon Valley investors and Wall Street investors increasingly disagree on what AI companies are worth. This clouds the outlook for trillions invested in AI.
- Stock market worries have delayed several medium-sized IPOs (initial public offerings, a company's first sale of shares on the stock market).
- Sentiment on Anthropic's giant IPO is cooling; it is coming later in the year than investors expected and is likely weeks away from its roadshow (the pitch tour to investors before listing).
- Two large public investors said Tuesday bankers should value Anthropic nearer $1.5 trillion, not the $2 trillion some bankers floated early on.

## Why public investors are wary

- Venture capitalists still compete to invest at ever higher prices. Public investors are retreating to big, safe stocks amid rising interest rates and high oil prices.
- Samantha Lau (CIO, small and mid-cap growth equities, AllianceBernstein): new listings must be priced right and she does not see it; the only way to open the market is to be conservative.
- OpenAI and Anthropic will need steady financing even after IPOs to cover hundreds of billions in data center commitments.
- Anthropic has committed to at least 14.8 gigawatts of compute that could cost well over $500 billion over the next decade (The Information reported). Reuters this week reported data from Anthropic's draft IPO prospectus (the official disclosure document), renewing focus on its commitments and risks.
- OpenAI is gaining with business customers and is in early talks to raise about $30 billion pre-IPO, likely from some of the same investors.
- Lau likes the agent software tech firms are showing, but asks who pays before the payoff, especially as higher rates raise borrowing costs: "who's paying for it?"

## Deals stalling

- Oura (smart ring) delayed its IPO Tuesday, the day before final pricing; investors said its valuation ask was too high (per people familiar).
- SB Energy (SoftBank-owned power and data center firm building a big Ohio facility for OpenAI) has not started marketing nearly a month after filing. Bankers discuss about $60 billion; investors balk, partly over its dependence on OpenAI for revenue.
- Data center IPOs coming: Nscale (London neocloud, meaning a newer AI-focused cloud provider) and Switch (private-equity-owned). Investors dislike their heavy debt and need for more funding as rates rise.
- Early next year: CyrusOne and Silver Lake-owned Vantage Data Centers, expected to seek billions at valuations in the many tens of billions.
- One seasoned IPO investor said the pushback reminds bankers and private equity owners that investors are not "price takers."

## Venture froth continues

- Instinct, a viral AI consumer assistant (rivals include Meta and OpenAI), raised $1 billion at a $10 billion valuation a year after founding. Backers: Sequoia, Benchmark, Coatue.
- VCs privately complain about startups using loose financial metrics and selling big stakes in secondary sales (selling existing shares privately) long before IPO prep.
- Sequoia's Pat Grady, in a presentation to limited partners (the investors in a VC fund) posted publicly last week, said VCs now broadly believe AI has reached artificial general intelligence (AI matching or beating humans at all tasks). He also said valuations are "totally insane" and "emblematic of the froth."
- Evan Skorpen (Lead Edge Capital): VCs think about the upside case; public investors must hold stocks daily and focus on what can go wrong.

## The SpaceX comparison and Anthropic's advantages

- SpaceX raised nearly $86 billion in June, the largest IPO ever. Its stock is up about 10%, valuing it around $2 trillion, beating the market and rewarding Founders Fund, Valor and Sequoia.
- Anthropic's possible boosts: big mutual funds already bought shares privately at lower prices; Nvidia, Alphabet and Amazon could add cash; individual investors may pile in.
- Ashley MacNeill (Vista Equity Partners): SpaceX gains plus Anthropic anticipation may make funds reject smaller deals. Private valuations are not fully carrying into public markets, and it is acute now because of jumbo IPOs.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics: the gap between private and public valuations is a key test of whether AI spending pays off.
- Compute scarcity costs money: Anthropic's 14.8 GW and $500B+ commitments need ongoing financing.
- Model labs vs clouds: data center builders (SB Energy, Nscale, Switch) face doubts over debt and dependence on one lab.
