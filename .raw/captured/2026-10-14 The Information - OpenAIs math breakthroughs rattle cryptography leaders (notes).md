# Notes: OpenAI's Math Breakthroughs Rattle Cryptography Leaders

The Information, Tiffany Li, 2026-10-14.

## Cryptographers sound alarm over AI math

- OpenAI's release of more than 300 math solutions from an internal model sparked serious concern among prominent cryptographers.
- Experts worry that AI advances in mathematics could yield new algorithms capable of cracking encryption methods protecting online payments and private messaging.
- Public key cryptography underpins internet security by using math problems that are simple to calculate in one direction with a public key, but computationally difficult to reverse without a private key.
- Whitfield Diffie, who co-invented public key cryptography in 1976 with the Diffie-Hellman algorithm, stated he anticipates a vast change in mathematics and cryptanalysis within one to five years.
- Co-inventor Martin Hellman expects AI-driven attacks to disrupt cybersecurity in the short term, but predicts AI will ultimately help patch vulnerabilities in the long run.
- Johns Hopkins University professor Matthew Green warned that frontier AI models could make a fundamental break in elliptic curve cryptography within two years.
- Web security firm Cloudflare found in 2025 that a single elliptic curve algorithm, X25519, secures the majority of internet connections.
- Justin Drake, a security researcher at the Ethereum Foundation, called on the cryptocurrency industry to prepare for bunker mode due to potential AI math advances.

## OpenAI research and withheld results

- OpenAI's published collection of math papers contained no cryptography findings, leading experts to suspect the company withheld results or shared them privately with the U.S. government first.
- Silverfort CEO Hed Kovetz noted the public may never learn about certain sensitive cryptographic breakthroughs due to national security concerns.
- OpenAI has put significant resources into cryptography, previously releasing theoretical results in quantum complexity and lattice cryptography from its Astra model in September 2026.
- OpenAI employs top cryptography specialists on its technical staff, including Harvard University professor Boaz Barak.
- Princeton University professor Ewin Tang argued widely studied core encryption algorithms were deliberately designed to resist mathematical breakthroughs, making them harder to break than implementation code.
- Tang noted less-studied algorithms, such as post-quantum methods designed to resist quantum computers, remain more vulnerable to AI discovery.
- In July 2026, Anthropic published research that largely broke a post-quantum algorithm proposed to the National Institute of Standards and Technology, sharing findings early with the U.S. government.

## Security incidents and industry news

- The FBI arrested a Saudi national working at OpenAI who allegedly placed a sophisticated recording device wrapped in toilet paper into a conference room television USB port.
- Dario Amodei had warned about information hazards before leaving OpenAI in 2021, writing memos on an air-gapped computer disconnected from networks to prevent espionage.
- Microsoft Azure president Girish Bablani asked leaders across his unit to find ways to reduce operating costs by at least 20%.
- JPMorgan CFO Jeremy Barnum stated the bank is monitoring AI capital expenditure exposure and blocking external AI agents such as Meta's Muse and Instinct from accessing client accounts.
- AI persona startup Character.ai filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy after facing legal, regulatory, and operational headwinds.
- In Apple's trade secrets lawsuit against OpenAI, a judge granted Apple expedited access to OpenAI's hiring materials and interview checklists for former Apple employees.
- OpenAI entered early talks to acquire cybersecurity firm Trail of Bits, while Anthropic agreed to license technology and hire engineers from data security startup Opaque Systems.
- Tencent raised roughly $5.7 billion in bond sales to fund AI capital expenditure including chip purchases.
- Apple announced HomeView, a $299 smart home display with a 7-inch screen for kitchen counters or wall mounts.
