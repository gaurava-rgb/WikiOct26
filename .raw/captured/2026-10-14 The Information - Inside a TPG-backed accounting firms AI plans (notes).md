# Notes: Inside a TPG-Backed Accounting Firm's Plans for AI and More Acquisitions

The Information, Alex Eichenstein, 2026-10-14.

## The core story

- Smith + Howard, an accounting firm backed by private equity giant TPG, is preparing a new round of acquisitions, including two deals expected to close by the end of 2026.
- The firm is exploring using artificial intelligence to handle basic routine work where tasks involve repetitive steps, according to CEO Sean Taylor.
- Smith + Howard hopes AI will allow it to expand without hiring accountants at the same pace as in the past.
- This strategy reflects a broader bet across private equity and AI labs that software can increase profitability when consolidating professional services businesses.

## Growth and acquisition targets

- Smith + Howard employs more than 800 workers across seven states and expects roughly $175 million in revenue in 2026.
- The firm's revenue has grown 10% to 15% annually for the past decade, excluding acquisitions.
- Broad Sky Partners previously backed the firm before selling its stake to TPG in August 2026.
- The firm aims to add 20% to 25% of forward revenue through acquisitions, lifting target purchase size to between $5 million and $50 million in annual revenue compared with $2 million to $20 million previously.
- Smith + Howard plans further acquisitions in 2027 and is expanding across more U.S. cities while adding specialized services.

## Passing savings versus retaining profits

- Private equity firms and AI labs tout AI's ability to lift portfolio company returns, including OpenAI's majority-owned Deployment Co., which launched in May 2026 with backing from TPG.
- Venture firms are pursuing similar strategies: Thrive Capital launched Thrive Holdings to buy and modernize firms like accountants, while General Catalyst is pursuing AI rollups.
- A central question is whether companies pass AI cost savings to customers through lower prices, which would erase financial gains.
- Brian Goffman, managing director of AI at TPG, argued that accounting firms hold onto savings rather than passing them on, noting audit fees continued climbing after offshoring reduced labor costs.
- Goffman argued that while passing cost cuts to customers can spark a race to the bottom, the accounting sector avoids that trap.
- Taylor noted private equity investors were previously divided between those excited about AI productivity and those scared it would reduce billable hours and revenue.

## Companywide Claude deployment

- Smith + Howard rolled Anthropic's Claude out to all employees to experiment and propose automation uses.
- Taylor said automation will start with basic tasks such as industry-specific audits, while complex financial statement audits remain much harder to automate.
- The firm is searching for a C-suite executive to lead AI initiatives and is designing new client software products.
- Through TPG, Smith + Howard brought in external AI trainers partnering with labs like Anthropic, providing several weeks of employee training in exchange for minimum spending commitments.
- TPG maintains relationships with major AI labs including Anthropic, Microsoft, and Google.

## Valuation multiples and revenue per employee

- Accounting firms with stable client rosters but little technology trade at low valuations of five to six times EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization).
- Established accounting firms receiving direct private equity backing command valuations of 12 times EBITDA or higher.
- TPG is willing to pay premium prices because it believes it can generate extra value by applying AI across acquired firms.
- Unlike offshoring which delivers immediate cost savings, AI requires upfront capital spending and several years to pay off.
- TPG relies on revenue per employee as its primary metric to track whether AI investments produce real financial payoffs.
