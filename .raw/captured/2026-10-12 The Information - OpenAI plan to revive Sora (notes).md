# Notes: OpenAI’s Plan To Revive Sora

The Information, AI Agenda newsletter, Stephanie Palazzolo and Cory Weinberg, 2026-10-12.

## Sora was shut down

- Earlier in 2026 OpenAI shut down its video model Sora and the TikTok-like app built on it.
- That was part of a wider cull of server-heavy "side projects", even as rivals xAI and Google kept pushing their own video models.
- Making and running Sora cost so much that OpenAI moved the resources to its regular large language models and ChatGPT, which make more economic sense.
- OpenAI dropped Sora as part of a push to cut side projects and stay focused on ChatGPT and on fighting Anthropic in coding and enterprise.

## The plan to bring it back

- A stealthy startup building AI video for filmmakers, first revealed by The Information the month before, is in talks with OpenAI to access Sora, tweak it, and sell software or other services built on it, per a person with knowledge of the talks.
- The startup was co-founded in recent months by Hollywood mogul Jeffrey Katzenberg, former Sora research lead Bill Peebles, and former Dropbox CFO Sujay Jaswa. Its name is not known.
- It is unclear whether the startup will get Sora's weights. Weights are the learned "settings" that decide how a model answers.
- If the deal goes through, the startup would get deep enough access to heavily customize the model, the person said.
- AI labs usually guard weights closely, but sharing them here would not be surprising, because Peebles led Sora and already knows how it was built and trained.
- If a deal is completed, OpenAI would get equity (an ownership stake) in the startup. The size of the stake could not be learned.
- The deal would let OpenAI salvage some return on the money and time it spent on Sora.

## Signs OpenAI is drifting from its focus push

- In recent months OpenAI has backslid a bit on cutting side projects.
- It revived its robotics efforts.
- It hired a Patreon co-founder to lead a new "creator product" division.
- It is pushing hard on a costly personal-device project.

## Big number: OpenAI revenue

- OpenAI recently told investors its annualized revenue was approaching $50 billion at the end of September 2026, per a person with knowledge of the communications. Annualized revenue means the current monthly pace times 12.
- That is about $20 billion less than figures in other recent reports, including The Information's own.

## Other news in the newsletter

- Vice President J.D. Vance said the U.S. government is limiting Microsoft's ability to sponsor visas for some immigrant workers, citing an "ongoing investigation" into claims Microsoft laid off Americans while hiring more immigrants.
- Axios reported that top executives at AI firms including Anthropic and OpenAI are gaming out scenarios for public revolt after a catastrophic AI event.
- Anthropic said its AI used the internet to inadvertently fill out government forms and submit a false homicide crime tip to police. In response, Trump administration officials said they now require AI companies to report and correct security incidents.
- The three safety researchers OpenAI fired the week before said they were punished for raising safety concerns.
- CrowdStrike said the attacker in a recent hack of South Korean financial institutions used an open-source Chinese AI agent called Artex plus Chinese large language models.

## Deals

- Firmus, a Nvidia-backed Australian AI data center firm, withdrew its planned IPO at a valuation above $30 billion and is exploring a $2 billion to $3 billion private round with existing investors instead.
- TypeSafe AI, whose model outputs decisions instead of text, raised $870 million in a Series A at a $7.5 billion valuation, led by Andreessen Horowitz.
- Amazon is in talks to buy Israeli AI startup Decart, whose software helps developers switch between AI chips, at around $7 billion, per the Wall Street Journal.
- Nvidia is in early talks to buy, or invest more in, Reflection AI, an open-weight model maker valued at about $25 billion, per the Financial Times.
- Nvidia committed $1 billion over five years to U.S. "super intelligence" research at national labs and universities, covering quantum computing, healthcare and energy.
- GlobalFoundries signed a $2 billion, five-year deal with TSMC to make silicon interposers (a packaging part used in AI chips) in Malta, New York.
- Arena, formerly Chatbot Arena, which ranks AI models, raised a $200 million Series B co-led by Lightspeed and Khosla Ventures.
- Hone, founded by former OpenAI and Cognition staff to build agents that run whole business operations, raised a $60 million seed led by Benchmark and Index Ventures.
- Anthropic quietly signed a multiyear cloud deal with bitcoin miner IREN, part of a push that now spans more than 15 gigawatts of data center leasing commitments across more than 10 cloud providers.
