# Notes: Cracks Emerge in AI's Debt-Fueled Data Center Boom

The Information, Dakin Campbell, 2026-09-30 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Bond markets are getting tough for lower-rated borrowers. Financing the data center boom is getting more expensive, and investors are pickier about which projects they back.
- The bank loan market is also strained. Some lenders, Société Générale, Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corp. (SMBC) and Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (MUFG), are more selective on data center deals, say people arranging them.
- Result: some planned data centers could struggle to get loans, putting AI growth plans at risk.

## Pressure from big tech borrowing

- Amazon, Google and Microsoft together will spend about $700 billion on capital expenditures this year, and around that level for the next few years. Capital expenditures (capex) are spending on long-lived assets like buildings and servers.
- They have issued nearly $160 billion in investment-grade debt this year, flooding the market. Investment grade means bonds rated safe by credit agencies.
- The "spread" (extra yield investors demand over safe benchmarks) on hyperscaler debt rose about 0.25 percentage points this year, versus 0.04 for the broader investment-grade market. Hyperscalers are the giant cloud companies.
- Big tech is also pushing spending into separate ventures that borrow in the high-yield market (junk bonds, riskier debt that pays more). Developers building for Anthropic and OpenAI also use it. About $55 billion of AI-related high-yield bonds have sold this year.

## Concessions in recent deals

- CleanSpark, a bitcoin miner moving into AI data centers, is building one for Meta. To sell $2.3 billion in bonds this month it offered one of the biggest discounts of the past year: investors paid 98.5 cents per dollar of principal.
- Its coupon (annual interest rate) of 7.875% was already high for such projects. It also agreed to repay principal over time, lowering refinancing risk for buyers.
- A discount boosts investor returns because they still get the full dollar back at the end.
- Notable because Meta, the end tenant, is investment grade, which usually reassures lenders. Investors wanted extra pay for construction and project risk.
- Per Morgan Stanley data (it led 13 of the last 20 such deals in 12 months), all four high-yield data center bond deals since July sold at a discount. Of the first 10 in that period, only three did.
- Mid-August: Zenith Arc LLC, backed by a Coatue Management venture and infrastructure startup Fluidstack, sold debt at 99.5 cents to fund a data center leased by trading firm Jane Street. Yields rose further once it traded. Yields move opposite to prices.
- Connor Minnaar, fixed-income portfolio manager at Manulife Investment Management, called discounts "a newer development in the current market." Early in the year investors accepted almost any structure.
- If a project needs 12% returns and borrowing costs near that, margins get so thin the project may no longer make sense.

## Macro pressure

- Government bond yields are spiking on fears that high inflation will push the Federal Reserve to raise rates hard.
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said last week government yields climbed partly because heavy tech debt issuance is crowding out investors.
- Higher government yields force riskier projects to pay even more. One banker described a move up in quality: investors now skip long-build or inexperienced-developer projects for safer debt at similar returns.
- Treasury volatility is shaking the IPO market too. Anthropic's draft IPO paperwork reportedly shows a $42 billion net loss last year and over half a trillion dollars in compute and infrastructure commitments. Anthropic was expected to reveal its filing as soon as earlier this month but has not.
- SB Energy and Nscale, building capacity for OpenAI and Anthropic, made IPO filings public in recent weeks; timing is unclear.

## Banks pulling back

- Bank loans are slower, carry more lender protections, and some release money only as milestones are hit.
- Two people with knowledge: SocGen and SMBC are more selective; MUFG, a bigger industry lender in recent years, is stepping back.
- Past deals: SocGen led a $7.1 billion financing for the first OpenAI-Oracle Stargate site. MUFG and JPMorgan Chase led $38 billion for two Oracle projects. SMBC helped lead $18 billion for Oracle's New Mexico project.
- No major deal has been pulled, and developers are still paying up to keep projects moving. Minnaar: it is "still a story of concessions," a race to get capacity online.
- Wake-up call: last week Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the New Mexico project's developer (owned by Blue Owl Capital) after power delays. Force majeure is a contract clause that can delay or waive duties when events outside one's control intervene.
- People familiar say the loan is well structured and would not free Oracle from lease payments even without power. But one banker said it could push banks to demand tougher protections, cutting lending supply further.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics: the buildout's limit may shift from chips and power to the cost of money, with junk-bond discounts now standard.
- Compute scarcity: if speculative projects stop pencilling out, future capacity for Anthropic and OpenAI could slow.
- Meta and Amazon journeys: Meta's tenancy did not spare CleanSpark from concessions; Amazon is part of the $700 billion capex wave that is crowding the bond market.
