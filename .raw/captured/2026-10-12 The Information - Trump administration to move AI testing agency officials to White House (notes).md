# Notes: Trump Administration Plans to Move AI Testing Agency Officials to White House

The Information, Leo Schwartz, 2026-10-12 (exclusive).

## The core story

- CAISSI is the small Commerce Department agency that tests new AI models. Its full name is now the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence.
- In September 2026 the Trump administration started restructuring CAISSI so that three of its senior officials report directly to the White House, per a White House official.
- For months officials weighed moving all of CAISSI into the White House. Chief of Staff Susie Wiles and other senior officials settled on the smaller move to better fit CAISSI into White House decision-making on AI.
- The three officials, not yet named because their move is still being processed, will report to Michael Kratsios, director of the Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP, the White House science office), and Sean Cairncross, the National Cyber Director.
- A senior Commerce official said Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick invited Kratsios and Cairncross to co-manage CAISSI six months before publication.
- The White House and Commerce have been confused about the move, which is still being finalized. The White House official said Commerce's lack of coordination shows why CAISSI leadership needs to move.

## A troubled agency

- CAISSI recently renamed itself to match Trump's preferred new word for AI, "Super Intelligence." It is the agency's third name in less than two years.
- Since its creation under Biden, the agency has suffered from limited resources, staff upheaval and bureaucratic nitpicking.
- The debate over where to house CAISSI has left staff confused about their status in recent weeks, per two people familiar.
- Some officials want CAISSI out of Commerce so its staff get better resources, per people close to the administration. Kratsios has argued for moving it to the Department of Energy, where it could use the national laboratories, The Information reported earlier.
- CAISSI has only about 40 staff and about $15 million in annual funding, yet it plays a key role in testing new AI models for capabilities and safety.

## Pre-deployment testing

- Its technical staff matter more now that the administration is dealing with increasingly capable cyber models.
- Under a White House framework finalized in August 2026 for testing models before release, CAISSI works with the better-funded National Security Agency (NSA) to test frontier models like OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Mythos before broader release, per four people familiar.
- In a September 30 blog post, Google said it had submitted its latest model, Gemini 4 Argon, to the White House for the voluntary testing process. Google still has not widely released that model.
- The White House still has not published the testing framework, and the split of duties between CAISSI and the NSA remains unclear, which worries industry.
- Google, OpenAI and Anthropic recently explored forming their own industry group to supplement CAISSI's model testing, partly because they worry the agency lacks resources, The Information reported earlier.

## Left out of the new task force

- CAISSI's lack of political support and money worries AI policy experts, lawmakers, frontier labs and some administration officials.
- In early October 2026 Trump announced a new SI (super intelligence) task force with many senior officials, but nobody from Commerce and no mention of CAISSI.
- Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, who also oversees the NSA, was named head of the task force.
- People close to the White House and the AI industry said bringing CAISSI into the White House would be welcome, since it could raise the agency's political weight and streamline model testing.

## Political football: history

- The Biden administration created the agency as the AI Safety Institute under a 2023 executive order. It was meant to be staffed by technical experts, test frontier models worldwide, and work with a U.K. partner agency, industry and policy groups on AI risk standards.
- The incoming Trump administration was skeptical. Under AI czar David Sacks, who favored light regulation, the White House renamed it the Center for AI Standards and Innovation.
- The administration hired AI testing expert Collin Burns to lead it, but fired him within days because he had worked at Anthropic, The Washington Post reported.
- The administration often froze the agency out of wider AI talks, per a former White House official. That stayed true after Anthropic announced Mythos in April 2026, when the Office of the National Cyber Director led the White House response.
- The White House leaned more on CAISSI's expertise over the summer, including after the administration "imposed export controls on Anthropic" in June 2026 over security concerns about Mythos and Fable.
- CAISSI has formal agreements with many major AI labs to evaluate their models.

## Turnover

- In July 2026 director Chris Fall unexpectedly resigned after three months; the agency is still looking for a new head, per two people familiar.
- Meanwhile Arvind Raman, director of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST, the Commerce agency that houses CAISSI), is overseeing it.
- Cyber lead Andrew Fasano left in June 2026 to join Anthropic, per his LinkedIn.
- Key staffer Paul Christiano joined OpenAI's nonprofit board in September 2026, though his LinkedIn still lists him as a CAISSI adviser.
- White House spokesperson Liz Huston said Trump is "ensuring America leads the world in Super Intelligence."
