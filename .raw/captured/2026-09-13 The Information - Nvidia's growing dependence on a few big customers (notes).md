# Notes: Nvidia's Growing Dependence On a Few Big Customers

The Information, Martin Peers and Qianer Liu, 2026-09-13 (Sunday Insights column).

## The core story

- Nvidia's sales lean more and more on a handful of buyers, and some of those buyers may cut back over time.
- That gives CEO Jensen Huang strong reasons to grow new customers by investing in neoclouds and AI firms. A neocloud is a newer cloud company that mainly rents out AI chips, such as CoreWeave or Nebius.

## Customer concentration, by the numbers

- "Customer concentration" means a big share of sales comes from a few customers. That is risky: losing one hurts a lot.
- Nvidia discloses how many customers each make up more than 10% of sales.
- First half of this fiscal year (ending July): three such customers made up 44% of total sales.
- Last fiscal year: two customers made up 36%.
- Fiscal 2023: no customer reached 10%.
- The data center business (AI chips sold into data centers) grew from $15 billion in fiscal 2023 to $193.7 billion last year, and is on track to double this year.
- Investor Michael Burry, known for predicting the 2008 mortgage crisis, has called this concentration a major risk in recent months.

## Who the big customers probably are

- It is messy. The top three in the latest filing likely include Dell or Hon Hai (Foxconn), which build servers with Nvidia chips and resell them to many companies.
- Dell said revenue from "AI-optimized servers" doubled in Q2 to $16.4 billion, helping overall revenue grow 58%.
- Hon Hai's first-half revenue rose 35%, also driven by AI equipment.
- In February CFO Colette Kress said the five biggest cloud firms "and hyperscalers" made up more than 50% of data center revenue. A hyperscaler is a giant cloud operator. The phrase likely covers Meta and SpaceX.
- The top three could include SpaceX, Meta or Microsoft, all spending heavily on Nvidia-stocked data centers.
- Google and Amazon buy lots of Nvidia chips but also use their own chip designs, so they are less likely to be in the top group.
- Microsoft, Meta and SpaceX are also making their own chips, which could shrink their Nvidia orders later.

## Nvidia's diversification push

- It invests in chip buyers such as CoreWeave and Nebius to seed new demand.
- On Wednesday it added several Australian companies to its AI Factory program, where it supplies chips and networking gear to firms building and running data centers.
- It promoted similar build-outs in India and Armenia this year, aiming at "sovereign" customers (national, government-linked AI projects) and neoclouds beyond its big U.S. buyers.

## Payment terms: the new risk

- Accounts receivable means money customers owe Nvidia for products.
- July: 70% of receivables came from five customers. January: three customers made up 56%. End of fiscal 2025: two customers made up 33%.
- Dell's accounts payable (money it owes suppliers) jumped 48% in the first half to $49.7 billion at end of July. The article says Dell likely owes much of that to Nvidia.
- Nvidia's receivables rose 64% in the first half to $63 billion. Nvidia said this was due to "extended payment terms on large, multiquarter agreements with certain investment-grade customers." Investment-grade means a customer with a strong credit rating, judged unlikely to default.
- It stretched payment terms to 60 days from 45 the prior quarter, and a filing says it may offer 90 days up to one year for big data center builds.
- Longer terms cut how much cash Nvidia collects each quarter and tie it more closely to the health of the customers it is lending time to.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics beat: Nvidia is quietly acting like a lender, giving buyers up to a year to pay, which links its fate to its customers' finances.
- Model labs vs clouds beat: the biggest buyers (Microsoft, Meta, SpaceX, Google, Amazon) are all building their own chips, so Nvidia seeds neoclouds and sovereign buyers as a hedge.
- Compute scarcity beat: the data center chip business went from $15 billion to $193.7 billion in a few years, showing how fast demand grew.
