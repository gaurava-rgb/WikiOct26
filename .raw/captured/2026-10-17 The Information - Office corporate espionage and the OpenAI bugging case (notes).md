# Notes: Your Office Isn’t Spy Proof

The Information, Abram Brown, 2026-10-17 (The Weekend Newsletter).

## The OpenAI espionage scandal

- The Federal Bureau of Investigation arrested former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan for allegedly planting recording devices in OpenAI offices.
- Alfozan allegedly placed a listening device wrapped in toilet paper inside an OpenAI conference room.
- The United States Department of Justice press release prominently described Alfozan as a national of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, implying an effort to spy for Saudi Arabia.
- Alfozan attempted to plant two listening devices; he mislaid a second bug inside a bag and retrieved it from the OpenAI lost and found.

## Vulnerabilities from rapid hiring and cheap bugs

- Corporate security consultants warn that rapid hiring in the intensely competitive AI sector increases insider threat risks.
- Former FBI counterintelligence head Bill Priestap (now leader of security consultancy Trenchcoat Advisors) said companies recruiting under intense pressure often bring candidates on board with minimal background understanding.
- San Francisco private investigator David Abbott noted that commercial listening devices cost less than $100 and easily hide inside phone chargers, fake coffee cups, or fake smoke alarms.
- Abbott warned that finding one bug in an office sweep usually implies additional devices are present.

## Commercial counter-surveillance startups

- Security startup Strider Technologies, led by Eric and Greg Levesque in Salt Lake City, has raised $110 million to date.
- Strider sells AI-driven software that monitors corporate emails and internal communications to detect suspicious behavior, digital exhaust, and corporate espionage.
- Greg Levesque stated companies frequently face legitimate engineers or scientists who were coerced or approached by outside handlers rather than trained foreign intelligence officers.

- OpenAI journey: highlights physical and insider security vulnerabilities at frontier labs amidst intense talent competition.
- AI race geopolitics: foreign governments target frontier lab models, weights, and roadmaps through corporate espionage.
- Enterprise security: rapid growth in AI development drives demand for insider threat monitoring and defensive software.
