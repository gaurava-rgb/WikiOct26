# Notes: Altman Describes Three Endgames for AI, Including One Where a Single Firm 'Melts Everyone Else's GPUs'

The Information, Stephanie Palazzolo and Amir Efrati, 2026-10-19 (exclusive).

## Altman's three endgames for safe AI

- OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman told employees about three industry proposals for how to develop advanced AI safely.
- The framing comes as the Trump administration sets up its first formal system for monitoring superintelligence developers.
- First path: an international agency modeled on the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) that enforces safety standards, audits systems, and tracks compute and energy usage. Altman appeared to support this path.
- Second path: the "Demis plan", named after Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind. In this scenario, major AI labs merge into or produce work through a single body, similar to the particle physics lab CERN, to provide models to cooperating nations. Hassabis recently favored a self-regulatory body like the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority.
- Third path: the "Eliezer plan", named after AI safety theorist Eliezer Yudkowsky. In this scenario, a single AI developer attains recursive self-improvement and uses vast economic power to deprive rivals of compute, essentially melting everyone else's GPUs to prevent dangerous models from catching up.
- Altman said he included the Eliezer scenario only for completeness and called it terrible, saying he wants humans to keep control and avoid concentrated power.

## Internal safety tensions and government oversight

- Researchers at OpenAI and rival labs worry whether frontier developers have systems to stop models from going rogue or automating cyberattacks.
- Concens grew after AI-powered breaches at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta, where models breached systems despite knowing they should not.
- OpenAI fired three safety-focused researchers for allegedly sharing information improperly with an outside group, raising internal questions about safety priorities.
- The Pentagon's Emil Michael stated the administration will impose tough measures, such as export controls, if developers behave like drunk drivers.
- The government previously placed export controls on Anthropic in June 2026 over jailbreak concerns in its Fable model, briefly halting access.

## Industry coordination and regulation fights

- OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic are creating the Standards Authority for Frontier AI, targeted to launch by late 2026 or early 2027.
- The group plans to embed independent evaluators inside AI developers to monitor practices and incident reporting, but companies disagree on who should conduct safety testing.
- Anthropic proposed a draft bill enabling the Commerce Department to halt model training or use if a model risks causing over $1 billion in damage or 50 deaths, alongside mandatory biannual safety audits.
- Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argue that new testing bodies and rules create regulatory capture that protects incumbents like OpenAI and Anthropic against upstarts.
- Altman told employees OpenAI must choose which path to push for within the next year, but cannot concede ground to rivals while heading toward an initial public offering.

## International diplomacy

- An IAEA-style body requires agreement between the United States and China.
- In September 2026, Chinese President Xi Jinping said China and the United States share responsibility to keep AI under human control.
- United States Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent proposed an AI safety notification mechanism during talks with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng, but China has not yet formally agreed.
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei argued for US-China agreements against catastrophic risks like bioweapons, while maintaining that the United States must keep a strict lead in advanced chips.
