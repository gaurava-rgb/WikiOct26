# Notes: Microsoft Slashes Internal Claude Spending by a Third

The Information, Aaron Holmes, Jyoti Mann and Kevin McLaughlin, 2026-10-05 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Microsoft and Meta, two of Anthropic's biggest corporate customers, are pushing their own staff off Claude and onto in-house tools and models. Cost is the main driver.
- Both remain large Claude users, and Microsoft's spend on Claude for its customers keeps rising. But the move adds to headwinds for Anthropic ahead of its IPO.

## Key facts and numbers

- Microsoft internal Claude spend: projected at $1 billion+ a year as of May 2026, now cut by more than a third (per two people; the cut per one).
- Microsoft also pays Anthropic at least $2 billion a year to power Copilot features for customers. That has grown with Copilot subscribers and offset the internal cut, so total payments are roughly flat from earlier highs (per one person).
- In July 2026 Microsoft began routing complex Copilot queries to OpenAI's GPT-5.6 by default (per one person).
- Microsoft's cloud and AI group (60,000+ staff) cut per-employee AI budgets from $100,000 a month to about $10,000 in most cases (per one person).
- GitHub Copilot's "auto" mode sends most queries to OpenAI models, which Microsoft does not pay for under its OpenAI deal.
- Meta Claude Code users: about 60,000 earlier in 2026, about 30,000 recently (internal data; person familiar). Spring layoffs cut about 10% of 78,000 staff.
- Meta in-house tools: MetaCode 30,000+ users; Muse Code (sold externally since August) 6,000+ employee users.
- Meta spent over $105 million on Claude Code in a recent 28-day period (internal data).
- Anthropic context: annualized revenue passed $65 billion in July 2026 (reported); about 6,000 customers spent $100,000+ in the year to June; 1,000+ spent $1 million+; 100+ spent $10 million+ (company to investors). Two customers made nearly a quarter of 2025 revenue (Reuters, draft prospectus).
- OpenAI annualized revenue jumped about 70% in recent months to nearly $70 billion.

## Named people and roles

- Scott Guthrie (Azure) and Jay Parikh (developer tools), Microsoft: told staff to watch spend and prefer non-Anthropic models. Parikh's August memo: shifting to OpenAI models gives "greater value from our token investment."
- Mark Zuckerberg: launched Meta Enterprise Platform last week to sell AI to businesses.
- Microsoft spokesperson: confirmed OpenAI models are the internal coding default; engineers can still pick Claude.

## Change versus earlier reporting

- A June 2026 memo (reported earlier) had Meta on pace for billions in internal AI spend; Meta then added token budgets and a usage dashboard after "tokenmaxxing" and a "Claudeonomics" leaderboard drove a spring spike.
- Other customers (Cognition; and on privacy grounds Palantir, Nvidia, Booz Allen) have also sought cheaper or safer options.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Who pays for AI: token budgets now behave like any other cost line, and big buyers ration them.
- Meta journey: building its own models (Muse Spark) lets it cut a supplier and sell to enterprises.
- Switching Costs for coding agents look low when a platform owner controls the default model.
