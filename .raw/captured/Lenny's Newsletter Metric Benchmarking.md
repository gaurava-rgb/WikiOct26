# Metric benchmarking

Good means healthy, usually median or above. Great means best in class, usually top quartile or decile.
Read these directionally—there’s a lot of variance by industry and business model.

## Engagement and activity metrics

- New user activation rate: Good is 35%, Great is 50%+ (Source: Lenny Rachitsky and Yuriy Timen; Userpilot).
- Number of products used: Majority of customers using multiple products. For example, Datadog has 58% of customers using 4+ products, while CrowdStrike has 51% using 6+ products (Source: Datadog, CrowdStrike).

## Retention metrics

- Net revenue retention: Good is 100%, Great is 120%+ (Source: CRV; Aleph and Benchmarkit; Clouded Judgement).
- Gross revenue retention: Good is 90%, Great is 95%+ (Source: High Alpha; SaaS Capital).
- Annual logo retention: Good is 85%, Great is 95%+ (Source: Bessemer; CRV).
- 6-month user retention: Good is 30% for marketplaces and 25% for social; Great is 50% for marketplaces and 45% for social (Source: Lenny Rachitsky and Casey Winters).

## Cost efficiency metrics

- Cost per unit: decreases each time total volume doubles by 15% for Good, 20%+ for Great (Note: from solar/energy panels and batteries; Source: Our World in Data; McDonald & Schrattenholzer).
- Gross margin for software: Good is 75%, Great is 80%+ (Source: Clouded Judgement; Aleph and Benchmarkit).
- Gross margin for AI apps: Good is 50%, Great is 60%+ (Source: ICONIQ; Bessemer).
- Gross margin for marketplaces: Good is 70%, Great is 80%+ (Source: eBay, Etsy, Airbnb; DoorDash note: heavily managed marketplaces run lower).
- CAC payback period: Good is under 18 months, Great is under 12 months (Source: Aleph and Benchmarkit; KeyBanc and Sapphire; Dan Hockenmaier).
