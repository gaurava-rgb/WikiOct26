# Notes: Why Apple Is Betting on Foldable iPhones

The Information, Tiffany Li, 2026-09-20 (Sunday Insights).

## The core story

- New Apple CEO John Ternus showed the first foldable iPhone, the iPhone Duo, at an event earlier in September 2026.
- Many Americans may never have seriously thought about buying a foldable. A foldable phone has hinged halves that open into one large glass screen.
- Apple is betting that the demand which drove double-digit foldable growth outside North America for Huawei, Samsung and others will lift its flagship iPhone sales.
- Foldable demand should also soften an expected drop in global smartphone shipments in 2026.

## The forecasts

- Price: the iPhone Duo starts at $1,999 with 256 gigabytes of storage. It is far pricier than any earlier iPhone.
- IDC (a market research firm) expects 10 million Duo units sold in the first 12 months after launch.
- IDC projects those 10 million units will bring in $27 billion, counting pricier higher-storage models. That would be about 6% of Apple's expected fiscal 2026 revenue, per brokerage forecasts tracked by Yahoo.
- Linda Sui, founder of Smart Analytics Global, projects 6 million Duo units sold in 2026 alone.

## Why Apple needs it

- iPhone revenue was mostly flat from 2022 to 2025. It rose just 4% in the fiscal year ending September 2025, to nearly $210 billion.
- Better cameras and chips each year had not changed that much.
- Exception: iPhone sales jumped 22% in the June quarter on the iPhone 17 line, partly from buyers who last upgraded four years ago.
- Francisco Jeronimo, IDC vice president for data and analytics in Europe, Middle East and Africa: the foldable brings novelty and can attract high-end buyers willing to pay whatever Apple charges for its newest product.
- Jeronimo also thinks the Duo could get people upgrading more often again, chasing the newest must-have model.

## The foldable market

- Samsung launched the first mainstream foldable in 2019 and has been touting pre-orders for foldables such as the $1,900 Galaxy Z Fold8.
- Per IDC, global foldable revenue rose 16% last year to $27 billion. Non-foldable smartphone sales rose 7% to $550 billion.
- Most growth has been in Asian markets like China and South Korea. Foldables were just 2% of North American smartphone sales last year (Smart Analytics Global).

## Why the U.S. could grow

- Sui sees room in the U.S. because Apple customers already pay up. Owners of Pro and Pro Max iPhones could easily move to the Duo.
- Trade-ins of some recent iPhones could cut almost $900 off the sticker price.
- Installment plans, which spread a phone's cost over many months, are growing more popular and could help buyers justify the price. (In the U.S., carriers and Apple commonly let buyers pay off a phone monthly instead of upfront.)
- Apple's star power may grow U.S. foldable use overall, including cheaper rivals from Samsung and others.
- Sui: "Apple is going to educate the market and drive up the market adoption rate."

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Apple journey beat: a new CEO (Ternus) and a new form factor are Apple's answer to years of flat iPhone revenue.
- Pattern worth noting: Apple again enters a category late (Samsung went first in 2019) and is expected to expand it rather than invent it.
- Retail angle: trade-ins and installment plans are how a $1,999 phone gets sold in stores and carrier shops.
