# Notes: OpenAI and Anthropic Should Cap Compute for R&D, Say AI Safety Advocates

The Information, Tiffany Li, 2026-10-21 (policy analysis).

## The proposal to cap research compute

- AI safety advocates and researchers are urging leading AI labs to place explicit caps on the proportion of compute dedicated to research and development as a way to slow runaway capability growth.
- The AI Futures Project, an AI forecasting nonprofit, published a framework titled "How to Pace The US Frontier" authored by researcher Thomas Larsen.
- The proposal suggests allocating 70 percent of compute to customer inference, 25 percent to alignment and safety monitoring, and capping research and development at just 5 percent.
- Advocates argue that compute allocation is concrete, verifiable, and difficult to manipulate compared to subjective intelligence benchmarks.
- However, AI labs heavily resist compute caps; Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei previously identified raw compute as the primary driver of capability gains, while Demis Hassabis called compute the central bottleneck in AI progress.

## Current lab compute splits and geopolitical challenges

- OpenAI recently shifted approximately 10 percent of its total compute capacity toward alignment and safety tasks following recent security breaches.
- In July 2026, Anthropic allocated about 6 percent of its AI R&D compute to safety research, with a comparable share devoted to safeguards against harmful outputs.
- Policy experts note that unilateral US compute limits face steep geopolitical hurdles: without an agreement with China, American labs fear Chinese models will rapidly overtake Western frontier labs.
- Former Google policy lead Aalok Mehta noted compute constraints could simply push researchers to develop more efficient algorithms, as seen in Chinese labs.
- Anthropic's draft registration statement indicated it could spend up to $750 billion on computing over the next several years, having generated $16.3 billion in revenue and an $80 billion net loss in the first half of 2026.
- SpaceX reported third-quarter revenue of $14.6 billion, with $7.5 billion from its Super Intelligence division spanning Cursor, Grok Bot, and neocloud compute rentals.
- Former AWS enterprise sales leader Mike Clayville rejoined Amazon as vice president after six years at Stripe.
