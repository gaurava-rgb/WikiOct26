# Notes: Why Blackstone Thinks AI Bottlenecks Could Work in its Favor

The Information, Alex Eichenstein and Nick Wingfield, 2026-10-22.

## Bottlenecks as pricing power

- On its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, alternative asset manager Blackstone argued that physical constraints limiting the artificial intelligence buildout could increase the value of its holdings.
- Chief Executive Steve Schwarzman stated that electric power availability bottlenecks and rising local opposition to data centers make new capacity harder to build, lifting the value of existing operating facilities.
- President Jon Gray noted that Blackstone can demand higher lease returns on uncommitted data center projects to compensate for rising interest rates and borrowing costs.
- Blackstone reported that its digital infrastructure assets experienced extraordinary leasing momentum, significantly outperforming its traditional real estate and private equity portfolios.
- Jon Gray reiterated that AI computing demand continues to outpace the supply of available compute chips and data centers.

## Neocloud exposure and funding uncertainty

- Rising financing costs and debt anxiety have hit Blackstone portfolio companies.
- Shares in CoreWeave dropped another 10% on Thursday following steep declines earlier in October, and bond yields spiked. Blackstone has provided billions of dollars in loans to CoreWeave.
- Australian GPU cloud provider Firmus abruptly cancelled its planned initial public offering in October. Blackstone has provided both equity financing and loans to Firmus.
- Blackstone's joint venture with Google, announced in May 2026 with an initial $5 billion equity commitment to build a 500-megawatt TPU cloud, could require up to $25 billion in total capital and has faced site delays.
- Jas Khaira, head of AI investing at Blackstone, cautioned at a conference that the firm has not identified where all the public and private debt will come from to finance trillions of dollars in industry infrastructure.

## Market reactions

- Meta Platforms shares fell 7% on Thursday as investor optimism over third-quarter results gave way to dilution worries surrounding its new non-voting Class C shares intended to fund AI spending.
- Google formed a partnership with energy software firm Voltus to pay residential and commercial customers to curb electricity usage during peak periods.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Neocloud shakeout story: shows private credit giant Blackstone navigating mounting default risks on billions loaned to CoreWeave and Firmus while defending data center returns.
- Data center bottlenecks: highlights Blackstone's thesis that power scarcity and local opposition to data centers protect existing asset values and support higher lease pricing.
- AI capex financing: underscores Jas Khaira's acknowledgment that the financial markets lack clarity on who will fund trillions in AI infrastructure debt.
