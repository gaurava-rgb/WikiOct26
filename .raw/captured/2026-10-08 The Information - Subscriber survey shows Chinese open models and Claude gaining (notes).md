# Notes: Chinese Open Models Win Over One in Five of The Information’s Subscribers

Applied AI newsletter, Amir Efrati (executive editor), 2026-10-08. Based on a survey of The Information's subscribers taken in September 2026.

## Returns from AI

- Nearly two-thirds of subscribers surveyed said their organizations build AI applications.
- A third said their return from AI is a "multiple of what we spend."
- Another 17% said returns are positive but lower than hoped; 30% said it is too soon to tell; only 10% said AI spending has been a waste.
- The article contrasts this with a 2025 MIT study that found just 5% of custom-made enterprise AI tools reached production (real everyday use).
- The result was first shared at AI Agenda Live in September 2026.
- Likely reasons for the progress: AI models can now use other applications for complex tasks, can work in the background for hours, and write better code.
- Caveat: The Information's readers lean toward enterprises with software experience, which may tilt the result.

## Chatbots: Claude and Gemini up, ChatGPT still first

- Over the past year, subscribers' use of Anthropic's Claude and Google's Gemini rose at the expense of OpenAI's ChatGPT.
- ChatGPT still leads, and has regained some ground versus Claude in recent months.
- Gemini's comeback is probably due to Google signing more enterprise deals or tying Gemini into its core search engine.
- On Wednesday, Oct. 7, 2026, OpenAI launched visual answers in ChatGPT, to catch up with a similar Gemini feature that shows videos or images in some answers.
- Subscribers use Microsoft Copilot less, even as Microsoft signs up more corporate customers.
- SpaceXAI's Grok, which has looser settings than other chatbots, made a small comeback.

## Agents and coding tools

- Only 12% regularly use OpenClaw, the open-source AI agent technology, even among these tech-forward readers.
- Still, Anthropic, OpenAI and others add OpenClaw-like features every week to power persistent agents (agents that keep running for you over time), such as ChatGPT Dots, Grok Bot and Microsoft Autopilot.
- In coding assistants, the early GitHub Copilot and Cursor duopoly (two-company market) has vanished.
- Claude Code now leads. OpenAI's Codex surged in 2026 as it improved, and Cursor is not far behind.

## Open and Chinese models

- A quarter of subscribers use Perplexity (which also sells an agent for complex tasks across apps), and a quarter use models from Chinese developer DeepSeek.
- About one-sixth regularly use Moonshot's Kimi K3, which the article says shows the rise of Chinese open-weight alternatives to U.S. closed-source developers. Open-weight means the model's trained settings are published so anyone can run it.
- 46% use at least some open-source AI models for work; 21% use Chinese ones.
- That gap suggests some use open-weight models from U.S. firms such as Nvidia, Google or Thinking Machines Lab.
- Many open-source users still lean heavily on closed models like GPT and Claude. Only 20% use open-source models for most of their AI workloads.

## How fast things changed

- 12 months earlier, 59% of readers said they had never used an AI agent.
- Then, one of the most advanced agents was OpenAI's "deep research", which mostly did web-based retrieval and analysis.
- A year ago, more than two-thirds of subscribers correctly predicted AI progress would speed up.

## Jobs

- Nearly half say AI has not changed their hiring.
- 22% say they are hiring fewer workers because of AI; 10% say they are hiring more.
- Per the survey and broader U.S. government data, jobs are not disappearing for now, even as AI changes existing roles.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: a year-over-year snapshot, from 59% never using an agent to a third reporting multiple-times returns.
- Company journeys: Anthropic (Claude, Claude Code) and Google (Gemini) gained on OpenAI, though ChatGPT still leads and is fighting back.
- Chinese open-weight models (DeepSeek, Kimi K3) reach one in five readers, a beat for the open-model and price-pressure story.
- Nvidia and Google appear as open-weight model makers, not only chip and cloud sellers.
