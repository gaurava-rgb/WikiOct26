# Notes: Microsoft Cloud President Asks Leaders to Find 20% Cost Cuts

The Information, Kevin McLaughlin and Aaron Holmes, 2026-10-13 (exclusive).

## The cost-cut ask

- Microsoft has discussed significant cost reductions in Azure, its cloud server rental unit, per a person with direct knowledge.
- In recent weeks Azure president Girish Bablani told senior leaders in his group, about 11,500 employees, to find ideas to cut costs at least 20%. It is unclear if that would include layoffs.
- Nothing is final and plans could change, this person said.
- Bablani runs just one unit within Azure, which develops software for the cloud server business and is not part of the data center team. The broader cloud division has about 60,000 employees.
- One option is dropping contractors who work for the cloud unit.
- The talks come even as Azure revenue growth has sped up, a sign of how much data center spending weighs on Microsoft's finances.

## Microsoft denies it

- A Microsoft spokesperson said Azure leadership "has not directed teams to develop cost-cutting plans," that every responsible company plans for a range of scenarios, and called the report "inaccurate and careless reporting."

## Talent costs and AI budgets

- Some Azure leaders privately cite another reason for cuts: the cost of keeping talent. Rivals such as Anthropic and Meta have hired or tried to hire Microsoft staff, and Microsoft has had to match competing offers.
- Cloud chief Scott Guthrie has talked about wanting to raise AI spending budgets for existing employees as the company automates more work, per a person with knowledge.
- Microsoft has been raising AI spending per employee in recent months and pushing staff to use AI for productivity in sales and software development.
- Microsoft's total headcount in June 2026 was slightly lower than a year earlier, after several waves of job cuts and voluntary buyouts.

## Other clouds are cutting too

- Oracle and Amazon Web Services have also made cuts, including layoffs, despite faster revenue growth, because rising capital spending (big up-front spending) on AI data centers has hurt their free cash flow (cash left after running the business and paying for investment).

## Reorg around Arun Ulag

- Microsoft is reorganizing the cloud unit around senior executive Arun Ulag, one of a small group Nadella has been elevating as longtime executives scale back or retire.
- Ulag previously ran Azure's database products. He will now also oversee executives working on Azure Core, which provides computing power to customers such as OpenAI: Bablani, Billy Anders, Douglas Phillips and Mark Russinovich.
- Guthrie announced the changes internally on Monday (2026-10-12) and said Ulag will keep reporting to him.
- Charlie Bell, who ran security before moving to an advisory role earlier in 2026, joins Ulag's leadership team while still reporting to Nadella. (A correction: an earlier version wrongly said Bell reports to Ulag.)

## Azure's numbers

- Microsoft only began reporting Azure revenue in dollars in September 2026.
- Azure made $101.9 billion in the 12 months ending June 2026, up 40% from a year earlier, and grew 43% in the June quarter alone.
- AWS made nearly $150 billion over the same 12 months, with 37% growth in the second quarter of 2026.
- Azure's growth was held back by Microsoft's decision at the start of 2025 to pause many data center projects, The Information has reported.
- Azure is now about 31% of Microsoft's total revenue.

## Cash flow squeeze

- Microsoft generated $20 billion of free cash flow in the June 2026 quarter, up more than $4 billion from the first quarter but down 20% from the same quarter a year earlier, as AI data center spending takes a toll.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: hyperscaler capex is now squeezing cash flow hard enough that even a fast-growing cloud looks for cost cuts.
- Amazon journey: AWS still leads Azure on revenue ($150 billion vs $101.9 billion), though Azure grows faster, and AWS is cutting too.
- Anthropic journey: Anthropic is poaching Microsoft talent, pushing up Microsoft's retention costs.
- OpenAI journey: Azure Core, now under Ulag, provides compute to OpenAI.
