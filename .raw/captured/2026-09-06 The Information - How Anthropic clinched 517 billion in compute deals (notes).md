# Notes: How Anthropic Clinched $517 Billion in Compute Deals in 11 Months

The Information, Valida Pau, 2026-09-06 (Sunday Insights analysis). Tiffany Li contributed.

## The core story

- Over the past year Anthropic rushed to sign cloud computing deals (SpaceX, Google and others) to keep up with demand for Claude Code and Cowork, which surprised the company this year.
- With a possibly record-setting IPO (first public share sale) coming, investors want to know if that compute will be enough.
- Anthropic has never disclosed its total compute. The Information built an estimate from public statements and its own past reporting.
- Since October, Anthropic has agreed to at least 14.8 gigawatts of compute it could use over several years. Before October it had 1 to 2 GW (per a person familiar).
- Total potential cost: up to $517 billion, mostly over the next decade, covering rented cloud capacity, chips and data center leases.
- That is far above the $180 billion it told investors in December 2025 it would spend on renting servers through 2029.

## Caveats on the number

- There may be unannounced deals, and Anthropic may not use all the capacity it signed for.
- Hard to compare with OpenAI because Anthropic's deals often run past 2030. Example: a 20-year direct lease with former crypto miner Riot Platform in Texas. Most known deals run three to six years.

## Versus OpenAI

- Anthropic's planned capacity looks much smaller than OpenAI's, even though Anthropic's annualized revenue (current monthly revenue times 12) passed $65 billion per Bloomberg, above OpenAI's more than $40 billion as of July.
- OpenAI told investors in April it plans 30 GW by 2030. Last month it projected about $750 billion of compute spending through 2030 (per a person familiar), up from $665 billion earlier this year.
- OpenAI executives say their compute deals (Oracle, Microsoft, CoreWeave and others) give them an edge.
- Scale check: 1 GW can power a city the size of San Francisco.
- Investors want compute and spending updates in the IPO prospectus (the formal disclosure document), which could come out in weeks. Enough servers decides whether Anthropic can grow without hurting margins (profit per dollar of sales) or turning customers away.

## Who supplies Anthropic

- CFO Krishna Rao has made compute deals a priority.
- Amazon and Google, early partners and investors, are still the biggest: 11 GW costing more than $300 billion over about 10 years.
- Microsoft (November): at least $30 billion for roughly 1 GW of Azure servers running Nvidia chips. Notable because Microsoft is OpenAI's longtime backer.
- SpaceX (May): $1.25 billion a month through May 2029 for AI servers, up to $45 billion total.
- Smaller GPU buyers ("neoclouds"): last month, $80 billion of contracts with Lambda and U.K.-based Nscale for at least 460 megawatts over six years.

## Leasing and building its own

- November: plan to invest $50 billion with cloud startup Fluidstack to build its own data centers in Texas and New York, starting to come online this year.
- A lease with former bitcoin miner TeraWulf for a Kentucky AI data center that will eventually supply 401 MW.
- Chips it plans to buy: in April, multiple gigawatts of Google TPUs (tensor processing units, Google's AI chips, designed by Broadcom); in July, 2 GW of AMD chips.

## Why the capacity may not show up

- Developers need land near substations and power and gas lines, plus local permits.
- Towns push back over noise and energy strain. There are shortages of components and grid connections.
- SpaceX is laying groundwork for a foundry to make turbine vanes and blades for natural gas turbines, per The Information.
- Contracts can often be torn up. In the SpaceX deal, either side can cancel with 90 days' notice.
- The Wall Street Journal first reported OpenAI's latest projections and some Anthropic deals.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Compute scarcity: a lab with more revenue than OpenAI still has much less locked-in capacity, so compute, not demand, is the limit.
- Model labs vs clouds: Anthropic now buys from Amazon, Google, Microsoft, SpaceX and neoclouds, and is starting to build and lease its own.
- AI economics: $517 billion in possible commitments against $65 billion annualized revenue, and those commitments are soft (90-day exits).
- Agents: the demand surge is driven by Claude Code and Cowork, agent-style products.
