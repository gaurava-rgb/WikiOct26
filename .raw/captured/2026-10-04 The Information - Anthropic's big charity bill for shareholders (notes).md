# Notes: Anthropic's Big Charity Bill for Shareholders

The Information, Cory Weinberg, 2026-10-04 (Sunday Insights).

## The core story

- Financials Anthropic shared with prospective IPO investors show a large cost from its employee charity match: the company matches stock that staff pledge to charity with its own stock.
- That match is a non-cash expense (no cash leaves the company), but it dilutes other shareholders because new shares are issued. Dilution means each existing share owns a smaller slice.
- The reporter's analysis: after the IPO, the expense could grow into the billions as employees cash in on pledges.

## Key facts and numbers

- More than $660 million in charity-match expense from October 2025 to March 2026 (per investors).
- About $125 million in Q1 2026: 10% of employee expenses and 2% of total operating costs. Later quarters not known.
- 2025 charitable contributions: $540 million (figures shared with investors). The next highest comparable non-cash donation in Fortune 500 filings was BlackRock at $109 million (Calcbench data).
- Match terms: 3-to-1 for employees who joined in 2024 or earlier, on up to half their stock; 1-to-1 on up to a quarter of stock for newer hires.
- Anthropic excluded the expense (with stock compensation and a large legal settlement) from its adjusted operating profit. Adjusted profit is a company-chosen measure that strips out non-cash or one-off costs.
- Valuation path: under $20 billion two years ago, $965 billion in May 2026, expected $1.5 trillion or more at IPO (reporter's account).
- Anthropic will likely be the largest listed public benefit corporation in the U.S. A public benefit corporation is legally allowed to weigh a public mission alongside profit.

## Named people and roles

- Dario Amodei, CEO, and six co-founders: pledged to give away at least 80% of their wealth; not eligible for the match.
- Pranav Ghai, Calcbench CEO: other firms' similar expenses are "trivial" by comparison; excluding charity from adjusted profit is "not common at all."
- Former employees: group chats discuss where to give IPO money (global poverty, AI safety, animal welfare).

## Change versus earlier views

- The match was framed by executives as a recruiting tool and proof of mission. This article reframes it as a material shareholder cost and an operating-loss driver.
- Many staff align with effective altruism (a movement using reasoning and giving to tackle neglected global problems). Donations may hand AI safety groups Anthropic equity, deepening ties some critics call too cozy.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Who pays: public investors will partly fund Anthropic's culture and mission through dilution.
- A company journey note for Anthropic's IPO: watch adjusted versus reported profit, since several real costs sit outside the adjusted figure.
- Links to the Microsoft/Meta Claude cutback story (same week) on Anthropic's pre-IPO pressures.
