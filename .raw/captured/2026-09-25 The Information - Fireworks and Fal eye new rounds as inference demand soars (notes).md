# Notes: Fireworks, Fal Consider New Rounds as Inference Demand Soars

The Information, Valida Pau, Stephanie Palazzolo and Aaron Holmes, 2026-09-25 (exclusive).

## The core story

- Startups that sell access to AI models and servers, such as Fal and Fireworks AI, are booming as developers use them to run models fast. Investors are rushing in.
- "Inference" means running a trained AI model to get answers. Inference providers rent out that service through an API, a simple plug-in point for developers.

## Fal

- Specializes in inference for video and image generation models, such as Google's Nano Banana models.
- Has talked to investors about raising at a $15 billion valuation (per two people). Talks are early; it could aim for $17 billion to $20 billion (per a third person).
- Either way, that roughly doubles the $8 billion valuation from a round this spring. Fal is five years old.
- Annualized revenue jumped to $800 million, double its March pace (per one person). Annualized means the current monthly rate times 12.

## Fireworks AI

- One of the biggest inference providers by revenue. Considering a new round that could target a $30 billion valuation (per a third person), nearly double its July round.
- On Friday it said it completed a $132 million sale of employee stock, led by Atreides Management, at a $17.5 billion valuation, the same as its July round. An employee stock sale (a "secondary") lets staff cash out shares; the company raises no new money.
- Annualized revenue hit $1 billion by July, five times a year earlier. More recent revenue could not be learned.

## Peers

- Baseten is in talks to raise at a $26 billion valuation (reported by Axios).
- Modal is in talks at about $15 billion, roughly triple its value from a round four months ago (reported by Bloomberg).

## Why demand is strong

- These firms give access to frontier models and help customers customize open-source models.
- Open-source models are often cheaper than OpenAI's and Anthropic's.
- Running open models on your own chips is hard to set up and expensive given the chip shortage, so many developers prefer an easy API from Fireworks or Fal.

## The risks

- Price war: closed-model makers like OpenAI and Anthropic are cutting prices harder. Replit CEO Amjad Masad said at The Information's AI Agenda Live conference Wednesday that Replit probably uses less open-source AI now than at the start of the year, because some OpenAI models got so cheap.
- Margin squeeze: the compute these firms rent from cloud providers is getting more expensive.
- Gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of delivering the service. Inference providers have historically run about 50%, versus 70%-plus at the best software firms.
- Their answer: better optimize how models run on chips. Together AI, which offers inference and rents AI servers, has also been buying servers and renting them out from its own data centers.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Compute scarcity: chip shortages push developers to rent inference rather than run models themselves, which creates a middle layer of fast-growing firms.
- Model labs vs clouds: inference startups sit between open-source models and closed labs, and lab price cuts directly threaten them.
- AI economics: valuations doubling and tripling in months sit on top of roughly 50% gross margins and rising rented-compute costs.
