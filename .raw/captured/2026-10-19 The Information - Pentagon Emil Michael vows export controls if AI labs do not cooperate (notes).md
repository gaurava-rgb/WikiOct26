# Notes: Pentagon’s Emil Michael Vows Export Controls if AI Labs Don’t Cooperate With Government

The Information, Leo Schwartz and Amir Efrati, 2026-10-19 (interview).

## Enforcement threats and the drunk driver analogy

- Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering Emil Michael warned in an interview that the Trump administration will use export controls and other executive authorities against AI frontier labs that fail to cooperate on national security.
- Michael serves as one of four leaders on President Trump's Super Intelligence Force task force, acting as government liaison to frontier AI companies on cybersecurity breaches and hacks.
- Michael likened frontier labs calling for government regulation to drunk drivers demanding someone else pull them over rather than self-regulating.
- Michael affirmed the administration readiness to impose stringent measures, pointing to export controls placed on Anthropic in June 2026 that cut off model access to non-United States users.

## Feud and ongoing engagement with Anthropic

- Michael previously led a public feud with Anthropic, pushing for the Defense Department to designate the AI lab as a supply chain risk after Anthropic refused to remove safety guardrails.
- Michael argued against anthropomorphizing AI models, criticizing Anthropic for giving Claude the autonomy to end conversations with abusive users.
- Michael stated he will not permit Anthropic inside the Defense Department as long as that moral principle is embedded in its software.
- Despite the dispute and ongoing litigation, Michael emphasized that both sides act as adults; he stated he has met Anthropic co-founder and Washington representative Tom Brown many times to discuss incident response protocols.

## Chinese open source and corporate espionage risks

- Michael acknowledged growing national security concerns regarding Chinese open-source AI models gaining traction in the United States, given their allegiance to the Chinese government.
- Rather than mandating model weight audits, Michael favored promoting domestic open-source alternatives like Reflection, Thinking Machines, and Nvidia Nemotron, acknowledging none are at the frontier yet.
- Michael highlighted corporate espionage risks following the arrest of an OpenAI Saudi national engineer for planting bugs, recommending tech firms use FBI background screening services for foreign national personnel.

## Defense Department supply chain investments

- Through the Office of Strategic Capital, the Defense Department is preparing loans to bolster domestic microelectronics and reduce dependence on ASML and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co (TSMC).
- The department has already issued hundreds of millions of dollars in loans for drones, rare earths, and critical minerals, and is targeting drone motors and batteries sourced from adversaries.
- Michael stated he had no visibility into whether software errors from Palantir contributed to a February 2026 United States missile strike in Iran that killed over 150 people.

- Anthropic journey: highlights intense political and regulatory tensions with the Pentagon over Claude safety guardrails and supply chain risk labeling.
- Trump administration tech policy: shifts away from mandatory safety frameworks toward national security compliance enforced by export controls.
- OpenAI journey and enterprise security: underscores government alarm over foreign espionage at frontier AI labs.
- Defense tech and hardware supply chains: reveals Pentagon financing strategy to build domestic alternatives to TSMC and ASML fabrication dependencies.
