# Notes: Andreessen Horowitz to Drop ‘Crypto’ From Name of Flagship Blockchain Fund

The Information, Leo Schwartz, 2026-10-22.

## Rebranding to a16z frontier

- Venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz informed limited partners that its dedicated blockchain unit, a16z crypto, will change its name to a16z frontier.
- The rebranded vehicle will broaden its investment scope beyond digital assets and blockchain into emerging frontier technologies.
- Across five dedicated funds launched since 2018, the firm's crypto division raised nearly $10 billion in committed capital.
- General partner Ali Yahya, who joined Andreessen Horowitz in 2017 as the crypto group's first full-time member, is departing to build a startup.
- Under general partner Chris Dixon, the fund backed high-profile crypto firms such as Coinbase, Uniswap, and Sky Mavis, raising a peak $4.5 billion vehicle in 2022.
- The unit's latest fund, announced in May 2026, closed at $2.2 billion, less than half the size of its 2022 fund, reflecting a broader pullback in blockchain startup enthusiasm.
- The unit had already branched out by investing in non-blockchain startups, including prediction market Kalshi and compute market infrastructure firm Ornn.

## Broad shift across venture firms

- Andreessen Horowitz follows other leading crypto-native venture capital firms in expanding beyond pure blockchain mandates.
- In May 2026, Haun Ventures, led by former a16z partner Katie Haun, expanded its mandate to non-crypto financial services startups across $1 billion in new capital.
- In August 2026, Paradigm announced its $1.2 billion fund would invest in artificial intelligence, robotics, and other technical frontiers alongside blockchain.
- Chris Dixon acknowledged on social media that non-financial consumer Web3 use cases like play-to-earn gaming and decentralized social networks had fallen short, arguing that crypto had entered a financial era focused on stablecoins and agentic payments.

## Political and regulatory landscape

- The firm spent tens of millions of dollars backing crypto-friendly political candidates through the Fairshake super PAC network during the 2024 election cycle.
- Co-founder Marc Andreessen publicly supported Donald Trump following regulatory friction with the Biden administration.
- Despite legislative passage of the Genius Act in mid-2025 to regulate dollar-backed stablecoins, the industry faced an extended downturn in 2026 compounded by the failure of the Clarity Act in Congress.
