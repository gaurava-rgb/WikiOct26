# Notes: OpenAI's 'Pacing 2.0' Problem: How Smart Is Too Smart?

The Information, Stephanie Palazzolo and Amir Efrati, 2026-10-20 (newsletter analysis).

## Pacing 1.0 versus Pacing 2.0

- Sam Altman introduced the concept of "pacing 2.0" internally at OpenAI.
- "Pacing 1.0" describes the current approach: continuously releasing frontier models that pass internal safety checks while disclosing associated safety risks.
- "Pacing 2.0" demands setting formal speed limits on recursive self-improvement and establishing objective thresholds for when to halt development or public release of ultra-advanced AI.
- Defining thresholds is challenging: measuring whether a model is 10 times or 10,000 times smarter than a human scientist lacks clear technical metrics.
- Altman warned OpenAI cannot afford to yield competitive ground now, but must decide on its long-term pacing approach within a year.

## Big compute deals and security incidents

- Anthropic is negotiating a second compute deal with developer Nscale valued at around $45 billion, following an earlier $45 billion six-year agreement for 460 megawatts of Nvidia Vera Rubin GPUs at Nscale's West Virginia campus.
- Cybersecurity firm NorForge reported a Chinese-speaking hacker used DeepSeek and Anthropic models with roughly $5,000 in tokens to compromise websites of more than 100 companies.
- The United States government sanctioned a Chinese startup and venture capital firm tied to Linwei Ding, a former Google software engineer imprisoned for stealing Google AI trade secrets.
- AI chip developer Tenstorrent raised $1.5 billion in Series E funding at a $6.2 billion valuation, led by Trace Capital and Innovation X.
- Note-taking tool Otter.ai raised $320 million from General Catalyst's Customer Value Fund.
- Robotic grasping startup Eka Robotics raised $150 million Series A at a $1.45 billion post-money valuation led by Andreessen Horowitz and F-Prime.
