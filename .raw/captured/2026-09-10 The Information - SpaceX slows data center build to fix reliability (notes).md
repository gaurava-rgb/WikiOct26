# Notes: SpaceX Overhauls Data Center Build-Out, Potentially Slowing Expansion

The Information, Grace Kay, 2026-09-10 (exclusive).

## The core story

- A new team of SpaceX rocket engineers now runs the data centers of xAI (Musk's AI company, acquired by SpaceX earlier in 2026). They are trading speed for reliability, which may slow new campuses (per people familiar).
- Changes: install more backup power and cooling, and test it before a site goes live instead of adding it later (per two people). Use fewer temporary power and cooling systems (per one person).
- In some cases they are redesigning parts of existing data centers.
- This reverses xAI's 2024 approach at Colossus, its Tennessee campus, where Musk put speed and computing capacity ahead of reliability.
- SpaceX did not respond to requests for comment.

## Why reliability became urgent

- Early on, frequent outages forced AI researchers to pause projects for hours and lose some training progress (per one person).
- Reliability matters more now that SpaceX rents computing capacity to other companies, such as Anthropic and Google.
- The new managers told staff this summer they were brought in to improve reliability in Tennessee and Mississippi (per two people).
- Reliability had improved earlier this year, but there are signs of new problems: last week, construction work hit a power line and caused a high-profile outage at a Memphis data center. It knocked some Grok models offline and hurt some compute customers, SpaceX said.
- Musk on X afterward: the company is "taking corrective action to ensure this does not happen again."

## How the fast build worked, and its costs

- SpaceX says the AI unit brought its first data center online with 100,000 GPUs (graphics chips used for AI) in 122 days. Such a build usually takes years. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang called the effort "superhuman" and "unbelievable."
- SpaceX even cited build speed in its June IPO paperwork (filings to list shares on the stock market).
- xAI hired thousands of contractors and ran tasks in parallel: cooling pipes, electrical and network cabling all at once, often in the same areas.
- Big cloud firms usually build in phases to keep things reliable (per one person).
- xAI brought minimum power and cooling online first and added some backups months later. It relied on temporary gear like dozens of gas turbines since opening (per one person).
- The first two data centers had only a fraction of the redundancy (spare backup systems) of other hyperscalers (giant cloud operators). One equipment failure could take whole clusters offline.
- Team members could approve plan changes and buy equipment as needed rather than optimizing cost, creating a mix of different parts instead of uniform racks and servers. That may also hurt reliability.
- SpaceX probably will not fully abandon fast building (per one person).

## Musk's involvement

- Musk seems to back the shift. He has visited Memphis almost weekly, most Sundays, including this past Sunday (per two people).

## Leadership exodus

- SpaceX closed the xAI deal in February while xAI was burning cash heavily. A few months later, SpaceX leaders held fact-finding meetings with the data center team. Similar meetings at xAI's Palo Alto office came shortly before dozens of engineers left.
- In late June, Musk replaced data center veterans with people from the rocket launch team.
- Wesley Salandro (7+ years at SpaceX, VP of production for Falcon and Dragon rockets) took over from Dan Rowland, who led infrastructure engineering, once reported to Musk, and earlier worked on Tesla's Dojo supercomputer. Rowland left soon after.
- SpaceX asked rocket and Starlink engineers in Bastrop, Texas and Cape Canaveral, Florida to volunteer for 6 to 8 week rotations. More than 1,300 volunteered; over 300 joined in the past month (per two people).
- More than a dozen data center leaders left in recent weeks, including Jake Palmer (physical infrastructure), Logan Beach (security), Tiffany Wilson (procurement), Pablo Mendoza (finance). Aakash Verma (Duke Energy site engineer) and Brent Mayo (site builds manager) joined OpenAI in August. Designer Liz Balke now works on Anthropic's infrastructure team.
- SpaceX added middle managers to a once-flat structure, so workers have less freedom to order equipment or decide onsite, which can slow building (per two people).

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Compute scarcity has a quality side: fast-built capacity breaks, and outages now hit paying customers like Anthropic and Google.
- Model labs vs clouds: SpaceX/xAI is becoming a compute landlord, renting capacity to rival labs.
- Talent flows between labs (xAI staff to OpenAI and Anthropic) show infrastructure skills are in demand.
