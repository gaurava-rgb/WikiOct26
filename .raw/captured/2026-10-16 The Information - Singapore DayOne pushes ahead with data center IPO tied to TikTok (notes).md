# Notes: Singapore’s DayOne Pushes Ahead With Data Center IPO Tied to TikTok

The Information, Claudia Chong and Juro Osawa, 2026-10-16 (exclusive).

## The core story

- DayOne Data Centers, a Singapore-based data center operator, is preparing for an initial public offering in New York to raise as much as $5 billion.
- ByteDance accounts for nearly 70% of DayOne revenue (69.2% in the first half of 2026 and about the same in 2025), per two people with knowledge of operations.
- DayOne revenue from ByteDance supports conventional uses like video streaming and e-commerce transactions, not AI training or inference.
- DayOne acknowledges that this high customer concentration creates a significant business risk.

## Corporate origins and international expansion

- DayOne originated four years ago as the overseas unit of Chinese data center giant GDS Holdings.
- To navigate United States and China geopolitical tensions, GDS created GDS International, based in neutral Singapore, with independent management.
- In late 2024, GDS International was spun off after raising $1.87 billion in Series A and B rounds, reducing GDS ownership to 35.6%.
- In January 2025, the unit rebranded as DayOne; top shareholders Coatue Management, Hillhouse, and GDS now each own about 19%.
- As of September 2026, ByteDance had committed to 838 megawatts in capacity across DayOne sites in Malaysia, Thailand, and Finland.
- In Finland, DayOne partnered with Nordic firm Hyperco on two data centers in Kouvola and Lahti; TikTok is investing 2 billion euros ($2.2 billion) under Project Clover to store European user data locally.
- In 2022, TikTok Shop users in Southeast Asia quadrupled spending to $4.4 billion.

## AI ambitions and diversification away from ByteDance

- DayOne builds data centers and manages power, connectivity, and cooling, but does not provide computing servers or chips.
- Because United States export rules restrict advanced Nvidia chips to Chinese firms, DayOne cannot work directly with Chinese companies on AI data centers.
- Instead, DayOne is expanding AI data center capacity for Western cloud providers, led by Oracle.
- Oracle is DayOne second-largest customer, representing 15% of first-half 2026 revenue.
- For contracts starting in 2027, ByteDance represents less than 50% of total commitments.
- The next three largest customers after ByteDance and Oracle are non-Chinese companies.
- Chinese AI developers can still indirectly benefit DayOne by renting Nvidia servers installed in overseas data centers operated by Western clouds.

## Market context and rival IPO stumbles

- DayOne is pursuing its New York IPO despite investor hesitation that derailed rival AI data center listings.
- In Australia, Nvidia-backed neocloud Firmus Technologies scrapped its $5.5 billion IPO last week due to concerns over its $30.6 billion valuation and debt pile.
- Blackstone-backed data center operator AirTrunk postponed a $1.5 billion real estate investment trust listing in Singapore.
- In June 2026, Firmus agreed to work with DayOne on a data center in Batam, Indonesia, with at least 170,000 advanced Nvidia server chips.
- Meta Platforms announced in September 2026 that it would lease capacity from Firmus facilities in Southeast Asia.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift story: shows how data center physical infrastructure (power, cooling, space) separates from GPU ownership.
- Neocloud and infrastructure shakeout: contrasts conventional data center cash flows with troubled debt-heavy pure-play AI clouds like Firmus.
- Geopolitics and export controls: Chinese tech firms must rely on overseas third-party data centers in Southeast Asia and Europe to stay compliant with regulations.
