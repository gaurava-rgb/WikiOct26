# Notes: Nvidia Moves to Back GPU Cloud Provider Firmus After Investors Balk at Its IPO

The Information, Phoebe Liu, 2026-10-20 (exclusive).

## Nvidia's rescue talks with Firmus

- Nvidia is negotiating to invest hundreds of millions of dollars in Australian GPU cloud provider Firmus after Firmus canceled its planned initial public offering earlier in October.
- Firmus had sought a $30 billion valuation to raise $5 billion on the Australian Securities Exchange, but pulled the deal amid investor skepticism over high valuation, speculative revenue, and general market volatility.
- Firmus has raised over $13 billion to date, including roughly $10 billion in debt, from backers such as Blackstone, Coatue Management, Jane Street, and Nvidia across three prior rounds.
- Firmus is now attempting to raise at least $2 billion privately, with Nvidia poised to contribute substantial equity capital.

## Nvidia's $170 billion neocloud exposure

- The Information estimates Nvidia has committed more than $170 billion to support neoclouds and large customers through credit guarantees, equity investments, and chip allocations.
- In June 2026, Nvidia agreed to backstop Firmus' purchase of server racks housing at least 170,000 Rubin and Blackwell GPUs valued at roughly $15 billion, destined for a data center in Batam, Indonesia built by developer DayOne.
- Neoclouds are vital to Nvidia because major cloud hyperscalers like Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are developing proprietary server chips to lessen reliance on Nvidia GPUs.
- An internal Nvidia team co-led by Jensen Huang's son-in-law Nico Caprez helps neoclouds locate scarce data center sites with electrical power.

## Adjusting credit support terms

- Nvidia originally offered debt backstops in exchange for a cut of future cloud revenues, but larger neoclouds balked at handing over revenue shares.
- In response, Nvidia revised its credit support framework, offering shorter-term backstops in exchange for a smaller percentage of revenues.
- While Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang insisted CoreWeave does not need further credit backstops, smaller players like Firmus face heavy refinancing needs amid rising borrowing costs.
