# Notes: Uber's M&A Blitz

The Information, Martin Peers, 2026-10-06 (The Briefing newsletter column).

## The core story

- Food delivery is now in a growth-by-acquisition era. M&A means mergers and acquisitions: buying other companies.
- Tuesday: Uber announced a $2.3 billion purchase of ezCater, a business catering company.
- A few weeks earlier, DoorDash bought a college campus meal-delivery business from Wonder for $300 million.
- Peers says neither deal will excite investors, but both show Uber and DoorDash doing whatever they can to keep growth going.

## Europe consolidation

- Both have been rolling up European food delivery: DoorDash bought Deliveroo and Wolt; Uber is buying Delivery Hero.
- But only a handful of such targets exist.
- Uber and DoorDash dominate restaurant delivery and, more and more, groceries. Growing by adding new subscribers keeps getting harder.

## Growth numbers

- Both would be fine without deals.
- DoorDash bookings growth (the total value of orders placed) slowed to 19% at end of 2024 from 36% at end of 2021, then sped back up to 36% over the past 18 months. Much of that came from Deliveroo, but the business had rebounded even before that deal closed.
- Uber's food delivery bookings growth also sped up over the past 18 months, without Delivery Hero (not yet closed). Uber's ride-hailing business slowed in the same period.
- The Delivery Hero deal is much bigger than either DoorDash European deal. Uber is not buying all of it, but the parts it is buying had gross bookings last year of roughly half of Uber Eats. So the hit to Uber's growth rate will be more visible.

## Smaller deals

- DoorDash bought SevenRooms, a hotel software firm, last year.
- Uber, over roughly the past 12 months, bought parking app SpotHero, luxury travel firm Blacklane, and a data collection firm for a new AI data labeling business (labeling data used to train AI models).
- Both throw off lots of cash. Peers's read: when a deal looks promising, both just go for it.

## Skydance is born

- The Ellison family's Paramount Skydance closed its $110 billion purchase of Warner Bros. Discovery on Tuesday, as planned. The new company is called Skydance.
- It owns the Warner Bros. and Paramount studios, HBO Max and Paramount+, CBS and many cable channels.
- Shares (ticker SKYD) fell 25 cents to $9.53, well below the $12 a share paid by the Ellisons and other investors, who put in $47 billion of new equity (owner money, not loans) to fund the deal.
- Biggest issue: $79 billion of debt. Peers expects the press to call it "debt-laden."

## Other news

- Goldman Sachs is promoting Jane Dunlevie, a senior tech banker who worked on deals for OpenAI, Stripe and Instacart, to co-lead its technology, media and telecom investment banking group (per an internal memo seen by The Information).
- Kling AI, spun out of Chinese video app Kuaishou, picked banks for a Hong Kong IPO that could raise at least $1 billion (Bloomberg).
- Anthropic is expanding and restructuring its cybersecurity access programs so more defenders can use its most advanced Claude models against possible AI attacks.
- Pinterest named Amazon executive James Dibbo as CFO, its fourth C-suite shakeup this year.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Retail (boxes and delivery): delivery platforms have saturated consumer growth and now buy adjacent niches (corporate catering, campus meals, groceries) to keep bookings up.
- Company journeys: Uber is quietly moving into AI by starting a data labeling business, a supplier role in the AI shift.
- Amazon journey footnote: an Amazon executive leaving to become Pinterest's CFO.
