# Notes: Zuckerberg’s Big Biotech Moment

The Information, The Weekend newsletter, Amy Dockser Marcus, 2026-10-10.

## The Human Genome Project as the yardstick

- The Human Genome Project began in 1990, when Mark Zuckerberg was six. It was a multibillion-dollar international effort led by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and the Department of Energy (DOE).
- Ten years later, in 2000, it announced the first sequencing of the human genetic code. President Bill Clinton said "We are learning the language in which God created life."
- This week another science collaboration was compared to that project, at a reception hosted by Biohub, the main philanthropy of Zuckerberg and his wife, Priscilla Chan.
- The writer frames the event as one of several recent signs of a changing of the scientific guard from major universities to big tech companies.

## The Biohub reception

- The event was at the Treehouse Hotel in Sunnyvale, Calif., with around 100 tech executives, scientists and academics.
- Zuckerberg said he and Chan started the first version of what is now Biohub almost 10 years ago to the day, aiming to cure and prevent all diseases by the end of the century.
- "We got a lot of eye rolls," Zuckerberg said. Thanks to AI and other tech advances, he said, "That no longer seems quite so crazy."
- Lori Goler, president of the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, announced that Biohub is partnering with the NIH and the DOE in a $1.8 billion investment to build new technologies and scientific infrastructure for "AI-powered biology."
- Biohub launched its "Virtual Biology Initiative" in April 2026 with a $500 million commitment.
- At the event Biohub also said Meta, Google DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs (a DeepMind spinoff) agreed to put in $300 million.
- Goler said "We were inspired by the Human Genome Project," and hopes the initiative will have the same level of impact.
- Goler will speak on the future of AI biology at The Information's WTF Summit later in October.

## Science's private work and public moments

- The writer notes labs are quiet places where scientists focus on not contaminating experiments, but results that reach the public spark debate or celebration.
- Examples: the 2000 White House event for the genome scientists, and this week's videos of Nobel Prize winners, such as Stanford neuroscientist Karl Deisseroth telling his kids he won the Nobel Prize for medicine.

## Tech leaders take over the science establishment

- The writer calls Zuckerberg one of the most prominent symbols of how AI is changing science and bringing new leaders into a field once dominated by universities.
- President Trump appointed Zuckerberg in March 2026 to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST, the White House's science advisory panel).
- Other members include Google's Sergey Brin, Oracle's Larry Ellison and Nvidia's Jensen Huang. Of the council's 13 members, only one is an academic researcher.
- On Oct. 8, 2026, Brin and Huang, with other tech figures, received the National Medals of Science from President Trump.
- The same day, Anthropic said it is expanding its partnership with the government: it will give $150 million worth of AI credits and other support to the Genesis Mission.
- The Genesis Mission was launched by Trump in 2025 to use AI to speed up scientific discoveries.

## How the partnership came about

- A Biohub team went to Washington in July 2026 for the first Genesis Mission Summit. The new NIH and DOE partnership grew out of that visit.
- The writer reads the joint stage appearance as shared faith that Silicon Valley and AI are now the favored engines for understanding cells and disease.
- The day after, Goler told the writer Biohub plans to host an annual AI and bio event. "It really is a cultural moment," she said.

## Side note: book pick, "The AGI Chronicles" by Kevin Roose

- The newsletter's reading pick is Kevin Roose's book on the race to build artificial general intelligence (AGI, AI as capable as humans across tasks), which he calls "the strangest decade in our history."
- Per the book, just before Altman, Oracle's Larry Ellison and Trump announced Project Stargate in January 2025, Altman texted Jeff Bezos to check they could use the name, since Amazon owns MGM, which owns the "Stargate" franchise.
- Per the book, Google's Gemini was almost called Titan, a Demis Hassabis suggestion, but lawyers vetoed it as "too aggressive."
- Per Roose, many AI leaders told him they missed being underdogs, when people thought they were crazy to believe AGI was possible.
- The reviewer (Abram Brown) argues the AI race has warped its contestants and magnified their worst tendencies, and expects that to continue.
