# Notes: How Microsoft's New Office Chief Fixed Copilot and Won Over Nadella

The Information, Aaron Holmes, 2026-10-12 (exclusive). Kevin McLaughlin contributed.

## The Anthropic bet

- In summer 2025 customers complained that Copilot, Microsoft's AI assistant, was buggy and weak at automating work in Excel and PowerPoint.
- Charles Lamanna, then a Microsoft president, proposed to CEO Satya Nadella that Microsoft consider paying for Anthropic models to make Copilot better. Lamanna said that at the time it "was heresy."
- Commercial chief Judson Althoff was skeptical, per another employee with knowledge: Anthropic models would cost far more than OpenAI's, which Microsoft can use at no extra cost under its OpenAI partnership.
- Lamanna won Nadella over with demos showing Anthropic models were then better at the math Excel needs and at making good-looking PowerPoints.
- Lamanna, 38: "if you have a belief or a thesis, you just have to go all the way with it."
- Lamanna said the shift mattered because Copilot had been designed around one model maker, OpenAI. He warned that assuming "model monogamy" too long would bake in dependence on one model's behavior and make adding new models very hard later.

## Results so far

- Copilot's corporate users have more than doubled since the start of 2026 to over 30 million.
- Customers such as AT&T say Copilot has improved over the past year.
- Microsoft remains in an "existential battle" to win enterprise customers as AI reshuffles industry power.

## Lamanna's growing role

- Months after the pitch, Nadella gave Lamanna oversight of thousands of Copilot developers, merging a messy set of separate engineering teams.
- In March 2026 Nadella named Lamanna executive vice president over Copilot engineering, reporting directly to him. He shares Copilot oversight with EVP Jacob Andreou.
- Earlier in October 2026 Nadella also put him in charge of Office, after the July 2026 retirement of Rajesh Jha, the EVP who had run Office and Windows among other things.
- Lamanna also runs Teams. His organization is about 25,000 people, more than triple what he oversaw a year earlier.
- Microsoft has over 220,000 employees. Its largest divisions, sales and Azure cloud, each have more than 60,000 full-time staff.

## Succession talk

- Multiple senior executives see Lamanna as a possible successor to Nadella as CEO. Like Nadella he is seen as a wonk who digs into engineering detail, not a charismatic salesman. The two meet weekly.
- Over the past year Nadella has brought Lamanna to quarterly board meetings, along with Andreou, Xbox CEO Asha Sharma, security chief Hayete Gallot and EVP Arun Ulag (who runs Azure's database products).
- Some senior executives read this as a deliberate effort to introduce the board to a range of possible successors.
- Lamanna has not ruled out being a CEO again: "in the abstract, yes," but for now he is focused on Copilot.

## Career background

- He first joined Microsoft as a Notre Dame undergrad intern on OneCare, a now-defunct Windows antivirus program.
- After graduating in 2009 he was an engineer in Microsoft's early cloud unit, left in 2012 to co-found MetricsHub, which Microsoft bought a year later for an undisclosed sum.
- He rose in Scott Guthrie's cloud unit to run business applications, including Dynamics 365 (customer relationship software) and Power Apps (low-code app builder).
- He ran a "no jerks" policy, a break from Microsoft's old cutthroat culture and its ranking system that pitted staff against each other.
- In June 2025 he moved into a role reporting to Jha and by that summer took more ownership of Copilot.

## Why Copilot was a mess

- Separate groups across Windows and Office built their own Copilot features. Jha rewarded groups whose features customers adopted fast by adding those features to every version of Copilot.
- Employees called it a tangled mess; some executives said it caused infighting and credit-stealing.
- By March 2026 Jha announced he would step down, and Nadella unified the Copilot engineering groups under Lamanna. Staff describe a more cohesive unit that moves faster.
- VP of engineering Soufiane Loukili: "If we just stuck with the way we were doing things, we would have fallen behind."

## Agent mode and customers

- After Microsoft added Anthropic models in fall 2025, Office engineers built "agent mode," which steers OpenAI and Anthropic models to spend more time reasoning when automating Office tasks. It went into preview that fall.
- Customers once had to turn agent mode on by hand. Lamanna found it worked better for nearly every task and by spring 2026 made it the default. Internal customer satisfaction data then rose sharply, per someone familiar.
- AT&T VP Mark Austin said AT&T staff use Copilot for the "lion's share" of personal productivity automation. AT&T grew its Copilot licenses over the past year to about 18,000 seats.
- National Life Group CIO Nimesh Mehta said staff found Copilot essentially "useless" in 2023, and much improved when retested in spring 2026. But most of his employees use ChatGPT, and he has not switched back because the early experience left a "sour taste."

## Cost, margins and the model router

- Improving Copilot's gross margins (revenue left after the direct cost of serving it) while keeping customers happy is a top Lamanna priority, per someone with knowledge.
- Over the past six months, as OpenAI models caught back up to Anthropic's, Lamanna had Copilot lean more on OpenAI models for agent mode. That improved margins because Microsoft runs OpenAI models for free, this person said.
- His engineers built a model router (software that picks which model handles a request) using a homegrown LLM that picks the cheapest model able to do a task without losing quality.
- The vast majority of Office Copilot users use "auto" mode, letting the router choose, which also helped margins.
- Microsoft's payments to Anthropic for customer Copilot use grew in 2026 and are on track to exceed $2 billion a year. Swapping in OpenAI models for agent mode and other advanced features has slowed that growth even as usage rises.
- The goal now: pick the right mix of models per task to cut cost without hurting quality, which helps Microsoft compete on Office pricing against alternatives, including Anthropic and OpenAI products. Both labs are key partners and fierce rivals.

## "The Costco of AI"

- Lamanna has told staff Microsoft should be "the Costco of AI," the place everyone from broke students to millionaires goes for the best value at the best price.
- He said Microsoft will not be "some boutique" that only top coders can access at a huge price, and will drive prices down.
- His challenge: get corporate customers to spend more each quarter on Microsoft AI and on the existing Office, Windows and security bundle, while making Copilot cheaper to run.

## New products

- Lamanna drove Copilot Cowork in Office, which resembles Anthropic's Claude Cowork (an AI tool that handles complex computer tasks like managing files).
- He also led the Autopilot AI agent, inspired by the viral success of OpenClaw. Autopilot aims to make Copilot an "always-on" agent that can use other vendors' tools like Workday, so Microsoft software becomes the default place for most work.
- VP Omar Shahine, who leads the Autopilot team, talks daily with Lamanna about outside tools they test, such as Meta's Muse and Instinct's AI agents. Using outside tools is unorthodox at Microsoft. Shahine: "the industry is going through a huge change right now."
- The week before publication Microsoft launched a revamped Copilot super-app combining 365 Copilot with Autopilot and Copilot Cowork, which cost extra based on usage.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI shift 2024-2026 story: Microsoft dropping single-model dependence on OpenAI and routing between models is a clear case of models being treated as swappable inputs.
- Anthropic journey: Microsoft is a $2 billion-plus a year Copilot customer, but is actively shifting work back to free OpenAI models to protect margins.
- OpenAI journey: the free OpenAI license is now Microsoft's main margin lever, which bears out the prediction that Microsoft would favor the free model.
- Seats plus consumption pricing: the super-app charges extra by usage on top of seats.
