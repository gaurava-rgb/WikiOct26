# Notes: Google Is Paying About 100 Digital Publishers for AI Overviews

The Information, Alix Coutures and Catherine Perloff, 2026-09-29 (exclusive; later updated with details on news programs and the August opt-out date).

## The core story

- Google has been paying roughly 100 digital publishers based on how much their content contributes to AI answers in search and other products (per people familiar with the tests).
- The pilot covers AI Overviews (AI summaries at the top of search results), AI Mode and the Gemini chatbot. It started less than a year ago (per a person close to Google).
- Big shift: Google long argued it should not pay publishers whose content answers queries, because search sends them traffic. AI Overviews sharply cut that traffic, leading to complaints and lawsuits.
- Digiday first reported the program but not the payment amounts or publisher count.

## How much it pays

- For several small and midsize sites, payments are less than one-tenth of 1% of their ad revenue (per people familiar).
- One publisher that joined a few months ago has earned about $50,000 to $60,000 so far, not a big part of its business.
- An early joiner earns more than $1 million a year, a bigger slice of its revenue.
- Smaller sites earned less than $1,000 over several months.
- Some larger publishers are staying out because payouts are small, hoping to pressure Google and others to pay more (per three people).
- Others join to learn what content Google's AI values and adapt. One participating executive said Google seems to be testing a deal where it uses the content and decides for itself what it is worth.
- Publishers track usage and pay in Google Search Console (Google's site-performance dashboard).
- If enough publishers stay, this could become the model for how other AI companies pay websites.

## How value is judged

- Not every source counts. If the model checked 100 sites for an answer, perhaps only five (5%) would meaningfully improve it.
- Some participants do not know how pay is calculated or what counts as a meaningful contribution; payments can swing month to month without explanation.
- Pattern so far: topics less covered online but with strong user interest, such as anime and gaming, seem to earn more.
- Why Google needs publishers: models trained on old internet content go stale. Google checks answers against fresh search content to cut hallucinations (when a model confidently states something plainly wrong).

## Google's evolving stance

- After years of resisting, and under pressure from regulators and lawmakers abroad, Google increasingly signed licensing deals with news publishers.
- 2024: licensed Reddit data to train Gemini.
- January 2025: deal with the Associated Press for a real-time news feed in the Gemini app. That program includes more than 200 publications and is testing audio briefings.
- Recent months: paying publishers such as The Washington Post and The Guardian to make their content more visible in AI article overviews in Google News.

## The traffic damage

- World History Encyclopedia (not in the pilot) says Google traffic fell 25% to 35% in each of the past two years and is down about 50% from 2024. Founder Jan van der Crabben said pages with an AI Overview were hit hardest.

## Legal and regulatory pressure

- Penske Media sued Google twice in four months:
  - September 2025: claimed AI Overviews drained traffic and cut affiliate revenue (commissions from shopping links), and illegally tied AI Overview inclusion to search indexing.
  - January: sought damages based on a federal court finding that Google illegally monopolized ad tech, which lowered prices Penske could charge for ads.
- Google rejected both, saying publishers can block indexing and that advertisers and publishers choose Google because it is more affordable.
- June: U.K. regulators ordered Google to let publishers opt out of AI search features without losing regular search ranking.
- December 2025: the European Commission opened an antitrust probe into whether Google uses publisher content for AI without fair pay or a real choice to refuse.
- August: Google finished an opt-out letting publishers refuse use in AI Overviews, AI Mode or Google Discover without losing search visibility.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics: a first working price tag for "content that feeds AI answers," set by Google itself, and mostly tiny.
- Agents and the open web: AI answers cut clicks (about 50% traffic loss for one site since 2024), weakening the old search-for-traffic bargain.
- Retail angle: Penske's lost affiliate revenue shows how AI answers can reroute shopping discovery away from publisher sites.
