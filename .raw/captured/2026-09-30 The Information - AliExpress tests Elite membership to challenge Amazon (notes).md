# Notes: Alibaba's AliExpress Tests Membership Program in Challenge to Amazon

The Information, Juro Osawa (Jing Yang contributed), 2026-09-30 (exclusive).

## The core story

- AliExpress, Alibaba's international shopping site, is testing a VIP membership called **Elite** to win and keep big spenders, in a challenge to Amazon (per a person with direct knowledge). Not previously reported.
- Invitation only, for customers who spend more than $800 a year on AliExpress.
- Pilot markets: Australia, the U.K., Germany and Spain. Next month it is planned for more European markets and South Korea.
- A U.S. launch is planned, but timing is undecided.
- AliExpress internally projects 1 million Elite members by end of this year.

## How Elite differs from Prime

- Amazon Prime charges a paid subscription. Elite charges no fee.
- Benefits: an extra 5% off a wide range of products, round-the-clock online customer service, and hassle-free returns.

## Why now: the cheap-parcel model is under pressure

- Shein and Temu led a hard price war that shook the industry. Their growth slowed sharply last year after the U.S. raised tariffs (import taxes) on e-commerce parcels. The EU is copying that move.
- Platforms built on shipping straight from China (Shein, Temu, AliExpress) are shifting toward branded goods and premium services instead of rock-bottom prices.
- TikTok Shop is testing TikTok Shop Plus this year, a paid membership similar to Prime.
- In November the EU adds a 2 euro handling fee per parcel from outside the region, on top of a 3 euro levy per parcel worth up to 150 euros.
- Independent analyst Juozas Kaziukenas: Europe is now the least favorable market for direct-from-China apps because of the per-item fee plus VAT (a sales tax). He said the apps could not absorb the fees, so they raised prices and cut marketing, which hurt repeat buying and "effectively killed new customer acquisition."

## Alibaba's home market is weak

- Alibaba's China e-commerce business made more than $16 billion in revenue in the quarter through June, still its largest source.
- That was down 8% amid weak Chinese consumer spending, which pushes Alibaba to grow abroad.

## Copying Amazon's playbook

- AliExpress is testing next-day delivery in European cities with strong logistics, such as Madrid, Paris and Warsaw, starting with items shipped from local warehouses. It may expand to more markets.
- Monthly active users in Europe as of June: AliExpress 157.6 million vs Amazon 193.9 million (from disclosures under Europe's Digital Services Act, an EU law that forces big platforms to report user numbers).

## The cost of the earlier price war

- A few years ago AliExpress fought Shein and Temu by overhauling its supply chain and launching **Choice**, with ultra-low prices and sometimes free delivery.
- Choice lifted revenue but drove major losses. In the quarter through March 2024, Alibaba's international e-commerce arm lost $566 million before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. That measure (often called EBITDA) shows operating results before financing costs and accounting charges for wear on assets.

## The pivot is partly working

- Over the past year Alibaba raised the share of branded goods on AliExpress, instead of cheaper items from unknown makers. In Spain, Germany and Poland, branded goods are now about 50% (per the person).
- AliExpress posted an operating profit in the quarter through June. Operating profit means money left after running costs, before interest and taxes.
- Revenue of Alibaba's international e-commerce division (AliExpress plus businesses like Southeast Asia's Lazada) fell 1% year over year to $4.09 billion that quarter.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Retail beat: the cheap-box era (direct-from-China parcels) is ending under tariffs and per-parcel fees, so players turn to brands, local warehouses and next-day delivery.
- Amazon journey beat: Prime's model of loyalty perks and fast delivery is now the template rivals copy (AliExpress Elite, TikTok Shop Plus), though Elite skips the fee.
- Retail economics: AliExpress traded growth for profit, showing the earlier price war was not sustainable.
