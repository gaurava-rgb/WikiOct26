# Notes: DeepSeek Bets Big on Huawei Chips to Bypass U.S. Export Controls

The Information, Juro Osawa and Qianer Liu, 2026-09-21 (exclusive).

## The core story

- DeepSeek CEO Liang Wenfeng told investors that training models on more Chinese-made chips is a major priority. He expects Huawei to start delivering training chips as early as Q4 2026 (per two people with direct knowledge).
- Training (teaching a model from data) needs more powerful chips than running a finished model. So using Huawei for training is a bigger step than using it for running models.
- Both companies are working together to escape U.S. export controls (rules that block sales of advanced chips to China).
- Liang called this one of DeepSeek's biggest bets and said it "has to work."
- He expects a new batch of Huawei training chips in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027, without saying how much supply.
- DeepSeek and Huawei did not immediately comment.

## The secretive investor meeting

- The remarks came at a closed-door meeting on Sunday, as DeepSeek finalizes its second funding round: 50 billion yuan ($7.5 billion) at a 500 billion yuan valuation.
- Investors had to come to the Beijing or Hangzhou offices; Liang joined online. They handed over phones, devices and bags and got pen and paper for notes.
- Reason: the transcript of a long video call with investors in May (Liang's first time talking to a large investor group) leaked to Chinese media and social media. DeepSeek paused the second round for a week after that.

## Still leaning on Nvidia

- DeepSeek began testing more Huawei chips last year because of ongoing limits on Nvidia's advanced chips (The Information reported).
- Liang thinks Huawei chips will match Nvidia's within a few years, so DeepSeek should adapt first.
- But DeepSeek still trains new models on Nvidia chips, likely from the black market.
- Liang said DeepSeek is investing about 30 billion yuan to expand computing capacity. It raised 50 billion yuan in June.
- In the May call (transcript confirmed by The Information), Liang estimated a model on par with OpenAI's largest would need about 50,000 Nvidia GB300 chips or 200,000 Huawei Ascend 950 chips. He said then that Huawei could not supply enough.

## Bigger models

- Model size is measured in parameters (the numbers a model learns in training that shape its answers). Bigger models need more chips for training and inference (running the model to answer questions).
- DeepSeek is training a 2-trillion-parameter model, bigger than its V4 flagship at 1.4 trillion. It plans an 8-trillion-parameter model.
- Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 has 2.8 trillion and Moonshot is building bigger. Anthropic and OpenAI do not disclose sizes; industry estimates put their latest at several trillion or more.

## Huawei's supply limits

- Huawei is held back by limited access to advanced memory (the part that lets AI chips move lots of data fast) and other component shortages (per two people).
- Timing fits Huawei's roadmap from last week: Ascend 950DT (for training and part of inference) in Q4; the stronger 960DT in Q1 2027.
- Huawei said in April its chips handled part of training DeepSeek's smaller V4-Flash, and the two adapted the V4 family to Huawei's Ascend systems.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Compute scarcity, China edition: export controls push DeepSeek toward Huawei, but memory shortages cap Huawei's supply.
- The 4-to-1 chip ratio (200,000 Huawei vs 50,000 Nvidia) is a concrete measure of the gap.
- The model-size race keeps scaling compute needs, feeding AI economics pressure.
