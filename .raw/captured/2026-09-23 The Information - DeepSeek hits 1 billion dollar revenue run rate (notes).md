# Notes: DeepSeek's Annualized Revenue Hits $1 Billion as Startup Finalizes $7.5 Billion Fundraising

The Information, Juro Osawa and Qianer Liu, 2026-09-23 (exclusive).

## The core story

- DeepSeek's annualized revenue run rate (current monthly revenue times 12) hit $1 billion, more than double a few months earlier (per two people with direct knowledge). CEO Liang Wenfeng shared it at a recent investor meeting.
- DeepSeek aims to close its second round by end of October: 50 billion yuan ($7.5 billion) at a 500 billion yuan valuation (about $75 billion). It is also preparing to go public on the Shanghai Stock Exchange.

## What drove growth

- A price hike last month raised model costs for customers by 2.3 to 4.5 times. DeepSeek is still among the cheapest major models.
- Liang said the hike did not shrink the customer base because demand stays strong.
- V4.1-Flash (released earlier this month) and the older V4-Flash perform like much larger models. Customers worldwide use them to power autonomous agents (AI that carries out tasks on its own) cheaply.

## Margins

- Revenue is tiny next to Anthropic and OpenAI, but margins are healthy.
- In July, The Information reported a run rate of $400 million to $500 million and an 82.9% gross margin on API sales in the first seven months of 2026. Gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving it. That beat Anthropic and OpenAI over similar periods.
- The reason: low running costs, by making infrastructure efficient so tasks need fewer chips.

## Revenue is not the priority

- Liang told investors at Sunday's meeting (held under strict anti-leak rules) that making more revenue is not a priority.
- Revenue comes almost entirely from API access (other companies' software calling its models). The chatbot app is free, has no ads, and earns nothing.
- More than 70% of computing capacity goes to training new models; under 30% to inference (running existing models).

## Gaming chips for everyday tasks

- Internal tests show DeepSeek's smaller models run well on gaming graphics chips and can handle most everyday user tasks.
- Using gaming chips for inference would ease the shortage without pulling top chips from training.
- Precedent: after R1's release last year, black-market demand and prices for Nvidia gaming chips in China surged.

## Chinese rivals rising

- Cheap open-source Chinese models are closing the gap with U.S. frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI.
- Z.ai (maker of GLM models, Beijing) said its run rate reached $1.8 billion earlier this month.
- MiniMax said its run rate jumped from $150 million in February to $800 million in August.

## Risks

- Biggest challenge: getting enough compute to train new models under U.S. export limits on Nvidia chips. Liang expects Huawei training chips as early as Q4 2026.
- China's internet regulator is investigating DeepSeek and Moonshot AI after Anthropic alleged both routed sensitive user data to Claude models (reported Tuesday).

## Why it matters for the wiki

- AI economics: a lab with 82.9% gross margins by running cheap, efficient models, a contrast to U.S. labs.
- Price hikes without customer loss suggest demand for cheap agent-capable models is strong.
- Compute scarcity shapes strategy: 70/30 training vs inference split, and gaming chips as a workaround.
