# Notes: The Attractive Economics Behind Anthropic Data Center Sprint

The Information, AI Infrastructure newsletter, Ann Davis Vaughan, 2026-10-11.

## The core question

- Anthropic is pushing its partners to speed up the $530 billion of data center leasing deals it has signed.
- The reason: every server Anthropic plugs in sooner to run its products could be "wildly profitable," per fresh analysis of upcoming AI data center sites, including one it will rent near Baton Rouge, Louisiana.

## The Louisiana site

- The author attended a kickoff event the prior week in rural St. Francisville, Louisiana.
- There Anthropic will run Google's AI server chips at a 330-megawatt site, starting in the second quarter of 2027.
- The developer, Hut 8, wants to grow the site to 1 GW, which would cost many tens of billions of dollars to build and fill with chips.
- Fluidstack will lease and operate the site; Anthropic will supply the Google chips.
- Hut 8, based in Miami, is fairly new to AI infrastructure and runs a legacy bitcoin mining business with Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump.
- Don Jr. attended, along with Louisiana's governor (Jeff Landry), 300 bankers, investors and utility officials, and Anthropic's head of compute, James Bradbury. The site is a muddy 627-acre field with partly built buildings.

## The scale of Anthropic's compute race

- Hut 8 is one of many "fresh horses" Anthropic has added to switch on data centers and grow revenue before and after its IPO, expected next month (November 2026).
- Anthropic has committed to rent or lease about 15 GW of capacity in the coming years from AWS, Google, SpaceXAI and more than a dozen other cloud and data center firms.
- Anthropic also has a multiyear cloud deal with publicly traded IREN, another crypto miner turned AI server provider, that has not been announced, per people familiar.
- Measured AI, an AI construction newsletter, projects Anthropic's capacity at 1.4 GW in 2025, 5 GW in 2026, 9.5 GW in 2027 and 15+ GW in 2028.
- For scale, Microsoft's whole Azure cloud ran at about 5 GW at the end of 2023, serving all customers, before the AI buildout sped up.
- Anthropic's ability to win the compute race against OpenAI will depend more and more on Hut 8, TeraWulf, IREN, Nscale and other current or former bitcoin firms, some of which struggle to raise money from public investors at the valuations they want.

## Valuable energy rights

- These partners matter because they earlier picked up grid-connected power rights at old aluminum smelters, failed green hydrogen sites, renewables-rich land and other overlooked places that bigger cloud providers missed or turned down.
- Such rights are rare and very valuable, given long waits to connect new projects to the grid.
- Example: Apollo-backed Stream Data Centers just bought two failed hydrogen project sites from Plug Power, whose grid connections may be attractive. Anthropic is in talks to lease up to 1 GW of future capacity from Stream.

## Building faster

- Anthropic is pushing partners to move fast. Cooling and electrical equipment leader Vertiv is redesigning products for rapid installation, including at the Hut 8 Louisiana campus.
- Vertiv CEO Giordano Albertazzi said Vertiv packages power modules with batteries, switchboards, cooling and fire protection into prefabricated bundles built in its factories, cutting typical installation time in half.
- Albertazzi: "The whole way of building data centers is changing" so they can be "deployed at speed."

## Why speed is worth so much

- The Louisiana campus will use 330 MW of power to produce 245 MW of usable compute capacity.
- If that capacity comes online even one month early, Anthropic could make an extra $1.3 billion or more in revenue, per investor and analyst estimates. That assumes all of it serves answers (inference, running a trained model) rather than training new models.

## High margin

- Even on schedule, roughly $1.3 billion of revenue a month from a quarter-gigawatt of capacity is very attractive.
- At disclosed rates for recent long-term AI contracts, Anthropic might pay about $200 million a month to lease chips from a cloud provider, says Christian Okoye of Occam Edge, which advises infrastructure investors, lenders and developers.
- That implies an 85% gross profit margin in the Louisiana example, if used only for inference. Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of serving it, as a share of revenue.
- Anthropic is cutting out cloud middlemen in some projects, so its gross margin could get even better.
- Gross margins exclude model-training costs, payroll and other business expenses.
- Okoye's analysis covers sites with Nvidia GPUs and alternatives like Google TPUs (tensor processing units, Google's AI chips). GPUs cost more to rent than TPUs, but Nvidia claims GPUs make far more tokens per watt than any other chip.
- Strong inference economics help explain how Anthropic went from spending about $2.30 on operations per $1 of revenue in spring 2025 to a slight profit on that basis in the June 2026 quarter.

## Caveats

- At the start of 2026, Anthropic projected its model-training costs would be more than twice its inference spending.
- Its server-rental spending spree this year, from SpaceX and others, is sure to depress margins.
- Big Claude customers Microsoft and Meta have cut spending with Anthropic, and it faces more competition from OpenAI and open source models.
- For now, Anthropic still acts as if it has pricing power over most customers, per colleague Kevin's recent reporting.
- A problem for the wider industry and capital markets: Anthropic may be the only company with such pricing power. Nobody else running generative AI apps, including OpenAI, seems to earn anywhere near Anthropic's gross margin from inference.

## Getting power fast

- Developers working with Anthropic say it focuses on getting power quickly, even if that means cutting electricity use when the grid is strained, running on backup power, or building farther from customers (which adds delay as results travel over fiber).
- Hut 8 CEO Asher Genoot said he constantly asks utilities and partners "what can we do to speed up some of those builds."

## Compute Heat Rate

- The strong economics explain why AI developers like Anthropic would not balk at paying higher power prices, even as rising utility bills become a sensitive issue for consumers.
- Energy analyst Hans Royal of Royal Powers Energy tracks the "Compute Heat Rate": the electricity price an AI inference data center could pay and still earn a return.
- In Q3 2026, facilities powering commercial AI apps could make money even at a power cost of $5,631 per megawatt-hour, more than 100 times the typical wholesale grid price of $50 per MWh.
- Royal said the most advanced AI makers, such as Anthropic, could technically still profit at $12,791 per MWh.
- The author reads this as a sign power companies could charge AI data centers far more than they do now.

## A better trade: flexible power

- Instead of simply paying more, Anthropic and partners could use their inference economics to persuade utilities to give AI data centers partial power sooner, if they agree to leave the grid for a few peak hours a year.
- A new paper from Royal Powers and Occam Edge says Anthropic's earnings would cover occasionally switching off.
- Companies are building ways to switch automatically to backup power or move computing elsewhere when the grid is strained.
- This is another reason more AI data centers could lower power costs for everyone: the utility earns more revenue on the same assets, and costs spread over more ratepayers.

## Local finances in Louisiana

- West Feliciana Parish President Kenny Havard got a state bill passed letting local officials cut residents' property taxes 25%, to match the tax discount the parish gives Hut 8.
- Hut 8's tax payments on the site are expected to eventually triple the parish's annual revenue to $130 million from $40 million.
- The author's caveat: all of this assumes energy, chips, construction capacity and debt capital stay plentiful, "still a big 'if.'"

## Other news

- Google is unlocking about 890 MW, nearly a reactor's worth, by funding upgrades at 11 reactors, plus a contract for another 2.7 GW, in a major supply deal with Constellation Energy. This eases a power shortage on the Mid-Atlantic grid where AI demand is surging.
- VEIR, a Microsoft-backed maker of superconducting power delivery systems that pivoted into AI data centers, closed an oversubscribed $110 million Series C, bringing total funding to $225 million. It plans a commercial deployment, likely with a neocloud, for 800-volt DC data center power.

## Why it matters for the wiki

- Anthropic journey: capacity path from 1.4 GW (2025) to 15+ GW (2028), an IPO expected in November 2026, and site-level inference margins near 85%.
- AI shift 2024-2026 story: power, not chips, sets the pace, and former bitcoin miners with grid rights have become key AI suppliers.
- OpenAI journey: no rival, including OpenAI, seems to match Anthropic's inference gross margin.
- Google journey: Google TPUs fill Anthropic sites, and Google is buying nuclear power upgrades from Constellation.
